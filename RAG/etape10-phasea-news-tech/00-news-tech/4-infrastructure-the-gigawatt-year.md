# 4. Infrastructure: the gigawatt year

Kimi K3 (July, 2.8T parameters, open weights promised), Thinking Machines' Inkling (July, 975B), DeepSeek V4 (planned), and China's WAICO governance bid (July) sketched a deliberate open-weight offensive running parallel to Western API moats. All items are single-sourced; the pattern is noted, the individual claims are not confirmed.

### 4. Infrastructure: the gigawatt year

Five AI data centers were projected to cross 1 GW in 2026 (Epoch AI); Stargate Abilene scaled toward 450,000 GB200s; NVIDIA lined up $500B+ in Wall-Street financing for AI compute (August); Broadcom targeted H2 2026 for OpenAI-designed accelerators. The cost of the buildout reached consumers (Apple/Microsoft price hikes, June; NVIDIA's reported 15% AI-server price increase, August). Data centers passed 4% of US electricity (2024, rising) — the energy constraint is now the binding one on the roadmap.

### 5. Chips: NVIDIA's platform lock-in vs the custom-silicon rebellion

GTC's Vera Rubin platform (March) entrenched NVIDIA's full-stack story (CPU+GPU+NVLink+Ethernet+DPU), while Cerebras (wafer-scale CS-4/CS-6), Xiaomi (3nm), Apple (M6/M5 Ultra, A20 Pro 2nm, Intel/Samsung foundry talks), Intel (Nova Lake bLLC vs AMD X3D) and AMD (Zen 6) each attacked a different layer. TSMC remained the common denominator — and the single point of failure every diversification talk circled.

### 6. Cloud reliability: the multi-cloud fragility window

July–August 2026 delivered an unusual cluster: GCP europe-west4 (~15h, power+cooling), Azure West US (~5h, automation bug), AWS us-west-2 twice (network-boundary), plus an August week hitting all three hyperscalers and a 12-hour outage in a region live under five weeks. The lesson the trade press drew: multi-region is not a resilience strategy by itself — region age, correlated automation, and management-plane dependencies matter as much as geography.

### 7. Cybersecurity: ransomware steady, supply chain dominant, agents expand the attack surface

Verizon DBIR 2026 anchored the year (48% of breaches involved ransomware; 69% of victims didn't pay; $139,875 median payment). The ITRC's 280.6M supply-chain notifications, the EY/ShinyHunters third-party-platform breach, ChipSoft's hospital-disconnecting ransomware, and the Hugging Face/OpenAI credential incident each illustrated a different third-party/agent failure mode. State-linked pressure appeared at both ends: Russia's ChatGPT influence operation shut down (August), Taiwan prosecuting AI-server smuggling to China (August). Microsoft's two Patch Tuesdays (570 and ~400 CVEs) showed vulnerability volume still climbing, with AI assistance now part of the patching pipeline itself.

### 8. Regulation: the EU moved first, the US negotiated, China bifurcated

2 August 2026 was the year's regulatory hinge: the EU AI Office's GPAI enforcement powers and Article 50 transparency duties went live (with high-risk obligations delayed to 2027–2028 by the Digital Omnibus). The US took a transactional path — reported customer-by-customer government review of GPT-5.6 (June), congressional testimony demands (August). China paired domestic control (AI companion law, Qwen-for-Apple-Intelligence) with a global governance bid (WAICO). The result by September: three regulatory stacks, one fractured market.
