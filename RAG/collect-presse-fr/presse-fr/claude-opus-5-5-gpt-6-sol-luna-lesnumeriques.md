## Full summary

On 22 September 2026, Anthropic and OpenAI launched new frontier models within ninety minutes of each other, a rare simultaneous release that undercuts the labs' own recent calls to slow down AI progress. Ten days earlier, on 12 September, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei had published a long essay titled "We Must Pace the Frontier," urging the small group of leading AI labs to collectively moderate the pace of capability gains so that safety could catch up with power. He clarified that this moderation did not imply any freeze on model training. The article frames the back-to-back launches as ironic given that context.

Anthropic unveiled **Claude Opus 5.5**, its first release since that appeal. It was tested before publication by external evaluators including METR and Frontier Design. Anthropic claims performance comparable to Claude Fable 5.1 on most tasks and says it beats GPT-6 Astra on several benchmarks, including Terminal-Bench 4.0 and Humanity's Last Exam. Opus 5.5 tops seven of nine benchmarks shown, and beats GPT-6 Astra on four of the six tests where Astra was evaluated, with Astra retaining the advantage on AutomationBench and Terminal-Bench-Science. Pricing drops to $4 per million input tokens and $20 output (versus $5/$25 for Opus 5); combined with leaner token consumption, Anthropic estimates a 40% saving on common workloads.

Ninety minutes later, OpenAI presented **GPT-6 Sol and Luna**, lighter variants of its flagship GPT-6 Astra. Sol targets complex coding and autonomous agents; Luna targets high-volume repetitive tasks. Pricing is $2/$10 per million tokens for Sol and $0.10/$0.50 for Luna. The announced halving is calculated against promotional GPT-5.6 Sol prices; the official July grid listed GPT-5.6 Sol at $5/$30. On AutomationBench, GPT-6 Sol in xhigh effort scores 33.2% for $0.27 per task, ahead of Claude Opus 5 at 26.9% (the comparison excludes the newer Opus 5.5). Both models roll out progressively to Plus, Pro, Business, Enterprise and Edu subscribers, while Free and Go users access Luna via the desktop app.

The article concludes that in a single morning the two leading generative-AI rivals refreshed their lineups and cut API prices, ten days after Amodei's essay.
