## Full summary

Published 22 September 2026 by José Billon, this BDM article analyzes the latest edition of the **Global AI Diffusion** report from the **Microsoft AI Economy Institute** for the second quarter of 2026. The institute measures the share of the world's population aged 15 to 64 that has used a generative-AI product, based on its own aggregated and anonymized telemetry. The indicator is adjusted for operating-system and device market shares, internet penetration, and each country's population — a precaution to keep in mind when reading the figures.

**Global adoption rises:** In Q2 2026, **18.8% of the world's working-age population used AI**, up from 17.8% the previous quarter. The increase affects almost every economy tracked. The **United Arab Emirates (73.3%)** and **Singapore (64.3%)** retain a wide lead. **South Korea** posts the largest absolute gain of the quarter, +3.5 points over Q1 — its third consecutive quarter with the biggest rise, bringing its one-year gain to nearly 15 points. **France ranks 4th with 49.6%** of users among the working-age population, up from 5th place with 44% in the previous edition.

**A widening North-South gap:** In Northern countries, 28.8% of the working-age population used AI in Q2, versus 27.5% the previous quarter. In Southern countries, the share rose from 15.4% to 16.2%. The gap between the two zones now reaches **12.6 points**, versus 12.1 points the previous quarter and 10.6 points a year earlier. Beyond usage share, the **use cases differ**: based on an analysis of a sample of Copilot conversations, self-directed learning weighs 29% more in Southern usage than its global weight, and tutoring 32% more. In the North, usage leans more toward shopping, information search, and feedback requests. Microsoft cautions these results may partly reflect a younger population in the South.

**The rise of open-weight models:** Open-weight models are gaining ground. At the end of 2025, a public OpenRouter analysis of more than 100,000 billion tokens estimated their share at about one-third of the volume exchanged on the platform — a share that now reaches **75%**, according to Microsoft's own analysis of the same data. This share continued to grow beyond the launch peaks of DeepSeek, Kimi, Qwen and GPT-OSS, a sign that adoption goes beyond simple experimentation. This dynamic could particularly benefit Southern countries, where open weights lower the cost of AI access and make adaptation to local languages easier.

## Key points

- Microsoft AI Economy Institute's Global AI Diffusion report (Q2 2026) shows global adoption at 18.8% of the working-age population (up from 17.8%).
- UAE (73.3%) and Singapore (64.3%) lead; South Korea posts the biggest quarterly gain (+3.5 pts, ~15 pts over a year).
- France ranks 4th worldwide with 49.6%, up from 5th with 44% in the previous edition.
- The North-South gap widens to 12.6 points (vs 12.1 the previous quarter and 10.6 a year earlier).
- Use cases differ: learning/tutoring dominate in the South; shopping and information search in the North.
- Open-weight models now account for 75% of OpenRouter volume (up from ~one-third at end-2025).
- Open weights may reduce access inequalities and ease localization for Southern countries.
- Microsoft cautions the methodology adjusts for OS/device share, internet penetration and population.
