# 34. Deep Dive — Apple in 2026 (M5 Ultra, M6, Mac Lineup)

## 34. Deep Dive — Apple in 2026 (M5 Ultra, M6, Mac Lineup)

- **M5 Ultra:** quad-die fusion of two M5 Max dies via 4.4 TB/s UltraFusion interconnect; 36-core CPU (12 super-performance + 24 performance), 80-core GPU, per-core Neural Accelerator; 512GB unified memory at 1.2 TB/s; 1.25x CPU / 1.8x graphics / 1.3x memory-bandwidth / 4.3x peak AI vs M3 Ultra (vendor claims) [official].
- **Cluster story:** Thunderbolt 5 + RDMA lets users link up to four M5 Ultra Mac Studios for on-device LLM inference "with hundreds of billions of parameters" [official]; 33 simultaneous 8K ProRes streams claimed [official].
- **Enterprise angle:** The Information reported the unusually early August announcement was driven by enterprise AI demand [secondary].
- **M6:** 2nm; dual 16-core Neural Engine; VP Sri Santhanam on-device AI messaging [secondary]; powers the new Mac mini [official].
- **Mac mini (M5 Pro / M6):** from $899 (M6) and $1,699 (M5 Pro, 18-core CPU/40-core GPU); 10GbE standard on M5 Pro; base 16GB RAM criticized by reviewers; $899 = price increase vs prior $599, "value proposition gone" per The Verge [official]/[independent].
- **Mac Studio (M5 Max / M5 Ultra):** from $1,999 (M5 Max) and $5,499 (M5 Ultra, 96GB/1TB); 512GB config pulled March 2026, returning late Oct 2026 at >$10K [official]/[secondary].
- **PCIe 6.0 saga:** press release initially claimed PCIe 6.0 slots; Apple scrubbed the reference; MacGeneration found PCIe 6.0 hardware present but unenabled — speculation ties it to AI-accelerator clustering [secondary].
- **SSD:** ~14 GB/s sequential reads (Engadget) [independent].
- **macOS 26 / WWDC 2026 / Vision Pro / MacBook updates:** not researched — see §16 gaps.
- **Analyst framing:** Mac mini as the "ultimate LLM" box for enthusiasts — if you buy the RAM [independent].

## 35. Quarterly Recap — The Three Acts of 2026

### Q1 (Feb–Mar): Launches and debunks
- Intel's Panther Lake (launched at CES, early January) met its first rival in AMD's Ryzen AI 400 "Gorgon Point" shipping 1H 2026; early comparisons framed a three-way fight with Snapdragon X2 [secondary].
- The Windows 12 saga: a viral March report claimed an AI-focused Windows 12 was coming in 2026; within days Windows Central, Windows Latest, and TweakTown dismantled it and PCWorld retracted — Microsoft's 2026 was about fixing Windows 11 [secondary].
- Take-Two held the GTA VI November 19, 2026 line and said marketing would begin in summer 2026 [secondary].
- Apple quietly pulled the 512GB Mac Studio configuration as memory prices bit [secondary].

### Q2 (Apr–Jun): Leaks, confirmations, events
- April: leaker Jaykihn claimed Intel canceled Xe3P Celestial discrete gaming GPUs; VideoCardz reported Xe3P reserved for Crescent Island AI accelerators [unverified]/[secondary].
- May: AMD confirmed AM5 support through 2029 and board partners showed Zen 6-ready boards at Computex [secondary]; RDNA 5 board-partner consensus slipped to late 2027–early 2028 [secondary].
- June: Microsoft's Build 2026 confirmed no Windows 12 while teasing NVIDIA N1X Arm laptops and a Surface Laptop Ultra [secondary]; Nintendo's financials showed Mario Kart World at 15.39M [secondary].

### Q3 (Jul–Sep): Apple strikes, memory bites, mini PCs rise
- August 25: Apple announced M5 Ultra and M6, a new Mac mini ($899) and Mac Studio ($5,499) — the first Mac launch of 2026 [secondary]; reviewers praised the performance but flagged the 16GB base RAM and higher pricing as memory-crisis casualties [independent].
- September: HUMAIN unveiled Snapdragon X2 Elite "Horizon" AI laptops at LEAP 2026 [secondary]; ASUS launched the Ascent QN10 mini PC at $1,349.99 [secondary]; Valve open-sourced Lepton (Android→Linux porting tool) and priced the Steam Frame at $1,059 [secondary].
- The GPU deadlock deepened: leakers argued over whether RTX 60 lands in H1 2027 or 2028, while street prices for RTX 50 cards climbed to $4,000–$5,000 [unverified]/[secondary].
- September 22: M6 Mac mini and M5 Ultra Mac Studio reached customers [secondary].
