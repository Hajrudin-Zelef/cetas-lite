# adoption-de-l-ia-la-france-s-installe-a-la-4-place-du-classement-mondial

<!-- source: https://www.blogdumoderateur.com/adoption-ia-france-4e-place-classement-mondial/ -->

Artificial intelligence continues to spread around the world, but the access gaps between countries are not closing. This is confirmed by the latest edition of the Global AI Diffusion report, published by the Microsoft AI Economy Institute for the second quarter of 2026.

The institute measures the share of the world's population aged 15 to 64 that has used a generative AI product, based on its own telemetry, aggregated and anonymized. The indicator is adjusted according to the market shares of operating systems and devices, Internet penetration and the population of each country, a precaution to keep in mind when reading the figures that follow.

## Global AI adoption continues to progress

In the second quarter of 2026, 18.8% of working-age people worldwide used AI, compared with 17.8% in the previous quarter. The increase affects almost all economies tracked by the report.

The United Arab Emirates (73.3%) and Singapore (64.3%) retain a wide lead over the rest of the ranking. South Korea recorded the largest absolute increase of the quarter, with 3.5 points more than in the first quarter. This is the third consecutive quarter in which the country has shown the largest increase, bringing its gain to nearly 15 points over one year.

France ranks 4th with 49.6% of working-age users. It thus continues its progression in the global ranking: in the previous edition of the report, it was 5th with 44% of working-age users.

## A gap that continues to widen between countries of the North and the South

The study also tells us that the gap between countries of the North and the South is widening. In the countries of the North, 28.8% of working-age people used AI in the second quarter, compared with 27.5% in the previous quarter. In the countries of the South, this share rose from 15.4% to 16.2%. The gap between the two zones now reaches 12.6 points, compared with 12.1 points in the previous quarter and 10.6 points a year earlier.

Beyond the share of use, it is the uses that differ between countries of the North and the South. According to the analysis of a sample of conversations with Copilot, self-directed learning accounts for 29% more of uses in the South than its weight globally, and school support 32% more. In the North, uses lean more toward shopping, information seeking and requests for feedback.

Microsoft nevertheless calls for caution regarding these results, which could partly be explained by a younger population in the countries of the South.

## The rise of open weight models

Open weight models are gaining ground in the AI ecosystem. At the end of 2025, a public analysis by OpenRouter covering more than 100,000 billion tokens estimated their share at about one third of the volume exchanged on the platform, a share that now reaches 75%, according to Microsoft's own analysis of the same data. Moreover, this share continued to grow beyond the launch peaks of DeepSeek, Kimi, Qwen and GPT-OSS, a sign that adoption goes beyond the simple experimentation phase.

This dynamic could particularly benefit the countries of the South, where open weights lower the cost of access to AI and make it easier to adapt it to local languages.
