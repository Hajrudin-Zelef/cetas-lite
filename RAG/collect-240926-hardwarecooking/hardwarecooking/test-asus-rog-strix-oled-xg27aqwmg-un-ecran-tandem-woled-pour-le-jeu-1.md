# test-asus-rog-strix-oled-xg27aqwmg-un-ecran-tandem-woled-pour-le-jeu

<!-- source: https://www.hardwarecooking.fr/test-asus-rog-strix-oled-xg27aqwmg/ -->

Today in the Hardware Kitchen, we are testing the **ASUS ROG Strix OLED XG27AQWMG** monitor, a 27″ model based on a **4th generation Tandem WOLED** panel that clearly targets gamers with high-end technical specifications. We will therefore see in detail throughout this test whether this **ASUS** reference is a good model or not. To do this, we will measure color accuracy, uniformity, contrast, white and black luminance, not to mention color spaces and gamma level.

## ASUS ROG Strix OLED XG27AQWMG Monitor Test, summary:

Let's get straight to the point and properly introduce this monitor from ROG. With a diagonal of precisely **26.5″**, we have a **fourth-generation Tandem WOLED** panel from XX with a **QHD** resolution (2560 x 1440 pixels) and a **TrueBlack Glossy coating**. This coating features an optical layer without haze, offering deep blacks under any lighting while reducing ambient reflections by around 38% compared to a previous-generation glossy WOLED coating.

The refresh rate is **280 Hz** with an announced response time of **0.03 ms GTG**. For **VRR**, we have **G-Sync Compatible and AMD FreeSync Premium Pro** certification, which eliminates tearing effects and other such pleasantries. Note that we have a flat, non-curved panel here. Also, as befits a high-end monitor, ASUS announces that the monitor ships with a Delta E of less than 2 for excellent color accuracy.

The announced brightness is **350 nits in SDR** and **1500 nits in HDR**. For contrast, we are dealing with WOLED technology and the contrast is therefore enormous with an announced value of **1500000:1**; the same goes for viewing angles of 178° both vertically and horizontally. As for color spaces, the brand announces that the **DCI-P3 gamut is covered at 99.5%**. The color depth is **10-bit** and the monitor can therefore display approximately 1.07 billion colors. There is a VESA 100 x 100 mm mount at the rear to attach the monitor to a stand if needed, and the net weight without the stand is 4.8 kg (6.7 kg with the stand).

Let's now talk a bit more about the technical specifics of a **Tandem WOLED panel**. This type of panel offers 15% higher brightness compared to a WOLED panel. Images therefore come out with more intensity, and we are dealing here with a screen that even benefits from **VESA DisplayHDR 500 True Black** certification, guaranteeing excellent contrast. But that is not the only advantage, since the color volume is also greater by around 25%, meaning this screen is announced at 99.5% of the DCI-P3 gamut. Likewise, thanks to a four-layer design, the longevity of this type of panel is increased with 60% additional lifespan compared to a previous-generation WOLED screen, reducing the risk of burn-in.

And to further protect its ROG monitors from the risk of burn-in, ASUS combines advanced hardware thermal dissipation (custom heatsinks, sometimes graphene film) with a dedicated software suite called **ASUS OLED Care** (and **OLED Care Pro** on recent models). This solution integrates passive and configurable optimizations via the screen or the **DisplayWidget Center** application, such as intelligent detection of fixed elements like logos and the taskbar to reduce their brightness (Taskbar & Logo Detection), or pixel shifting near black borders. On the Pro variant, the addition of a **Neo proximity sensor** even allows the display to turn off in the background as soon as the user moves away from the desk, thus guaranteeing maximum protection of the panel without compromising daily user comfort.

Regarding the **price**, this **ASUS ROG Strix OLED XG27AQWMG** monitor is currently available **at Boulanger for €529.99** or **from €590 on the Amazon marketplace** at the time of writing. We will return to the subject of price in the conclusion of this test.

**Good to know**: do not trust so-called "dynamic" contrast. It does not represent the same values when compared between multiple screens. It is above all a big commercial bullshit to play who has the biggest by displaying insane values to impress novice customers. You should trust only the so-called "typical" contrast. For information, the dynamic contrast is announced at 12M:1 for this screen.

**Good to know**: a 10-bit panel will have 1024 shades of red, 1024 shades of green and 1024 shades of blue for a total of 1024 x 1024 x 1024 or approximately 1.07 billion colors. An 8-bit panel means we have 256 shades of red with 256 shades of green, but also 256 shades of blue for a total of 256 x 256 x 256 or approximately 16.7 million colors.

But here we are talking about color shades, color depth in short. This does not increase the color space since it is not about adding redder reds, but only about providing more shades between the extremes that are covered by the color space. Thus, when you make gradients in Photoshop for example, there will no longer be that "staircase" impression.

## Design and assembly, what does it look like?

To talk about the design of this screen with a high-end positioning and a target aimed at gamers, the lines are therefore quite elaborate for a relatively "aggressive" design, this is exactly what we expect from a screen in the ROG Strix range. We have a thin frame around the panel since we are on OLED.

So, just under the panel is the backlit ROG logo, which also acts as a Neo proximity sensor for all OLED Care features.

The back of the screen is also visually elaborate with a large backlit ROG logo on the right.

For the stand column, it has at the bottom cable routing to perfect its cable management. The whole is solid and high quality.

At the top is a thread allowing the installation of accessories such as ring lights or its camera/webcam for streamers. Also, the ROG logo is inscribed on the column.
