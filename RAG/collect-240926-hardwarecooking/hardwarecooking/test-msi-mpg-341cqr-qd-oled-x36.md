# test-msi-mpg-341cqr-qd-oled-x36

<!-- source: https://www.hardwarecooking.fr/test-msi-mpg-341cqr-qd-oled-x36/ -->

Today in the Hardware Kitchen, after a 240 Hz QD-OLED monitor from **MSI**, we return to another model from the dragon brand with the **MPG 341CQR QD-OLED X36**. This is therefore a 34″ 21:9 format monitor also featuring a QD-OLED panel and running this time at 360 Hz. We will therefore see in detail throughout this test whether this MSI reference is a good model or not. To do this, we will measure color accuracy, uniformity, contrast, white and black luminance without forgetting the color spaces and the gamma level.

## Review of the MSI MPG 341CQR QD-OLED X36 monitor, summary:

Let’s get straight to the point and properly introduce this monitor from MSI. On a **34″** diagonal in **21:9** format, we have a **QD-OLED Penta Tandem** panel of the fifth generation integrating a 5-layer light emission structure to improve brightness efficiency while reducing energy consumption.

This monitor is based on an **UWQHD** resolution (**3440 x 1440 pixels**) with a **DarkArmor** coating for deeper blacks and better scratch resistance. The refresh rate is **360 Hz** for an announced response time of **0.03 ms in GTG**. For VRR, we have **G-Sync Compatible and AMD FreeSync Premium Pro** certification which eliminates tearing effects and other joys of this kind. Note that we have here a **1800R** curvature. Also, as a high-end monitor demands, MSI announces that the monitor is delivered with a **Delta E below 2** for excellent color accuracy.

We find **VESA DisplayHDR True Black 500** and **VESA ClearMR 18000** certification. The announced brightness is **300 nits in standard** and **1300 nits in HDR**. For contrast, we are on OLED and the contrast is therefore enormous with an announced value of 1500000:1, the same goes for viewing angles of 178° vertically and horizontally. As for color spaces, the brand announces that the **DCI-P3 gamut is 99% covered**. The color depth is 10 bits and the monitor can therefore display approximately 1.07 billion colors. We find a VESA 100 x 100 mm at the back to attach the monitor to a stand if needed and the net weight without the stand is 7.77 kg (13.49 kg with the stand).

We of course find **several features with artificial intelligence**, such as real-time detection of human presence used to turn off the screen when no one is in front of it and therefore save energy, but also prevent panel wear. All visual data is processed locally and in real time by an NPU chip and is instantly converted into digital signals without any storage. No image is recorded or transmitted, which guarantees total confidentiality on the device and protects user data at all times.

To talk about the price, this **MSI MPG 341CQR QD-OLED X36** monitor is currently **on the Amazon marketplace from 930 euros** or even for **891 euros at PCcomponentes** while dangerously approaching the 1000 euro mark at major French retailers. We will return to the subject of price in the conclusion of this test.

**Good to know**: do not trust so-called “dynamic” contrasts. This does not represent the same values when compared between several monitors. It is above all a big commercial bullshit to play who has the biggest by displaying insane values to impress novice customers. You should only trust the so-called “typical” contrast. For information, the dynamic contrast is announced at 12M:1 for this monitor.

**Good to know**: a 10-bit panel will have 1024 shades of red, 1024 shades of green and 1024 shades of blue for a total of 1024 x 1024 x 1024 or approximately 1.07 billion colors. An 8-bit panel means we have 256 shades of red with 256 shades of green, but also 256 shades of blue for a total of 256 x 256 x 256 or approximately 16.7 million colors.

But this is about color shades, color depths in short. This does not increase the color space since it is not about adding redder reds, but only about providing more shades between the extremes that are covered by the color space. Thus, when you make gradients in Photoshop for example, there will no longer be this “staircase” impression.

## Design and assembly, how does it look?

To talk about the design of this monitor, which as a reminder targets gamers, we ultimately have an almost understated product when viewed from the front. A borderless design as we like, especially when using multiple monitors, and a more substantial bottom bezel as we see every time. It's attractive, carefully made, clean, we like it.

So, just below the panel and the MSI logo on the bottom bezel is the AI Care Sensor module. It is what allows the monitor (or rather an AI chip running on an NPU algorithm) to detect human presence and adjust the screen brightness or simply put it in standby.

The back of the monitor is also visually detailed with, on the left, a large brand dragon logo and striped effects.

On the upper part, we can find a small RGB zone with the logo in the center.

As for the stand column, it features cable routing at the bottom to perfect its cable management. The whole thing is sturdy and high quality.

Speaking of the square base, which ultimately takes up fairly little space on the desk, it has an almost rectangular shape and is particularly stable.

To attach the stand column, everything is done without screws. Indeed, you simply snap the clips in from the top and then lower it to secure it in place. Then, slide the base onto the end of the column and tighten the screw by hand. It's as easy as pie.

The connectivity is located at the bottom of the monitor and the power supply is on the other side. We will have the following ports:

- 2x HDMI 2.1 compatible with VRR and ALLM at 120 Hz
- 1x DisplayPort 2.1a UHBR13.5
- 2x USB 3.2 Type-A at 5 Gbps
- 1x USB Type-C DP Alt Mode 98W
