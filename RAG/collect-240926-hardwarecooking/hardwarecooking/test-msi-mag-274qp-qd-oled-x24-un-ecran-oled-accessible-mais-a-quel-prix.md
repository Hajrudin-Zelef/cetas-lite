# test-msi-mag-274qp-qd-oled-x24-un-ecran-oled-accessible-mais-a-quel-prix

<!-- source: https://www.hardwarecooking.fr/test-msi-mag-274qp-qd-oled-x24/ -->

Today in the Hardware Kitchen, we are testing the **MSI MAG 274QP QD-OLED X24** monitor, a 27″ model based on a QD-OLED panel that clearly targets gamers with high-end technical specifications. We will therefore see in detail throughout this test whether this **MSI** reference is a good model or not. To do this, we will measure color accuracy, uniformity, contrast, white and black luminance, not to mention color spaces and gamma level.

## MSI MPG 274QP QD-OLED X24 Monitor Test, Summary:

Let us get straight to the point and properly present this monitor from MSI. With a diagonal of precisely **26.5″**, we have a third-generation **QD-OLED** panel with a **QHD** resolution (2560 x 1440 pixels). The refresh rate is **240 Hz** and the announced response time is **0.03 ms GTG**. Likewise, MSI announces a **Delta E of less than 2**.

For VRR, we have a **G-Sync Compatible and AMD FreeSync Premium** certification which eliminates tearing effects and other joys of that kind. Note that we have a flat panel here and not a curved one. Also, as befits a high-end monitor, MSI announces that the monitor comes with a Delta E of less than 2 for excellent color accuracy.

We find a **VESA ClearMR 13000** certification. The announced brightness is **200 nits standard and 400 nits in HDR**. For contrast, we are dealing with OLED and the contrast is therefore enormous with an announced value of **1500000:1**, the same goes for viewing angles of 178° vertically and horizontally. As for color spaces, the brand announces that the DCI-P3 gamut is covered at 99%. The color depth is **10 bits** and the monitor can therefore display approximately 1.07 billion colors. We find a VESA 100 x 100 mm mount at the back to attach the monitor to a stand if needed, and the net weight without the stand is 6.7 kg (9.4 kg with the stand).

MSI offers with its monitor a whole suite of features to protect the panel from burn-in issues. Thus, in OLED Care 2.0 there will be a panel refresh every 24 hours instead of 16 hours previously, reducing the risk of interruptions during a gaming or work session. Rest assured, this refresh occurs when the screen enters standby mode or turns off. Accompanied by a three-year warranty, we will also find the classic features of pixel shifting, static image detection, border detection, taskbar, or logo detection. Everything necessary to extend the life of the screen.

Speaking of **price**, this **MSI MAG 274QP QD-OLED X24** monitor is currently between 340 euros and 390 euros depending on the retailer. It will be displayed for example at **347 euros at PCcomponentes** or from **385 euros on the Amazon marketplace**. We will return to the subject of price in the conclusion of this test.

**Good to know**: do not trust so-called "dynamic" contrasts. They do not represent the same values when compared between several monitors. It is above all a big commercial bullshit to play who has the biggest by displaying insane values to impress novice customers. You should only trust so-called "typical" contrast. For information, the dynamic contrast is announced at 12M:1 for this monitor.

**Good to know**: a 10-bit panel will have 1024 shades of red, 1024 shades of green and 1024 shades of blue for a total of 1024 x 1024 x 1024 or approximately 1.07 billion colors. An 8-bit panel means we have 256 shades of red with 256 shades of green, but also 256 shades of blue for a total of 256 x 256 x 256 or approximately 16.7 million colors.

But this is about color shades, color depth in short. This does not increase the color space since it is not about adding redder reds, but only about providing more shades between the extremes that are covered by the color space. Thus, when you make gradients in Photoshop for example, there will no longer be that "staircase" impression.

## Design and assembly, how does it look?

To talk about the design of this monitor aimed at gamers, we ultimately have an almost understated product when viewed from the front. A borderless design as we like, especially when doing multi-monitor setups, and a more substantial bottom bezel as we see every time. It's nice, neat, clean, we like it.

So, just below the panel we have the only real bezel with the MSI logo in the middle.

The back of the monitor is also visually detailed with, on the left, at the top, a nice MSI dragon badge. In the center, we also find a glossy black strip with the MSI logo in the middle.

For the stand column, it features cable routing at the bottom to perfect its cable management. The whole thing is solid and well-made.

To talk about the square base, which ultimately takes up fairly little space on the desk, it has an almost rectangular shape and is particularly stable.

To attach the stand column, it's all done without screws. Indeed, you simply clip it in from the top and then lower it to secure it in its place. Then, slide the base onto the end of the column and hand-tighten the screw. It's as simple as can be.

The connectivity is located at the bottom of the monitor and the power supply is on the other side. We will have the following ports:

- 2x HDMI 2.1 with console mode
- 1x DisplayPort 1.4a
