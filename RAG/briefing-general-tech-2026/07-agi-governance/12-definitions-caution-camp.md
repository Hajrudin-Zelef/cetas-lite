# Definitions, timelines and the caution camp

<a id="g08-12"></a>
### 8.12 Definitions, timelines and the caution camp

This chapter closes where the summer's argument actually lives: not in
the communiqués, which largely agree with each other, but in the
definitions and dates, which do not. The September 2026 record
contains, side by side, a categorical arrival claim, a hedged era
claim, a refusal to label, a premature-proclamation warning, and a
paced-frontier essay — and they cannot all be describing the same
thing, because they are not using the same words to mean the same
thing.

**The definition problem.** None of the principals operates with a
shared, operational definition of AGI, and several of them have said
so. Huang's "AGI has arrived" (§8.1) is quantified in deployed GPU
systems — an infrastructure metric pressed into service as a
capability claim. Brockman's "not unreasonable to feel… the AGI era"
(§8.2) is explicitly about the reasonableness of a feeling, which
moves the claim from the world into the observer. Legg's "minimal AGI"
(§8.3) is the only version with a qualifier attached, and the
qualifier is doing the definitional work the others leave implicit:
*minimal* relative to what stronger version is left unsaid. OpenAI's
own refusal to call Astra AGI (§8.1–8.2) implies a bar the company has
not published. And the 22-country declaration (§8.7) sidesteps the
word entirely, speaking instead of "human direction, insight and
control" — a formulation that governs the *relationship* between
humans and systems without requiring agreement on what the systems
are. The declaration's drafters may have understood something the
CEOs have not: that you can regulate the relationship before you
agree on the definition.

**The dated timelines on record in 2026.** Only a handful of forecasts
in this chapter carry both a date and a named author in 2026. They
are collected here, with the unconfirmed and secondary material
clearly separated:

| Forecast | Author | Date stated | Content |
|---|---|---|---|
| AGI arrival | Jensen Huang (NVIDIA) | 06/09/2026 | "AGI has arrived" — categorical, personal (X post) |
| AGI era | Greg Brockman (OpenAI) | 03/09/2026 | "Not unreasonable to feel… in the AGI era" — hedged, personal |
| Minimal AGI | Shane Legg (DeepMind Institute) | 16/09/2026 | 50% chance by 2028 — probabilistic, qualified |
| Swarm risk | Dario Amodei (Anthropic) | 12/09/2026 | 6–12 month window — dated operational warning |
| AGI proclamation | Shane Legg (DeepMind Institute) | 16/09/2026 | Premature to proclaim — explicit caution |
| RSI moratorium | OpenAI (institutional) | 21/09/2026 | Not unless/until safe — conditional, institutional |
| Median AGI forecast | Metaculus (aggregator) | 2026 | Median November 2033 — **secondary aggregator, not a lab position** |

The Metaculus entry requires the flag it carries: it is a prediction-
market median drawn from secondary aggregators, not a statement by any
lab, and it sits a full five years beyond Legg's 50%-by-2028 — a
measure of how far the cautious institutional forecast and the
aggregated crowd forecast have diverged. The rest of the table's
"timelines" come, as the verified-facts note records, from secondary
aggregators rather than named 2026 statements, and are not promoted
here beyond that.

**The caution camp.** If there is a coherent "camp" in the September
record, it is not the doomers and not the accelerationists — it is
the pacers and the definers. Amodei's 4,000-word case for pacing over
freezing (§8.4); Legg's "premature to proclaim" with a qualified 50%
(§8.3); OpenAI's conditional RSI moratorium and non-binding standards
(§8.5–8.6); the declaration's "direction, insight and control" without
the AGI word (§8.7). What unites them is not a shared timeline — their
dates span "has arrived" to "2033" — but a shared procedural instinct:
*slow the claim, specify the mechanism, build the institution.* Against
them stands, essentially, one X post: Huang's. The summer's most
consequential sentence about AGI may turn out to have been written by
the man who sells the shovels — and the autumn's most consequential
work is being done by the people building the institutions that will
decide whether he was right.

**What September 22 leaves open.** As this chapter closes: the Altman
Security Council briefing is planned for September 23 and has not
happened; the Trump–Xi summit is planned for September 24 and has not
happened; the administration's AI event is planned for September 23;
the Shenzhen follow-up to Bessent–He Lifeng is projected for roughly
two months out; China's answer on the incident line is unclear; the
verification institution is to be "explored"; the RSI moratorium is
conditional; the standards are non-binding; and the definition of AGI
remains — as it was in February — unwritten. The one rumor that would
have settled the definitional question by fiat — the "insider" claim,
attributed to Wissner-Gross, that OpenAI would "launch as AGI" a
model code-named "Doug/Bel" in November 2026 — was **debunked by
explainx.ai as resting on a single anonymous source**, and is excluded
from this dossier accordingly. What remains is the record above:
three weeks in which the most powerful people in AI argued, in
public, about whether the future had arrived — and then scheduled the
meetings at which they might decide what to do about it.
