## 8. AGI debate and governance

**Coverage:** February – September 2026 · **Status as of September 22, 2026**

The months covered by this dossier did not settle the question of artificial
general intelligence — they made it unavoidable. What changed in the summer of
2026 is not the underlying capability curve, which continued its familiar
trajectory, but the willingness of the people who steer it to say the words in
public. A chief executive declared on X that "AGI has arrived." Another lab
president said, in a briefing, that it is "not unreasonable" to feel that the
AGI era has begun. A third published a 4,000-word essay arguing that the
frontier must be *paced*, not frozen. And within a single week of September, a
think tank was launched at DeepMind, OpenAI published institutional standards
for "the next phase of AI," twenty-two countries signed a declaration on
human control, Washington and Beijing formalized an AI dialogue with a
proposed incident line, and Sam Altman was announced to brief the UN Security
Council.

This chapter reconstructs that sequence in chronological order, using only
verified facts. Every statement is attributed to its actual author, every
date is the one carried by a source, and everything that is merely planned
after September 22, 2026 is labeled as such — announced, not accomplished.
Where a claim circulates but could not be confirmed against a primary source,
it is marked unconfirmed. The final section collects the dated timelines and
forecasts on record in 2026, including the forecasts drawn from secondary
aggregators, clearly flagged as such.

A terminological note before starting: none of the lab leaders discussed
below uses the same definition of AGI, and several of them say so explicitly.
The disagreement about the word is part of the story, not a prelude to it.
