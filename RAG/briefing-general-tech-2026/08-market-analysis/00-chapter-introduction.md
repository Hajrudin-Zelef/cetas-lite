# Market, deals and cross-cutting analysis — introduction

<a id="g09"></a>
## 9. Market, deals and cross-cutting analysis

The AI-dossier chapters tell a story of models, products and policies; this one steps
back and reads the same eight months (February–September 2026) as an industrial story.
The through-line is simple: compute stopped being the scarce input somewhere in the
second half of the decade, and *memory* took its place. Everything else in this chapter
— the gigawatt-scale forward commitments, the warrant structures, the US-China stack
competition, the migration of inference toward agentic workloads, and the LPDDR
inference-chip class that followed — is downstream of that inversion. The facts below
are verified and dated; interpretations are flagged as such, per the anti-fabrication
rule that governs the whole dossier.

The window also matters for what it contains: October and November 2025 appear in the
table because two of the defining deals of this cycle (AMD–OpenAI, Qualcomm's AI200)
predate the February 2026 lower bound and shaped everything after. A deal dated after
22/09/2026 is described as announced or planned, not as realized. One event closes the
window at the level of market narrative: on 21/09/2026 AMD crossed a $1 trillion market
capitalization — $613.50 per share, up 9.6%, roughly $1,005 billion per Reuters — in a
sector-wide AI rally. It was the first pure-play logic chipmaker other than Nvidia to
join the trillion club, and it was priced on inference economics, not on the old
training narrative.
