# What to watch next

<a id="g09-5"></a>
### 9.5 What to watch next

Everything in this section is dated after 22/09/2026 and is therefore, by the dossier's
rule, announced or planned — not realized. The list is ordered by expected date.

- **23/09/2026 — Altman at the UN Security Council.** Sam Altman addresses the UNSC the
day after this dossier's cutoff. The verified fact is the appearance; the substance is
unknown. Watch for whether frontier-lab governance gets a multilateral hearing or
remains a national conversation.
- **24/09/2026 — Trump–Xi meeting.** The US and Chinese leaders meet one day after the
Altman appearance. Export controls were off the Bessent–He Lifeng agenda on 20/09;
whether the leaders' meeting reopens that file or confirms the managed-competition
pattern of section 9.3 is the question.
- **H2 2026 — Nvidia Rubin volume ramp.** The Rubin platform's move to volume production
is the single most consequential hardware event of the half-year: it sets the
competitive baseline against which AMD's Helios, Meta's Iris and the LPDDR inference
class are all priced.
- **September 2026 onward — Helios deliveries.** AMD's Helios platform begins deliveries
in the window's final month, carrying the MI450 commitments to Meta and the first OpenAI
volumes. Execution on Helios is the test of whether the warrant-structured deals of
section 9.1 convert into deployed gigawatts.
- **End of 2026 — OpenAI at massive scale.** The OpenAI–AMD agreement's deployment curve
steepens toward year-end; the first real read on the 6 GW commitment's pace comes from
what is actually online by December.
- **04/10/2026 — "Googlebook" sales begin.** The first consumer-hardware sales date of
the autumn window; a data point on whether the memory repricing of section 9.2 is being
passed through to consumer device pricing or absorbed in margin.
- **23/10/2026 — iPhone Duo sales begin.** Apple's autumn hardware, arriving into the
worst memory market in fifteen years. Pricing and availability will be read as a signal
on how far up the stack the DRAM/NAND repricing has traveled.
- **Q1 2027 — Huawei Ascend 960DT.** The first of the two announced Ascend milestones;
the test of the system-level compensation strategy described in section 9.3.
- **2027 — 1.6T networking rollouts (Meta, Oracle).** The next Ethernet speed generation
moves into deployment at two of the largest buyers, a data point for the open-fabric
thesis and for UALink's competitive window.
- **2027 — UALink products.** The open interconnect coalition's hardware arrives
(announced). The NVLink-versus-UALink contest of section 9.3 gets its first empirical
round.
- **2027–2028 — 800V DC mass deployment.** Rack-scale power distribution steps up a
voltage class; the infrastructure detail that determines how dense the agentic-inference
racks of section 9.4 can get.
- **Q3 2027 — Huawei Ascend 960PR.** The second Ascend milestone; by then the
PyTorch-backend ecosystem support noted in section 9.3 will have had a full year to
mature.
- **Q4 2027 — SuperPoD full availability.** Huawei's rack-scale system bet reaches its
announced target; the moment the system-level compensation strategy can be judged as a
product rather than a plan.

The thread connecting the watch items is the same as the thread connecting the chapter:
memory allocation, stack completeness, and inference economics. If the DRAM/NAND
repricing persists into 2027, the LPDDR inference class graduates from hedge to
mainstream. If UALink ships credibly, the interconnect layer commoditizes and Nvidia's
moat narrows to software. If Helios and Rubin both execute, the compute market of 2027
is a genuine duopoly for the first time — and the warrant structures of 2025–2026 will
look, in retrospect, like the moment the industry's balance of power was renegotiated.

The calendar above is deliberately hardware- and diplomacy-heavy: those are
the layers where announced dates are verifiable and where the chapter's three
threads — memory allocation, stack completeness, inference economics —
converge into observable events. Two meta-patterns are worth carrying out of
the window. First, the industry's bottleneck vocabulary changed in 2026 from
gigawatts to wafer starts; the companies that priced the second bottleneck
early — the LPDDR inference class, the disaggregated serving stacks — hold
the advantageous positions for 2027. Second, the US-China competition
settled, at least through September, into managed asymmetry rather than
escalation; the 24/09 leaders' meeting is the first scheduled test of whether
that management holds. Everything else is execution: Helios and Rubin
ramping, UALink shipping, Ascend maturing, and the memory market deciding
whether 2026 was a spike or a plateau.
