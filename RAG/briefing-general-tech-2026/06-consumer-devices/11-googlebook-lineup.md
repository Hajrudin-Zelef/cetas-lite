# Googlebook lineup, pricing and availability

<a id="g07-11"></a>
### 7.11 Googlebook lineup, pricing and availability

On September 21, 2026 — eleven days after Apple's keynote and one day before this
dossier's cutoff — Google turned the Googlebook from an announcement into a product line.
In a blog post, John Solomon, Google's VP and General Manager for Laptops, revealed that
all five OEM partners had a model ready: Acer, ASUS, Dell, HP, and Lenovo. Five
manufacturers, five price points, two chip architectures, one operating system. The
retail launch is scheduled for early October — announced and planned, not yet executed,
as of September 22.

The lineup, as confirmed in Solomon's post and the partners' materials:

| Model | Price (USD) | Chip | Key specs (confirmed) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acer Googlebook 14 | $899 | Intel Core Ultra 5 (Series 3 / Panther Lake) | 14" 2.8K OLED touch, 16 GB RAM, 512 GB storage |
| ASUS Googlebook 14 | $1,299 | Intel Core Ultra Series 3 (Panther Lake) | (details not in verified sources) |
| Dell XPS Googlebook | $1,199 | Snapdragon X Elite | (details not in verified sources) |
| HP Googlebook 14 | $1,299 | Snapdragon X Elite | (details not in verified sources) |
| Lenovo Googlebook 15 | $1,099 | Intel Core Ultra Series 3 (Panther Lake) | (details not in verified sources) |

The pricing architecture is deliberate. The range runs from $899 to $1,299 — a $400 band
that covers the mainstream laptop market's sweet spot without reaching into premium
ultrabook territory. The two architectures split along partner lines: Acer, ASUS and
Lenovo use Intel's Core Ultra Series 3 (the Panther Lake generation), while Dell and HP
use Qualcomm's Snapdragon X Elite. The split is itself a statement: Google is not tying
the AI-laptop category to a single silicon vendor, and the presence of Snapdragon X
Elite — the chip that powered the first Copilot+ wave — alongside Intel's newest
generation suggests Google wants the OS, not the processor, to be the story.

Three nuances attach to the headline prices, and each is verified. First, the Acer's
$899 is a limited-time offer, per Techaeris — the entry price that anchors the whole
lineup's "starting at $899" framing is promotional, and the sustained price may be
higher. Second, retailer prices diverge from the announced MSRPs in some channels; the
table above records the official announced prices, not street prices. Third, the Canadian
entry price is CAD 1,299 — a reminder that the USD figures do not convert cleanly across
markets, and that regional pricing follows its own logic.

Availability is confirmed in unusual detail for a launch this fresh. ASUS opened
Canadian preorders for its Googlebook 14 on September 21, 2026 (via GlobeNewswire) —
the same day as Solomon's blog post, making it the first buyable Googlebook. The US
commercial launch is scheduled for October 4–5, 2026; Canada, the UK, Ireland, France,
Germany and Australia follow on October 5. Australia's pricing is confirmed at AUD 2,599
for a November availability window — notably higher than a straight currency conversion
of the USD prices would suggest, and a data point for the regional-pricing discussion
above. All post–September 22 dates are announced/planned.

Read as a whole, the September 21 reveal answers the question the May announcement left
open: what does an "AI-first laptop" cost, who makes it, and when can you buy one. The
answers — $899 to $1,299, five OEMs, early October — position the Googlebook not as a
Pixel-style halo product but as a genuine category play: Google supplying the OS and the
AI, partners supplying the hardware, prices aimed at the volume segment. Whether the
Android-17 desktop OS and system-level Gemini justify the category's existence will be
decided by reviews and sales in the fourth quarter. What is already decided is that
Google, having left the laptop business years ago, is back in it — with more partners,
lower prices, and a clearer thesis than it ever had before.

The architecture split, and why it matters:

| Partners | Chip | Generation |
|---|---|---|
| Acer, ASUS, Lenovo | Intel Core Ultra Series 3 ("Panther Lake") | Intel's newest mobile generation |
| Dell, HP | Snapdragon X Elite (Qualcomm) | The chip of the first Copilot+ wave |

Google's decision not to tie the category to a single silicon vendor is the
quiet strategic fact of the lineup. The Snapdragon X Elite is the incumbent
AI-PC chip — proven, shipping, the reference platform for the first
Copilot+ wave. Intel's Panther Lake is the challenger generation, new and
unproven at cutoff. By blessing both, Google makes the OS the constant and
the processor a variable — the inverse of the Copilot+ story, where the
chip was the constant and the OS the variable. It is also a hedge: if
Panther Lake stumbles or the X Elite ages, the category survives on the
other leg. For buyers, the implication is choice without fragmentation —
two architectures, one Googlebook software stack, at least in theory. In
practice, the history of multi-architecture platforms counsels waiting for
reviews before assuming parity.

The pricing nuances, each verified:

- The Acer's $899 is a limited-time offer (Techaeris). The lineup's
  "starting at $899" framing therefore describes a promotional window, not
  necessarily the sustained entry price — a distinction that matters for
  anyone modeling the category's price positioning after launch.
- Retailer prices diverge from announced MSRPs in some channels. The table
  in the main text records official announced prices; street prices will
  tell their own story, especially once the Acer promotion expires.
- Canada's entry price is CAD 1,299 — not a conversion of any USD figure in
  the lineup, and a reminder that regional pricing follows local market
  logic rather than exchange rates.
- Australia's AUD 2,599 (November) sits well above a straight conversion of
  the USD range, consistent with the Australian market's historically
  elevated laptop pricing — and a data point for buyers comparing regions.

The September 21 reveal answers every question May deferred — cost
($899–$1,299), makers (five OEMs), software (Android-17 desktop OS with
system-level Gemini), ship date (early October). The positioning is
volume, not halo. Whether the differentiation survives contact with
reviewers is the fourth quarter's question.
