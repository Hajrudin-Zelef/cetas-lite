The second half of 2026 will be remembered, in consumer hardware terms, as the season when the
three largest mobile ecosystems finally converged on the same two ideas at the same time: the
foldable phone as a mass-market object, and the AI-first laptop as a category in its own right.
Apple, seven years after Samsung commercialized the foldable form factor, stepped onto the stage
in September with the iPhone Duo. Samsung answered not with a product but with a tone — playful,
opportunistic, deliberately unsparing. And Google, four months after announcing its return to
laptops, turned the announcement into an actual retail lineup with five OEM partners and a
confirmed ship date. Behind this visible ferment, the industry was fighting a quieter and much
uglier battle: a memory-price crisis so severe that it earned its own portmanteau — "RAMageddon"
— and began pushing consumer prices upward across PCs, smartphones and tablets while pulling
unit shipments down.

This chapter covers all four threads. Everything in it is sourced from reporting published
between May and September 2026; where information concerns events scheduled after this
dossier's cutoff date (September 22, 2026), it is explicitly marked as announced or planned
rather than as fact.

## 7. Consumer devices

The hardware stories of 2026 do not distribute evenly across the year. The first half was
dominated by components and infrastructure — the memory crisis building through the spring, AI
PC share climbing steadily. Then September detonated: Apple's "Surprise and shine" keynote on
September 9 introduced the company's first foldable, Samsung's pre-planned counter-offensive
rolled out within days, and Google dropped a full five-model laptop lineup on September 21.
Each of these is treated below in detail; the chapter then closes with the structural forces —
AI PC adoption and the memory crisis — that frame all three launches.
