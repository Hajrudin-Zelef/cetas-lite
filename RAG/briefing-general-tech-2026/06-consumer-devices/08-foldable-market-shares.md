# Foldable market shares: projections

<a id="g07-8"></a>
### 7.8 Foldable market shares: projections

Every number in this section is a projection. That needs stating up front, because the
foldable market of late 2026 is a market in which the most important product has been
announced but not sold: the iPhone Duo goes on sale October 23, a month after this
dossier's cutoff, so there is no real post-launch sales data for it anywhere. What exists
instead is a set of forecasts from Counterpoint, TrendForce and IDC, built on pre-order
patterns, supply-chain checks, and the analysts' models of installed-base conversion. They
disagree on details and agree on shape: Samsung leads, Apple takes a large second place
almost immediately, and the category grows slowly in units while growing fast in value.

Start with the baseline. Counterpoint's estimate for 2025 puts Samsung at approximately 40%
of foldable shipments — the incumbent's share after seven years of category ownership.
TrendForce's 2026 projection, published September 10, 2026, has Samsung at approximately
7.1 million units and 35.1% of a roughly 20.2-million-unit market — still first, but down
about five points of share year over year. Apple's entry, in the same projection, is
approximately 5 million units and 24.8%, tied with Huawei at approximately 5 million units
and 24.8% each. The arithmetic is stark: Apple is forecast to capture in under three
months of selling (October 23 through year-end) a share that took Huawei years to build,
entirely on the strength of converting iPhone owners who have been waiting for Apple to
make a foldable.

IDC adds two longer-horizon projections that reframe the 2026 numbers. First, IDC expects
Apple to ship more than 10 million foldable units in its first year on sale — a figure that,
if realized, would make the Duo generation one of the most successful product launches in
the category's history by volume. Second, IDC projects Apple taking 40% of foldable
shipments in 2027 — the lead, outright, within fourteen months of launch. These are
aggressive forecasts, and they rest on an assumption worth naming: that the Duo's
compromises (weight, no Face ID, $1,999 entry price) do not materially dent demand among
the iPhone installed base. If that assumption holds, the category's center of gravity moves
to Cupertino by the end of 2027. If it fails, the 2026 tie with Huawei may prove to be the
high-water mark rather than the starting gun.

The value story diverges from the unit story, and the divergence favors Apple
disproportionately. IDC's figures put Apple at approximately 22% of 2026 foldable shipments
by units — close to TrendForce's 24.8% — but at 34% of the market by value, on an average
selling price of $2,400. The gap between 22% of units and 34% of revenue is the Duo's
pricing power expressed arithmetically: at $1,999 to $3,199, Apple sells fewer units per
dollar of market than any competitor and still takes a third of the money. This is the
familiar Apple pattern — minority unit share, plurality or majority value share — applied
to a category where it has never competed before.

Two structural observations close the section. First, the 2026 market's projected growth of
0.5% year over year means the foldable category is, by units, essentially flat — Apple's
entry is a share-redistribution event, not a category-expansion event, at least in its
first year. The pie is not growing; Apple is forecast to take a quarter of it anyway.
Second, Samsung's projected decline from ~40% to ~35.1% (TrendForce) or ~32% (the 2026
estimate cited in verified sources) is the arithmetic complement of Apple's entry: in a
flat market, a new entrant taking a quarter of shipments must take it from incumbents, and
the largest incumbent absorbs the largest loss. Whether Samsung's "7 years ahead" campaign
(section 7.6) can slow that redistribution will be measurable in the first-half 2027 data.
Until the Duo actually ships on October 23, all of this remains projection — but it is
projection with a consistent shape across three independent forecasters, and that
consistency is itself a fact worth recording.

Two different 2026 Apple figures — TrendForce's 24.8% and IDC's ~22% —
should not be read as a disagreement. They measure overlapping but distinct
things (TrendForce: share of a defined 20.2M-unit market; IDC: share of
shipments in its own taxonomy), they were published days apart from
different models, and a two-to-three-point spread between forecasters ten
days after an announcement is consensus, not conflict. The shape all three
agree on: Samsung first but falling, Apple second almost immediately,
Huawei tied with Apple, everyone else splitting the remaining ~15%.

The unit/value divergence is the section's most important arithmetic.
IDC's pair — ~22% of units, 34% of value, average price $2,400 — describes
the familiar Apple pattern applied to a new category: minority unit share,
outsized value share, because the Duo's $1,999–$3,199 pricing sits far above
the category mean. In a 20.2-million-unit market growing at 0.5%, that
pattern has a sharp implication: Apple is not expanding the pie; it is
repricing it. A quarter of the units at a third of the revenue means the
category's dollar growth in 2026 comes disproportionately from one product
— excellent for Apple's P&L, and a warning for every competitor whose
average price sits below $2,400.

The 2027 projection — 40% of shipments, over 10 million units in year one —
is the aggressive end of the forecast range, and it rests on the assumption
named in the main text: that weight, biometrics, and price do not dent
installed-base conversion. Note what the projection implies about Samsung:
in a flat-to-slowly-growing market, Apple's path to 40% runs directly
through Samsung's share, which the verified 2026 estimates already have
falling from ~40% (2025) toward ~35% (TrendForce) or ~32%. The "7 years
ahead" campaign of section 7.6 is, in this light, not just marketing — it
is the incumbent's attempt to slow a redistribution the forecasters have
already priced in. Whether humor and head starts outweigh a 53-gram deficit
and a $1,999 entry price is the empirical question of 2027.

Methodological note: no verified post-launch Duo sales data exists
anywhere in this chapter — the device goes on sale October 23, a month
after cutoff. Everything until the December-quarter reporting cycle is
projection, however well-sourced.
