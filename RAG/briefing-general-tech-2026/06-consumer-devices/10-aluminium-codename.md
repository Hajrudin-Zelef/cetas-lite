# 'Aluminium': codename, not a commercial name

<a id="g07-10"></a>
### 7.10 "Aluminium": codename, not a commercial name

A terminological clarification, because the reporting around Google's laptop project has
produced two names and only one of them is real. "Aluminium" — spelled the British way —
is the internal codename for the project, confirmed by Google itself (via The Verge's
May 2026 reporting). It is not, and was never presented as, the commercial name of the
operating system or the devices. Internal codenames leak; this one leaked with
confirmation, which makes it attributable but not commercial.

The second name requires more care. On September 11, 2026, a leaked Lenovo spec sheet
referred to "Googlebook OS" as the operating system's name. That name has not been
confirmed by Google. It appears in exactly one kind of source — a partner's leaked
document — and Google has neither adopted it in its own materials (including John
Solomon's September 21 blog post announcing the lineup) nor denied it. The responsible
treatment is to report the leak as a leak: "Googlebook OS" is what a Lenovo document
calls it; what Google calls it, in its own voice, remains the unnamed Android/ChromeOS
convergence described in May, marketed under the "Googlebook" device branding.

The distinction matters beyond pedantry, because it illustrates how the Googlebook story
has been assembled: part official announcement (May 12, the category and features),
part partner leak (September 11, the OS name), part official retail launch
(September 21, the devices and prices). Readers encountering "Googlebook OS" in the wild
should understand they are encountering Lenovo's vocabulary, not Google's — at least
until Google says otherwise.

A second open question from the same period concerns the fate of Chromebooks. Sources
conflict: some reporting treats the Googlebook line as the successor that displaces
ChromeOS devices, while other sources describe Chromebooks as continuing alongside the
new line. The contradiction is noted here because it is real — the verified sources do
not agree — and because it will matter to institutional buyers (schools, enterprises)
with Chromebook fleets. Until Google clarifies the roadmap, the safe statement is that
both accounts exist in reputable reporting and neither has been confirmed as the
company's position. This dossier does not resolve the contradiction; it records it.

The names, disambiguated:

| Name | What it is | Confirmed by |
|---|---|---|
| "Aluminium" | Internal project codename (British spelling) | Google, via The Verge (May 2026) |
| "Googlebook" | Device/category branding across the five OEM models | Google (May announcement; Solomon blog, 21/09/2026) |
| "Googlebook OS" | OS name in a leaked Lenovo spec sheet (11/09/2026) | Not confirmed by Google — partner vocabulary until Google adopts it |

The practical rule for readers: "Aluminium" is safe when discussing the
project's internal history; "Googlebook" is safe for the devices;
"Googlebook OS" should carry a qualifier — "as Lenovo's leaked spec sheet
calls it" — until Google uses the name itself. Conflating the three produces
exactly the kind of confident-sounding error this dossier exists to prevent.

The Chromebook question, stated as the contradiction it is:

| Position | Implication |
|---|---|
| Reporting strand A | Googlebook displaces Chromebooks — the convergence product succeeds the split era |
| Reporting strand B | Chromebooks continue alongside Googlebook — the education and budget tier persists |

Both strands appear in reputable reporting; neither is confirmed as
Google's position. For schools and enterprises with Chromebook fleets, the
question — decade-long future or coming migration — remains unanswered,
and Google's silence as of September 22 is itself a data point.
