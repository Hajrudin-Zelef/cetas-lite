# Galaxy Z Fold 8: lighter, thinner, IP48

<a id="g07-7"></a>
### 7.7 Galaxy Z Fold 8: lighter, thinner, IP48

Strip away the marketing and the Galaxy Z Fold 8 is a hardware statement in its own right —
and, on the dimensions Samsung has chosen to compete on, a formidable one. The confirmed
figures: 9.7 mm thick when folded, 4.5 mm when open, IP48 certified against dust and water,
and — for the Fold 8 Ultra variant — a weight of 215 grams. The headline number, though, is
the standard Fold 8's: 201 grams, per Samsung's official materials published around
September 9, 2026, under the title "From 253 Grams to 201 Grams: The Engineering Behind the
World's Lightest Fold."

The "world's lightest fold" claim deserves unpacking, because it is both true on the stated
terms and carefully bounded. The 253-gram starting point is the previous generation's
weight; the 201-gram endpoint is the Fold 8's. A 52-gram reduction — more than a fifth of
the device's mass — in a single generation is a genuine engineering achievement, involving
the hinge, the display stack, and the chassis in combination. And 201 grams puts the Fold 8
Samsung has effectively erased much of the weight penalty that made foldables
feel like a compromise in the pocket.

Set against the iPhone Duo, the contrast is the sharpest in the chapter:

| Dimension | iPhone Duo | Galaxy Z Fold 8 |
|---|---|---|
| Weight | 254 g | 201 g (Fold 8 Ultra: 215 g) |
| Thickness (closed / open) | 11.3 mm / 5.2 mm | 9.7 mm / 4.5 mm |
| Water/dust resistance | IP68 (6 m, 30 min, IEC 60529) | IP48 |
| Frame material | Grade-5 titanium | (not disclosed in verified sources) |
| Internal display | 7.6" Super Retina XDR OLED, 120 Hz | (size not in verified sources) |
| Biometrics | Side Touch ID, no Face ID | (not in verified sources) |

On thinness and weight, Samsung wins clearly: 1.6 mm thinner closed, 0.7 mm thinner open,
53 grams lighter — the Duo is roughly 26% heavier. On water resistance, Apple wins on
paper: IP68 (6 meters, 30 minutes) against IP48, though Apple's own caveat about degradation
with wear applies, and the practical difference for most users — rain, spills, brief
submersion — is smaller than the ratings suggest. The table's gaps are themselves
information: this dossier records only what verified sources confirm, and Samsung's
full display and biometric specifications for the Fold 8 were not among the verified
facts available at the cutoff.

The IP48 rating is worth a sentence of explanation, because it is unusual. The "4" in the
first digit denotes protection against solid objects over 1 mm — a meaningful dust rating
for a device with a hinge, where grit ingress has historically been a failure mode. The "8"
is the same top-tier water rating Apple claims. Samsung has thus certified the two failure
modes that actually kill foldables — dust in the hinge, water in the fold — while Apple
certifies water to a deeper rating but says nothing quantified about dust. Neither rating
tells the whole durability story; together they describe two different engineering bets
about what breaks first.

The commercial context, from Samsung's own reporting: 1.44 million Z-series preorders in
Korea, and first-month sales of the Fold 8 and Fold 8 Ultra up 30% versus the Fold 7
generation. These figures describe the incumbent's momentum going into the quarter when
Apple enters. They do not predict the outcome — the Duo's 70-country launch and the iPhone
installed base are forces of a different order — but they establish that Samsung is not
entering this fight from weakness. It is entering it 53 grams lighter, holding the
category's sales records, and running an advertising campaign that says so out loud.

The IP ratings, decoded — because the two numbers describe different bets:

| Rating | Solids (first digit) | Liquids (second digit) | Meaning for a foldable |
|---|---|---|---|
| iPhone Duo: IP68 | 6 — dust-tight | 8 — submersion to 6 m for 30 min (IEC 60529) | Strongest water rating; Apple's caveat: resistance degrades with wear |
| Galaxy Z Fold 8: IP48 | 4 — protected against solids over 1 mm | 8 — top-tier water protection | Certifies the hinge's historical enemy: grit ingress |

The comparison is less one-sided than the headline numbers suggest. Apple's
6-meter, 30-minute rating is the deeper certification, but it arrives with
the standard Apple caveat — water resistance is not permanent and degrades
with normal wear — which carries extra weight on a device whose defining
feature is a hinge that cycles hundreds of times a month. Samsung's "4" for
solids is the unusual digit: few phones certify dust protection at all, and
on a foldable it addresses the historically real failure mode of grit in
the hinge mechanism. Each company has certified what its engineering fears
most. Neither rating, on its own, tells you which device survives longer in
a pocket full of sand.

The weight story, arithmetically:

| Figure | Value |
|---|---|
| Galaxy Z Fold 7 generation (per Samsung) | 253 g |
| Galaxy Z Fold 8 (per Samsung, ~9/09/2026) | 201 g |
| Single-generation reduction | 52 g — over a fifth of the device's mass |
| iPhone Duo | 254 g |
| Duo vs. Fold 8 gap | +53 g, roughly 26% heavier |

Samsung's headline — "From 253 Grams to 201 Grams: The Engineering Behind
the World's Lightest Fold" — frames the achievement as a single-generation
leap, and the arithmetic supports the framing: cutting more than a fifth of
a foldable's mass in one cycle, across hinge, display stack, and chassis in
combination, is a genuine engineering result. The "world's lightest" claim
is bounded by the category — lightest foldable, not lightest phone — but
within those bounds it is Samsung's official figure and stands
uncontradicted at cutoff.

Side by side, the geometry is clear: Samsung leads on thinness and weight
— the dimensions felt in hand and pocket — while Apple leads on paper
water resistance and display surface engineering. Even the 215 g Fold 8
Ultra remains 39 g lighter than the Duo. Apple's bet is that display
quality and iOS integration outweigh grams; Samsung's is that grams are the
spec sheet's most honest number.
