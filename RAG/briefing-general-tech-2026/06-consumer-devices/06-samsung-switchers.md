# Samsung's offensive: the 'Switchers' campaign

<a id="g07-6"></a>
### 7.6 Samsung's offensive: the "Switchers" campaign

If Apple's September was a product launch, Samsung's September was a tone. The Korean
company's response to the iPhone Duo was not defensive — at no point did Samsung's messaging
suggest a company caught off guard — but opportunistic and openly offensive, built on a
single framing line: "we are 7 years ahead." Seven years is the claimed age of Samsung's foldable
program, and Samsung has wielded the number as
both a credential and a taunt: we have been folding phones since before some of our
competitors' current customers owned a smartphone.

The centerpiece is the "Switchers" campaign, whose advertisement dropped on September 15,
2026 (covered by Sammy Fans). The ad's premise is couples therapy: iPhone users sit down
with a therapist — played by Dr. Orna Guralnik, a recognizable figure from documentary
television — to work through their attachment to Apple. The session's grievances are the
familiar switcher anxieties rendered as relationship problems: the "green bubble" stigma of
non-iMessage messaging, the fear of losing AirDrop, the dread of transferring a decade of
photos and chats. The Galaxy Z Fold 8 is presented as the resolution — the partner who
understands. It is a funny ad, and a pointed one: every joke is aimed at a specific lock-in
mechanism.

The lock-in jokes land because Samsung has spent the preceding months dismantling the
technical barriers behind them. On August 26, 2026, Samsung announced that its Quick Share
is now AirDrop-compatible — meaning file transfers between Galaxy devices and iPhones work
across the previously walled garden — and the "easy transfer from iPhone" claim in the
Switchers campaign rests on Smart Switch plus that AirDrop compatibility. The campaign is
not merely mocking the green bubble; it is advertising that the bubble's practical
consequences have been engineered away. Whether iPhone users believe it is a marketing
question; that the technical work is real is a matter of record.

The campaign's timing reveals the planning. Hours before Apple's September 9 event, Samsung
released a "Welcome to Foldables" video — a pre-emptive strike that positioned Samsung as
the category's host, welcoming Apple as a late guest rather than a conquering hero. Two
days after the keynote, on September 11, Samsung New Zealand ran an advertisement featuring
a Tim Cook lookalike — the joke being unmistakable even where the likeness was legally
deniable. Around September 20, per the Korea JoongAng Daily, Samsung put up outdoor
displays in Seoul and abroad extending the foldables messaging. This is a coordinated,
multi-week, multi-market offensive executed with the confidence of a company that had its
materials ready before Apple's event began — because it had been planning them for months.

Underneath the comedy, Samsung has commercial numbers to brandish. The company reports
1.44 million Z-series preorders in Korea alone — a domestic figure that sets the scale of
the Fold 8 generation — and says the Galaxy Z Fold 8 and Fold 8 Ultra together sold 30%
more in their first month than the Fold 7 generation did. These are Samsung's own figures,
not independently audited, but they establish the context in which the "7 years ahead"
framing is more than bravado: the incumbent is growing, on its home turf, in the quarter
before Apple's entry.

The strategic read is straightforward. Samsung cannot prevent Apple from entering the
foldable market, and it cannot outsell the iPhone installed base's conversion potential in
the long run. What it can do — and is doing — is define the terms of the comparison before
the Duo reaches shelves: lighter, thinner, more experienced, and now interoperable with the
iPhone's own sharing protocols. Every day between September 9 and October 23 that the
conversation is about Samsung's seven-year head start is a day it is not about Apple's
nano-texture. That is the campaign's real objective, and it is being pursued with unusual
discipline.

The campaign's beats, in chronological order:

| Date | Beat | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 26/08/2026 | Quick Share becomes AirDrop-compatible; "easy transfer from iPhone" via Smart Switch | Samsung announcement |
| 9/09/2026 (hours before Apple's keynote) | "Welcome to Foldables" video — Samsung as the category's host, welcoming Apple | Samsung release |
| 11/09/2026 | Samsung New Zealand ad featuring a Tim Cook lookalike | NZ campaign |
| 15/09/2026 | "Switchers" ad: couples therapy with Dr. Orna Guralnik, "green bubble," AirDrop anxiety | Sammy Fans |
| ~20/09/2026 | Outdoor displays in Seoul and abroad extending the foldables messaging | Korea JoongAng Daily |

The sequence is the strategy. August 26 establishes the technical fact —
switching is now frictionless, because Quick Share talks to AirDrop and
Smart Switch moves the data. September 9 establishes the frame — Samsung
welcomes Apple to foldables, reversing the expected hierarchy of newcomer
and incumbent. September 11–20 executes the mockery — the lookalike, the
therapy session, the billboards — with each joke aimed at a specific Apple
lock-in mechanism: the green bubble as social stigma, AirDrop as practical
dependency, the transfer process as dread. The campaign is not random
comedy; it is a sequenced argument that every reason not to switch has been
removed, and the removal is the punchline.

It is worth distinguishing the campaign's two layers. The surface layer is
the humor — therapy, lookalikes, billboards — aimed at attention: owning
the conversation in the six weeks between Apple's announcement and the
Duo's October 23 sale. The substrate layer is the interoperability work,
aimed at conversion: AirDrop-compatible Quick Share is a genuine technical
concession to the reality that switchers live in mixed-device households,
and "easy transfer from iPhone" is a claim Samsung can now make without
asterisks. Competitors have mocked Apple before; fewer have paired the
mockery with the engineering that makes the joke actionable.

Samsung's commercial figures — 1.44 million Z-series preorders in Korea,
Fold 8/8 Ultra first-month sales up 30% versus the Fold 7 — are the
company's own, not independently audited. Their function is credentialing:
"7 years ahead" persuades better from an incumbent that is growing than
one that is defending.
