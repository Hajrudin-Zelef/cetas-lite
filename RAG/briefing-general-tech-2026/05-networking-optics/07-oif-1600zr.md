# OIF 1600ZR: coherent 1.6T over a single wavelength

<a id="g06-7"></a>
### 6.7 OIF 1600ZR: coherent 1.6T over a single wavelength

On September 9, 2026, the Optical Internetworking Forum (OIF) published the
**1600ZR Implementation Agreement** (announced via BusinessWire; covered by
Photonics Spectra on September 11). It defines an interoperable **1.6T
coherent interface carried over a single wavelength**, in amplified DWDM
single-span links up to **120 km** — exactly twice the 800ZR capacity it
succeeds.

The significance is architectural. 800ZR standardized 800G coherent in a
pluggable form factor and collapsed what used to require a transponder shelf
into a QSFP-DD/OSFP module, enabling IP-over-DWDM: routers connecting directly
over DWDM fiber without an intervening optical-transport layer. 1600ZR extends
that collapse to 1.6T per wavelength, which matters for the metro/DCI links
feeding AI clusters — the very links FS.com's "Scale-Across" portfolio (6.1)
targets. Single-wavelength 1.6T also halves the fiber and amplifier resources
per bit versus two 800G wavelengths, at the cost of more demanding DSP and
optics — which is why the agreement's arrival in September 2026 coincides with
the 224G/lane component ecosystem reaching maturity (MACOM's demos in 6.10,
Credo's ZeroFlap in 6.11).

The OIF scheduled a **multi-vendor interoperability demonstration at ECOC
2026, booth #2126** — the kind of interworking proof that the FS "verified on
NVIDIA" self-claim of section 6.2 was not. As of September 22, with ECOC
ongoing, no show report on the demo had been published yet; that is normal
timing, not a contradiction, and the reader should expect the results to land
in the days after the show closes.

#### From 800ZR to 1600ZR: the capacity doubling

The "2x 800ZR capacity" framing is the whole story in four characters.
800ZR (standardized in 2020) put 400G — later 800G in its ZR+ variants —
of coherent capacity into a pluggable module and made IP-over-DWDM a
deployable architecture: routers talking directly over DWDM fiber, no
transponder shelf in between. 1600ZR does the same trick one generation up:
**1.6T of coherent capacity on a single wavelength**, in a pluggable form
factor, interoperable across vendors. The single-wavelength qualifier is
doing real work here — it means the spectral efficiency doubled, not just
the box throughput. For a carrier or a hyperscaler lighting metro fiber,
that halves the wavelengths, amplifiers and coherent receivers per bit,
which is where the economics of the DCI build-out actually live.

#### 120 km single-span: reading the reach figure

The agreement specifies **amplified DWDM single-span links up to 120 km**.
"Single-span" means no intermediate regeneration or amplification huts —
one fiber run, amplifiers at the ends, 120 km of it. That reach envelope is
the metro/DCI sweet spot: it covers the regional data-center pairs that
the AI build-out keeps creating (the second hall across the metro area,
the availability zone in the neighboring region) while staying inside what
a pluggable coherent module can close optically. It is also, not
coincidentally, the same distance class as FS.com's "Scale-Across" 500 km
claim from section 6.1 — the pluggable covers the metro, the muxponder and
amplified line systems cover the regional.

#### The interoperability proof that counts

The OIF process is the institutional opposite of the vendor self-claim
dissected in section 6.2. An Implementation Agreement is written by a
multi-vendor forum; the ECOC booth #2126 demo puts modules from competing
vendors on the same fiber to prove they interwork. That is the
interoperability evidence the market actually trusts, because no single
vendor controls the test. The September 9 publication date — twelve days
before ECOC opened — was timed to make the agreement the news and the
booth the proof, in that order.
