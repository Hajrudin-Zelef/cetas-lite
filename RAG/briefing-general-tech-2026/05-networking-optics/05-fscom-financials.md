# FS.com H1 2026 financials

<a id="g06-5"></a>
### 6.5 FS.com H1 2026 financials

FS.com's first-half 2026 results were reported around August 25, 2026 (via
TipRanks), with two headline figures:

| Metric | H1 2026 | Year-on-year change |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | RMB 1.76 billion | +25.9% |
| Net profit | RMB 451.1 million | +65.1% |

The profit growth running well ahead of revenue growth is the number that
deserves attention: a 65.1% jump in net profit on 25.9% revenue growth implies
meaningful margin expansion, consistent with the shift the company itself
reports toward high-speed (100G+) optics for AI clusters — the premium segment
of the transceiver market, where per-unit margins exceed those of legacy
low-rate parts. This is the financial corroboration of the portfolio story in
section 6.1: the AI-cluster pull is visible in the P&L, not just in press
releases.

The usual caveats apply: these are company-reported half-year figures relayed
by financial press (TipRanks), not audited annual accounts, and they arrive in
a year when every optical vendor's numbers are flattered by the AI build-out.
But as a directional read they are consistent with the demand picture painted
by the rest of this chapter — 1.6T ramping to volume, coherent DCI steady,
and capacity being reserved ahead of 2027.

#### Margin expansion, decoded

The gap between the two growth rates — profit up 65.1%, revenue up 25.9% —
is the analytical core of the H1 release. In a hardware business, profit
growing at two-and-a-half times the rate of revenue means the *mix* changed:
a larger share of each yuan of revenue came from high-margin products. The
company's own narrative points at the cause — high-speed (100G+) optics for
AI clusters, the premium segment where a 1.6T or 800G module sells at
multiples of a legacy 10G part's price with better gross margin. The H1
numbers are thus the financial corroboration of the portfolio story in
section 6.1: FS is not just announcing AI-networking products, it is
already being paid for them, and the payment is rich enough to move the
company's overall margin.

#### The limits of a half-year read

Three cautions keep this section honest. First, these are company-reported
figures relayed by financial press (TipRanks) — directionally reliable,
but not audited annual accounts. Second, every optical vendor's 2026
numbers are flattered by the same AI cycle; FS's growth says as much about
the market as about the company, and the dossier has no verified
market-share figure to apportion the credit. Third, the half-year window
captures the 800G volume phase and only the very beginning of the 1.6T
ramp (section 6.6 dates volume to September 2026) — the 1.6T contribution
to these numbers is real but early. The H2 2026 results, when published,
will be the first to show a full half of 1.6T shipments.
