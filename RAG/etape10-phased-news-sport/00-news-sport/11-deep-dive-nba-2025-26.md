# 11. Deep dive — NBA 2025-26

## 11. Deep dive — NBA 2025-26

**Regular season.** Oklahoma City finished with the league's best record (64–18)
despite Jalen Williams missing most of the year (33 games, wrist injury). San Antonio
went 62–20 behind Victor Wembanyama's two-way leap, returning to the postseason for
the first time since 2019. Denver went 54–28. Luka Dončić led the league in scoring
(33.5 ppg) for the Lakers but a late hamstring injury cost him MVP momentum; Nikola
Jokić averaged a triple-double for the second straight season (27.7 ppg, 12.9 rpg,
10.7 apg) and became the first player ever to lead the NBA in both rebounds and
assists. `[independent]`

**MVP vote (announced 2026-05-17, Amazon Prime).** Shai Gilgeous-Alexander won his
second straight MVP — the first back-to-back winner since Jokić (2021, 2022) and the
first guard to repeat since Stephen Curry. He averaged 31.1 points on a career-high
55.3% shooting with 6.6 assists in 68 games, with eight 40-point games including a
season-high 55 in double overtime vs Indiana. Voting (100 media voters): 1.
Gilgeous-Alexander 939 points (83 first-place votes); 2. Jokić 634 (10 first-place);
3. Wembanyama 569; 4. Dončić 250; 5. Cade Cunningham 117. `[independent]`

Wembanyama, 22, was the youngest and first unanimous DPOY, leading the league in
blocks for the third straight season (25.0 ppg, 11.5 rpg, 3.1 apg, 3.1 bpg).
Mazzulla became the fourth Celtics coach to win Coach of the Year and the first since
1980, guiding Boston to the No. 2 seed with Jayson Tatum sidelined most of the year. `[independent]`

**Playoffs.** The Thunder swept through the first two rounds; the East produced a
Cavaliers–Pistons Game 7 in round one. The conference finals sent the New York Knicks
(East) and San Antonio Spurs (West) to the Finals. `[independent][secondary]`

**2026 NBA Finals: New York Knicks 4, San Antonio Spurs 1.** Game 5 (June 13, 2026):
Knicks 94, Spurs 90. Jalen Brunson poured in 45 points in the clincher and won Finals
MVP. It was New York's first championship since 1973 — 53 years — built around
Brunson, Karl-Anthony Towns and Josh Hart. `[independent]`

**Lottery and draft.** Lottery (May 10, Chicago): 1 Washington, 2 Utah, 3 Memphis,
4 Chicago, 5 LA Clippers (via Indiana), 6 Brooklyn, 7 Sacramento, 8 Atlanta (via New
Orleans), 9 Dallas, 10 Milwaukee, 11 Golden State, 12 Oklahoma City (via LA Clippers),
13 Miami, 14 Charlotte. Draft (June 23): 1 AJ Dybantsa (BYU, Washington), 2 Darryn
Peterson (Kansas, Utah), 3 Cameron Boozer (Duke, Memphis). Washington paired the top
pick with its in-season acquisitions of Trae Young and Anthony Davis; Utah paired its
pick with February arrival Jaren Jackson Jr., Lauri Markkanen and 2025 pick Ace
Bailey. `[independent]`
