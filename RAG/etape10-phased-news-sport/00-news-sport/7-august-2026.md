# 7. August 2026

## 7. August 2026

**Tour de France Femmes.** Demi Vollering won the 2026 edition — her second victory in
the race — cementing her status as the world's leading stage racer. `[independent]`

**European Athletics Championships (Birmingham, Alexander Stadium, mid-August).** Italy
topped the medal table for the second straight outdoor championships with 10 gold,
6 silver, 6 bronze (22 total), clinching it in the final event when the men's 4x400m
relay won by three hundredths of a second ahead of Great Britain. Hosts Great Britain
and Northern Ireland finished second with 9–8–2 (19), matching their best-ever gold
haul. Germany were third (6–10–5, 21), then the Netherlands (5–3–6) and Norway (4–0–2).
Standout performances: Amy Hunt (GBR) won four golds — the first athlete ever to win
four golds at a single European Championships (women's 100m in 11.00, 200m, and both
sprint relays); Dina Asher-Smith became the most decorated athlete in European
Championships history; Jakob Ingebrigtsen (NOR) won the 5000m in 13:15.29; Karsten
Warholm (NOR) won the 400m hurdles in 46.63 for his fourth European title; Mondo
Duplantis (SWE) won the pole vault at 6.15m; Gianmarco Tamberi (ITA) won the high jump
at 2.32m; Georgia Hunter Bell (GBR) won the women's 1500m. The championships drew
1,645 athletes across 50 events and 218,135 spectators, producing two world records,
four European records, 11 championship records and 40 national records, with a
reported TV audience of 17.2 million. It was the first edition underwritten by a
company rather than a government: Athletic Ventures (UK Athletics, London Marathon
Events, The Great Run Company) carried the full financial risk. `[independent][secondary]`

**2026-08-30 — US Open begins (Flushing Meadows, runs to September 13).** Total prize
money was a record $108 million, with $5.5 million to each singles champion. In the
men's draw, Ben Shelton beat Carlos Alcaraz in the quarterfinals. `[independent]`

**Summer transfer window.** The window ran July 1 → September 1 (England, Spain).
Premier League clubs spent a record ≈£3.5bn — about £3.48bn per Sports Mole,
surpassing the £3.19bn of summer 2025 — with four deals above £100m and 11 clubs
breaking their own transfer records. La Liga clubs spent about €748m. See the deep
dive in Section 10 for the full table. `[secondary]`

**World Cup legacy.** Host-city impact reporting continued through August; the
tournament's reported $15bn revenue figure circulated but rests on secondary
aggregation. `[unverified]`

## 8. September 2026 (through the 22nd)

**2026-09-13 — US Open finals.** Alexander Zverev defeated Ben Shelton
6–3, 7–6(2), 5–7, 6–2 to win his second Grand Slam of 2026 (after Roland-Garros).
Elena Rybakina defeated Aryna Sabalenka 6–4, 5–7, 6–2 for her second major of the
season (after the Australian Open) and third overall. Doubles champions: Christian
Harrison / Neal Skupski (men); Kateřina Siniaková / Taylor Townsend (women), completing
a career Grand Slam as a partnership. `[official][independent]`

**2026-09-16 — Formula 1 standings (official, season in progress).** With the season
still running at the research cutoff, the drivers' standings read: 1. Kimi Antonelli
292, 2. George Russell 211, 3. Lewis Hamilton 191, 4. Lando Norris 186, 5. Charles
Leclerc 167. Mercedes led the constructors' championship with 503 points. No 2026
champion is named in this file — the season was not over. `[official]`

**Transfer window closes (September 1).** The Premier League and La Liga windows shut;
deadline-day business included Manchester City's British-record £125m signing of Enzo
Fernández from Chelsea and Barcelona's late moves for Gabriel Jesus (from Arsenal) and
goalkeeper Dominik Livaković, plus Atlético Madrid's loan of Jonathan David from
Juventus. `[secondary]`

**New seasons begin.** The NFL's 2026 season kicked off in early September; the 2026-27
UEFA club competitions began their league phases around the third week of September.
