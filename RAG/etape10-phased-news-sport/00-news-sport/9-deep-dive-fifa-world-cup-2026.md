# 9. Deep dive — FIFA World Cup 2026

## 9. Deep dive — FIFA World Cup 2026

**Identity.** 23rd FIFA World Cup; first hosted by three nations (Canada, Mexico,
United States); first with 48 teams; 16 host cities (11 USA, 3 Mexico, 2 Canada);
June 11 – July 19, 2026 (39 days); final at MetLife Stadium, East Rutherford, New
Jersey. Mexico became the first country to host three World Cups (1970, 1986, 2026);
Canada hosted for the first time; the United States previously hosted in 1994. The
event returned to the traditional June–July slot after Qatar 2022's winter edition. `[secondary]`

**Format.** Twelve groups of four; the top two in each group plus the eight best
third-placed teams advanced to a new round of 32 (June 28 – July 3), then round of 16
(July 4–7), quarterfinals (July 9–11), semifinals (July 14–15), third-place match
(July 18) and final (July 19). Teams reaching the semifinals played eight matches.
The United States hosted 78 matches including all quarterfinals, semifinals and the
final; AT&T Stadium in Arlington hosted the most matches of any venue (nine); Canada
and Mexico hosted 13 each. `[secondary]`

**Group winners.** A Mexico, B Switzerland, C Brazil, D United States, E Germany,
F Netherlands, G Belgium, H Spain, I France, J Argentina, K Colombia, L England. The
eight best third-placed teams came from groups B, D, E, F, I, J, K and L. `[secondary]`

**Final, in detail.** Scoreless through 90 minutes; Ferran Torres broke the deadlock
in the 106th minute. Argentina did not manage a shot on target all match; Emiliano
Martínez made ten saves to keep the holders alive. Spain's triumph was built on the
tournament's best defense — one goal conceded in eight matches, a World Cup record —
and on the midfield command of Golden Ball winner Rodri. `[independent][secondary]`

**Records set.**
- Most goals per match since 1970: 2.96 (308 goals in 104 matches).
- Highest attendance in World Cup history: 6,810,966 (65,490 per match), beating 1994.
- Mbappé: all-time World Cup top scorer; first player to win back-to-back Golden Boots.
- Spain: fewest goals conceded by a champion (1); first nation to hold the men's and women's titles simultaneously.
- First semifinal lineup composed entirely of the FIFA ranking's top four. `[secondary]`

**Rule and format novelties.** 10-second substitution exits (else the replacement waits
a minute); 5-second visual countdowns for throw-ins and goal kicks with turnover
sanctions; expanded VAR covering wrongly awarded corners, certain attacking fouls and
second-yellow red cards; automatic red cards for mouth-covering confrontations and for
leaving the field in protest; head-to-head results replacing goal difference as the
first group-stage tiebreaker; mandatory three-minute hydration breaks in every half
(broadcasters permitted commercials; the minutes counted toward stoppage time). `[secondary]`

**Off-field.** FIFA opened disciplinary proceedings against Argentina after the
tournament: a "Las Malvinas son Argentinas" banner displayed following the 2–1
semifinal win over England raised a potential breach of the non-sporting
demonstrations rules, and post-final altercations with Spanish players were also
cited. Reported tournament revenue of $15 billion is widely repeated but rests on
secondary aggregation. `[secondary][unverified for revenue]`
