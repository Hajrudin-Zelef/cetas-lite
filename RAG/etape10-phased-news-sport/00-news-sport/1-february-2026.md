# 1. February 2026

## 1. February 2026

**2026-02-01 — Australian Open finals (Melbourne).** The season's first Grand Slam ended
with two finals decided in four sets. In the men's final, Carlos Alcaraz defeated Novak
Djokovic 2–6, 6–2, 6–3, 7–5, completing a career Grand Slam and becoming the youngest man
to win all four majors. In the women's final, Elena Rybakina defeated Aryna Sabalenka
6–4, 4–6, 6–4. `[independent]`

**2026-02-05 — NBA trade deadline.** Reported moves included the Washington Wizards
acquiring All-Stars Trae Young and Anthony Davis, and the Utah Jazz acquiring Jaren
Jackson Jr. — deals that later shaped the lottery and draft narrative. `[secondary]`

**2026-02-06 → 2026-02-22 — Milano Cortina Winter Olympics.** The XXV Olympic Winter
Games ran across Milan and Cortina d'Ampezzo, Italy. Norway topped the final medal
table with 18 gold, 12 silver, 11 bronze (41 total). The United States finished second
(12–12–9, 33), the Netherlands third on golds (10–7–3, 20), hosts Italy fourth
(10–6–14, 30) and Germany fifth (8–10–8, 26). Italy's 30 medals were a national Winter
Games record. `[secondary]`

**2026-02-08 — Super Bowl LX (Levi's Stadium, Santa Clara, California).** The Seattle
Seahawks defeated the New England Patriots 29–13 to win the franchise's second Lombardi
Trophy, the first since Super Bowl XLVIII (2014). Seattle's "Dark Side" defense sacked
Patriots quarterback Drake Maye six times and forced three turnovers; running back
Kenneth Walker III was named Super Bowl MVP after 135 rushing yards on 27 carries
(161 total yards). Quarterback Sam Darnold went 19-of-38 for 202 yards and a touchdown
with no turnovers; kicker Jason Myers made five field goals; Cooper Kupp led Seattle
with 6 catches for 61 yards. Maye finished with 295 passing yards, one touchdown, two
interceptions and a fumble; New England managed only 79 rushing yards and was held
scoreless until the fourth quarter. Seattle had been a 4.5-point favorite. `[independent]`

**Super Bowl LX audience.** Nielsen reported an average audience of 124.9 million
across NBC, Peacock, Telemundo, NBC Sports Digital and NFL+, making it the
second-most-watched Super Bowl in U.S. history (behind Super Bowl LIX's 127.7 million
in 2025) and the most-watched program in NBCUniversal's 100-year history. The peak
audience hit 137.8 million in the second quarter; the Bad Bunny halftime show averaged
128.2 million; Telemundo's 3.3 million was a Spanish-language Super Bowl record. This
was the first Super Bowl fully measured with Nielsen's Big Data + Panel methodology. `[secondary]`

**February football.** The Champions League knockout play-offs concluded, setting up the
round of 16; domestic leagues entered their decisive thirds. Paris Saint-Germain led
Ligue 1, Arsenal led the Premier League, Barcelona led La Liga. `[secondary]`
