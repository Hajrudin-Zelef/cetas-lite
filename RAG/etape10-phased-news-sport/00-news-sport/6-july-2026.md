# 6. July 2026

## 6. July 2026

**2026-07-04 → 2026-07-26 — Tour de France.** Starting in Barcelona and finishing in
Paris, the 113th Tour was won by Tadej Pogačar — his fifth yellow jersey, drawing him
level with Jacques Anquetil, Eddy Merckx, Bernard Hinault and Miguel Induráin. Final
podium: 1. Pogačar; 2. Remco Evenepoel at +6:26; 3. Isaac del Toro at +9:42, who also
won the white jersey (best young rider). Green jersey (points): Mads Pedersen.
Polka-dot jersey (mountains): Richard Carapaz. Mathieu van der Poel won the final
stage on the Champs-Élysées. `[independent][secondary]`

**World Cup knockout stage (July 4–11).** Round of 16 (July 4–7): England beat Mexico
3–2 at the Estadio Azteca — Mexico's first-ever World Cup defeat at the Azteca and the
final tournament match played in Mexico; Belgium beat the United States 4–1 (July 6);
Argentina beat Egypt to become the Americas' sole quarter-finalist. Quarterfinals
(July 9–11): Argentina survived a scare against Switzerland to book a semifinal with
England — Switzerland's first World Cup quarterfinal in 72 years (since hosting in
1954); Spain faced Belgium in the quarterfinals. All remaining knockout matches were
played in the United States. `[secondary]`

**2026-07-12 — Wimbledon finals.** Jannik Sinner defeated Alexander Zverev
6–7(7–9), 7–6(7–2), 6–3, 6–4 to defend his Wimbledon crown — his second straight title
and fifth Grand Slam overall, and the 10th successful men's title defense at
Wimbledon. Zverev, in his run to the final, replaced the absent Alcaraz as world No. 2.
In the women's final, Linda Nosková defeated Karolína Muchová in an all-Czech final to
win her first Grand Slam. Doubles champions: Harri Heliövaara / Henry Patten (men),
Guo Hanyu / Kristina Mladenovic (women), Marcelo Arévalo / Jeļena Ostapenko (mixed). `[independent][secondary]`

**2026-07-14 — World Cup semifinal 1 (Dallas): Spain 2, France 0.** Spain ended
France's title defense and reached the final. `[secondary]`

**2026-07-15 — World Cup semifinal 2 (Atlanta): Argentina 2, England 1.** The
defending champions beat England to set up a Spain–Argentina final. It was the first
time in history that the top four teams in the FIFA Men's World Ranking (Argentina,
Spain, France, England) all reached the semifinals, and only the third time all four
semifinalists were former champions (after 1970 and 1990). `[secondary]`

**2026-07-18 — World Cup third-place match (Miami): England 6, France 4.** England
took third in a ten-goal classic. `[secondary]`

**2026-07-19 — FIFA World Cup final (MetLife Stadium, East Rutherford).**
Spain 1, Argentina 0 after extra time. Ferran Torres scored the winner in the 106th
minute. Argentina failed to register a shot on target; Emiliano Martínez made ten
saves. Spain won their second World Cup (first since 2010), conceded only one goal in
the entire tournament (a record), and became the first nation to simultaneously hold
the men's and women's World Cup titles. The tournament set records: 104 matches, 308
goals (2.96 per match, the highest average since 1970), and 6,810,966 spectators
(65,490 per match) — the highest attendance in World Cup history, surpassing 1994.
Tournament revenue was reported at a record $15 billion. `[independent][secondary]`

**World Cup awards.** Golden Boot: Kylian Mbappé, 10 goals — his second consecutive
Golden Boot, making him the World Cup's all-time leading scorer and the first player
to win multiple Golden Boots. Golden Ball (best player): Rodri (Spain). Golden Glove:
Unai Simón (Spain). Young Player Award: Pau Cubarsí (Spain). Fair Play Trophy:
Netherlands. `[secondary]`

**World Cup aftermath and controversies.** FIFA opened disciplinary proceedings against
Argentina over two incidents: players displaying a "Las Malvinas son Argentinas" banner
after the 2–1 semifinal win over England (a potential breach of rules on non-sporting
demonstrations), and post-match altercations with Spanish players after the final. The
tournament also introduced lasting rule novelties: 10-second substitution exits,
5-second restart countdowns for throw-ins and goal kicks, expanded VAR (wrongly
awarded corners, certain attacking fouls, second-yellow red cards), automatic red cards
for mouth-covering confrontations and for leaving the field in protest, head-to-head
results as the first group tiebreaker, and mandatory three-minute hydration breaks in
each half. Debutant nations: Cape Verde, Curaçao, Jordan and Uzbekistan. `[secondary]`
