# Etape 10E — World News: Wars, Geopolitics, Elections, Economy, Fortunes (February 2026 – 22 September 2026)

**Phase:** 10E (News — World: conflicts, geopolitics, diplomacy, elections, economy, fortunes)
**Coverage period:** 1 February 2026 → 22 September 2026 (cutoff). Events announced or scheduled after 22 September 2026 are flagged as *planned*, not completed.
**Language:** English (per project rule).
**Cutoff note:** All facts are stated as of 22 September 2026 unless a different explicit date is given. Scheduled future events (e.g., the announced Trump–Xi meeting of 24 September 2026, the December 2026 G20, October 2026 elections) are reported only as announced plans.

## 1. Scope, method and provenance legend

### 1.1 Scope

- Armed conflicts and war diplomacy: Ukraine; Gaza/Middle East; the US–Israel–Iran war that began in late February 2026; Sudan; the Sahel (Mali, Burkina Faso, Niger, AES); and other conflicts (Myanmar, DRC/M23, Yemen/Red Sea, Syria, Haiti, Somalia) where sources allowed.
- Geopolitics: second-term US administration foreign policy; the US anti-cartel boat-strike campaign in the Caribbean and Eastern Pacific; EU; NATO (as reported); China–Taiwan; the US–China tariff war and the reported trade truce; G7, G20, BRICS summits.
- Elections and politics: national elections held February–September 2026 (Colombia, Peru); the scheduled US midterm elections of November 2026 and polling context; other scheduled votes flagged as planned.
- Economy and markets: inflation, central-bank decisions (Federal Reserve, ECB), energy prices (notably the oil shock tied to the Iran war), equity markets, and crypto assets.
- Fortunes: the Forbes 40th annual World's Billionaires list (March 2026) and the Hurun Global Rich List 2026 (March 2026), plus wealth-related events during the period.
- Major non-conflict headlines: natural catastrophes, aviation accidents and incidents, and other large-scale events (e.g., the death of Pope Francis and the election of Pope Leo XIV, the UN General Assembly opening).

### 1.2 Method

- Public web research conducted on 22 September 2026 using news wires (Reuters, AP), official institutional pages (ECB, PMO Canada), established newspapers (Le Monde, USA Today, Morningstar/Dow Jones), conflict monitors (ACLED as reported via press), UN/relief reporting (ReliefWeb/OCHA), and secondary outlets where primary reporting was unavailable.
- Every factual claim carries one provenance tag: `[official]` (government/institutional source), `[independent]` (wire or established press reporting), `[vendor-reported]` (data vendor or list publisher such as Forbes/Hurun), `[secondary]` (aggregators, blogs, or press republishing third-party material), `[unverified]` (single weak source or claim that could not be corroborated).
- Casualty figures and belligerent claims are always attributed to the reporting party and dated; they are never presented as independently verified.
- Conflicting figures and unresearched topics are collected in Section 4 (gaps and conflicts register), not silently resolved.

### 1.3 Provenance legend

- `[official]` — government, central bank, UN agency, or other primary institutional source.
- `[independent]` — wire service (Reuters, AP) or established newspaper/magazine reporting.
- `[vendor-reported]` — figures published by a commercial data vendor or ranking publisher (Forbes, Hurun, Aon, Munich Re), i.e., the vendor's own methodology.
- `[secondary]` — aggregators, blogs, commentary sites, or outlets republishing wire/press material without independent verification visible.
- `[unverified]` — claim seen in only one weak source, or not corroborated by any wire/institutional source consulted.
