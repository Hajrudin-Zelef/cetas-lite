## Full summary

This article guides hiring managers and candidates on what a data analyst job description should contain. It covers the different analyst types, core and additional responsibilities, sector-specific differences, qualifications and skills, and a ready-to-use job description template.

The article first distinguishes analyst roles: **data analyst** (reports and analyses for strategic decisions; statistics, SQL, visualization), **business analyst** (uses data tools to improve internal processes; SQL, project management), **business intelligence analyst** (similar to data analyst, focused on company performance; SQL, project management, visualization), **data engineer** (builds infrastructure to collect, store, transform data; orchestration tools like Airflow, programming), **analytics engineer** (hybrid of data engineer and analyst; dbt, orchestration, strong SQL), and **data scientist** (like an analyst but focused on predictive analysis; inferential statistics, machine learning, programming). It advises considering the business model and sector (healthcare, public sector, fintech).

Core responsibilities rest on three pillars: **automation** (reducing repetitive tasks, freeing time, reducing waste), **reporting** (the heart of the role—KPIs, insight visualization, stakeholder communication), and **data governance** ("garbage in, garbage out"—identifying and fixing data quality issues, documentation, tagging, classification, metadata management). Additional responsibilities: answering ad hoc data questions, coaching data users, and reducing technical debt. Sector differences: finance/healthcare involve specialized data (financial entries, patient records); marketing/e-commerce focus on customer behavior and trends; sales on funnel conversion rates.

Qualifications and skills: a bachelor's degree in a quantitative discipline (CS, math, economics) is often required, though online self-study and certifications (e.g., DataCamp's Data Analyst Certification) are increasingly accepted, especially for non-traditional profiles. Key knowledge/tools: **statistics and probability** (central tendency, dispersion, regression, sampling distributions, hypothesis testing), **spreadsheets** (Excel/Google Sheets), **SQL** (the analytics essential, common in technical interviews), **programming** (Python or R), **visualization tools** (Power BI, Tableau, Looker), and **engineering tools** (dbt for ELT transformations, Git/GitHub for version control). Soft skills: cross-functional collaboration, project management, handling ambiguous/complex problems, and attention to detail.

The article ends with a customizable job description template covering title, location, description, responsibilities, and qualifications, and points to DataCamp's Recruit and Data Jobs platforms. The template lists example qualifications: 2+ years experience (or relevant projects), strong SQL, visualization tools, statistical software as a plus, analytical/communication skills, and autonomy.

## Key points

- Distinguishes six analyst-adjacent roles: data analyst, business analyst, BI analyst, data engineer, analytics engineer, data scientist.
- Core responsibilities: automation, reporting, and data governance ("garbage in, garbage out").
- Additional duties: ad hoc data questions, user coaching, and reducing technical debt.
- Essential skills: statistics, spreadsheets, SQL, Python/R, visualization tools, dbt, Git/GitHub.
- Soft skills: cross-functional collaboration, project management, ambiguity handling, attention to detail.
- Sector context matters (finance/healthcare data specifics vs marketing/e-commerce customer behavior).
- Provides a full customizable job description template.
