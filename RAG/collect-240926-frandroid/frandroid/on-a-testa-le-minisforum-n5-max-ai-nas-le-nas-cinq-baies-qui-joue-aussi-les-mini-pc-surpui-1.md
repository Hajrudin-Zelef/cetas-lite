# on-a-testa-le-minisforum-n5-max-ai-nas-le-nas-cinq-baies-qui-joue-aussi-les-mini-pc-surpuissants

<!-- source: https://www.frandroid.com/marques/minisforum/3226029_on-a-teste-le-minisforum-n5-max-ai-nas-le-nas-cinq-baies-qui-joue-aussi-les-mini-pc-surpuissants -->

Having a NAS at home means always having your data close at hand without having to shell out thousands for a cloud subscription. In another vein, swapping your bulky tower for a mini-PC means saving space without (too much) losing performance.

Rather than offering two separate products, Minisforum therefore set out to combine the best of both worlds with the N5 MAX AI NAS which, as a bonus, packs top-tier components to handle any eventuality: an AMD Ryzen AI Max+ 395 processor, 10 GbE connectivity, and five 3.5-inch storage bays.

## A Careful Design, Built to Evolve

Minisforum's arrival in the NAS world logically coincides with the manufacturer's use of a new type, a new format of chassis. It is indeed difficult to fit five 3.5-inch bays into a classic mini-PC case. On the other hand, whether we are talking about the N5 NAS, the N5 PRO NAS, the N5 Air AI NAS, or the N5 MAX AI NAS, the philosophy, and therefore the chassis, are fairly similar. The key idea is to rest all the electronic components in the lower part of the NAS in order to create two genuine "chambers."

One part of the NAS is thus dedicated to storage space while the other is devoted to all the components, from the CPU to the M.2 SSDs, including the RAM and, of course, the entire cooling system. The intelligence of this design goes even further in the case of the N5 MAX AI NAS since the motherboard is mounted on rails: by simply removing two screws, you can slide it out to access the machine's innards. It is then very easy to upgrade the machine's configuration. Well done.

As for the NAS portion, it is a little less original, but still very well executed. The front panel is magnetic and comes off without difficulty, revealing the five 3.5-inch bays, which are fitted with a caddy to easily mount the storage unit. You can tell that Minisforum drew inspiration from the best solutions in the field; the whole thing exudes reliability, though with one remark: first of all, there is no question of locking each caddy into its bay. A shame, since it is a security option offered by many competitors.

That said, it takes nothing away from the overall aesthetics of a product that adopts a classic rectangular shape but makes good use of premium materials. The chassis is made of steel for greater robustness, even if this entails significant weight: the NAS measures 199 x 202.4 x 252.3 millimeters and, above all, weighs just a little over 5.8 kilograms. Quite something.

### Abundant and Modern Connectivity

This weight of almost 6 kg must immediately be put into perspective. First, by the materials used, since, as we said, the N5 MAX AI NAS is entirely made of steel for robustness that is beyond doubt. Also put into perspective by the integration of the power supply unit: this is fairly rare in the mini-PC world — a little less so on the NAS side — but it means only having cables to manage. Moreover, it takes nothing away from the power supply's output since Minisforum can offer us 250 W, a figure that is perfect given the components on board.

The N5 MAX AI NAS has a three-prong power port that you connect to the mains and which is of course only one of the many connectors available. On the rear of the product, you will indeed find an HDMI 2.1 port, one USB-A 2.0 and another 3.2 Gen 2. Even more interesting, Minisforum does not kid around with two USB4 V2 ports capable of 80 Gbps, but also of handling display (DP 2.0) and power delivery (15 W). Finally, two RJ45 ports are there to ensure the network connection: they are nothing less than 10 GbE!

*On the rear of the NAS, the connectivity is modern: on the left, the two USB4 ports and, on the right, the two RJ45 10 GbE ports.*

To round things off, the front of the mini-PC/NAS is also well equipped: two USB ports are indeed offered. The first is USB-C 3.2 Gen 2 while the second is nothing other than a third USB4 port (40 Gbps, DP 2.0, PD 15 W). Yes, we have a total of three USB4 ports! Finally, that is not all, because Minisforum has integrated one last USB port: it is a USB-A with the 3.2 Gen 1 standard found on the motherboard, inside the chassis. Its purpose? To allow you to plug in a USB key to deploy your preferred OS, for example.

### First-Rate Components

Choosing your OS may seem like an odd idea at first glance, but that would be quickly forgetting the versatility of the N5 MAX AI NAS. Indeed, to power its machine, Minisforum relies on AMD's very popular Ryzen AI MAX+ 395 CPU. The latter has 16 Zen 5 cores/32 threads with a maximum frequency of 5.1 GHz. Even better, it integrates a Radeon 8060S GPU with 40 RDNA 3.5 compute units up to 2.9 GHz. To complete the trio, AMD has prepared an NPU rated for a maximum of 50 TOPS, or 126 TOPS for the entire chip.

Answering to the code name Strix Halo, this chip has often been talked about insofar as it is the first CPU capable of convincingly running even the most demanding games, without of course needing a dedicated graphics card. Better still, it does so without blowing up power consumption: its TDP is given as 55 W by default with possible configuration between 45 and 120 W. It should therefore also not heat a compact PC excessively, which must also contend with the heat from a maximum of five hard drives.

*For our tests, we used Seagate IronWolf hard drives and Kingston DC600M SSDs.*

Five hard drives that form the heart of the NAS portion of the product. These are classic bays that rely on an equally classic SATA interface. Officially, they accept hard drives up to 32 TB for a maximum capacity of 160 TB. That said, Minisforum mentions a total capacity of 200 TB because you can also count on the presence of no fewer than five M.2 slots for SSDs up to 2280 of 8 TB each: 5 x 32 TB of hard drives plus 5 x 8 TB of M.2 SSDs, and we indeed get our 200 TB.

Note, however, that by default, the N5 MAX AI NAS ships with a preinstalled SSD. It is an M.2 2280 model with a capacity of 128 GB on which the MinisCloud operating system is installed. It is, however, entirely possible to replace it with a larger one and also to change the operating system, but we will come back to that very soon.

Before that, let us also mention the RAM portion: Minisforum offers, as a choice, 64 GB or 128 GB of LPDDR5 soldered to the CPU. This should be taken into account: while storage and connectivity are largely upgradable, the same is not true for the RAM which, let us remember, is shared between the CPU and the GPU.

### Freedom of the operating system

As we said, Minisforum ships its N5 MAX AI NAS with an M.2 SSD with a capacity of 128 GB on which the Miniscloud OS is preinstalled. Functional, this OS provides the bare minimum for such a versatile PC/NAS. Everything related to file sharing, user management, and backup is on the menu of an OS that nevertheless cannot compete with the software of NAS specialists that have been around for years. Synology of course, but also ASUSTOR, QNAP, or Terramaster do much better with software that is far more complete, more pleasant, and more functional.

*Still very imperfect, the Miniscloud OS interface still contains small portions in Chinese.*
