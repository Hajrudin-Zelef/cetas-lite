# test-de-l-iphone-18-pro-le-meilleur-iphone-est-aussi-le-plus-prudent-frandroid

<!-- source: https://www.frandroid.com/test/3254233_test-iphone-18-pro -->

After my review of the iPhone 17 Pro, here is, one year later, that of the 18 Pro. And yet, I ended up spending the year with an Air, which I hadn't reviewed at the time. I was too drawn to novelty, but the Air's compromises eventually wore me down. Admittedly, I'm doing 1.7 charges per day right now. Basically, I charge my smartphone almost twice a day, which is no longer sustainable.

Anyway, back to the Pro. And the 18 Pro arrives with a 150-euro price increase, without a standard iPhone 18 alongside it, and with a photo feature that everyone understood was more impressive to announce than to use. That didn't stop us from testing it thoroughly.

A quick note before we begin: we're not here to do Apple bashing, but we're not going to indulge in blind praise with superlatives either. That's never been our style. The iPhone Pro excels in many areas and still stumbles in others, and we're going to explain which ones.

## Design: the same iPhone, better finished, and no heavier

You have to put a 17 Pro next to it to find a difference, and even then. The aluminum unibody frame, the full-width camera plateau, the 8.75 mm thickness, everything is identical down to the tenth. Last year's cases fit this one. So, with a few exceptions, the vast majority of cases are compatible.

What changes is the back: the glass is finally the same color as the aluminum, and the two-tone look of the 17 Pro, which sparked debate, is gone. Black is black. Glacier is a straightforward sky blue, Silver is finally silver. Apple listened to the criticism, and it shows.

Weight is the point where I want to be precise: both smartphones have put on some bulk. The 18 Pro weighs 211 grams, 7 more than the 17 Pro, and I didn't feel it. The 18 Pro Max weighs 249 grams, 18 more than its predecessor, and everyone who has held it this week says the same thing: it's the limit.

I didn't test the Max, but I've had an Air for a year and I know what a few extra grams do to a jacket pocket. At 249, we're entering Duo territory, which weighs 254 and, unlike this one, folds.

The Dynamic Island has shrunk by about 25%, because the Face ID infrared receiver has moved under the screen, into a less dense pixel area to the left of the clock. Face ID doesn't seem to have lost anything. The island now displays three Live Activities in compact format, or two in medium format, which is useful once a week and pleasant the rest of the time. But that's just 1% more screen.

A word on the color, because it's the first thing you see and the last thing reviews talk about. Apple decided that the star of the year would be Bordeaux, and all the communication aligned with it, just like Cosmic Orange last year.

Except that the real success of this generation is the other new addition: Glacier, the blue. A light blue, almost powdery in the shade, that wakes up in the sun without ever turning garish, and that echoes the Sierra Blue of the iPhone 13 Pro that many never got over seeing disappear. If I'm making a big deal of it, it's because I like it a lot.

I find that it doesn't scream "new iPhone" ostentatiously, it just looks pretty. It's my choice, and judging by the feedback since launch, I'm not the only one. Given the place color has taken in Apple's marketing, it's no surprise that the Glacier is harder to find than expected.

Very distinctive colors always have a downside: they date the device as soon as the next generation arrives. That's worth keeping in mind.

Finally, a point that still leaves me just as puzzled: the Camera Control button. Two years after its arrival, I noticed that its capacitive function was now disabled by default. That says a lot about this famous button presented as an innovation. For my part, I still haven't gotten into the habit of using it. It's there, but I forget about it. A sort of appendage I don't quite know what to do with.

## Display: the best of the year, with one small caveat

The iPhone 18 Pro reuses the 17 Pro's screen: 6.3 inches, 2,622 x 1,206 pixels, adaptive 120 Hz, Ceramic Shield 2 with anti-reflective treatment. Apple announces nothing new, and that's precisely why it needed to be measured.
