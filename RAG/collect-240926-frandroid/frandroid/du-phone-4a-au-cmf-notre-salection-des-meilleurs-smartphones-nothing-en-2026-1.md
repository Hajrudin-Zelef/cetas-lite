The young brand Nothing offers smartphones with original transparent designs, as well as an OS with a very distinctive style. Another major selling point: excellent value for money. Note that Nothing doesn't just make smartphones, but also excellent wireless earbuds, as well as connected watches.

To help you make your choice within the range, here is a **quick overview of the catalog of phones offered by Nothing.**

With this new Phone (4a), Nothing refines a recipe that has already proven itself by injecting a dose of high-end into it. The visual identity remains the central pillar with this transparent design, enhanced by a Glyph system of 63 mini-LEDs that is more understated but more practical.

The big surprise comes from the integration of the periscopic zoom inherited from the Pro version, a rarity at this price of 369 euros that seriously beefs up the photographic score. The 50 Mpx main sensor shines with its sharpness and vivid colors, while the x3.5 optical zoom offers stunning results in broad daylight.

On the display side, the 1.5K AMOLED panel is a success: bright, accurate and smooth thanks to adaptive 120 Hz. Under the hood, the Snapdragon 7s Gen 4 ensures a smooth interface and exemplary thermal stability, even if the most demanding gamers will quickly reach the limits of the GPU on demanding titles. While battery life is solid and 55 W charging is efficient, we regret an Android update policy limited to three years.

In summary, Nothing delivers a singular, balanced smartphone that is particularly formidable in the photography department: an excellent mid-range option.

The Nothing Phone (4a) pro is the manufacturer's latest model. It must be said that good value for money is still there at Nothing…! A resounding success, in our view, for this phone under 500 euros.

Aesthetically, the phone retains a touch of originality with its camera block and an overall "industrial" design. The back incorporates the **Glyph Matrix**, a small circular screen, which displays for example the time or even order tracking or battery level.

In terms of performance, while the Snapdragon 7 Gen 4 doesn't seek to break raw power records, it shines with exemplary thermal management. The 6.83-inch AMOLED screen is a great success: despite a somewhat timid maximum brightness, its calibration and color accuracy are stunning.

The photo department is another pleasant surprise, notably thanks to the integration of an x3.5 periscopic telephoto lens. The latter offers sharp portraits and high-flying optical zoom.

The only notable downside: software support limited to three years of Android updates.

The Nothing Phone (3) is Nothing's high-end phone. As always, it relies on an atypical design. Its back is adorned with a new micro-LED Glyph, with asymmetric camera modules — an aesthetic choice that won't appeal to everyone.

The 6.67-inch AMOLED screen of the Nothing Phone (3) offers a dynamic refresh rate from 0 to 120 Hz. We have a good Delta E of 2.66 and good color coverage. In terms of brightness, we measured a peak of 1572 nits in HDR. This offers a good level of brightness.

In terms of performance, the Snapdragon 8s Gen 4 guarantees exemplary smoothness in daily use as well as in gaming, despite a tendency to heat up during long sessions. Battery life is solid thanks to a 5150 mAh battery.

The software side, with Nothing OS 3.5, is another strength of Nothing. This interface is clean, free of bloatware and highly customizable. The update policy has been improved: 5 years of Android updates, 7 years of security patches.

The photo department also progresses, with three 50 Mpx rear sensors, offering good results in wide-angle and portrait, even if the digital processing sometimes smooths details too much.

All in all, it's a successful high-end smartphone. Read our detailed review for our full opinion on the Phone (3).

The Phone (3a) is the little brother of the (3a) Pro. While aesthetically it presents a different face, it is however almost identical in terms of specs.

Only three points differ. In terms of the camera, the telephoto lens of the Pro model is more powerful than that of the (3a) (3x instead of 2x). As for the selfie lens, it is a bit less good on the little brother: 32 Mpx versus 50 Mpx on the Pro model. Another differentiating point: RAM goes from 12 to 8 GB.

It can be found from 349 euros in 8+128 GB. Its price has however already dropped significantly, almost competing with the CMF. A great deal, especially since it has few things less than the Pro model. Our review of the Nothing Phone (3a) is on Frandroid.

The Nothing Phone (2) is one of the most high-end smartphones at Nothing. Stocks are dwindling but its price has dropped dramatically over time and it is therefore a good deal right now.

The Nothing Phone (2) stands out with its refined design, featuring a tiny camera module, a slightly curved transparent back, a redesigned Glyph system, and thinner screen bezels than the first model. One does regret, however, that it is not water-resistant.

It is equipped with a 6.7-inch LTPO OLED screen with a 120 Hz refresh rate and a maximum brightness of 994 cd/m² — admittedly, better is now available in 2026.

The smartphone naturally runs on Nothing OS and is powered by a Snapdragon 8+ Gen 1 SoC. The camera sensors have been improved compared to the Nothing Phone (1), with a 50 MP main lens and a 32 MP front camera. The 4700 mAh battery supports 45 W fast charging.

Launched at 679 euros a year and a half ago, it can now be found at 400 euros from certain retailers. Find the Nothing Phone (2) review.

### What about the Nothing (2a)?

Note that Nothing also offers a range of Phone (2a) and (2a) Plus. While they were relevant not long ago, they are much less so now that the (3a) and (3a) Pro have been released.

They still feature the same Nothing philosophy with a transparent back, Glyph, and a large screen. No telephoto lens on these models, however.

When they launched in March and September 2024 respectively, they were priced at 349 and 449 euros. The good news is that they can now be found at 249 and 400 euros!

The Nothing Phone (4b) is the brand's new entry-level reference. It combines the bold aesthetics of the British manufacturer with a solid spec sheet for its price. It retains the luminous Glyph interface on the back and features a large 6.77-inch AMOLED screen at 120 Hz.

Powered by the Snapdragon 6 Gen 4 chip (which admittedly is not a powerhouse) and 8 GB of RAM, it is supported by the exemplary fluidity of Nothing OS. It thus offers a bloatware-free experience, which is not so common at this price.

On the photo side, its dual 50 MP rear module with optical stabilization ensures clear and detailed shots for everyday use. The 5200 mAh battery offers good battery life, despite its rather modest size for 2026. Charging is capped at 33W maximum, so you'll need to be patient when recharging.

Available at around €350, the Phone (4b) offers good value for money and should be considered against competitors from Xiaomi and Samsung.
