# j-ai-testa-le-mac-mini-m6-toujours-une-raussite-malgra-son-prix

Overall, this Mac mini M6 is a perfect machine for running small to medium-sized AI models and assistants (up to 9B in our tests). Moreover, it does so with minimal consumption (only 9 W at the GPU), but we still need to carry out other tests here to precisely measure the thermal envelope generated by all these models.

The 24 GB configuration is therefore a good compromise between AI efficiency and price, but it will limit you for larger models. On this front, the AMD Ryzen AI Max+ 395 unsurprisingly does much better with its unified memory pool that can go up to 128 GB. The Mac mini M6 can be configured up to 64 GB for the most demanding users, but 128 GB (and even 512 GB) are reserved for the Mac Studio.

## Cooling and noise

It was one of the great qualities of the M4 model: the Mac mini M6 is barely audible, including during heavy compilations or renders. The fans work well, but they are almost inaudible.

In terms of overall consumption, the machine will rarely exceed 40 W in normal use and may flirt with 46 W under heavy loads. The M6 chip can climb to 65 W, as on the M4, when all CPU and GPU cores are stressed, which is rarely the case.

We can feel here that the move to 2 nm makes it possible to maintain measured consumption despite the consequent performance gain compared to the previous generation.

## Price and availability

The Mac mini M4 was surely the best deal of recent years to equip yourself with a robust and versatile machine, whether for office work, creation or, now, AI. But the RAM and storage crisis is in full swing and Apple was forced, like many other manufacturers, to increase its prices.

The Mac mini M6 now starts at 1,049 euros, or 350 euros more than its predecessor. With a 350-euro gap, the entry-level model loses the status of a very good deal that made the M4 a success. The gains of the M6 chip are mainly justified for creatives and light AI uses, less so for office work alone.
