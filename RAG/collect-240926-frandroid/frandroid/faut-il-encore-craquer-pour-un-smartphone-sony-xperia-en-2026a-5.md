# faut-il-encore-craquer-pour-un-smartphone-sony-xperia-en-2026a

As I have a few minutes to waste, I will also give you my point of view: I have owned an Xperia Z, Z1, Z2, today I use a Z5 as well as a Z3 with a Beta 7.0 rom. I don't consider myself a SONY fanboy because if I found better elsewhere, I could easily leave the brand. However, when I look at what is offered on the market, I really don't want to take the plunge. What could decide me: 1) A "Bordless" screen? Why not? However, when I see the design of a Galaxy S8 screen, I feel like I'm going back to our good old cathode ray tubes. Not only are the edges rounded, which makes using the touch screen unpleasant (a friend regularly experiences this on his Galaxy S7) but moreover, the corners of the screen are round. The 80s may be musically fashionable (often to my great despair) but from there to bringing back the screen shapes of that era on smartphones, I don't endorse it. For me, a screen can be "Bordless" but must remain flat, with square corners. 2) A splendid Amoled screen? Being a web designer by profession, the distorted colors of Amoled screens hurt my eyes. I far prefer LCD technology, much more faithful when well calibrated and it turns out that SONY masters this field very well. 3) Better sound performance? My greatest passion is music, I am also a semi-professional DJ. And if there is one area where SONY crushes the competition, it's in the sound quality of its smartphones. First of all, it is one of the only manufacturers to intelligently place the speakers (on either side of the screen). Indeed, most competing models only offer a single speaker and when they are lucky enough to have two (which is still essential to obtain a stereo effect), they are generally located at the bottom of the device. But the point on which SONY emerges as a clear leader is in digital audio processing. There, I am not addressing users who listen to music on YouTube or on Spotify and Deezer (often without a Premium subscription), but true music lovers who like me swear only by uncompressed audio formats. Note that for a minimum amount of 500 â¬, there are, it is true, high-end audio players capable of rivaling my Xperia... My Z3 and my Z5 also have an integrated noise reduction system, which is always appreciated especially on public transport: I know nothing like it from the competition. 5) A better camera? I could be tempted by a smartphone with 2 rear lenses specially designed to use fashionable effects or a front lens designed to get the best selfies. The problem is that I only occasionally use my smartphone to take photos. Indeed, also passionate about photography since my adolescence during which I had the chance to cut my teeth on a Canon A-E1 (the older ones will appreciate), I prefer to use today a digital reflex, in this case a SONY Alpha 7 (well, SONY again...). That said, the Xperia range has nothing to be ashamed of compared to the competition in the field of photography, notably thanks to the excellent quality of its sensors. 6) Better battery life? It is true that on paper, there are now smartphones with battery capacities superior to those of the Xperia range. However, in my daily use, I am the only one among my relatives to never need to recharge my Z5 and Z3 during the day. The battery life of the models in the Z series has always been very satisfactory for me, presumably thanks to a sufficient screen resolution, the "Stamina" mode and SONY's expertise in batteries. 7) A new interface? I am a big fan of Google's Material Design and I find the design of IOS completely inconsistent. This is also one of the reasons that keep me on Android. Touch Wizz and other overlays of all kinds, which also have the disadvantage of being very resource-hungry, cannot suit me. SONY has always had the intelligence not to stray too far from the Stock Android interface, which presumably allows it to be the manufacturer to offer system updates the fastest and to be the only one (from memory) to offer the integration of the "Google Now" panel in its Launcher. 8) Superior build quality? Until the Z5, I think SONY had a wide lead over the competition. I have nothing against Samsung (I also owned a Galaxy Note 2), but I remember that not so long ago, the back panels of their high-end models were made of plastic, making me think of an aerated bandage while SONY used rather glass, a much nobler material in my opinion. And even if this build quality seems to have slightly degraded since the X range (with the possible exception of the XZ Premium), the Xperia still seem to keep an advantage at this level. Proof of this, my Z3 and not Z5 follow me every day, without cases or particular protections and are noticeably in the same state as when I bought them (only the Z3 has an issue with the Micro USB port flap). 9) An innovative design? There, we touch the sensitive point because nothing is more subjective than design... As you know from reading the various blogs devoted to technological objects, the main criticism made of the Xperia range is its lack of evolution in terms of design. The Xperia being often also described as "bricks" due to their taut lines, all competing products could therefore make me think of "pebbles"... Moreover, as soon as all screens become "Bordless", it will be impossible to differentiate a smartphone of brand X or Y. Also, I claim the right to like bricks and I hope with all my heart that SONY will not succumb to the fashion of roundness To return to the absence of evolution in terms of design, I will tell you an anecdote that enlightened me on the possible motivations of the Japanese manufacturer. A few weeks ago, I had the opportunity to see a report, partly devoted to a Sushi Master recognized as the best. This gentleman, a small Japanese man in his sixties, runs a restaurant located in an apartment in Tokyo and for which you have to reserve several months in advance. The journalist having asked him what were his motivations for accomplishing his craft, he responded this: "I have been making the same product since the age of fifteen and every day, I seek to perfect it". Without entering into a philosophical analysis, which I would be quite incapable of, I nevertheless understood what culturally separates us from the Japanese. We can easily draw a parallel with cuisine: If in our Western world we are very often in innovation (Nouvelle cuisine, Molecular cuisine,...), the Japanese would tend rather towards the search for perfection. SONY has not ceased, at least for the Z series that I know well (with the exception of the Z3+), to perfect its product by listening to users without however wanting to revolutionize it. It must be said that the very first Xperia Z, although imperfect, already brought a number of innovations later taken up by a certain number of manufacturers including water and dust resistance. Certain improvements that followed were often imperceptible for Bloggers, too eager to move on to testing the next smartphone at their disposal, but largely appreciable in the context of more extensive use. What will then be my next smartphone? Until the Z5, I changed smartphones practically every six months (in fact, at each release of a new SONY flagship). Since the latter, I must admit that no new smartphone has really attracted me. Neither the iPhones, for their too closed OS. Even less the Samsung for their "Pebble" side, their awful overlay and their dubious reliability (my Galaxy Note 2 had known a sudden death after 4 months of good and loyal service: resold immediately after repair under warranty). I hoped a lot from the Chinese models (the Xiaomi Mi MIX had seduced me on paper but the tests are too disappointing) but I am still waiting... The Nokia for now, also disappoint me. And even the X series, then the XZ series from SONY, even if they are more efficient, are for me less qualitative than the Z series. Also, I hope that my Z5 will not fail until the release of a model that suits me, otherwise I will most likely fall back on an Xperia XZ1, which however on paper is not super exciting...
