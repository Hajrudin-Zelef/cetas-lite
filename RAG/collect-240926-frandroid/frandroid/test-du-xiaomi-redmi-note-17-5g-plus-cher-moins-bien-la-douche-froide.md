# test-du-xiaomi-redmi-note-17-5g-plus-cher-moins-bien-la-douche-froide

<!-- source: https://www.frandroid.com/marques/xiaomi/3233989_test-du-xiaomi-redmi-note-17-5g-plus-cher-moins-bien-la-douche-froide -->

This year, the trajectory of the Redmi Note 17 5G stands out in the mid-range segment. While the Redmi Note family has historically built a solid reputation by offering oversized spec sheets at aggressive prices, this 2026 vintage is taking a harsh reality check.

Faced with soaring costs for RAM and electronic components, and keen to preserve its margins, Xiaomi is making a radical trade-off: cutting back on hardware while raising the price by 50 euros.

The Redmi Note 15 5G made an impression with a generous configuration of 8 GB of RAM, 256 GB of storage, a stabilized 108 MP camera module, and a curvy silhouette under the 300-euro mark.

Its direct heir takes the exact opposite approach. Bigger, noticeably heavier, and sporting a giant 7700 mAh battery, the Redmi Note 17 5G nevertheless sacrifices its Snapdragon 6-series processor, its ultra-wide-angle lens, optical stabilization, and its stereo speakers.

Unfortunately, it shouldn't be dismissed right away because it likely foreshadows a trend that will persist over the coming year.

## Xiaomi Redmi Note 17 5GDesign

The Redmi Note 17 5G marks a clear aesthetic break from the previous generation. Xiaomi abandons the soft curves of the Redmi Note 15 5G on both the front and back. This newcomer adopts the rigorous silhouette of current standards with flat edges and a completely flat screen. The brand has nonetheless softened the corners to preserve comfort in hand.

On the back, the camera module also changes philosophy. The centered and symmetrical integration of the Note 15 5G disappears in favor of an asymmetrical block offset to the left side. You can feel that the wind is shifting among Chinese manufacturers and that the camera circle is no longer in good favor.

This stylistic shift comes with a spectacular weight gain dictated by the integration of a 7700 mAh battery. While the Redmi Note 15 5G stood out with a record thinness of 7.35 mm, the Redmi Note 17 5G climbs to 8.45 mm in thickness. On the scale, it's even more glaring: the smartphone weighs 225 g, compared to only 178 g before. This 47 g jump noticeably weighs down the handling and makes prolonged one-handed use more tiring.

Its overall measurements also stretch to reach 169.70 mm in height by 79.14 mm in width, compared to 164 x 75.42 mm for its predecessor. Nevertheless, all of this is also justified by the size of the device, which reaches 6.99 inches! For reference, in 2012, Google launched the Nexus 7, a tablet… of 7 inches. It's comfortable to use, let's not kid ourselves, but more complicated to carry. In my pocket, it tends to slip out too easily.

In terms of finishes, the Redmi Note 17 5G comes in Celestial Purple, Sky Teal, and Black. Xiaomi combines matte and glossy textures here with metallic accents. Careful, there's no aluminum frame, that's reserved for the Pro models. Here, it's plastic. The front receives a Corning Gorilla Glass 7i with good durability and is covered at the factory with a protective film. A shame, however, to see a large chin at the bottom of the screen, but that's still common in this price range.

Finally, Xiaomi renews the IP65 water-resistance certification, ensuring total protection against dust and water jets, without however allowing complete immersion. Wet Touch 2.0 technology is also still part of the package to guarantee the screen's touch responsiveness with wet or slightly greasy fingers.

For those who aren't afraid of large smartphones, here we have a nice proposition at a measured price. Of course, not everything is rosy, and we'll see that, but if the goal is browsing and watching videos, its screen is a major ally.

## Xiaomi Redmi Note 17 5GScreen

6.99 inches, as I said above, is very large. Especially since we've lost the curves that reduced the feeling of size.

The panel is 120 Hz compatible and displays a Full HD+ resolution of 2396 x 1080 pixels, very close to the 2392 x 1080 pixels of the Note 15 5G. The stretching of the diagonal mechanically causes a drop in display density, which settles at 376 ppi versus 388 ppi before. To the naked eye, you won't notice a thing.

On the other hand, a small compromise on color depth. We go back to 8-bit versus 12-bit on the previous generation. Is that serious? Not if you don't have demanding uses like photo/video editing. In typical use, most of us won't see the difference. It mainly highlights Xiaomi's logic of cutting back everywhere during this difficult period in the components market.

And despite everything, the screen of the Redmi Note 17 5G is perfectly calibrated. We subjected it to our usual probe protocol using Portrait Displays' CalMAN Ultimate software.

Xiaomi promises a peak of 1800 nits and a high brightness mode (HBM) at 1200 nits, marking a significant decline compared to the 3200 nits announced on the Note 15 5G. Our measurements confirm this retreat: maximum brightness caps at 1161 nits in SDR and rises to 1760 nits in HDR. These figures are enough to decipher content in full sunlight, but the raw regression reaches nearly 44% compared to the previous generation.

Color fidelity, on the other hand, proves very satisfactory in its best profile (Standard). In SDR, the average DeltaE stands at 2.09. We drop below the target threshold of 3, the point at which the human eye can no longer discern color drift. In HDR, the average DeltaE rises to 5.62, remaining below the reference value of 6. Finally, the color temperature measured at 6279 K shows a very slightly warm dominance compared to the ideal standard of 6500 K. No color drifts strongly. It's a beautiful screen.

## Xiaomi Redmi Note 17 5GPerformance

Snapdragon 4 Gen 4 when we had a Snapdragon 6 Gen 3 on the Redmi Note 15 5G — that's a violent step backwards.

Even before running any benchmarks, we know in advance that Xiaomi's new model is less powerful than its predecessor. A disgrace.

The Snapdragon 4 Gen 4 is clearly below the Snapdragon 6 Gen 3, especially in the graphics department. Its Adreno 613 is an older generation and much less powerful than the Adreno 710 of the 6 Gen 3. In 3D gaming and GPU rendering, you feel it immediately.

I installed Genshin Impact and it was painful. I ventured to push the graphics options and the verdict came down: 15 fps. To get any semblance of playability, you have to set everything to very low at 30 fps. There, it oscillates between 27 and 30 fps. Clearly, this is not a companion for gaming, at least not for anything other than small games.

As for general behavior, I don't find it excellent either. I often observe sluggishness in the interface, it regularly lags, and apps don't always open smoothly. You can feel that the SoC is a bit underpowered.

And then there's the famous RAM question. From 8 GB, we drop to 6 GB on one of the 256 GB versions, the one I have for this test. A decline that doesn't prevent a nice price increase.

To cushion this hardware downgrade, Xiaomi integrates its Memory Extension software feature. The Note 17 5G can thus simulate up to 12 GB of total RAM by drawing from the flash memory, but it doesn't have the same impact.

As compensation for this degraded power, the Redmi Note 17 5G displays excellent thermal behavior. Its mid-range chassis uses a high thermal conductivity aluminum alloy. Above all, its increased volume and its thickness of 8.45 mm (compared to 7.35 mm on the Note 15 5G) significantly increase the passive thermal dissipation surface. Under heavy continuous load, the processor is never pushed beyond its physical limits. Thermal throttling proves virtually nonexistent, guaranteeing perfect performance stability during prolonged gaming or multitasking sessions. But that doesn't erase the micro-stutters.

## Xiaomi Redmi Note 17 5GSoftware

The Xiaomi Redmi Note 17 5G runs on HyperOS 3, based on Android 16. The manufacturer guarantees system updates for 4 years for Android versions and 6 years for security elements.

Quite a few preinstalled apps on this model. If we exclude Xiaomi's own, including a game center, a cleaner, or a Themes app, I still count 24! Some are useful, or used by many users, like Facebook or Instagram. Others aren't really essential (Temu, Booking, and several games).

No AI tools for writing or translation assistance on this Redmi. We only get photo editing tools. Here, I have two:

- Beautification
- Definition enhancement
- Image extension
- Magic eraser
- Sky replacement

Among them, definition enhancement is counterproductive; the result is worse than the original. We lose detail across the entire shot. However, the rest works well. Even the magic eraser is effective, including on faces, with a result I'll let you judge.

## Xiaomi Redmi Note 17 5GPhoto

On the photo side, the Redmi Note 17 5G pays a heavy price.

The rear module abandons the 108 MP high-definition sensor of the Redmi Note 15 5G in favor of a 50 MP sensor (f/1.8). Even more damaging for a device launched at 349 euros, the Note 17 5G loses physical optical stabilization (OIS). This is now reserved for the Pro variants of the range.

Without mechanical stabilization and with definition halved, nighttime shots as well as videos in motion become mechanically more susceptible to motion blur.

And that's not all — it loses versatility. Xiaomi simply removes the **8 MP ultra-wide-angle module** (f/2.2) present on the Redmi Note 15 5G. The Redmi Note 17 5G therefore ends up with a single rear viewing angle.

In addition, the loss of the 108 MP sensor eliminates the optical-quality in-sensor cropping that made it possible to exploit a 3x zoom and four focal lengths (24 mm, 28 mm, 35 mm and 72 mm) by cropping into the image. The 50 MP digital zoom very quickly shows its limits as soon as you try to isolate a subject.

On the front, the selfie camera housed in the central punch-hole also regresses. It drops back to **16 MP** with a darker f/2.5 aperture, compared to a **20 MP** (f/2.2) front sensor on the Note 15 5G.

To mask these hardware sacrifices, Xiaomi tries to compensate with software and artificial intelligence.

The rendering is good in daylight at x1. From x2 onward you can see AI lifting. At night, it's complicated. It's difficult to get successful shots. Above all, you need a lot of light.

And what bothered me the most was the shutter lag. It can vary and be fast or take its time. It's difficult to capture things on the fly. Besides, I always ended up with slightly blurry photos in those moments.

On the video side, don't expect ecstasy, you're limited to 1080p at 30 fps. However, Xiaomi brings two nice features: a **Dual Video** mode that lets you record simultaneously with the front and rear cameras for social networks, and a teleprompter, like on the Pixel 11 Pro.

Welcome software additions, but they don't replace the loss of an ultra-wide lens or that of hardware stabilization.

## Xiaomi Redmi Note 17 5GAudio

And here's yet another element that fell in the line of duty: stereo. The Redmi Note 17 5G now has only a single speaker. It's located at the bottom, next to the SIM tray. And it isn't good quality.

Everything is pushed toward the highs. No bass. It's really the bare minimum. Hard to use it for anything more than calls or the occasional YouTube video.

## Xiaomi Redmi Note 17 5GNetwork and communication

The Redmi Note 17 5G is, as its name suggests, 5G compatible. The cellular experience is decent. No network failures linked to the phone. Good reception in the Paris region.

The SIM tray can accommodate either two nanoSIMs or one nanoSIM and a microSD card to expand storage. No eSIM on this model, that's an option reserved for the Pro and Pro Max models.

There's also Wi-Fi 5 and Bluetooth 5.1, nothing new here.

## Xiaomi Redmi Note 17 5GBattery

Here's the big new feature of this Redmi Note 17 5G. Its battery takes a leap, going from 5520 mAh to 7700 mAh. Enough to justify the device's weight gain and stretched dimensions.

In practice, it doesn't produce the expected effect. On PCMark, we get a mixed-use score of 16h. That's a good value, but inexplicably, it's worse than that of the Redmi Note 15 5G, which climbed to 16h48! Yet the tests were carried out under the same conditions: automatic refresh rate, brightness set to 250 nits and volume at 50%.

The separate tests are better. For video streaming, it doesn't budge, the 2h30 of A l'Ouest rien de nouveau on Netflix consume 10%.

In gaming, it's much better with -9% in one hour on Genshin Impact, but as said above, 3D gaming isn't its forte.

In short, it's hard to get a clear idea. The screen is certainly bigger, but the SoC is less speedy and, above all, you gain 40% more battery capacity! I feel that this Redmi doesn't handle all tasks the same way and that it can overconsume on some. Overall, the experience remains good, but I clearly expected more.

On the other hand, don't panic, you'll get through a day of use without any problem.

On the charging side, it's wired only. It's 45 Watts as on the previous generation. Charging time is nonetheless longer, since the battery is bigger. Allow 22 minutes to reach 25%, 1 hour for 60% and nearly 2 hours for 100%.

One last small new feature, reverse charging: it goes from 18W to 22.5W.

## Xiaomi Redmi Note 17 5GPrice and availability

The Redmi Note 17 5G is available in 3 colors (Purple, Blue and Black). It launches on September 17, 2026, alongside the Note 17 4G, Note 17 Pro 5G and Note 17 Pro Max 5G.

In France, we get two versions:

- Redmi Note 17 5G 6+128 at 349 euros
- Redmi Note 17 5G 6+256 at 379 euros

And here, there's no point looking back, since last January, Xiaomi launched the Redmi Note 15 5G at 299 euros in a single, generous configuration of 8 GB of RAM + 256 GB of storage.

In reality, this Redmi embodies what I feared would happen: worse smartphones, sold at higher prices. Xiaomi told me so itself, there's the increase in the cost of RAM and more generally of components, but above all the manufacturer no longer necessarily wants to sacrifice its margins to sell ever more smartphones.

I can understand that, but on the other hand I see Samsung launching a Galaxy A18, also more expensive than its predecessor, but far less pared down. Here, we lose too much.

My advice is therefore to wait for a clear price drop or to snap up the latest Redmi Note 15 5G or their Pro versions. There's also the Phone (4b) from Nothing, which is better equipped for less money.

A 50 € cashback offer is available on the Redmi Note 17 5G from September 17, 2026 to January 3, 2027. This promotion temporarily brings the effective price of the phone down to 299 €, virtually absorbing the price increase throughout the launch period.
