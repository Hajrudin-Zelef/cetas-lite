# on-a-testa-le-minisforum-n5-max-ai-nas-le-nas-cinq-baies-qui-joue-aussi-les-mini-pc-surpuissants

<!-- source: https://www.frandroid.com/marques/minisforum/3226029_on-a-teste-le-minisforum-n5-max-ai-nas-le-nas-cinq-baies-qui-joue-aussi-les-mini-pc-surpuissants -->

Having a NAS at home means always having your data close at hand without having to shell out thousands for a cloud subscription. In another vein, swapping your bulky tower for a mini-PC means saving space without (too much) losing performance.

Rather than offering two separate products, Minisforum therefore set out to combine the best of both worlds with the N5 MAX AI NAS which, as a bonus, packs top-tier components to handle any eventuality: an AMD Ryzen AI Max+ 395 processor, 10 GbE connectivity, and five 3.5-inch storage bays.

## Technical Specifications

*The test was carried out using a product loaned by Minisforum.*

## A Careful Design, Built to Evolve

Minisforum's arrival in the NAS world logically coincides with the manufacturer's use of a new type, a new format of chassis. It is indeed difficult to fit five 3.5-inch bays into a classic mini-PC case. On the other hand, whether we are talking about the N5 NAS, the N5 PRO NAS, the N5 Air AI NAS, or the N5 MAX AI NAS, the philosophy, and therefore the chassis, are fairly similar. The key idea is to rest all the electronic components in the lower part of the NAS in order to create two genuine "chambers."

One part of the NAS is thus dedicated to storage space while the other is devoted to all the components, from the CPU to the M.2 SSDs, including the RAM and, of course, the entire cooling system. The intelligence of this design goes even further in the case of the N5 MAX AI NAS since the motherboard is mounted on rails: by simply removing two screws, you can slide it out to access the machine's innards. It is then very easy to upgrade the machine's configuration. Well done.

As for the NAS portion, it is a little less original, but still very well executed. The front panel is magnetic and comes off without difficulty, revealing the five 3.5-inch bays, which are fitted with a caddy to easily mount the storage unit. You can tell that Minisforum drew inspiration from the best solutions in the field; the whole thing exudes reliability, though with one remark: first of all, there is no question of locking each caddy into its bay. A shame, since it is a security option offered by many competitors.

That said, it takes nothing away from the overall aesthetics of a product that adopts a classic rectangular shape but makes good use of premium materials. The chassis is made of steel for greater robustness, even if this entails significant weight: the NAS measures 199 x 202.4 x 252.3 millimeters and, above all, weighs just a little over 5.8 kilograms. Quite something.

### Abundant and Modern Connectivity

This weight of almost 6 kg must immediately be put into perspective. First, by the materials used, since, as we said, the N5 MAX AI NAS is entirely made of steel for robustness that is beyond doubt. Also put into perspective by the integration of the power supply unit: this is fairly rare in the mini-PC world — a little less so on the NAS side — but it means only having cables to manage. Moreover, it takes nothing away from the power supply's output since Minisforum can offer us 250 W, a figure that is perfect given the components on board.

The N5 MAX AI NAS has a three-prong power port that you connect to the mains and which is of course only one of the many connectors available. On the rear of the product, you will indeed find an HDMI 2.1 port, one USB-A 2.0 and another 3.2 Gen 2. Even more interesting, Minisforum does not kid around with two USB4 V2 ports capable of 80 Gbps, but also of handling display (DP 2.0) and power delivery (15 W). Finally, two RJ45 ports are there to ensure the network connection: they are nothing less than 10 GbE!

*On the rear of the NAS, the connectivity is modern: on the left, the two USB4 ports and, on the right, the two RJ45 10 GbE ports.*

To round things off, the front of the mini-PC/NAS is also well equipped: two USB ports are indeed offered. The first is USB-C 3.2 Gen 2 while the second is nothing other than a third USB4 port (40 Gbps, DP 2.0, PD 15 W). Yes, we have a total of three USB4 ports! Finally, that is not all, because Minisforum has integrated one last USB port: it is a USB-A with the 3.2 Gen 1 standard found on the motherboard, inside the chassis. Its purpose? To allow you to plug in a USB key to deploy your preferred OS, for example.

### First-Rate Components

Choosing your OS may seem like an odd idea at first glance, but that would be quickly forgetting the versatility of the N5 MAX AI NAS. Indeed, to power its machine, Minisforum relies on AMD's very popular Ryzen AI MAX+ 395 CPU. The latter has 16 Zen 5 cores/32 threads with a maximum frequency of 5.1 GHz. Even better, it integrates a Radeon 8060S GPU with 40 RDNA 3.5 compute units up to 2.9 GHz. To complete the trio, AMD has prepared an NPU rated for a maximum of 50 TOPS, or 126 TOPS for the entire chip.

Answering to the code name Strix Halo, this chip has often been talked about insofar as it is the first CPU capable of convincingly running even the most demanding games, without of course needing a dedicated graphics card. Better still, it does so without blowing up power consumption: its TDP is given as 55 W by default with possible configuration between 45 and 120 W. It should therefore also not heat a compact PC excessively, which must also contend with the heat from a maximum of five hard drives.

*For our tests, we used Seagate IronWolf hard drives and Kingston DC600M SSDs.*

Five hard drives that form the heart of the NAS portion of the product. These are classic bays that rely on an equally classic SATA interface. Officially, they accept hard drives up to 32 TB for a maximum capacity of 160 TB. That said, Minisforum mentions a total capacity of 200 TB because you can also count on the presence of no fewer than five M.2 slots for SSDs up to 2280 of 8 TB each: 5 x 32 TB of hard drives plus 5 x 8 TB of M.2 SSDs, and we indeed get our 200 TB.

Note, however, that by default, the N5 MAX AI NAS ships with a preinstalled SSD. It is an M.2 2280 model with a capacity of 128 GB on which the MinisCloud operating system is installed. It is, however, entirely possible to replace it with a larger one and also to change the operating system, but we will come back to that very soon.

Before that, let us also mention the RAM portion: Minisforum offers, as a choice, 64 GB or 128 GB of LPDDR5 soldered to the CPU. This should be taken into account: while storage and connectivity are largely upgradable, the same is not true for the RAM which, let us remember, is shared between the CPU and the GPU.

### Freedom of the operating system

As we said, Minisforum ships its N5 MAX AI NAS with an M.2 SSD with a capacity of 128 GB on which the Miniscloud OS is preinstalled. Functional, this OS provides the bare minimum for such a versatile PC/NAS. Everything related to file sharing, user management, and backup is on the menu of an OS that nevertheless cannot compete with the software of NAS specialists that have been around for years. Synology of course, but also ASUSTOR, QNAP, or Terramaster do much better with software that is far more complete, more pleasant, and more functional.

*Still very imperfect, the Miniscloud OS interface still contains small portions in Chinese.*

Miniscloud sometimes lacks responsiveness and even though Minisforum keeps releasing updates, it still suffers from quite a few crippling bugs. More annoying still, the services and features are limited, despite the presence, for example, of a Docker manager precisely intended to open things up to many possibilities not provided by default. These are only a few examples, but the hardware monitoring portion of the PC/NAS is fairly limited, some options still appear in Chinese, and the interface is not always very "clean."

That said, Minisforum is aware of Miniscloud's limitations and it allows, even encourages, setting up your own OS. To do this, in the manner of certain manufacturers like Terramaster, a USB-A port has been placed internally to simplify the deployment of a live-OS on a USB key.

Of course, it is also possible to access the BIOS of the N5 MAX AI NAS to completely revise the boot order and decide to install the OS of your choice. Minisforum does things rather well since all Windows drivers are available for download from its website.

Better still, since we are talking about an AMD platform (CPU, GPU, NPU, and chipset), many drivers exist, whether we are talking about Windows or Linux. One can therefore very well create one's own configuration or rely on all-in-one solutions such as OpenMediaVault, Rockstor, TrueNAS, or Unraid. We were impressed by the ease with which we were able to deploy this kind of solution and, above all, by the efficiency with which the whole thing then worked.

For our more in-depth tests, however, we favored installing Windows 11 in order to fall back on something more classic in terms of a mini-PC. As we said, the software side poses no problem since all drivers are offered by Minisforum, but also because the AMD platform is an old acquaintance. As on any PC — which this N5 MAX AI NAS ultimately is — it takes only a few dozen minutes to have a Windows 11, perfectly functional... but without a license.

## And in use, how does it run?

We tested the N5 MAX AI NAS in its two configurations: as a NAS and as a mini-PC.

### In NAS use

Although the N5 MAX AI NAS has the advantage of combining the functions of a NAS and a mini-PC within a single machine, we decided to divide our tests into two main categories. We then began with the purely NAS operation of the machine.

An operation that involves inserting several units into the available bays. Unsurprisingly, everything we expect from a 2026 NAS is here: caddies capable of accepting 2.5-inch units (screws provided as needed) or 3.5-inch units (screwless system), RAID capabilities up to RAID 5 and RAID 6 for protection in case of failure, and the choice between ZFS and ext4. No, BTRFS is not on the menu.

We won't go back into that here, but at present, the Miniscloud OS does not do justice to Minisforum's hardware. Admittedly, it contains all the features of a 2026 NAS, but in most cases it remains fairly basic: the multimedia tools (Albums, Movies, Music) are very poor, there is no video surveillance module, the system monitoring has a horrible ergonomics and the tiny application portal highlights these limitations.

Even so, it remains usable. Data sharing poses no problem and the power of the configuration allows several people to work on the NAS without difficulty. Below, we measured the NAS's performance via CrystalDiskMark and we were a little surprised by the results. Not that the throughput is bad, but we are quite far from saturating the 10 GbE controller.

To settle the matter, we carried out the same CrystalDiskMark test after changing the operating system and swapping Miniscloud for Windows 11. As you can see, the results are nothing alike, proof that our storage units (Kingston DC600M SSDs) are not to blame: the network controller loses itself a little along the way, without it being scandalous.

Before taking fuller advantage of the N5 MAX AI NAS under Windows 11, we continued our NAS tests for a little "routine" check. We deliberately broke the RAID 6 array that we had created to assess the time needed to rebuild it. Well, you should know that the Ryzen AI MAX+ 395 works wonders: quite simply, we had never seen a NAS rebuild our array so quickly, made up of 100 GB spread across eight large files and 10 GB spread across more than 4,000 small files. A record, that's all.

### In mini-PC use

The other side of the N5 MAX AI NAS is its use as a mini-PC. There, you can install quite a few things, but we settled for Windows 11. As we said, the installation procedure posed no problem and within a few tens of minutes, we found ourselves on the desktop of Microsoft's OS. A useful point to note right away: it is possible to manage up to four screens thanks to the three USB4 ports that complement the HDMI port.

There is actually not much more to say: the N5 MAX AI NAS is then a Windows PC like any other Windows PC, knowing that the hardware configuration makes things extremely comfortable with its minimum of 64 GB of RAM, this 16-core Zen 5 processor and its 40 RDNA 3.5 compute units. Rather than dwelling on the machine's use, we instead suggest you discover its performance.

On Cinebench 2026, first of all, only the CPU part is tested. We indeed left out the GPU test. The Zen 5 architecture of the Ryzen AI Max+ 395 allows for honest single-thread/single-core scores, but it is obviously in multi-threads that things become more interesting: with more than 7,100 points, the N5 MAX AI NAS is clearly ahead of the GMKtec EVO-T2S that we tested recently, but also ahead of the MS-S1 Max by Minisforum. The hardware and, above all, software optimizations are not unrelated to this fine performance.

To drive the point home, we conducted measurements on Blender benchmark. There, the Ryzen AI MAX+ 395 again performs very well, but note above all that performance is much higher once the GPU part of the AMD processor is brought into play. Generally speaking, Radeon chips do far less than GeForce ones on this test, but between a GPU and a CPU, there is still no comparison.

We finish these "general-purpose" measurements with PCMark, the synthetic tool par excellence. By simulating countless usage scenarios for a machine (office work, video conferencing, photo retouching, video editing, 3D modeling, etc.), it offers a broad overview of the machine's potential and, it must be said, it is rather good. Nearly 10,000 points overall and, above all, more than 16,000 on both the productivity test and the digital content creation test, that's unprecedented on a mini-PC without a dedicated graphics card!

The power and therefore the impact of the Radeon 8060S solution needed to be verified more precisely. To do this, we set things straight with 3DMark and our three scenes of choice: Fire Strike, Time Spy Extreme and Steel Nomad. Each time, the results are very interesting. We remain far from a real graphics card, but the scores are high and suggest fine results in the face of real video games, even demanding ones.

*Shadow of the Tomb Raider* first. It runs rather very well, even without enabling any "assistance" like FSR. In 1920 x 1080, with details at minimum, we exceed 200 frames per second. So, naturally, we set the details to maximum and there, it's better than any mini-PC ever tested at Frandroid: 138 frames per second and the assurance of never dropping below 100 fps.

We move on to something much tougher, *Cyberpunk 2077* by the Poles at CD Projekt RED. A technological showcase widely used by NVIDIA, the game now also runs very well on AMD hardware and the N5 MAX AI NAS is there to bear witness. We stayed in 1920 x 1080 and enabled FSR 3.1 with frame generation, but what a slap. With details on "low," we flirt with 260 fps and in "ultra" we are still at nearly 170 fps, so why not enable ray tracing? It drops, of course, but FSR works wonders: in low ray tracing we are at 150 fps and in ultra ray tracing, we come close to 90 fps. Impressive.

Few are the scenarios for which the N5 MAX AI NAS might not be ready and, to drive the point home, we conducted some local AI tests with "generative" benchmarks designed by UL, already the publisher of PCMark and 3DMark. The Procyon software offers several scenarios, we retained two of them. For text generation — via Phi 3.5, Mistral 7B, and Llama 3.1 — the results are interesting, but not exactly something to jump through the roof about.

Much the same goes for image generation. There, Procyon is based on Stable Diffusion 1.5 and benefits from a model in FP16 precision. With 8.389 images per second, we are facing a configuration entirely capable, but one senses that it can do better. The fault no doubt lies in the fact that the NPU is not called upon: the processing power comes exclusively from the Radeon 8060S GPU.

## Power consumption, heat, and noise

The Ryzen AI MAX+ 395 is a fearsomely powerful processor, there is now little doubt about that. It has a remarkable CPU computing force, but clearly has nothing to be ashamed of in its integrated graphics solution. The problem is, such a chip, especially placed in a space as cramped as the N5 MAX AI NAS, must heat up, right?

*Interesting, the cooling solution deployed by Minisforum separates the CPU from the five SSDs.*

Well, let's admit that we were pleasantly surprised by the work of Minisforum's engineers. Indeed, despite the heaviest loads, the processor never reached the 85 °C threshold. If some users will find that hot, it remains well far from the limits set by AMD and no throttling was ever to be deplored. A fine performance made possible by the use of a system with 5 heat pipes and supported by two 80 mm blower-type fans.

The same goes for all the other components, whether we are talking about the 3.5-inch storage units — cooled by two 92 mm fans — or the M.2 format SSD units, three of which benefit from a beefy heatsink while the other two can count on a small dedicated fan (60 mm).

Alas, for noise, the assessment is less glowing. Oh, at Frandroid, we have already seen louder, but the "*silent even under full load*" vaunted by Minisforum is, at best a nice way of presenting things, at worst a lie. As a general rule, one should count around 36 dB, but as soon as one "loads the mule," it becomes complicated and with a maximum of 43 dB, the N5 MAX AI NAS even becomes very "loud."

Finally, one must not neglect the electrical consumption aspect of a machine that is certainly compact, but capable of measuring up to almost any situation. Minisforum can count on the relative sobriety of the Ryzen AI MAX+ 395, but under full load, we recorded up to 90 W for this processor alone and a maximum of about 167 W for the entire machine with five hard drives. That said, aside from the electricity bill, it's not too much of a problem: Minisforum has provided a 250 W power supply. We're fine.

## Price and availability

Despite some criticisms, particularly regarding the software, you will have understood that we rather appreciated this true Swiss Army knife offered to us by an admittedly very Chinese brand. Alas, Minisforum cannot work miracles and at the moment of the painful bill, few users will be tempted. The inflation we have been experiencing for a year now showing no sign of letting up, either.

Available since the summer, the N5 MAX AI NAS is sold in two variants. The first — which we tested — is built around the Ryzen AI MAX+ 395, which is accompanied "only" by 64 GB of RAM and a 128 GB SSD. This version is billed at **2,719 euros**. The second version has the same processor and the same SSD, but comes with 128 GB of RAM for the tidy sum of **3,839 euros**. Knowing that due to the very design of the CPU, the RAM is not upgradable, one will have to make the right choice.

It is useful to point out that however expensive this Minisforum PC may be, it is ultimately in line with most other PCs based on the Ryzen AI MAX+ 395. That is moreover where it stands out from the crowd: for an altogether very similar price, Minisforum manages to add all these NAS functionalities to give its mini-PC quite remarkable potential… but one that will doubtless not interest everyone.
