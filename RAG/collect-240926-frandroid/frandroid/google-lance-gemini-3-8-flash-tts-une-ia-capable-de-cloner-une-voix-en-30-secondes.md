# google-lance-gemini-3-8-flash-tts-une-ia-capable-de-cloner-une-voix-en-30-secondes

<!-- source: https://www.frandroid.com/marques/google/3258509_google-lance-gemini-3-8-flash-tts-une-ia-capable-de-cloner-une-voix-en-30-secondes -->

Google presented this Wednesday, September 23, two new tools, Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS and Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS.

Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS is aimed more at creative uses, such as video games, audiobooks, podcasts, or interactive media. Gemini 3.8 Flash-Lite TTS takes the same idea but with a more industrial objective: producing a lot of audio, for dubbing, high-volume content, or expressive voice agents.

Within the Gemini family, Google thus continues to separate the richest models from lighter versions.

These two models succeed Gemini 3.1 Flash TTS, launched in April 2026. Google claims clear progress on long content and the handling of two-voice dialogues compared to that generation, and above all adds a cheaper Flash-Lite tier, designed to produce audio in large quantities.

## Two models, two uses

The most spectacular promise concerns Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS. Google promises that you will be able to create a new voice from scratch, without a starting audio sample, with a simple instruction in natural language. It is possible to create a narrator's voice with a precise cadence, or a much more theatrical character, then adjust line by line the intonation, pace, accent, and listening signals (the "mhm," "yes" that punctuate a conversation).

The starting library also includes more than 2,000 ready-to-use voices, with more than 100 languages and dialects. Google even cites regional variants such as Mexican Spanish, Quebec French, or Scottish English.

Generating a voice over a long duration is designed to keep a stable timbre during several hours of continuous recording, useful for podcasts and audiobooks. Google also mentions two-speaker staging, capable of keeping two separate voices in the same script and handing the floor to each other as naturally as possible.

## Voice cloning, but regulated

The sensitive part concerns voice replication. Google announces that a coherent voice profile can be recreated from a 30-second sample, provided you use your own voice or a voice whose rights you hold. The group indicates that a consent verification is integrated, combined with SynthID marking and C2PA identifiers, a standard used to trace the origin and modifications of digital content.

SynthID here plays the role of an invisible watermark. Each audio clip generated by the Gemini Audio models must embed this mark directly in the file, in order to make AI-generated speech detectable. This is reassuring on paper, but it doesn't solve everything: detection still depends on the tools used on the other side, and an audio file can be re-encoded, cut, or republished under less clean conditions.

Google also plans voice remixing, announced for later. The idea will be to start from an existing voice and modify the timbre, pitch, pace, or accent with a text instruction.

The two models are available starting this Wednesday, September 23, in the Gemini API and Google AI Studio. Gemini 3.8 Flash TTS is then expected to arrive in Gemini Enterprise and Gemini Notebook, while Flash-Lite TTS is announced for Google Vids.
