# on-a-testa-le-minisforum-n5-max-ai-nas-le-nas-cinq-baies-qui-joue-aussi-les-mini-pc-surpuissants

Few are the scenarios for which the N5 MAX AI NAS might not be ready and, to drive the point home, we conducted some local AI tests with "generative" benchmarks designed by UL, already the publisher of PCMark and 3DMark. The Procyon software offers several scenarios, we retained two of them. For text generation — via Phi 3.5, Mistral 7B, and Llama 3.1 — the results are interesting, but not exactly something to jump through the roof about.

Much the same goes for image generation. There, Procyon is based on Stable Diffusion 1.5 and benefits from a model in FP16 precision. With 8.389 images per second, we are facing a configuration entirely capable, but one senses that it can do better. The fault no doubt lies in the fact that the NPU is not called upon: the processing power comes exclusively from the Radeon 8060S GPU.

## Power consumption, heat, and noise

The Ryzen AI MAX+ 395 is a fearsomely powerful processor, there is now little doubt about that. It has a remarkable CPU computing force, but clearly has nothing to be ashamed of in its integrated graphics solution. The problem is, such a chip, especially placed in a space as cramped as the N5 MAX AI NAS, must heat up, right?

*Interesting, the cooling solution deployed by Minisforum separates the CPU from the five SSDs.*

Well, let's admit that we were pleasantly surprised by the work of Minisforum's engineers. Indeed, despite the heaviest loads, the processor never reached the 85 °C threshold. If some users will find that hot, it remains well far from the limits set by AMD and no throttling was ever to be deplored. A fine performance made possible by the use of a system with 5 heat pipes and supported by two 80 mm blower-type fans.

The same goes for all the other components, whether we are talking about the 3.5-inch storage units — cooled by two 92 mm fans — or the M.2 format SSD units, three of which benefit from a beefy heatsink while the other two can count on a small dedicated fan (60 mm).

Alas, for noise, the assessment is less glowing. Oh, at Frandroid, we have already seen louder, but the "*silent even under full load*" vaunted by Minisforum is, at best a nice way of presenting things, at worst a lie. As a general rule, one should count around 36 dB, but as soon as one "loads the mule," it becomes complicated and with a maximum of 43 dB, the N5 MAX AI NAS even becomes very "loud."

Finally, one must not neglect the electrical consumption aspect of a machine that is certainly compact, but capable of measuring up to almost any situation. Minisforum can count on the relative sobriety of the Ryzen AI MAX+ 395, but under full load, we recorded up to 90 W for this processor alone and a maximum of about 167 W for the entire machine with five hard drives. That said, aside from the electricity bill, it's not too much of a problem: Minisforum has provided a 250 W power supply. We're fine.

## Price and availability

Despite some criticisms, particularly regarding the software, you will have understood that we rather appreciated this true Swiss Army knife offered to us by an admittedly very Chinese brand. Alas, Minisforum cannot work miracles and at the moment of the painful bill, few users will be tempted. The inflation we have been experiencing for a year now showing no sign of letting up, either.

Available since the summer, the N5 MAX AI NAS is sold in two variants. The first — which we tested — is built around the Ryzen AI MAX+ 395, which is accompanied "only" by 64 GB of RAM and a 128 GB SSD. This version is billed at **2,719 euros**. The second version has the same processor and the same SSD, but comes with 128 GB of RAM for the tidy sum of **3,839 euros**. Knowing that due to the very design of the CPU, the RAM is not upgradable, one will have to make the right choice.

It is useful to point out that however expensive this Minisforum PC may be, it is ultimately in line with most other PCs based on the Ryzen AI MAX+ 395. That is moreover where it stands out from the crowd: for an altogether very similar price, Minisforum manages to add all these NAS functionalities to give its mini-PC quite remarkable potential… but one that will doubtless not interest everyone.
