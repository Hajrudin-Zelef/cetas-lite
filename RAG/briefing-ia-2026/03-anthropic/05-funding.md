# Anthropic funding: Series G, Series H, run-rate

<a id="s03-16"></a>
### Series G (February 12): $30 billion at a $380 billion valuation

On February 12, 2026, Anthropic closes its Series G: $30 billion raised
at a $380 billion valuation. The order of magnitude exceeds the
understanding of previous venture-capital cycles: $30 billion in a
single round is more than most historic tech IPOs, and it comes in
February — before the release of Opus 4.6, before the Fable 5 saga,
before the official run-rate exceeded $47 billion in May. Series G
investors are therefore betting on a trajectory, not a balance sheet:
they are buying the hypothesis that the revenue curve, already
explosive, will keep steepening.

The $380 billion valuation installs Anthropic in a category of its own:
it is no longer a growing startup, it is a company whose implied value
rivals established large tech capitalizations — before any stock-market
listing. The ratio is instructive: $30 billion raised for a $380
billion valuation, a dilution of about 8% — founders and employees keep
most of the capital, and the lab buys itself three years of cash runway
to fund the training of subsequent generations. At this stage, the
raise is already no longer a question of survival: it is a strategic
weapon, simultaneously funding research, infrastructure, and the
constitution of a war chest against competitors.

<a id="s03-17"></a>
### Series H (May 28): $65 billion at $965 billion — the doubling in 105 days

On May 28, 2026, barely one hundred and five days after Series G,
Anthropic closes its Series H: $65 billion raised at a $965 billion
valuation. The figures are dizzying: in one quarter, the valuation was
multiplied by 2.5 (from $380 billion to $965 billion) and the amount
raised more than doubled (from $30 billion to $65 billion). No private
funding round in tech history had reached such amounts; Anthropic's
Series H alone exceeds the largest IPOs of the previous decade.

The calendar proximity to product events is anything but fortuitous:
Series H closes May 28, Opus 4.8 — 88.6% on SWE-bench Verified — came
out in May, and Fable 5 will come out June 9, twelve days later. Series
H investors therefore subscribed with knowledge of the pipeline: they
saw Opus 4.8, they know Fable 5 is coming, and they pay a $965 billion
valuation for a company whose official run-rate exceeds $47 billion in
May. The implied multiple — about 20 times the annualized run-rate,
based on the official figure — is high but not delirious for a company
whose revenues are growing exponentially: it bets the run-rate will
double again, which the unconfirmed July estimates (>$65 billion per
Bloomberg) and September (>$100 billion per the NYT) suggest — with all
due caveats on figures unconfirmed by Anthropic.

The G-then-H sequence also tells a story of reflexive confidence: each
raise funds the capabilities that justify the next, and each rising
valuation makes the next less dilutive. Between February and May 2026,
Anthropic raised $95 billion cumulatively — enough to fund several
generations of frontier models, the equivalent of several years of
training at the top of the scale — while keeping a capital structure
that leaves the founding teams strategic control. It is this financial
independence that enables, on June 12, the total-shutdown gesture: a
lab under cash pressure could never have afforded nineteen days without
a flagship.

<a id="s03-18"></a>
### The run-rate: $47, $65, $100 billion — reading an explosive curve

Anthropic's run-rate — annualized revenue based on the latest month — is
one of 2026's most scrutinized indicators, and one of the trickiest to
handle given how much sources diverge. The only official figure,
communicated by Anthropic itself, exceeds $47 billion in May 2026. That
is already a historic order of magnitude: in May, before the release of
Fable 5 (June 9) and Opus 5 (July 24), the lab generates at an
annualized pace nearly fifty billion dollars in revenue — enough, on
its own, to justify the $965 billion Series H valuation closed May 28.

Two journalistic estimates extend the curve, which must be cited with
their explicit caveats: more than $65 billion in July per Bloomberg
(unconfirmed by Anthropic) and more than $100 billion in September per
the New York Times (unconfirmed by Anthropic). If these figures are
accurate, the trajectory is staggering: the run-rate would have more
than doubled between May and September, from $47 billion to over
$100 billion in four months — a growth pace that makes the 20x multiple
paid in Series H look almost modest. But caution is in order: neither
Bloomberg nor the NYT cites Anthropic as a source, and the lab has not
confirmed these estimates. This dossier's anti-fabrication rule applies
here with particular rigor: between the official figure ($47 billion,
May) and the press estimates ($65 billion then $100 billion,
unconfirmed), there is the difference between fact and rumor — however
high-quality the rumor.

What can be stated without speculating is the coherence of the sequence:
Series G (February, $380 billion valuation), official run-rate
>$47 billion (May), Series H (May 28, $965 billion), Fable 5 launch
(June 9), rising press estimates (July, September), Fable 5.1 No. 1 in
the rankings (September 1). Each product milestone feeds the next in a
virtuous loop — better models, more revenue, more capital, better
models — whose execution speed, in 2026, has no equivalent in
industrial history. The question is no longer whether the curve is
exponential — May's official figure attests it — but how long the
exponential can hold before meeting its limits: physical (compute),
economic (the solvency of demand), or regulatory (June's lesson).
