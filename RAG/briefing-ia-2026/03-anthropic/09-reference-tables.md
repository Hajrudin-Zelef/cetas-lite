### Master chronology: eighteen dates for an extraordinary year

Anthropic's 2026 lends itself to a calendar reading, so exceptional is
the density of events: eighteen milestones in eight months, each of
which would have sufficed to make an ordinary lab's year. Here they are,
in order, with their scope:

|------|-----------|--------|
| Feb 12 | Series G: $30 billion at $380 billion valuation | The war chest |
| Apr 20–21 | AWS deal: >$100 billion over 10 years, 5 GW, primary cloud | The compute foundation |
| Mid-April | Opus 4.6: temporary retaking of the leadership | The race to the top |
| Apr 16 | Opus 4.7: temporary retaking of the leadership | The race to the top (continued) |
| May | Opus 4.8: 88.6% SWE-bench, $5/$25, 1M ctx | The quantified reference |
| May 28 | Series H: $65 billion at $965 billion valuation | The doubling in 105 days |
| June | Sonnet 5: 85.2% SWE-bench, $2/$10, 1M ctx | The value champion |
| Jun 9 | Fable 5 + Mythos 5 ("Mythos-class"), $10/$50 | The flagship and its double |

| Jun 30 | Withdrawal of the Commerce Dept. directive | The easing |

| Sep 17–18 | Publication: 26% of R&D "led" by Claude | The measured tipping point |

Three sequences stand out from this chronology. The spring sequence
(February–May) is that of armament: two gigantic raises ($95 billion
cumulatively), a record cloud commitment, and models that retake the
leadership in fits and starts — the lab equips itself with the decade's
means before even unveiling its flagship. The summer sequence
(June–July) is that of trial and consolidation: launch, suspension,
shutdown, restoration, settlement, second silicon pillar, democratization
of the flagship — seven weeks in which Anthropic successively faces the
American regulator, the federal judiciary, and the compute question, and
emerges with reinforced governance. The autumn sequence
(August–September) is that of demonstration: proteins validated in
wet-lab, Fermat formalized in 11 days, 26% of R&D piloted by Claude,
Fable 5.1 number 1 — the lab proves by example what its June mechanisms
and April–May investments made possible.

Also note the coincidences that are not: Series H closed May 28, twelve
days before Fable 5; the settlement approved July 20, two days before
AMD and four days before Opus 5; the agencies' advisory September 8,
three days before the anti-Zhipu report. Whether orchestrated or
fortuitous — the verified facts do not say — these calendar proximities
compose a year in which every front (financial, product, legal,
geopolitical, scientific) advances at the same time as the others, with
none serving as a bottleneck to the others. That is perhaps, at bottom,
Anthropic's most remarkable performance in 2026: not an isolated record,
but the capacity to fight eighteen battles at once without losing a
single one.

The 2026 Claude lineup sums up in a table of rare clarity — and this
clarity is itself a strategic fact, because it makes the lineup readable
at a glance for any decision-maker:

(The "n.c." mentions mark figures not communicated in the verified facts
— this dossier forbids itself from reconstructing them.)

Three lessons emerge. First lesson: the standardization of the million
tokens. Opus 4.8, Sonnet 5, Fable 5, Mythos 5 — all display 1M context
from release, between May and June 2026. Context length, a major axis
of differentiation in previous years, became a category prerequisite
within weeks: no summit model can now present itself without it, and
competition has shifted toward agentic scores and price. It is the
classic dynamic of technical standards — what distinguished yesterday
disqualifies its absence today — played at an unprecedented pace.

Second lesson: the price scale is mathematically regular. Sonnet 5
($2/$10), Opus 4.8 and Opus 5 ($5/$25), Fable 5 ($10/$50) — each tier is
worth exactly 2.5 times the previous one on input as on output, and Opus
5 is worth exactly half of Fable 5, as its promise displays
("quasi-Fable 5 for agentic use at half the price"). This regularity is
no accounting accident: it is a lineup architecture designed so the
customer instantly understands what they pay for — volume (Sonnet),
agentic performance (Opus), the extreme (Fable) — and so that each
technical advance mechanically translates into a lower price of
performance: between May (Opus 4.8) and July (Opus 5), at constant price
($5/$25), the lab went from an 88.6% SWE-bench model to a "quasi-Fable 5"
model.

Third lesson: the performance gap between tiers narrows faster than the
price gap. Sonnet 5 concedes only 3.4 SWE-bench points to Opus 4.8
(85.2% vs 88.6%) for a price divided by 2.5; Opus 5 promises
"quasi-Fable 5" agentic for half price. The consequence is twofold: on
one hand, the summit's premium becomes harder and harder to justify on
everyday tasks — it is justified only on the extreme, that is precisely
the sensitive capabilities that motivate the safeguards; on the other,
democratization accelerates — yesterday's summit becomes today's
standard within weeks, and it is this mechanic that carries the
run-rate beyond $47 billion as early as May. The 2026 lineup is
therefore not just a pricing grid: it is the year's economic engine.
