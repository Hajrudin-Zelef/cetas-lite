# Nvidia acquires Hugging Face: the promise of openness

<a id="s08-9"></a>
### 8.8. Nvidia acquires Hugging Face: ~$12.9 billion and the promise of openness

Announced on September 2, 2026, Nvidia's acquisition of Hugging Face
amounts to about $11.9 billion, plus about $1 billion in retention for
the teams — a total package of about $12.9 billion. Closing is planned
for the first half of 2027. And the stated commitment: Hugging Face
will remain "an open platform". Nothing less than the acquisition of
the "GitHub of AI" by the maker of the chips that train half the
planet.

The context illuminates the industrial logic. Hugging Face hosts
hundreds of thousands of models, datasets, and applications: it is
where the global AI community publishes, shares, evaluates, and
deploys. Nvidia, for its part, sells the chips on which a large part
of these models are trained — and develops an entire software
ecosystem to lock usage onto its hardware. By acquiring the reference
community platform, Nvidia buys the point of contact with millions of
developers and researchers: whoever holds the model hub steers the
formats, the benchmarks, the deployment pipelines — and, ultimately,
the hardware choices.

The financial terms speak to the strategic importance: ~$11.9 billion
for the platform, ~$1 billion in retention to keep the talent. That
billion in "retention" is no detail: in a community-platform
acquisition, the value lies as much in the team — its credibility
with the open-source community — as in the assets. Nvidia is paying
to keep the faces of openness in place, because a talent exodus would
sign the integration's failure.

The promise that Hugging Face will remain "an open platform" is the
deal's crux — and its main risk. All of Hugging Face's value rests on
its community's trust: researchers who publish their weights there,
companies that host their models there, many of which run on competing
hardware (AMD, Google TPU, Chinese chips). If the platform became a
mere Nvidia sales channel, that trust would erode, and the asset's
value with it. Nvidia knows it: the openness commitment is a
condition of success, not a boilerplate clause. It remains to be seen
how it will translate into facts after the planned H1 2027 closing —
governance, benchmark neutrality, support for competing hardware.

The implications for the ecosystem are immense. On one hand, Nvidia's
backing gives Hugging Face unprecedented means: infrastructure,
distribution, hardware integration. On the other, it concentrates in
a single actor's hands the "chips" layer and the "community hub"
layer — a vertical integration that will raise regulators'
eyebrows, even though closing is announced for the first half of 2027
with no reported blockage at this stage. For AMD, Intel, Google, and
competing chipmakers, the question is direct: will they keep seeing
their models promoted on an Nvidia-owned platform? The answer to that
question will determine whether the "GitHub of AI" remains a common
good or becomes a stronghold.
