# The SpaceX–xAI mega-merger: $1,250 billion in stock

<a id="s08"></a>
## 8. Industry, deals and funding

2026 was not only the year of models: it was the year of amounts. Never
has the artificial intelligence industry seen so much capital move so
fast — thirteen-figure mergers, fundraises that double in three months,
historic IPOs, acquisitions of developer platforms, and geopolitical
battles over takeovers. This section tells each deal in full, with its
context, terms, and implications, inventing nothing beyond verified facts.

The overall picture is striking. At the start of the year, a
$1,250 billion merger redraws the map of giants; in spring, Anthropic
raises $65 billion after raising $30 billion three months earlier; in
June, SpaceX goes public at a record valuation of nearly
$1,770 billion; and throughout the year, compute capacity deals seal
alliances worth over $100 billion over ten years between AI labs and
infrastructure providers. Meanwhile, Beijing issues its first veto on
an acquisition, and Europe lines up its own champions: Mistral at ~$24
billion, Cohere and Aleph Alpha merged at ~$20 billion, a German data
center campus at €11–13 billion.

What stands out is not only the scale of the amounts, but their
concentration. 2026's deals are counted in tens of billions the way
they were counted in hundreds of millions five years ago. AI has become
a heavy-infrastructure industry: fundraises finance gigawatts, mergers
combine rockets and models, and acquisitions target the platforms where
tomorrow's agents are built. Understanding these moves means
understanding where power now lies in the ecosystem.

<a id="s08-2"></a>
### 8.1. The SpaceX–xAI mega-merger: $1,250 billion in stock

Announced on February 2, 2026, the merger of SpaceX and xAI is the
most spectacular deal of the year: an all-stock transaction valuing the
combined entity at $1,250 billion. Unprecedented at this scale for a
merger involving an AI lab. The message is clear: rockets and models are
no longer two separate industries, but two faces of the same
infrastructure.

The context of the deal is worth recalling. SpaceX, champion of
reusable launchers and the Starlink constellation, and xAI, the AI lab
founded by Elon Musk, already shared a reference shareholder and a
vision: building the physical infrastructure of the AI era — from the
ground to orbit. By merging, the two entities create an integrated
giant able to finance colossal data centers, deploy orbital capacity,
and capture recurring revenues (launches, connectivity, compute) to
fuel the race for models.

The terms are those of a merger between financial equals, not a cash
buyout: "all-stock" means xAI's shareholders are paid in shares of
the combined entity, with no cash leaving the coffers. It is the sign
of a deal designed for the long haul: nobody sells, everyone aligns
on the shared trajectory. At $1,250 billion, the valuation immediately
places the merged entity among the largest private — then public —
companies on the planet.

The implications go beyond the two companies' perimeter. First, this
merger validates the vertical-integration thesis: in the AI race,
owning infrastructure (launchers, satellites, data centers, energy)
counts as much as owning algorithms. Second, it sets a valuation
precedent: four months later, SpaceX's IPO will value the company at
~$1,770 billion — a spectacular increase over the merger base. Finally,
it concentrates unprecedented industrial power in the hands of a
single group, which will not fail to raise questions among regulators,
even though the deal itself was not blocked.

This merger should also be read as a signal to markets: AI is no
longer a software sector among others, it is a heavy industry
consolidating the way aviation or energy did in their time. When
$1,250 billion of value aggregates in a single 100%-in-stock
transaction, the entire hierarchy of technological capitalism is
reshuffled. "Pure" labs — those that own neither infrastructure nor
massive recurring revenues — will have to find their place in this
new landscape, either by allying with hyperscalers or by raising
comparable amounts.
