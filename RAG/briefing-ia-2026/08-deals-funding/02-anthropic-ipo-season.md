# Anthropic hypergrowth and the 2026 IPO season

<a id="s08-3"></a>
### 8.2. Anthropic's hypergrowth: Series G then Series H

On February 12, 2026, Anthropic announces its Series G: $30 billion
raised at a $380 billion valuation. At that date, it is the year's
biggest AI fundraise — a record that will last only three months. For
on May 28, 2026, Anthropic returns with its Series H: $65 billion
raised at a $965 billion valuation, nearly triple the February
valuation. Two rounds, one hundred days apart, and a trajectory that
defies every precedent in venture capital.

Comparing the two rounds tells the acceleration story. In February,
$30 billion at $380 billion: already an amount that would have made
headlines in any previous year, and which then represented the annual
ceiling for AI raises. In May, $65 billion at $965 billion: the amount
raised more than doubled (+117%), and the valuation was multiplied by
about 2.5. In other words, between the two rounds, the market
revalued Anthropic by nearly $600 billion in a quarter. That is the
pace of hypergrowth: each round finances the next, and each round
reshuffles the valuation deck.

The context explains this frenzy. Anthropic is at the heart of the
year's structuring "compute" deals: in April, its agreement with
Amazon commits it to spending over $100 billion on AWS over ten years;
in July, AMD commits to supplying it with up to 2 gigawatts of
capacity and investing up to $5 billion in the company. These
commitments run into the tens of billions: raises must match the
invoices. Series G then Series H are the financial fuel of an
infrastructure strategy, not mere software-growth rounds.

The implications are multiple. For Anthropic, these two rounds buy
time and capacity: the time to convert compute commitments into
revenue, the leverage to negotiate from a position of strength with
chip and cloud suppliers. For the market, the $965 billion Series H
installs Anthropic in the club of companies valued near $1,000
billion — territory historically reserved for the most established
public giants. For competitors, the bar is set very high: raising
$65 billion in one round requires access to pools of sovereign and
institutional capital that few labs can mobilize.

One question remains that the numbers raise without resolving: at a
$965 billion private valuation, what is the next step? Anthropic IPO
filings reported in 2026 — with a targeted October/November listing,
whose completion is unverified — suggest the public-market route is
being considered. But between a $965 billion private valuation and a
successful listing lies an entire market to convince. The G→H
trajectory demonstrates one thing in any case: in 2026, the value of
a leading AI lab is now measured in fractions of a trillion, and it
can double in a quarter.

<a id="s08-4"></a>
### 8.3. IPO season: OpenAI, SpaceX, Anthropic, Moonshot

On June 8, 2026, OpenAI confidentially files its IPO paperwork, based
on a private valuation of $852 billion. The "confidential" filing — a
procedure that allows preparing the operation out of the public eye
before the announcement — signals that the planet's most famous lab
is preparing to take the public-market plunge. At a reference private
valuation of $852 billion, OpenAI's IPO looms as one of the largest
in market history, across all industries.

Four days later, on June 12, 2026, it is SpaceX that actually goes
public — and how. Listed on Nasdaq under the ticker SPCX, the company
raises $75 billion at a price of $135 per share, for a valuation of
about $1,770 billion (~$1.77 trillion). It is a historic record: never
had an IPO reached such amounts, or such a valuation. And the market
applauds: the stock gains 19% on its first day of trading,
demonstrating that investor appetite for the rockets + AI combination
born of the February merger is immense.

The comparison between the two operations is instructive. OpenAI, in
June, is only at the confidential filing stage: the $852 billion
valuation is a private reference, not yet a market price. SpaceX, for
its part, took the plunge: $75 billion raised, $135 per share,
~$1,770 billion in effective valuation, +19% on day one. One prepares,
the other consecrates. And the gap between the $1,250 billion
SpaceX–xAI merger valuation of February and the ~$1,770 billion of
the June IPO measures the premium the public market grants the
integrated-AI story: over $500 billion in value created in four months
between the merger announcement and the listing.

On Anthropic's side, IPO filings were reported in 2026, with a
targeted listing in October/November 2026 — but the completion of this
IPO is unverified according to available sources. This information
must therefore be handled with caution: the intent or preparation of
an IPO is not an IPO. If confirmed, after a $965 billion Series H,
Anthropic's listing would also be fought at trillion scale. But as
things stand, only this can be stated: filings were reported, an
October/November 2026 timeline was mentioned, and nothing confirms
that the operation took place.

Finally, China is not sitting out: Moonshot is targeting a Hong Kong
IPO, aiming to raise about $3 billion at a valuation of about $50
billion, with a filing submitted on September 4, 2026. The scale is
different — $3 billion raised versus $75 billion for SpaceX, $50
billion in valuation versus $1,770 billion — but the signal matters:
Chinese labs are also seeking access to public markets to finance
their race. Hong Kong, rather than Shanghai or Shenzhen, as the
listing venue: the choice says something about the desire to attract
international capital while remaining within China's regulatory orbit.

Taken together, 2026's IPO season tells the story of AI's financial
maturation: labs move from private venture capital to public markets,
and the amounts at stake — $75 billion raised in one go, trillion-scale
valuations — permanently install AI at the top of the global stock
market hierarchy. OpenAI is preparing, SpaceX set a historic record,
Anthropic is lying in wait (completion unverified), Moonshot carries
China's colors in Hong Kong.
