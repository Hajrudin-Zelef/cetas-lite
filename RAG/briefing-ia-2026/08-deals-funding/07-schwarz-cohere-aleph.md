# Schwarz–Cohere and the Cohere + Aleph Alpha merger

<a id="s08-12"></a>
### 8.11. Schwarz–Cohere: €500 million and the €11–13 billion German campus

The Schwarz Group — German retail giant turned sovereign-cloud player
via its StackIT subsidiary — invests €500 million in Cohere's upcoming
Series E. Important clarification: this investment is not part of the
Cohere + Aleph Alpha merger (see section 8.12); it is a distinct
operation, a funding round of the Canadian-European lab. €500 million
from a German retail conglomerate into an AI lab: European digital
sovereignty is also financed outside tech's traditional circuits.

The context is Schwarz's infrastructure strategy via StackIT. Alongside
the investment in Cohere, the group is pursuing a German data center
campus project estimated at €11 to €13 billion, with the ambition of
deploying up to 100,000 chips. Again, important clarification: this is
a separate project — not a component of the Cohere deal, not an annex
of the merger. Three distinct objects, then: €500 million in Cohere's
Series E; €11–13 billion in data center campus via StackIT; the
Cohere–Aleph Alpha merger at ~$20 billion. Confusing them would be
getting the analysis wrong.

The €500 million investment in the Series E tells the meeting of two
worlds: German retail, sitting on colossal cash flows and an obsession
with data sovereignty (a retailer's customer data is a strategic
asset), and an AI lab positioned on enterprise models. For Schwarz,
it is a foothold in the "intelligence" layer that will run on its own
StackIT infrastructure. For Cohere, it is a Europe-anchored investor,
with concrete industrial use cases — a profile complementary to
classic venture funds.

The €11–13 billion campus with up to 100,000 chips changes scale: this
is no longer funding a lab, but building a national infrastructure.
100,000 chips is the order of magnitude of the world's largest
clusters; €11 to €13 billion is a state investment disguised as a
private project — or the reverse. Via StackIT, a retailer becomes the
builder of Germany's AI infrastructure. It is one of 2026's most
singular stories: European compute sovereignty will come not only
from American hyperscalers implanted locally, but also from domestic
players converting their financial power into gigawatts.

The implications for Europe are direct. With Mistral (~$24 billion),
the Cohere–Aleph Alpha merger (~$20 billion), and the Schwarz/StackIT
pair (€500 million + €11–13 billion in infrastructure), the continent
lines up in 2026 a complete ecosystem: labs, capital, infrastructure.
Execution remains: 100,000 chips cannot be ordered, powered, and
cooled with a snap of the fingers, and €11–13 billion remains a
project estimate. But the direction is set, and it owes nothing to
chance: it is an assumed industrial strategy, financed by European
private balance sheets.

<a id="s08-13"></a>
### 8.12. Cohere + Aleph Alpha merger: ~$20 billion for a transatlantic champion

On September 16, 2026, Cohere and Aleph Alpha sign a definitive merger
agreement, for an amount of about $20 billion. Two labs born on the
two shores of the Atlantic — Cohere in Canada with a strong European
anchor, Aleph Alpha in Germany, champion of "sovereign" AI — unite to
create a world-scale player. It is the largest intra-European merger
(in the broad sense) in AI history, and one of the year's most
structuring deals for the "third pole".

The context is consolidation. Facing American giants that raise $30
then $65 billion in a few months and Chinese players that mobilize
~$9 billion in eight weeks, mid-sized labs have only two options:
backing or merger. Cohere and Aleph Alpha choose the second, with a
clear industrial logic: combine Cohere's enterprise models and Aleph
Alpha's "sovereignty" expertise, pool training costs, and weigh
together against hyperscalers in European public and private tenders.

The terms — definitive agreement signed September 16, ~$20 billion —
place the merged entity in the same valuation category as Mistral
(~$24 billion). Two European champions at ~$20 billion each: the
emergence of a credible continental duopoly, able to carry the
"sovereign AI" offer for European administrations and large
companies. The signing date, in mid-September, also inscribes the
operation in the dense back-to-school 2026 sequence: Mistral's raise
on September 8, Z.ai's raise on September 13, the Cohere–Aleph Alpha
merger on September 16 — three weeks that redrew the world map.

The implications go beyond the two companies. For European customers —
states, banks, industrial firms subject to sovereignty requirements —
the merger creates a single critical-size counterpart, able to rival
American offers on large contracts. For talent, it is a reinforced
magnet: a $20 billion lab retains its researchers better than an
isolated player. For American competitors, it is a signal: the
European market will not be a mere distribution territory, but a
market defended by capitalized local champions.

The integration question remains: merging two cultures, two
technology stacks, two customer portfolios across two continents is a
perilous exercise, and tech history is full of $20 billion mergers
that destroyed more value than they created. The definitive agreement
is only the beginning; execution will tell whether the bet was good.
But on paper, the logic is crystal clear: in 2026's AI, size is a
condition of survival, and $20 billion beats two times $10 billion.
