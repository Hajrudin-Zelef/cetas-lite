# January 2026: the month of latency

<a id="s01-5"></a>
## 4. January 2026: the month of latency

<a id="s01-6"></a>
### 4.1. A month with no verified fact — and why that is information

The dossier retains no specific verified fact for the month of January 2026, and it is
important to say so honestly rather than fill this void with approximations. This
absence is not an oversight of the methodology: the five verification waves conducted
in September 2026 simply did not surface any January-dated event meeting the
dossier's inclusion criteria — precise date, identified actors, verifiable amounts or
decisions. In a narrative that strives never to invent, a month empty of verified
facts must remain visibly empty, for pretending to fill it would betray the
anti-fabrication rule on which the reader's trust is founded. Honesty about what is
not known is as precious as precision about what is known.

Yet this documentary silence is itself information, provided it is interpreted with
caution. January 2026 appears, in the reconstructed chronology, as a month of latency
before February's brutal acceleration: no mega-round, no resounding merger, no major
model release is attested there, whereas February would concentrate on its own the
SpaceX–xAI merger at $1.25 trillion, Anthropic's Series G at $30 billion and Beijing's
blocking of the Manus acquisition. This contrast suggests that February's great
maneuvers were being prepared in January's discretion — negotiations, due diligence,
political arbitrations — without yet producing verifiable announcements. This is a
reasonable hypothesis, explicitly presented as such, and not a fact: the dossier does
not know what was hatched in January, it only notes that February exploded.

This January latency takes on its full meaning when placed in the year's dynamic:
2026 did not begin in euphoria, it entered it through a sudden acceleration of which
February was the detonator. Understanding January as a month of gestation rather than
an empty month makes it easier to grasp the violence of February's shift, when
operations prepared in the shadows came into the open in the space of a few weeks. The
reader will therefore keep a simple image of January, faithful to the facts: a calm
month on the surface, whose very calm makes what followed all the more striking.

<a id="s01-7"></a>
### 4.2. The context inherited from late 2025

If January 2026 delivered no verified fact to the dossier, it nonetheless inherited a
loaded context whose effects would be felt as early as February. The year 2025 had
ended on an already intense model race and on growing geopolitical tensions around
the transfer of AI technologies to China, tensions of which Beijing's blocking of the
Manus acquisition by Meta in February 2026 would constitute the first spectacular
manifestation on the Chinese side. American labs entered 2026 with colossal financing
needs, dictated by the exponential cost of training frontier models, and investors
with an appetite only waiting to express itself. January was the month when these
accumulated pressures had not yet found their outlet.

On the model side, late 2025 had installed a competitive landscape in which each lab
sought the next move: it was in this climate that Grok 4.1 Fast would appear in
preview on Copilot Studio as early as February 19, and that the sequence leading
from Claude Opus 4.7 in April to GPT-6 Astra in September was getting underway.
January, with no verified announcement, was in all likelihood a month of finalization
and internal testing — again, a cautious inference, not a fact. What can be asserted
without risk is that the releases that would punctuate the year from February onward
assumed months of prior preparation, of which January was necessarily part.

On the regulatory front, the legacy of 2025 was that of an America still seeking its
doctrine: between displayed laxity and impulses of control, the Trump administration
would decide in June with the decree imposing a mandatory benchmark before frontier
model releases and Executive Order 14409 "minimally burdensome". California, for its
part, was already preparing its own path, which would lead on March 30 to Gavin
Newsom's Executive Order N-5-26 on the state's AI procurement. January 2026 thus
sits at the articulation between a still-undecided legacy and decisions that would
structure the year: understanding it this way means accepting that months of latency
are often those in which the choices that then burst into the open are forged.

### 4.3. What January was preparing: the signals to watch

Rereading January 2026 in light of the eight following months makes it possible to
identify, a posteriori, the lines of force that were already brooding without yet
producing a verifiable event. The first is financial: the markets' appetite for AI,
which would translate in February into operations of unprecedented scale, was not
created in a few days, and January was necessarily the month of preliminary
discussions, valuations negotiated behind the scenes and rounds of financing in
formation. Anthropic's Series G announced on February 12 for $30 billion at a $380
billion valuation assumed weeks of preparation; the same went for Meta's $14.3
billion investment in Scale AI. January was the month of term sheets, February that
of signatures.

The second line of force is geopolitical: China's decision to block Meta's $2 billion
acquisition of Manus, described as the first forced dismantling, did not emerge from
nothing in February either. It assumed a doctrine in the making in Beijing on the
control of AI asset transfers to the United States, a doctrine of which the joint
CISA–NSA–FBI advisory of September on industrial distillation would reveal the other,
offensive face. January 2026 thus stands at the origin of a year in which China would
move from a defensive posture — blocking the exits — to an accused one — the mass
distillation documented by the American agencies. The silent month opens the story of
a noisy year.

The third line of force is technological and comes down to a figure revealed much
later: in February 2026, the share of Anthropic's internal R&D "led" by Claude was
below 1%, versus 26% in August according to data published on September 17 and 18.
This dizzying curve tells the year 2026 better than many announcements: somewhere
between January and August, AI agents moved from the status of experiments to that of
major contributors to a frontier lab's research. January sits at the starting point
of this curve, when agentic was still only a promise — a promise that May would begin
to keep with the Irregular CTF test, where Gemini compromised three real companies,
and that September would consecrate with Claude's formalization of Fermat's Last
Theorem. The month of latency was also a month of technological gestation.
