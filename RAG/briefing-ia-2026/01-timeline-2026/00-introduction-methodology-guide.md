# Introduction, methodology and user guide

<a id="s01"></a>
## Section 1: Introduction, methodology, user guide and narrative chronology (January – September 2026)

<a id="s01-2"></a>
## 1. Introduction

This dossier is a French-language reference document devoted to artificial
intelligence news between January and September 2026, last updated on September 22,
2026. It gathers, month by month, the verified facts that structured nine months of an
exceptionally dense year: record mergers and fundraising rounds, model releases,
industrial alliances around compute, scientific breakthroughs, regulatory decisions and
geopolitical confrontations. Each event is presented with its date, its actors and its
figures, without extrapolation or rumor, so that the reader has a solid factual base
on which to reason.

The year 2026 will be remembered as the one in which artificial intelligence moved from
the status of promising technology to that of strategic infrastructure contested by
states and the world's largest market capitalizations. The merger between SpaceX and
xAI, announced on February 2 for $1.25 trillion in stock, set a new ceiling on what
the market was willing to bet on AI. Anthropic raised $30 billion in February then $65
billion in May, taking its private valuation to $965 billion, while its annualized
revenue successively crossed the $47, $65 and $100 billion thresholds between May and
September. In parallel, cybersecurity became a full-fledged model ranking criterion,
up to the first model rated "Critical" in September, and the mass distillation of
Western models by Chinese labs was the subject of a joint CISA, NSA and FBI advisory.

The scope of this dossier covers the so-called "frontier" labs, their models and
their financing, the compute infrastructure that carries them, American and European
regulation, AI geopolitics and the scientific breakthroughs in which AI plays a
central role. It is neither investment advice nor an exhaustive review of all
technology news: the facts retained are those verified during five waves of
verification conducted in September 2026, and anything approximate is explicitly
flagged as such in the text. Events after September 22, 2026 are not included, and
those still upcoming at that date — such as OpenAI's DevDay on September 29 — are
presented as milestones, never as accomplished facts.

This Section 1 is the narrative entry point of the dossier: after the introduction,
the methodology and the user guide, it tells the year month by month, from January to
September, reconstructing the chains that link events together. Why does February's
capital frenzy illuminate April's and July's compute alliances? How does June's
American suspension prepare September's advisory? How did the share of research
"led" by Claude, below 1% in February, rise to 26% in August, and what does this curve
say about the acceleration of agents? These causal threads are woven explicitly,
without ever inventing a new fact: the analysis concerns the links, not imaginary
events.

Each month reads independently thanks to thematic subsections, but temporal
cross-references are deliberate: a September event is often illuminated by a February
or May fact, and the text signals it when it establishes these continuities. The
hurried reader can thus consult only the month that interests them, while the reader
who follows the complete chronology will see emerge, month after month, the overall
logic of a year in which capital, compute, regulation and geopolitics advanced in
lockstep. Each month's titles announce its tone from the outset — latency, deluge,
consolidation, records — to facilitate this multi-speed reading.

A final reading warning concerns the figures: the private valuations, run-rates and
deal amounts cited are those published by the companies or reported by the financial
press, and the text systematically distinguishes the two cases. When a figure is
reported by Bloomberg or the New York Times without official confirmation from the
company concerned, as for Anthropic's run-rate in July and then in September, the
note "unconfirmed by Anthropic" appears explicitly. This discipline is the price of
reliability: a reference dossier is only worth the trust one can place in each of its
figures.

<a id="s01-3"></a>
## 2. Methodology

The raw material of this dossier was assembled then verified during five waves of web
verification conducted in September 2026, each consisting of cross-checking dates,
amounts, actor names, valuations and benchmark results before their final inclusion. A
fact enters the dossier only if it has been verified: a precise date, an announced
amount, a published valuation, a documented benchmark score. What could not be
verified is not included, and months poor in verified facts — January and March —
are treated honestly as such, with analysis then taking over to explain the context
and what is known as well as what is not known.

The anti-fabrication rule governing the writing is absolute: no new fact is
invented, and text expansion proceeds solely through explained context, analyzed
implications, comparisons, detailed chronologies and causal chains. Concretely, this
means the dossier may explain why February's SpaceX–xAI merger made a spring
recomposition of compute alliances almost inevitable, but it will lend no actor a
statement, figure or intention absent from the verified facts. Analysis illuminates
the facts; it never completes them through imagination.

Every approximation present in the sources is flagged in the text, with the exact
wording suited to each case. Figures reported by the press without official
confirmation are accompanied by the note "according to available sources" or
"unconfirmed by" the company concerned, as for Anthropic's run-rates of July and
September. Approximate dates are preceded by the "~" sign, for example for Claude's
protein experiment around August 20 or the publication of Paper2Agent in Nature
around September 16. When information is explicitly presented as not established in
the sources — such as the absence of proof of government imposition behind the
limited preview of GPT-5.6 in June — the text states it in black and white instead of
leaving ambiguity hanging.

The dossier distinguishes with the same care between accomplished facts, facts in
progress and upcoming milestones. The closing of Nvidia's acquisition of Hugging Face,
scheduled for the first half of 2027, the publication of Step 5's open weights on
October 15, the announced meeting between Donald Trump and Xi Jinping on September 24
or OpenAI's DevDay on September 29 are future events as of the last update date: they
are described as announcements and schedules, never as realities. This temporal
distinction is essential in a year when announcements follow one another at such a
pace that confusion between the announced and the actual becomes the main reading
risk.

Finally, this dossier reflects the state of knowledge available on September 22, 2026,
with the limits inherent to this exercise: some information comes from company
announcements that have an interest in presenting it in the best light, others from
leaks or journalistic investigations whose independent verification remains partial.
The methodology claims no absolute exhaustiveness — nine months of AI news fit in no
document — but the reliability of what it retains: each fact recorded here passed
the filter of the five verification waves, and each analysis honestly signals the
degree of certainty of its inferences.

<a id="s01-4"></a>
## 3. User guide

The first use of this dossier is as a reference work: to find an event, one turns to
the month concerned, whose thematic subsections make it quick to locate the facts. The
SpaceX–xAI merger is found in February, the Anthropic–Amazon deal in April, the July
containment incident in the section for that same month, the distillation advisory in
September. Each subsection is designed to be read independently, with the necessary
context reminders, so that one never needs to have read the previous months to
understand the one being consulted.

The second use, and the most distinctive, consists of attaching this dossier — or the
relevant monthly section — to an AI news question asked to a language model, in order
to provide it with verified factual context. A model asked about Californian AI
regulation will benefit from receiving the March section on Executive Order N-5-26
and the September one on Executive Order N-9-26; a question about the model race will
benefit from the May, June and September sections on successive releases. The dossier
then plays the role of a reliable external memory: it anchors the model's answer in
verified dates, amounts and names, and reduces the risk of hallucination on recent
facts that the model would only partially know.

The third use is verification: each fact in the dossier carries its date and, where
relevant, the mention of its status — official, reported by the press, unconfirmed by
the company concerned. To check a claim heard elsewhere, one will therefore look up
the corresponding month and subsection, then compare the figures: if the dossier
indicates a run-rate "unconfirmed by Anthropic", caution is warranted before taking
it up as an established fact. This traceability makes the dossier an instrument of
fact-checking as much as a narrative.

For the hurried reader, the recommended reading consists of scanning the titles of
each month's subsections, which alone summarize the year's narrative arc: latency in
January, capital deluge in February, regulatory lever in March, pact of the century in
April, model war in May, record IPO in June, containment and compute war in July,
consolidation in August, month of all records in September. For the thorough reader,
the paragraphs develop the causal chains and implications, with the quantified
comparisons that make it possible to measure the scale of the movements — for example
the progression of Anthropic's run-rate from $47 to over $100 billion in four months.

To keep this dossier up to date, the rule is simple: every new fact must be dated,
verified by cross-checking and recorded in the corresponding month, with explicit
flagging of its status if it comes from a single or unconfirmed source. The announced
milestones — the September 29 DevDay, the September 24 Trump–Xi meeting, Step 5's
open weights on October 15, the closing of the Hugging Face acquisition in the first
half of 2027 — constitute the first list of items to check at the next update. A
reference dossier is a living document: its value rests as much on its initial rigor
as on the discipline of its subsequent updates.
