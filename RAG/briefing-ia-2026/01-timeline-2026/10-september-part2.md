# September 2026 (part 2): regulation, diplomacy, consumer agents, markets

<a id="s01-56"></a>
### 12.6. Regulation and diplomacy: Newsom, Amodei, Bessent–He Lifeng, G20

On September 18, 2026, Gavin Newsom signed Executive Order N-9-26, ordering a study
on a kill switch — an emergency stop for AI systems — and on embedded verification,
with recommendations expected on November 16. Six months after the March 30 EO N-5-26
on procurement, California moved from regulation by purchases to regulation by
intrinsic security: no longer only conditioning public contracts, but studying the
technical mechanisms for stopping and verifying systems themselves. The Californian
sequence — N-5-26 in March, laws SB 813 and AB 1405 signed on September 9, N-9-26 on
September 18 — sketched a complete and methodical doctrine, in contrast with the
federal restraint of June's "minimally burdensome". The kill switch, a concept
popularized by debates on existential risks, thus entered Californian positive law —
at the study stage, admittedly, but with a close deadline of November 16.

Six days earlier, on September 12, Dario Amodei published his essay "We Must Pace the
Frontier" — "We must pace the frontier" — proposing a three-step plan to pace
frontier model development, and received the public approval of Sam Altman and Elon
Musk — an unprecedented alignment of the three leaders of the world's most powerful
labs. Two days later, on September 14, Mustafa Suleyman published the draft Code of
Conduct for Humanist AI — AI remains a subordinate tool, no rights or personhood for
systems — submitted to a six-week public consultation. The conjunction of the two
texts, two days apart, marked a philosophical moment of the year: while models
formalized Fermat and designed proteins, their creators publicly debated the pace to
impose on them and the status to refuse them. On September 15, the security
coordination between OpenAI, Anthropic and Google — confirmed the same day — gave
these debates an operational translation.

On the diplomatic front, on September 20, Scott Bessent met He Lifeng in New York
for about eight hours of talks, during which an AI incident-notification mechanism
was proposed — a confidence measure directly inspired by the year's incidents, from
July's containment to the distillation advisory — with the announced meeting between
Donald Trump and Xi Jinping on September 24 in sight. And on September 1 and 2, the
Chapel Hill G20 unanimously adopted the "Carolina Principles" — non-binding, with
formal adoption by leaders scheduled in December in Doral. September's diplomatic
architecture was thus complete: non-binding multilateral principles at the G20,
Sino-American bilateral in New York, lab coordination on the 15th, leaders' essays
and codes of conduct — a normative effervescence commensurate with a year in which
AI had become the states' business.

### 12.7. Consumer agents: Meta Muse, Grok in Copilot, iOS 27

On September 8, 2026, Meta launched Meta Muse, its consumer personal agent:
execution in a Muse Secure VM — a dedicated secure virtual machine — connectors to
Gmail, Spotify, Ticketmaster and OpenTable, a free offering plus two tiers at $20 and
$100 per month, reserved to the United States for those over 18, available on iOS,
Android, the web and WhatsApp, with AI glasses planned. Each detail of this product
sheet tells a strategy: the Secure VM answered the worries born of the year's
incidents — Irregular in May, containment in July — by promising partitioned
execution; the connectors — messaging, music, ticketing, reservations — sketched the
agent as a digital butler; distribution via WhatsApp gave Meta a channel of three
billion potential users; and the planned AI glasses announced the next form factor.
Meta Muse was Meta's consumer answer to ChatGPT Work and Scout, in a year when every
giant wanted its agent.

Four days later, on September 12, Satya Nadella announced the arrival of Grok models
in Copilot: selectable in Word, Excel and PowerPoint, first for Frontier customers,
with administrator opt-in, and excluded from the European Union, the United Kingdom
and EFTA during the preview. This announcement closed the loop opened on February 19
with the preview of Grok 4.1 Fast on Copilot Studio: in seven months, xAI had moved
from the developers' sandbox to the world's most used productivity applications. The
temporary exclusion of Europe — EU, UK, EFTA — during the preview also said
something of the regulatory landscape: between the Digital Omnibus in force since
July 27 and the requirements to come, Microsoft preferred a cautious approach by
geography. And the administrator opt-in answered CIOs' fears: the Grok agent would
enter the enterprise only on explicit decision.

On September 14, Apple launched iOS 27 with Siri AI in beta — on waitlist, reserved
for iPhone 15 Pro and newer — endowed with cross-app personal context and
accompanied by over 20 new Apple Intelligence features, with integrations of Claude,
GPT and Gemini mentioned conditionally. The "conditional" wording deserves emphasis:
as of the dossier's last update date, these third-party integrations were not
confirmed, and the dossier sticks to this status. Apple's strategy contrasted with
its competitors': no proprietary flagship model put forward, but a system assistant
deeply integrated into the phone — cross-app personal context — and a progressive
beta on recent devices. Between Meta Muse on the 8th, Grok in Copilot on the 12th
and Siri AI on the 14th, the second week of September 2026 was the one when agentic
became the giants' official consumer battleground — each with its doctrine: Meta the
social butler, Microsoft the office colleague, Apple the pocket companion.

### 12.8. Markets and models: Fugu, Z.ai, mergers and a fanfare-filled month end

On September 11, 2026, Sakana launched Fugu Max at $2 and $6 per million and Fugu
Ultra v2.0 at $5 and $30 per million — with an important conceptual clarification:
these were not foundation models but learned orchestrators, which route tasks to a
pool of existing models. This architecture — intelligence in the routing rather than
in the weights — proposed a third way between the pre-training giants and
aggregators like OpenRouter: instead of always training bigger, learn to choose, for
each task, the best model in the pool. The pricing — aligned with Grok's for Max, with
Opus's for Ultra — positioned Fugu as an optimization overlay sold at the model's
price, an audacious bet for which 2026, with its fragmented offerings, offered the
ideal terrain.

On September 13, Z.ai raised approx. $5 billion — some $2 billion in equity and $3
billion in bonds — its second big round after approx. $4 billion in July, confirming
the Chinese lab as one of the best-funded of its generation despite the September 8
advisory which cited it among the six accused of distillation. The coexistence, five
days apart, of the American accusation and the Chinese mega-round illustrates the
year's complexity: the same labs could be both accused of siphoning Western models
and capable of raising billions on the promise of their own models — GLM-5.3 had
just come out on August 14, GLM-5-Image followed. The same September 13, xAI
announced Grok 4.8 with 2.5 trillion parameters — 2.5T — and an in-house C++ stack,
displaying a scale ambition that answered DeepSeek V4-Pro's 1.6 trillion and Kimi
K3's 2.8 trillion.

On September 16, the definitive merger agreement between Cohere and Aleph Alpha —
approx. $20 billion, Cohere name retained, dual headquarters Toronto/Berlin, over
1,000 employees, closing subject to regulators in late 2026 — created the
transatlantic enterprise-AI champion, with Schwarz Group's support of €500 million
in the Series E. The dossier specifies that the €11–13 billion German data center
campus via StackIT was a separate project, to avoid any confusion: the merger was
worth $20 billion, the German infrastructure fell under another arrangement. This
merger, one month after Stripe's acquisition of OpenRouter and two weeks after
Nvidia's of Hugging Face, completed the autumn's consolidation: European enterprise
AI now had its champion, backed by open-weight — Command A+ in May — and claimed
sovereignty.

The month's end brought its share of releases and records: Step 5 Preview on
September 20 — a 600-billion-parameter MoE with 27 billion active, one-million
context, $1 and $2.70 per million, open weights announced for October 15; Qwen Image
2.1 the same day in research-only license; DeepSeek V4.1 Flash on September 10 —
552-billion-parameter MoE, 8 to 16 billion active, 1M context, MIT license, Causal
Encoder-Decoder architecture — which replaced V4 Pro on the API as early as
September 14, accompanied by "abliterated" forks; Kimi K3 generally available on
Bedrock on September 18; and, on September 21, AMD crossed $1 trillion in market
capitalization — up 9.95% to approx. $615, $1,004.8 billion at close — becoming the
fourth American chipmaker, carried by a rally fueled by agentic demand in the wake
of the Muse effect. The same September 21, Grok 4.7 came out after five delays, at
unchanged pricing of $2 and $6, but Elon Musk publicly downgraded it even before its
release — an episode that said much about the internal tensions of the model race.
And meanwhile, Moonshot filed its Hong Kong IPO dossier on September 4 — approx. $3
billion targeted for a $50 billion valuation — while OpenAI's DevDay loomed on
September 29 as the next milestone. September 2026 ended as it had begun: in
fanfare, and without respite.
