# Muse Image, Video, Code and Glimmer: the creative portfolio

<a id="s04-9"></a>
### Muse Image, Muse Video, Muse Code, Muse Glimmer: the creative and developer portfolio

Around the personal agent, Meta deployed in 2026 a product
portfolio covering visual creation and software development —
two terrains where models are judged on the merits. On July 7,
2026 came Muse Image, an agentic image generation and editing
tool, ranked No. 2 on the Arena with an Elo score of 1280,
behind GPT Image 2. The same lineage yields a Muse Video
preview, ranked No. 3 on the Arena in text-to-video. In August,
two more launches: Muse Code, a terminal agent based on Muse
Spark 1.2, and Muse Glimmer, a 30-billion-parameter model under
the Apache 2.0 license marking Meta's return to open source.
Four products, four complementary bets.

Muse Image deserves a closer look at its sheet: "agentic" image
generation and editing, No. 2 on the Arena, Elo 1280, behind GPT
Image 2. The "agentic" qualifier is essential: this is not a
simple image generator you hand a prompt, but a system able to
carry a visual project — understanding an intent, proposing
variants, retouching, iterating. It is the same philosophy as
Meta Muse applied to creation: the AI no longer merely executes,
it leads. The Arena ranking — that arena where models face off
on human judgments — gives an independent measure: No. 2
worldwide, with an Elo of 1280, behind GPT Image 2. Second place
is a top-tier performance; it also says first place remains to
be taken, and that it is held by OpenAI. The Meta-OpenAI
rivalry, already lively on agents, is therefore also playing out
on image.

The Muse Video preview, No. 3 on the Arena in text-to-video,
extends the same logic to generative video. The "preview" status
matters: Meta shows without quite delivering, testing the
market's and the community's reaction before freezing the
product. Third place on the text-to-video arena, in a still
young and volatile segment, is a credible outsider positioning:
not the leader, but in the leading pack, with headroom.
Generative video is the segment where gaps widen fastest — each
model generation makes a visible leap — and being present there,
even in preview, is a strategic necessity for anyone claiming to
cover creation.

Muse Code, launched in August, is a terminal agent based on
Muse Spark 1.2. The positioning is crystal clear: go fetch
developers where they work, in the terminal, with an agent able
to act on code — read, modify, test, commit. The choice of Muse
Spark 1.2 as the base, rather than version 1.3 released on
September 2, is explained by the calendar: in August, 1.3 did
not exist yet. But it also says something about the lineage's
maturity: from version 1.2, Muse Spark was judged solid enough
to carry a demanding developer product. For Meta, the stake is
clear: developers are AI's prescribers, the ones who choose
models for their companies. A terminal agent adopted by
developers is a Trojan horse toward enterprise deployments.

Muse Glimmer, also launched in August, is of another nature: 30
billion parameters, Apache 2.0 license, and above all a
political signal — Meta's return to open. After the pivot that
pauses LLaMA, Glimmer shows openness is not abandoned; it is
changing vehicles. Thirty billion parameters places it in the
"reasonable" category: capable enough for serious uses, compact
enough to deploy without disproportionate infrastructure. The
Apache 2.0 license, the market's most permissive, is a strong
gesture to the community: use it, modify it, commercialize it,
with no strings attached. It is with that type of license that
LLaMA won over developers; Glimmer resumes the recipe, under a
new name.

Taken together, these four products sketch a segment-occupation
strategy. Image and video for creation, where the cultural and
media battle plays out. Code for developers, where the
prescriber battle plays out. Glimmer for the open-source
community, where the ecosystem battle plays out. And Meta Muse,
the personal agent, as the flagship product capitalizing on all
the rest. Each product has its success metric — an Arena
ranking, developer adoption, a community — and none alone
carries the Muse Spark lineage's destiny. It is a portfolio
strategy, not a single bet.

Also note the calendar sequence: Muse Image on July 7, Muse
Code and Muse Glimmer in August, Meta Muse on September 8.
Three months of rapid-fire launches, recalling — all proportions
kept — Google's Flash cadence. Meta has plainly decided 2026
would be the year of product demonstration: after years of
research and promises, it needs objects people can touch. The
risk of such a barrage is dispersion: too many products, not
enough attention for each. But the reverse risk — launching
nothing while the competition occupies the field — had become
unbearable.

Finally, there is an internal reading of this portfolio: it
validates the Muse Spark lineage as a platform. One model base
carrying a consumer personal agent, a developer terminal agent,
visual creation tools, and an open 30-billion-parameter model is
the demonstration that a lineage can be generalist without being
mediocre everywhere. That was precisely the reproach leveled at
LLaMA 5 — a contested release, fuzzy positioning. Muse Spark,
by contrast, arrives with products proving its versatility. The
pivot described in the previous subsection is therefore not just
a name change: it is a change of method, where each model must
justify its existence through products.
