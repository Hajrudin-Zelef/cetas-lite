# Cross-cutting readings: September calendar and section conclusion

**The September calendar: ten days that changed everything.**
The calendar density deserves to be visualized, because it explains the
impression of acceleration:

- 09/04: Moonshot IPO filing in Hong Kong; publication of
  LLaDA-Image-Turbo (inclusionAI/Ant).
- 09/08: U.S. advisory AA26-251A on industrial distillation; Mistral's
  €3B raise (European record).
- 09/10: announcement of DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (552B, MIT).
- 09/11: Fugu Max and Fugu Ultra v2.0 (Sakana); Anthropic report
  accusing Zhipu of massive distillation of Claude.
- 09/13: Z.ai's ~$5B raise.
- 09/14: launch of iOS 27 and the Siri AI beta; V4.1 Flash replaces V4
  Pro on the DeepSeek API.
- 09/16: definitive Cohere + Aleph Alpha merger agreement.
- 09/18: Kimi K3 in GA on AWS Bedrock.
- 09/20: Step 5 Preview (StepFun); Qwen Image 2.1 (research-only
  license).

In seventeen days, the sector saw: two giant raises, an IPO project, a
transatlantic merger, three major model releases, a GA on Bedrock, a
regulatory advisory, a public distillation accusation, and the launch
of Apple's new assistant.
It is this pace — more than any isolated fact — that defines the period:
the AI of September 2026 is no longer an industry that publishes papers,
it is an industry chaining market operations on a weekly cadence.

---

At the end of this section, three lines of force emerge for the
summer–mid-September 2026 period.
First, China dominates the open-weight tempo: DeepSeek publishes three
models in two months under the MIT license, Moonshot opens the weights
of the largest model ever published (Kimi K3, 2.8T), StepFun promises
its own for October 15, and even financial actors like Ant follow the
movement.
But this dominance comes with a counterpart: advisory AA26-251A of
September 8 and Anthropic's September 11 report make industrial
distillation the sore subject, with documented volumes — 151 million
exchanges for Qwen, 23 million for Kimi K3 — giving the controversy
unprecedented materiality.
Second, Europe builds its answer through capital and sovereignty:
Mistral raises €3B at over €21B valuation with Samsung in the round and
secures thousands of Vera Rubin GPUs in Europe with Microsoft; Cohere
merges with Aleph Alpha to build a ~$20B transatlantic stack explicitly
freed from the U.S. CLOUD Act, with a rigorous distinction to maintain
between the merger, the Schwarz Group's €500M in the Series E, and the
€11–13B of the StackIT campus.
Third, business models diversify: Sakana sells orchestration rather
than foundation models, Apple locks personal AI onto its hardware with
Siri AI in beta, Stability AI invents licensed music generation with
the record majors, Inflection AI attempts a comeback via the life
assistant, and Alibaba itself experiments with the research-only license
on Qwen Image 2.1.
The autumn 2026 market therefore no longer boils down to "who has the
best model": it is fought over licenses, jurisdictions, token prices,
and orchestration layers — all terrains where this section's facts give
every decision-maker material to arbitrate.

For a decision-maker reading this section in September 2026, three
concrete arbitrations arise, fed solely by the facts above.
First arbitration, the model supplier: should one bet on the cheap
Chinese token (DeepSeek V4-Flash at $0.14/$0.27, Step 5 Preview at
$1/$2.70), on maximum capacity (Kimi K3 at $3/$15, 2.8T open weights),
or on Western enterprise operability (Command A+, Apache 2.0, native
citations, 48 languages)?
The answer depends less on benchmarks — absent from the verified core —
than on the risk profile: exposure to the distillation controversy,
license requirements (MIT versus research-only), and data-sovereignty
constraints.
Second arbitration, jurisdiction: the Mistral offering (European
compute, American partner) and the Cohere–Aleph Alpha offering
(transatlantic stack freed from the CLOUD Act) are not interchangeable;
the choice commits the company's compliance for years, and the merger's
closing — expected end of 2026 subject to regulatory approvals — is the
milestone to watch before committing.
Third arbitration, the business model of AI itself: buy tokens (the
foundation labs), buy routing (Sakana and its Fugu orchestrators), or
buy system integration (Apple and Siri AI, locked to iPhone 15 Pro and
later)?
These three layers — raw material, orchestration, distribution — are now
distinct markets, with distinct prices ($0.14 to $30 per million tokens
depending on layer and vendor).

What to watch in the coming weeks, always from the verified facts?
October 15, 2026: does StepFun keep its open-weights promise for Step 5
Preview? It is a dated commitment, hence verifiable, and it will
determine whether the lab durably joins the club of the big openers.
End of 2026: does the Cohere–Aleph Alpha merger obtain its regulatory
approvals (Canada, Germany, possibly EU) and close as planned? This is
the full-scale test of the "sovereignty through merger" thesis.
Moonshot's Hong Kong IPO: does the September 4 filing lead to a deal at
the announced terms (~$3B raised, ~$50B valuation), and how does the
market price the distillation risk documented by advisory AA26-251A?
GLM-5.3's weight security review: how long does this unprecedented gate
for the lineage last, and does it become a standard other labs adopt?
Finally, Siri AI's exit from beta: will Apple expand beyond the waitlist
and iPhone 15 Pro+, and will it confirm — or not — the Claude, GPT, and
Gemini integrations currently in the conditional, with only ChatGPT
documented?
Each of these milestones is a fact in the making, not speculation: they
are all anchored in dated commitments or ongoing processes documented in
this section.
This is the reading method this dossier recommends: follow verifiable
commitments — publication dates, filings, regulatory approvals — rather
than narratives.
September 2026 showed it: in an industry chaining operations on a weekly
cadence, only dated facts allow distinguishing signal from noise.
