# Z.ai / GLM: raises, distillation accusation, security move

<a id="s06-5"></a>
### Z.ai / GLM: giant raises, a distillation accusation, and an unprecedented security move

Z.ai — the commercial structure linked to Zhipu, the Chinese lab behind
the GLM family — concentrates in September 2026 three news items which,
taken together, tell the complexity of the moment: a colossal fundraise,
a frontal accusation from Anthropic, and a first in weight-security
review.
To understand the sequence, the product trajectory must first be
recalled: in February 2026, GLM-5.2 was released open-source and
established itself as the #1 open-source model at launch — a milestone
that installed the GLM family in the top trio of open models, alongside
DeepSeek and Qwen.
This open-source-leader status makes what follows all the more
significant.

On August 14, 2026, Zhipu published GLM-5.3, oriented toward coding and
agentic use, built on the GLM-5.2 base.
The striking fact is not just the specialization — coding and agentic
are the most hotly contested segments of the 2026 market — but the
decision that accompanies it: the weights are being held back for
security review, a first for the GLM lineage.
This is a doctrinal turning point: until now, the family published its
weights without this kind of gate; from now on, the publisher introduces
a control delay between announcement and availability.
Several readings coexist: responsible maturity in the face of malicious-
use risks from code models, Chinese or international regulatory pressure,
or a desire to keep a commercial head start on the API version.
Whatever the motivation, the signal is that the era of immediate and
unconditional weight publication is reaching its limit, even among the
historical champions of openness.
Also in August, Zhipu published GLM-5-Image, which extends the family to
image generation — following the general movement of labs rounding out
their text offering with a visual modality.

On September 13, 2026, Z.ai raised about $5 billion — about $2 billion
in equity and about $3 billion in debt — making it the lab's second big
raise after the roughly $4 billion already raised in July.
Nine billion dollars in two months: the order of magnitude places Z.ai
among the most massive private financing operations in AI history, and
testifies to investor appetite that shows no sign of abating despite the
controversies.
The mixed equity/debt structure is itself interesting: the debt portion
(about $3B) suggests financing backed by assets — typically compute or
infrastructure — while the equity portion (about $2B) funds growth and
R&D.
This is the financing model of "capital-intensive labs": raise debt for
hardware, capital for talent and research.
The comparison with Moonshot is instructive: where Moonshot targets
public markets with a Hong Kong IPO (~$3B for a ~$50B valuation), Z.ai
stacks giant private rounds — two coexisting financing strategies within
the Chinese ecosystem.

Two days before this raise, on September 11, 2026, Anthropic published
a report accusing Zhipu of massive distillation of Claude.
The accusation is frontal, by name, and comes from a direct competitor:
this is no longer a generic government advisory, it is a market actor
publicly saying "we were copied".
Massive distillation of Claude, if proven, would mean that GLM models'
capabilities owe in part to the exploitation of Claude's outputs —
calling into question the originality of the displayed progress.
Z.ai is also cited in advisory AA26-251A of September 8, which documents
industrial-scale distillation sector-wide.
The calendar coincidence — advisory on the 8th, Anthropic report on the
11th, $5B raise on the 13th — is striking: within one week, the lab goes
from the defendants' bench to the record-raise table.
For the investors who subscribed to the September 13 raise, the question
of the solidity of Zhipu's intellectual property is now central: it is a
legal and reputational risk weighing on the valuation.
For the industry, the Anthropic–Zhipu confrontation is a precedent: it
is the first time a major Western lab has so publicly accused a Chinese
lab of distillation, opening the way to potential lawsuits or technical
API-protection measures.

In summary, Z.ai/GLM illustrates the Chinese paradox of September 2026:
recognized product excellence (GLM-5.2 #1 open-source, agentic GLM-5.3,
GLM-5-Image), an extraordinary financing capacity (~$9B in two months),
but maximum exposure on the distillation front — U.S. advisory and
Anthropic accusation.
The fact that GLM-5.3's weights are being held back for security review,
a first for the lineage, can also be read in this light: in a climate of
suspicion over training methods, showing clean hands on safety becomes a
strategic asset.
The lab now plays on two boards: convincing investors of its models'
value, and convincing observers of its methods' legitimacy.
