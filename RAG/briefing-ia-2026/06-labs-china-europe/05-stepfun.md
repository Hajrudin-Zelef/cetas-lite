# StepFun: Step 5 Preview

<a id="s06-6"></a>
### StepFun: Step 5 Preview, the discreet challenger

StepFun, a Chinese lab less in the media spotlight than DeepSeek or
Moonshot, nonetheless scored a major hit on September 20, 2026 with
Step 5 Preview.
The specifications are solid: a 600-billion-parameter MoE total,
27 billion active, a one-million-token context window, priced at $1 per
million input tokens and $2.70 for output.
Let us position this model in the landscape: with 600B total parameters,
it sits just above DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (552B) and far below Kimi K3
(2.8T); with 27B active parameters, it is markedly more "dense" at
inference than V4.1 Flash (8–16B active), suggesting a different trade-
off between capacity and serving cost.
On price, $1/$2.70 places it between the highly aggressive DeepSeek
V4-Flash ($0.14/$0.27) and premium Kimi K3 ($3/$15): it is the
"performant mid-range" segment, the one targeting demanding production
workloads without luxury billing.
The million-token context window, once again, confirms the Chinese norm
of the period.

The announcement accompanying Step 5 Preview is forward-looking: open
weights are announced for October 15, 2026.
This is a promise, not yet a publication — and in a market where open-
weight announcements are multiplying, the deadline is a verifiable
commitment the community will hold them to.
If StepFun keeps its schedule, Step 5 will join the club of the big open
models of autumn 2026, alongside Kimi K3 (2.8T) and DeepSeek V4.1 Flash
(552B, MIT).
At 600B parameters, it would slot between the two in size, offering a
credible intermediate option.
The "preview first, weights later" strategy is also a way to capture
media attention twice: once at the API announcement, once at the weight
publication — a well-rehearsed communications mechanic in the ecosystem.

StepFun is also cited in advisory AA26-251A of September 8, 2026 on
industrial-scale distillation.
The briefing does not detail the volumes attributed to the lab, unlike
the 151 million exchanges documented for Alibaba or the 23 million for
Moonshot — but the mere citation places StepFun within U.S. authorities'
surveillance perimeter.
This is revealing of the phenomenon's scale: even second-tier labs in
media terms are concerned, suggesting that distillation is a systemic
practice of the ecosystem, not the work of a few "bad apples".
For StepFun, the stakes are reputational: just as it seeks to establish
itself with Step 5 Preview, being associated — even without detailed
volumes — with an industrial-distillation file complicates its narrative
as a deserving challenger.
This is the dilemma of all Chinese labs of the period: openness and
performance attract developers, but the shadow of distillation attracts
regulators.

In summary, StepFun embodies the "second wave" of Chinese labs: less
visible than the headliners, but capable of publishing a 600B MoE at a
million-context with aggressive pricing and a dated open-weights promise.
If October 15 is kept, the lab will earn its stripes as a first-rank
open-weight actor; otherwise, the announcement will remain a media coup
without follow-through.
Either way, Step 5 Preview confirms that Chinese competition is no
longer limited to two or three names: it is an entire ecosystem now
producing world-class models at a sustained pace.
