# Cross-cutting readings: distillation, licenses, capital

**Distillation as the regulatory through-line.**
Advisory AA26-251A of September 8, 2026 cites five labs from this
section: DeepSeek (industrial distillation; the "5.6M$" training cost of
R1/V3 judged misleading), Alibaba (151M+ exchanges May–Jul 2026 to
distill Qwen), Moonshot (23M+ exchanges for Kimi K3), Z.ai, and StepFun.
Added on September 11, Anthropic's report accusing Zhipu by name of
massive distillation of Claude.
The picture is striking: within one week, distillation moves from the
status of unofficial practice to that of a file documented by a U.S.
authority and a first-rank competitor.
The volumes — 151 million exchanges in three months for Qwen — give
unprecedented materiality to a phenomenon hitherto discussed in abstract
terms.
And the undermining of DeepSeek's "5.6M$" weakens the founding narrative
of Chinese efficiency: if the advertised training costs are misleading
and if capabilities owe in part to the distillation of competitors'
models, then the apparent cost advantage of Chinese labs must be
reassessed.
For legal departments worldwide, the practical consequence is immediate:
harden API terms of use against distillation, instrument detection of
mass-siphoning behaviors, and provision for litigation risk.
For investors — those of Z.ai's September 13 raise, those of Moonshot's
September 4 filing — the solidity of intellectual property becomes a
valuation variable.

**Licenses, the mirror of strategies.**
The period offers a complete panorama of openness policies: MIT
(DeepSeek V4-Flash-0731, DeepSeek V4.1 Flash) — maximum openness, free
commercial use; Apache 2.0 (Command A+, on Hugging Face) — openness with
a patent clause, favored by enterprises; weights held back for security
review (GLM-5.3, a first for the lineage) — conditional openness, a new
gate between announcement and publication; research-only license (Qwen
Image 2.1, 09/20) — openness as trompe-l'œil, without commercial use.
This gradient shows that "open" is no longer a binary status but a
spectrum of strategies: each lab doses openness according to its goals —
developer adoption, commercial protection, legal compliance.
The underlying trend is toward tightening: GLM-5.3 inaugurates prior
security review, Qwen Image 2.1 locks down commercial use.
The golden age of immediate and unconditional weight publication seems
to be closing, even if Moonshot, with Kimi K3, offered its apotheosis —
2.8T open parameters — a few weeks earlier.

**The capital: who raises, how much, for what.**
September 2026 is also a historic financing month: Z.ai raised ~$5B on
09/13 (~$2B in equity + ~$3B in debt), its second big raise after ~$4B in
July — i.e., ~$9B in two months; Moonshot targets a Hong Kong IPO (~$3B
raised, ~$50B valuation, 09/04 filing); Mistral raised €3B on 09/08 at
over €21B valuation (~$24B, with Samsung) — a European record; Cohere
and Aleph Alpha merge for ~$20B (definitive agreement of 09/16, closing
expected end of 2026, subject to Canada/Germany/possibly EU approvals);
Stability AI closed $76M on 08/25 (total $232M).
Two models coexist: giant private rounds (Z.ai, Mistral) and the stock-
market route (Moonshot in Hong Kong).
And an analytical warning is in order, already stated in the Cohere
section: the Schwarz Group's €500M goes to Cohere's next Series E — not
to the merger — and the €11–13B concern the German data center campus
via StackIT (up to 100,000 chips) — a separate infrastructure project.
Adding these figures to the merger's valuation would be an error: rigor
about each amount's perimeter is the condition of an honest reading of
the deals.

**Two European philosophies of sovereignty.**
Mistral and the Cohere–Aleph Alpha combination embody two different
answers to the same question — how to build a sovereign European AI.
Mistral chooses "hosting" sovereignty: thousands of Vera Rubin GPUs in
its European data centers, financed by a multi-billion-dollar deal with
Microsoft — cutting-edge hardware under European jurisdiction, but an
American partner in the capital and the technology.
Cohere–Aleph Alpha chooses "ownership" sovereignty: a ~$20B entity,
dual Toronto/Berlin headquarters, more than 1,000 employees, research
hub in Heidelberg, explicitly built to sidestep the U.S. CLOUD Act — no
American parent company, claimed legal watertightness.
One maximizes available computing power quickly; the other maximizes
legal independence.
Both theses are defensible, they target different clients — those for
whom data localization suffices, and those for whom only the absence of
American control counts — and they can coexist.
But they draw a lasting fault line in the European ecosystem: pragmatic
sovereignty backed by hyperscalers versus uncompromising sovereignty
backed by capital independence.
