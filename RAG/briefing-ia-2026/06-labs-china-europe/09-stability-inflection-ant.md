# Stability AI, Inflection AI and Ant / inclusionAI

<a id="s06-11"></a>
### Stability AI: Series B and licensed music

Stability AI, the British lab known for Stable Diffusion, closed its
Series B on August 25, 2026: $76 million, for a total raised of $232
million.
The round's composition is the most telling information: among the new
investors are EA (Electronic Arts), Sony Music Group, Universal Music
Group, Warner Music Group, AMD Ventures, and Pacific Alliance Ventures;
returning to the round are Coatue, Greycroft, Kadmos, Sean Parker, and
Eric Schmidt.
The simultaneous presence of the three record majors — Sony Music,
Universal, Warner — in the capital of a generative AI lab is a political
fact as much as a financial one: historically they have been the toughest
plaintiffs or negotiators against generative music AI, and seeing them
invest rather than sue signals a change of era, from confrontation to
partnership.
EA brings the video-game endorsement, a heavy consumer of procedural
generation; AMD Ventures, that of a chipmaker seeking to place its
hardware in the AI value chain.
On governance, Thomas Laffont (Coatue) joins the board, alongside James
Cameron, Sean Parker, and Dana Settle — a board mixing venture capital,
Hollywood, and tech, in the image of the lab's "creative" positioning.

The product justifying this round is called Stable Audio 3.0: a family
of open-weight music models, trained on fully licensed data, accompanied
by a DAW plugin and the StableAudio.com site.
Each element of this description is an answer to a past controversy.
"Fully licensed data": this is the direct answer to the lawsuits and
criticism over wild training of audio models — Stability AI buys legal
peace upstream, and turns the constraint into a commercial argument.
"Open-weight": the lab stays faithful to its openness doctrine, even on
a segment as sensitive as music.
"DAW plugin": integration into digital audio workstations (producers'
software) anchors the product in real workflows, rather than in a
gadgety web demo.
StableAudio.com provides the consumer showcase.
The loop closes with the cap table: the majors invest in a lab whose
music models are trained on licensed data — potentially their own —
creating an unprecedented alignment of interests between rightsholders
and generative AI.
If this business model holds — data licensing, value sharing, tools for
creators — it could become the template for "responsible generation" in
the creative industries, well beyond music.

### Inflection AI: Pi's return after the Microsoft "raid"

On July 21, 2026, Inflection AI announced the launch of Inflection AI
Labs — presented as a public experimentation engine doubled with a
research activity — and of Pi Journeys.
This is the lab's return to the public stage after the Microsoft "raid",
which had drained it of most of its founders and teams in 2024 to
integrate them into Microsoft AI.
That the lab is still capable, two years later, of launching an
ambitious public program testifies to a successful — or at least engaged
— reconstruction.
Inflection AI Labs, as a "public experimentation engine", suggests a
method: testing ideas in real conditions, with real users, rather than
in a laboratory vacuum.
It is also a positioning: at a time when big labs communicate via press
releases, Inflection claims the ground of open experimentation.

Pi Journeys adapts Pi — the lab's "empathetic" personal assistant —
along life stages: parenthood, family caregiving, career change, aging.
The choice of segments is significant: these are moments of
vulnerability and transition, where a trusted assistant can bring lasting
value — and where competition from generalist chatbots is least well
positioned.
The program comes with new approaches to memory and fine-tuning:
long-term memory — remembering the user over months — is the nerve center
of the personal-assistant war, and Inflection makes it an explicit
research axis.
The lab publishes on this occasion its first report, the "State of
Consumer AI Research Report", which marks its entry into the published-
research arena — a rite of passage for scientific credibility.
Finally, Pi itself is updated: voice, memory, and agentic tools —
reminders, task lists, shopping.
One reads here the general convergence of 2026: all assistants, even
the most "companion-like", become agentic and actionable.
Pi's historic promise — empathy, relationship — is now doubled with
concrete utility: remind, organize, buy.
It remains to be seen whether the market, which has meanwhile seen the
agents of all the big labs pass through, still reserves a place for an
assistant positioned on gentleness rather than power.

### Ant / inclusionAI: LLaDA-Image-Turbo

On September 4, 2026, inclusionAI — the structure linked to Ant —
published LLaDA-Image-Turbo.
The name alone tells of the positioning: LLaDA is Ant's family of
language diffusion models, "Image" extends the modality to visuals, and
"Turbo" signals speed optimization — in line with the general movement
observed in August with Alibaba's Qwen2.5-Image-Lightning (10× faster).
The briefing documents neither detailed specifications, nor license,
nor pricing: this is therefore a milestone to record as an activity
signal — Ant remains present in the model race — rather than a release
to evaluate on the merits.
Its place in this section is due to what it illustrates: in September
2026, even Chinese fintech actors deem it necessary to publish in image
generation, a sign that the visual modality has become a mandatory
battleground for any lab that counts.
