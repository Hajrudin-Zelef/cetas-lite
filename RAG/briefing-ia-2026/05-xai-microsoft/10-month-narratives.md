# Month narratives (June, September) and the economics of models

<a id="s05-41"></a>
### June 2026: the month Microsoft delivered everything

June 2026 deserves its own narrative, because it is the month Microsoft moved from promise to delivery on all fronts.
June 2–3: Build at Fort Mason, San Francisco — Suleyman announces the seven MAI models "from scratch, no distillation," and unveils Scout and Project Solara.
The month opens with a declaration of independence: Microsoft is no longer OpenAI's junior, it is a full-fledged lab with a complete range.
June 12: the SpaceX IPO — $75B at $135, ~$1.77T, historic record, +19% day 1.
Not a Microsoft announcement, but the month's financial context: while Microsoft deploys its models, the Musk ecosystem raises the largest IPO in history and arms itself for the race.
June 16: Copilot Cowork worldwide GA — long-running cloud agents via Work IQ, Frontier program adoption record, per-credit billing at $0.01.
Two weeks after Build's announcements, Microsoft ships at planetary scale: the execution proof that gives the promises credit.
In sixteen days, Microsoft thus announced its models, watched its rival raise massively, and put its cloud agents into worldwide GA.
It is a pace few companies can sustain: announcing and shipping in the same month, at global scale, on products as complex as long-running agents.
The contrast with the rest of the year is instructive: September will see xAI chaining delays (Grok 4.7, five delays) while Microsoft chains normative (Code of Conduct) and commercial (Transcribe-2 on September 3) deliveries.
June 2026 is the month Microsoft seized the initiative — and never let it go.
The symbolic geography must also be noted: Build is held at Fort Mason, San Francisco — in the heart of Silicon Valley territory, a few kilometers from OpenAI's and Anthropic's headquarters.
Announcing independence from OpenAI from San Francisco is doing it on the adversary's turf: a staging choice that says a lot about Suleyman's assurance.
Finally, June 2026 lays the foundations of the entire second half: the MAI models announced in June will fuel September's comparisons (Thinking-1 vs Sonnet/Opus 4.6); Cowork, GA'd in June, will be the reception ground for third-party models (Anthropic, GPT-5.5, soon Grok via Copilot); Scout, unveiled in June, will be framed by September's Code.
None of what plays out in September would have been possible without Microsoft's June.

<a id="s05-42"></a>
### September 2026: ten days that changed everything

From September 3 to September 21, 2026, eighteen days — and at the heart, ten days of exceptional density — redefined the landscape.
September 3: MAI-Transcribe-2 — #1 on FLEURS, 10× GPT-Transcribe, $0.10/hour on promo.
Microsoft opens the month with a technical and commercial show of force on an infrastructure segment.
September 12: Nadella announces Grok in Copilot — Word, Excel, PowerPoint; Frontier customers first; admin opt-in; EU/UK/EFTA excluded in preview.
The diplomatic earthquake: Musk's models enter Microsoft's flagship product, announced by the CEO himself.
September 13: xAI announces Grok 4.8 — 2.5 trillion parameters, in-house C++ stack.
The technical earthquake: scale as argument, the very day we learn Grok enters Microsoft.
September 14: Microsoft AI publishes the draft Code of Conduct for Humanist AI — 37 pages, six weeks of consultation, clash with Anthropic.
The normative earthquake: while xAI races for size, Microsoft races for norms.
September 21: Grok 4.7 release — five delays, unchanged prices, Musk publicly downgrades it.
The minor-key epilogue: the modest model shipping in the shadow of the announced giant.
Read this sequence as a two-player chess game.
Microsoft plays September 3 (Transcribe-2): a solid, low-key technical move that installs measured superiority.
xAI responds September 12–13 with a double move: the Copilot integration (massive distribution) and the 4.8 announcement (technical excess).
Microsoft counterattacks September 14 on ground where xAI cannot follow: norms, philosophy, governance — the Code of Conduct.
And on September 21, xAI closes the sequence with a weak move (4.7), like a player who has exhausted his ammunition.
The ten days' tally: xAI won the attention battle (2.5T parameters, Nadella announcing Grok), Microsoft won the substance battle (Transcribe-2 #1, 37-page Code, proven GA and distribution).
Attention dissipates, substance remains: that is the lesson 2026 repeats every quarter.
The media saturation must also be measured: five major events in eighteen days, three in three days (September 12, 13, 14).
At that pace, each announcement cannibalizes the previous one — Grok 4.7, released September 21, got only eight days of attention shared with 4.8.
That may be why Musk downgraded it in advance: in such a saturated month, a modest model could only exist mediacally by being presented as modest.
Finally, September 2026 is the month when the two strategies — independence through models (Microsoft) and independence through capital (xAI) — clashed most directly.
The winner is not designated: but the battleground has changed — it is no longer "who has the best model," it is "who has the best distribution, the best norms, and the strongest balance sheet."

### What 2027 will have to decide: the open questions

2026 laid the foundations; 2027 will have to decide the questions the verified facts leave open — here they are, strictly deduced from the dossier.
Will Grok 4.8 ship, and at what price? 2.5 trillion parameters on an in-house C++ stack: the September 13 announcement is a promise; 2027 must convert it into a product — with a pricing equation the current $2/$6 cannot sustain without a cost breakthrough.
Is the V9 base exhausted? Three post-training upgrades (4.5, 4.6, 4.7) and five delays on the last: if post-training plateaus, 4.8 must be a refounding — and xAI's iterative cycle must reinvent itself.
Will Grok in Copilot pass the preview? The EU/UK/EFTA exclusion and admin opt-in are temporary locks: their lifting — or their retention — will say whether integrating a third-party model into M365 is viable at global regulatory scale.
Will the Code of Conduct survive its consultation? Six weeks of public debate, revised version late 2026 to guide 2027: the final text will say whether Microsoft maintains the frontal rejection of model welfare or softens it — and the Suleyman–Amodei clash will gain or lose intensity accordingly.
Will Cowork 1, the in-house fine-tune, replace Anthropic and GPT-5.5 in Cowork? "Coming" is the only indication: if Microsoft internalizes long-running agents on its own models, that is the end of third-party dependence on the most strategic segment — and a new chapter of the war with Anthropic.
Will the Solara gadgets exist? First devices late 2026/2027, reference designs for Qualcomm and MediaTek, four pilots (AccuWeather, Best Buy, CVS Health, Target): 2027 will tell whether Microsoft's chip-to-cloud produces objects or remains a bodiless platform.
MAI-Transcribe-2's price after December 31? $0.10/hour is an introductory price: the permanent tariff will reveal whether the 3.6× division was durable conquest or temporary subsidy.
Will Colossus v2 deliver on its promises? 550,000 GPUs, "world's largest AI supercomputer": the infrastructure must prove it actually trains 2.5T-parameter models — and at what energy cost.
Is the $1.5B/month burn sustainable? Backed by the SpaceX–xAI merger ($1.25 trillion) and the IPO ($75B raised), yes short-term: but 2027 must show a path to balance, or to permanent raises.
Finally, the question of questions: will the Microsoft–xAI alliance survive the Musk–Microsoft rivalry? Grok in Copilot is business, not friendship: the day interests diverge — on prices, data, governance — the integration will be the first hostage.
2026 was the year of foundations; 2027 will be the year of truth tests.
Each of these questions has a binary answer, and each will redraw the landscape according to the answer.

### The economics of models in 2026: the great deflation

An economic thread runs through all of 2026: models become better and cheaper at the same time — a deflation of unprecedented violence.
On xAI's side: three successive versions (4.5, 4.6, 4.7) at the same $2/$6 price, with claimed rising capabilities ("Opus-class," Bedrock distribution, GitHub Copilot).
The customer pays the same and gets better: that is the definition of a real price cut, even if the nominal price does not move.
On Microsoft's side: MAI-Transcribe-2 at $0.10/hour on promo through December 31 versus $0.36 for the 1.5 — a 3.6× division of the face price, for a model better on all fronts (60 languages versus 43, #1 on FLEURS, 10× faster than GPT-Transcribe).
On Cowork's side: long-running agents billed per credit at $0.01 — cent granularity, making each task's cost legible and predictable.
This deflation has three engines, all visible in the facts.
First engine: compute scale — Colossus v2 (550,000 GPUs) and cloud infrastructures lower the marginal cost of inference.
Second engine: architectural efficiency — 35B-active MoE (MAI-Thinking-1), light Flash models (5B for Code-1-Flash), in-house C++ stack (Grok 4.8): doing more with less compute per request.
Third engine: competition — when xAI freezes its prices and Microsoft divides its own by 3.6, nobody can afford to stay expensive without crushing justification.
But this deflation is funded: xAI burns $1.5B/month, and the slashed prices ($2/$6, $0.10/hour on promo) are conquest prices, not equilibrium prices.
The question 2026 does not resolve — and 2027 must decide — is sustainability: will costs fall fast enough to meet prices, or must prices rise back toward costs?
Technology history suggests the first outcome long-term, but the short term belongs to subsidies.
For enterprise buyers, the lesson is double: enjoy low prices to build, but do not size your business cases on promotional tariffs (the Transcribe-2 promo stops December 31, and xAI's $2/$6 is backed by a $1.5B monthly burn).
For labs, the lesson is harsher: in a deflationary market, only those survive who have scale (Colossus v2), efficiency (MoE, Flash, in-house stack), or the balance sheet (SpaceX–xAI merger, Microsoft treasury).
The others must nest in defensible premium segments — or disappear.
2026 is the year AI became a commodity in price while remaining a marvel in performance: that is exactly what happens to technologies that win.
