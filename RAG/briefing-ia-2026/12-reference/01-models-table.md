**Notes on reading the table.**

- The em dash "—" means "not disclosed": it implies neither the absence of the data nor its value.
- The "promo" prices (Gemini 3.8 Flash through end of 2026, MAI-Transcribe-2 through 12/31) are temporary by design.
- Per-minute pricing (GPT-Live-1, Gemini 3.8 Live) and per-audio-hour pricing (MAI-Transcribe-2) are not comparable with per-million-token prices.
- "Announced on" (Grok 4.8) is not equivalent to "released on": announced specifications remain to be verified.
- Total parameter counts of MoE models (up to 2.8T) cannot be compared with dense models: only active parameters determine per-token cost.
- Fugu Max and Fugu Ultra v2.0 are orchestrators (learned routers), not foundation models: their prices cover a routing service.
- The one-million-token context is the most common standard in the table (10 models), followed by 500K (Grok 4.5, 4.6).
- Models with no disclosed price (16 of 37) are not necessarily free: pricing may be reserved for enterprise clients or not yet set.
- "Month only" dates (Opus 4.6, Opus 4.8, Sonnet 5, DeepSeek V4-Flash-0731, Kimi K3, DeepSeek V4-Pro-0813, Muse Glimmer) are less precise than day dates.
- Any reuse of the table must be dated: prices and availability change fast.
- Prices are given excluding taxes and volume discounts: a large client's actual invoice may differ significantly from the public rate.
- The same model may appear under several commercial names depending on the channel (direct API, Copilot, partnership): verify the model's identity before any price comparison, as with Grok 4.1 Fast in Copilot Studio preview.
- Announced contexts are theoretical maximums: the quality of exploitation over long windows (recall fidelity) varies by model and is not measured here.

**Family-by-family analysis.**

**OpenAI.** The GPT-5.6 trio (Sol, Terra, Luna, released July 9) segments the offering by price: $5/$30 per million tokens for Sol, $2.50/$15 for Terra, $1/$6 for Luna — a premium/mid-range/economy range covering several inference budgets. GPT-Live-1 (July 8) follows a per-minute pricing model ($0.05/min), suited to real-time uses like voice. GPT-6 Astra (September 3) marks a new generation at $10/$50 per M, the high-end price tier already seen on Fable 5 / Mythos 5 (June 9, $10/$50 per M, 1M context). No parameter counts are disclosed for these models, which remains the norm at OpenAI since GPT-4: performance is judged on benchmarks and context, not on stated size.

The Sol/Terra/Luna segmentation resembles a car lineup: a single technological platform offered at three price tiers, a strategy unprecedented at this scale in the table.

**Anthropic.** The Claude family covers 2026 with a rapid cadence: Opus 4.6 (04/2026) and 4.7 (April 16) close together, then Opus 4.8 (05/2026, $5/$25 per M, 1M context), Sonnet 5 (06/2026, $2/$10 per M, 1M context) and Opus 5 (July 24, $5/$25 per M). The one-million-token context has become the house standard, and pricing stays readable: $5/$25 for high-end Opus, $2/$10 for Sonnet. Here too, no parameters are published. The rapid succession of versions 4.6 → 4.7 → 4.8 illustrates a strategy of incremental iterations rather than annual ruptures.

No price is missing on the three priced models: the house says little about model sizes but a lot about prices — the opposite of Microsoft.

**Google.** The Gemini lineup remains centered on the performance/price ratio: Gemini 3.5 Flash (May 19, $1.50/$9 per M) then Gemini 3.8 Flash (September 2, $0.75/$3.75 per M on promotion through end of 2026), with a one-million-token context in both cases. The price drop between the two Flash generations (roughly halved) illustrates the rapid deflation of inference costs. Gemini 3.8 Live (September 15) introduces per-minute audio pricing ($0.005/$0.018 per audio minute), consistent with the rise of voice interfaces.

The halving of the price between Gemini 3.5 and 3.8 Flash in four months captures the scale of the deflation: for equal service, inference cost was cut in half.

**Microsoft.** The MAI series launched June 2 shows a complete multimodal strategy: reasoning (MAI-Thinking-1, 35B active parameters as MoE, 256K context), code (MAI-Code-1-Flash, 5B parameters), image (MAI-Image-2.5), voice (MAI-Voice-2) and transcription (MAI-Transcribe-1.5, 43 languages, then MAI-Transcribe-2 on September 3 at $0.10/audio hour on promo through 12/31). It is the only family in the table to publish architectures openly (MoE, small dense models), reflecting an efficiency positioning.

It is also the only family whose single launch day covers five modalities: the catalog reads as a multimodal platform rather than a succession of isolated models. Muse Spark 1.3 (September 2) and Muse Glimmer (08/2026, 30B parameters) complete the catalog with no disclosed price.

**xAI.** The Grok line advances by monthly iterations: Grok 4.5 (July 8), 4.6 (August 12), 4.7 (September 21, after five delays), all at $2/$6 per M with 500K context, plus Grok 4.1 Fast (February 19) with no price and Grok 4.8 (announced September 13, 2.5T parameters, C++ stack). Stable pricing at $2/$6 per M makes Grok an aggressive mid-range option, while the announced 2.5T parameters of Grok 4.8 place it among the largest models in the table. The rapid release cadence comes with some schedule instability, visible in the Grok 4.7 delays.

Keeping a single $2/$6 price across three generations suggests differentiation is played on capabilities, not on price.

**Chinese.** Chinese laboratories dominate transparency on MoE architectures: DeepSeek V4-Flash-0731 (07/2026, 284B total of which 13B active, $0.14/$0.27 per M), DeepSeek V4-Pro-0813 (08/2026, 1.6T total of which 49B active, 1M context), DeepSeek V4.1 Flash (September 10, 552B total of which 8–16B active, 1M context), Kimi K3 (07/2026, 2.8T MoE, $3/$15 per M, 1M context), Step 5 Preview (September 20, 600B total of which 27B active, $1/$2.70 per M, 1M context) and GLM-5.3 (August 14, no published figures). The contrast between total parameters (up to 2.8T) and active parameters (8B to 49B) shows the MoE logic: lots of stored capacity, little compute per token. DeepSeek remains the price-cutting champion ($0.14/$0.27 per M).

Every quantified model in this family is an MoE: architectural transparency is a commercial argument there as much as a technical one.

**European (and others).** The table holds few European foundation models: Command A+ (May 20, 218B total of which 25B active, 128K context, no price) is the work of Cohere, a Canadian laboratory, and the two Fugu offerings (Max at $2/$6 per M, Ultra v2.0 at $5/$30 per M, launched September 11) are explicitly orchestrators — learned routers — and not foundation models. This segment illustrates two trends: on the one hand the emergence of an orchestration layer that selects the most suitable model per query, on the other the relative discretion of European labs in this table of 2026 releases, which remains dominated by American and Chinese actors.

The table lists no European foundation model: that absence is itself a data point, to be cross-checked with other sources before drawing conclusions from it.

**Highlights by family (derived from the table).**

- **OpenAI:** the most segmented price range in the table, from $1/M input (Luna) to $10/M (Astra); the only actor with general-purpose per-minute pricing (GPT-Live-1, $0.05/min); no published parameter counts.
- **Anthropic:** five releases in four months (April to July); one-million-token context wherever disclosed; stable, readable pricing ($5/$25 for Opus, $2/$10 for Sonnet).
- **Google:** Flash price halved between 3.5 and 3.8; time-limited promotion (end of 2026); per-minute audio pricing with Gemini 3.8 Live.
- **Microsoft:** the only complete multimodal family launched on a single day (June 2: reasoning, code, image, voice, transcription); the only one to openly publish model sizes (5B, 30B, 35B active); transcription announced across 43 languages.
- **xAI:** a single $2/$6 per M price maintained from Grok 4.5 to Grok 4.7; five delays before the September 21 release of Grok 4.7; Grok 4.8 announced at 2.5T parameters.
- **Chinese:** six models, all MoE where the architecture is quantified; active parameters accounting for roughly 1.5% to 11% of the total; the widest price range, from the cheapest in the table (DeepSeek V4-Flash-0731 at $0.14/$0.27) to $3/$15 (Kimi K3).
- **European and others:** a single non-American Western foundation model in the table (Command A+, Canadian); Fugu illustrates the emergence of an orchestration layer (learned routers) distinct from foundation models.
