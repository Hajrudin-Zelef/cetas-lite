# The compute deals: Anthropic, OpenAI and the Helios ecosystem

<a id="s07-7"></a>
### 7.6 The compute deals: Anthropic, OpenAI, and the Helios ecosystem

On July 22, 2026, the day before the Advancing AI event, AMD announced its partnership with Anthropic.
The lab commits to up to 2 GW of MI450-series deployed in Helios racks.
The first gigawatt is expected as early as the first half of 2027, according to available sources.
It is a contract of unprecedented scale for AMD, which changes its status in the industry.
Two gigawatts is the equivalent of a large city's electricity consumption, dedicated to AI.
The announcement, placed just before the event, set the tone for the week.

The collaboration is not limited to hardware purchase: it is multi-year and technical.
Claude, Anthropic's model, will be used to optimize ROCm, AMD's software stack.
It is a dizzying idea: AI improving the tools used to run AI.
This virtuous circle could accelerate AMD's software progress, its historic weak point against the leader.
For in AI compute, software counts as much as silicon, if not more.
A software stack optimized by the best models becomes a differentiating asset.
Anthropic thus brings AMD far more than orders: decisive expertise.

The financial side of the agreement is just as significant.
AMD is investing up to $5 billion in Anthropic, according to available sources.
It is no longer a simple customer-supplier relationship, it is a capitalistic partnership.
By taking a stake, AMD aligns its interests with the lab's over the long term.
It is also a signal sent to the market: AMD believes in Anthropic enough to put billions into it.
These cross-arrangements, where the chip seller invests in the buyer, are becoming the norm in 2026.
They lock in relationships and make partnerships quasi-irreversible.

On OpenAI's side, the agreement with AMD takes a different but equally massive form.
OpenAI holds warrants allowing it to go up to 10% of AMD's capital.
The agreement covers 6 GW, a volume beyond anything seen until then.
GPT workloads have already been running on Helios racks for three months.
And a "massive scale" deployment is planned for the end of 2026.
Each element of this announcement deserves attention, so unprecedented is the scale.

The warrants up to 10% of capital are a remarkable deal structure.
They give OpenAI a potentially major stake in its supplier.
It is a way to align interests without immediately disbursing billions in cash.
If AMD succeeds in its bet, OpenAI will capture part of the value.
If AMD fails, the warrants will be worth nothing, and OpenAI will have lost nothing.
It is an elegant instrument, which turns a customer into a quasi-shareholder.
And it is a spectacular vote of confidence in AMD's trajectory.

The fact that GPT workloads have been running on Helios for three months is perhaps the most important information.
It means the agreement is not a promise, but a reality already in production.
Three months of operation is the time needed to validate a system at large scale.
Teething problems, if any, had time to appear and be treated.
The "massive scale" deployment planned for end of 2026 therefore rests on a proven base.
It is the difference between an announcement and a validation: OpenAI tested before committing massively.
For AMD, it is the best publicity there is.

Meta, for its part, was present on the stage of the July 23 event.
Meta executives went on stage, lending their endorsement to AMD's offensive.
Meta is among the adopters of Helios racks, alongside OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, and Oracle.
This adopter list is impressive in its diversity: labs and hyperscalers rub shoulders there.
It shows Helios is not one customer's toy, but a general-purpose platform.
For a hesitant buyer, seeing these names aligned is a decisive argument.
It is the network effect starting to play in AMD's favor.

Taken together, these deals sketch a new geography of compute.
AI labs secure gigawatts from AMD as one secures deposits.
The amounts at stake, 2 GW here, 6 GW there, are dizzying and show the scale of demand.
The financial structures, investments and warrants, durably bind the partners.
We are witnessing the formation of capitalistic alliances around compute, as there were around oil.
Compute has become a strategic resource negotiated as such.
And AMD, yesterday's outsider, is today at the center of these dealings.

These announcements also validate the supply-diversification thesis.
None of the big players wants to depend on a single supplier, however dominant.
By signing with AMD, OpenAI, Anthropic, and Meta give themselves a credible second source.
It is insurance against disruptions, price hikes, and geopolitical tensions.
For AMD, each signature reinforces the next, in a virtuous circle.
The risk for Nvidia is not losing its leadership overnight.
It is seeing built, contract after contract, a viable alternative to its ecosystem.

The timeline drawn by these announcements makes 2027 a pivotal year.
The first gigawatt for Anthropic is expected as early as the first half of 2027.
The "massive scale" deployment for OpenAI is planned for the end of 2026.
In other words, the next six months will see these contracts move from paper to concrete.
It is in this window that AMD's industrial credibility will be played out.
Delivering gigawatts on time is a logistics challenge of unprecedented scale for the foundry.
The investors who took AMD past the trillion on September 21 are betting precisely on this execution.
2027 will be the year of truth, and everyone knows it.

This concentration of mega-contracts also carries a risk for AMD.
When a significant share of growth rests on a handful of customers, dependence is strong.
A delay, a strategy change, or a renegotiation can have disproportionate effects.
It is the flip side of gigawatt deals: they are structuring, in both directions.
AMD will therefore have to diversify its customer base beyond the names announced in July.
The adopter list, which includes Microsoft and Oracle, shows this work is underway.
But vigilance remains warranted: concentration is a strength as long as it lasts, a weakness if it turns.

Using Claude to optimize ROCm is perhaps the subtlest bet of the Anthropic agreement.
ROCm, AMD's software stack, has long been considered its Achilles' heel.
By entrusting it to optimization by Anthropic's models, AMD attempts an unprecedented shortcut.
If AI can accelerate software development, the historic lag could close faster than expected.
It is a bet on the idea that software, like the rest, now obeys scaling laws.
And it is a signal to developers: the AMD stack will progress fast.
In an industry where software makes the difference, it is a weighty argument.

Meta executives' presence on the stage of the July 23 event was not decorative.
In the semiconductor industry, going on a supplier's stage is a political act.
It means: we publicly validate your roadmap and commit to it.
For AMD, it was the endorsement of one of the world's most demanding buyers.
For Meta, it was a way to pressure its other suppliers by displaying an alternative.
This stage diplomacy is a well-rehearsed ritual of the industry.
And in 2026, it played out around the Helios racks.
