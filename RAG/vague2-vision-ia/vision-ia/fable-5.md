## Full summary

This June 10, 2026 edition of the Vision-IA newsletter is led by Anthropic unveiling two new models, Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5, both surpassing the Opus generation. The standout: Fable 5 completed a code migration at Stripe in a single day, where engineers had estimated two months. Mythos 5 autonomously designs drug candidates, to the point that Anthropic chose to restrict it for cybersecurity reasons. The newsletter frames this as a shift from assistive tool to genuine autonomous collaborator, and notes that the maker voluntarily capping its own model shows the frontier is no longer raw power but what we allow the machine to do.

A widely discussed essay on One Useful Thing recounts hours spent with Claude 5 Fable: a technological leap surpassing all public models tested, yet an ambivalent experience—"delightful" but "unnerving." It handles hours-long tasks (sophisticated academic articles, poetry, complete interactive games) with very little instruction, raising questions about our relationship with AI changing in nature, not just degree.

Google DeepMind launched Gemini 3.5 Live Translate, near-instant voice translation preserving tone and naturalness, integrated into Google AI Studio, Google Translate, and Google Meet. Google also slashed its entry-level AI subscription price, pressuring OpenAI and Anthropic in a price war aimed at capturing subscribers before profitability. The "Research" section covers Anthropic showing its agents excel at code but struggle in biology because biological databases are designed for humans, not agents (same Ebola query returning inconsistent results: 266, then 106, then 5); Microsoft launching seven proprietary models (including a general reasoning model) to become OpenAI's direct rival after ending the exclusive partnership in April 2026; MIT Technology Review anticipating a 300% rise in autonomous agent adoption in two years (30-50% productivity gains, 86% of HR leaders seeing it as core to their future role—sponsored content); ReasonAlloc (training-free hierarchical KV-cache allocation improving long-chain reasoning, tested on MATH-500 and AIME 2024); and 1X opening a "World Model Lab" for its NEO humanoid robot, led by ex-Luma AI's Samarth Sinha.

Additional briefs include OpenAI abandoning full AI-research automation (Altman and Pachocki advocating a human-machine "tandem" and an international body able to slow frontier models); a judge cancelling a trial after both sides used AI without authorization; a German court holding Google liable for false AI Overviews ("its own words"); CrowdStrike reporting Chinese state actors behind 50%+ of cyberattacks on tech firms' AI assets; China's $295B AI plan requiring 80% domestic chips over five years; JPMorgan deploying longer-lived autonomous agents in 2026; the Pentagon listing Alibaba and Baidu as Chinese military companies; Google DeepMind boosting European robotics; SpaceX's orbital data center ambition (one satellite with a Nvidia GB300 rack's power; Google estimating ~10,000 tightly coupled satellites for real training); and GM's vehicle-to-grid plus sodium-ion storage.
