# Anthropic a passé la semaine à essayer de convaincre Trump

## Full summary

This June 18, 2026 edition of the Vision-IA newsletter leads with Anthropic spending nearly the entire week fighting to bring its models back online. A Trump administration order required cutting access to all foreign nationals—including those on US soil and even Anthropic's own employees. Unable to filter finely, Anthropic had to block Fable 5 and Mythos 5 for absolutely everyone. According to The Verge, this is the first time US export controls have been used to restrict access to an AI model this way; an expert quoted says "To my knowledge, this is the first time US export controls have been used to control access to an AI model in this way." The government invoked "national security authorities" without detailing the legal basis. The story was covered by The Verge, Ars Technica, Wired, and Natural 20 AI; Anthropic published its own statement. The newsletter frames this as the first time an AI model is treated as a strategic technology subject to the same regime as weaponry or advanced semiconductors, forcing every US lab to rethink who can technically access its models.

The edition also covers Noam Shazeer, VP of Engineering at Google and co-lead of Gemini, leaving for OpenAI. Co-author of the foundational "Attention Is All You Need" paper (the Transformers), he had only returned to Google in 2024 via a $2.7 billion Character.AI deal. It's the year's second major recruitment earthquake after Karpathy joined Anthropic, confirmed by CNBC Tech, The Decoder, and Hacker News. The G7, meeting in Évian, France, devoted a full working lunch to AI, with the CEOs of OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic at the table alongside heads of state for the first time, discussing "safe, fast, and effective" deployment—eclipsed by Washington's decision to block foreign access to its most powerful models. Anthropic improved interoperability between its design assistant and Claude Code for a smoother design-to-code flow.

The "Research" section covers OpenAI researchers proposing a method to estimate a model's failure rate after deployment, filling blind spots in classic safety tests; Google's AMIE matching primary-care physicians in managing complex conditions (not just one-off diagnosis), with a study in Nature; a Microsoft researcher building a functional neural network from goats, bridges, and ice ramps in the Age of Empires II map editor—and analyzing 315 papers showing more than half assume LLMs have human traits before running experiments; Amazon predicting commercially useful quantum computing in 5-7 years (with a caution about optimistic quantum timelines); and Pew reporting only 16% of Americans expect a positive AI impact.

Additional briefs include Microsoft selling OpenAI models in China (Bloomberg), which OpenAI and Anthropic refuse to do—ByteDance reportedly spending over $1B/year via Microsoft, with Azure AI China revenue tripling annually, raising distillation risk and OpenAI-Microsoft tensions; OpenAI acquiring Ona for persistent cloud workspaces for Codex (5M weekly users, +400%); Kimi K2.7 Code's HighSpeed mode (~6x faster, ~180 tokens/sec); Nvidia/CMU/Berkeley using coding agents to teach robots fine grasping (eight-robot fleet reaching up to 99% success); Midjourney unveiling The Midjourney Scanner, a full-body ultrasound (MRI-comparable ambition, clinical validation pending); Google's $99.99 Gemini-powered Home Speaker; Tensordyne's Napier inference rack claiming 13x the throughput of Nvidia's NVL72 GB300 (logarithmic-space compute); Shenzhen VR teleoperation of humanoids at IO-AI Tech; Odyssey's $1.45B valuation for world models; and Framer launching AI agents for site building (used by Miro and Perplexity teams).
