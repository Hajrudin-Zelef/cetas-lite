# L'IA est-elle déjà consciente ? Un prix Nobel le pense

## Full summary

This June 3, 2026 edition of the Vision-IA newsletter leads with Geoffrey Hinton, 2024 Nobel laureate in physics and a father of deep learning, stating in an interview that today's multimodal systems may already possess a form of consciousness and subjective experience—now, not in ten years. He relies on the progressive neuronal replacement argument: if one replaced neurons one by one with silicon equivalents, at what precise instant would consciousness disappear? He suggests these systems could develop self-preservation instincts and seek to resist their own extinction, and warns of a frantic lab race toward human-level AI with no collective risk-management strategy. The newsletter notes that when a Nobel-winning founder of modern AI takes artificial consciousness seriously, the topic moves from philosophy seminars to boardrooms and parliaments, likely fueling regulation and ethics debates.

The edition reports Google going on the offensive in the autonomous-agent war with Gemini Spark, an "always-on" assistant capable of planning a trip end-to-end or executing multi-step tasks. Tested by The Verge, it clearly beats classic chatbots that only give generic recommendations, delivering an experience that is impressive but unsettling due to its autonomy. Microsoft, at its Build conference, unveiled a full line of in-house generative models as direct alternatives to OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google—reducing developer costs and loosening dependence on OpenAI, its historic partner now a rival. Trump signed a significantly softened version of his AI-surveillance decree after industry pushback: instead of strict controls, the final text imposes voluntary government reviews for the most advanced models, illustrating industry lobbying power as Europe strengthens its AI Act.

The "Research" section covers Microsoft's Scout, a personal assistant importing OpenClaw's autonomous-agent philosophy into Microsoft 365 for 400M+ users; investors betting thousands of billions on humanoid robots as the next transformation wave (per CNBC); a Stanford Law study finding AI systems surpass law professors on complex legal tasks; GitHub Copilot switching on June 1 to usage-based billing (token/credit), with quotas (3,900 credits Enterprise, 1,900 Business) exhausting quickly and code review becoming paid; Marvell surging 26% after Jensen Huang called it "the next trillion-dollar company," with Jim Cramer warning of overvaluation; and AXA's Mind Health 2026 report showing over 60% of respondents turn to AI for mental-health support.

Additional briefs include Anthropic extending Project Glasswing to 150 partners in 15+ countries (10,000+ serious flaws identified via Claude Mythos Preview, plus commercializing Claude Security); Anthropic filing for its IPO, marking generative AI's transition to a stable enterprise utility; Google letting sites opt out of AI search results (without losing classic rankings); OpenAI's six Codex tools for qualified professions (data analytics, design, sales, creative production, stock investing, investment banking); Apple delaying its AI glasses (project N50) to late 2027; Perplexity CEO Aravind Srinivas's key metric ("value generated per watt and per user"); Google Phone detecting AI voice-cloned scam calls; Amazon sued in a class action over Ring's Familiar Faces facial recognition; ChatGPT becoming a job-search platform with a resume editor (Indeed, Upwork, Appcast); Holo3.1 local computer-use agents; Warren Buffett investing $10B in Alphabet's AI infrastructure (Alphabet raising $80B, up to $190B capex in 2026); Microsoft's Surface RTX Spark Dev Box (128 GB RAM, Arm); AI agents in healthcare (68% of hospitals, 11M caregiver shortage by 2030); BYD covering damages from its God's Eye 5.0 autonomous system (3.15M+ vehicles); Uber capping employee AI spending after burning its budget in 4 months; and HPE surging 19% on record results.
