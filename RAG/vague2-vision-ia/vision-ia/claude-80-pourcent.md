## Full summary

This June 8, 2026 edition of the Vision-IA newsletter leads with Anthropic revealing that over 80% of the production code merged into its own systems is now written by Claude itself. The AI assistant has become the primary developer of its own house, and the code is not merely suggested—it goes directly to production. This symbolic threshold shows the maturity of coding models: when the company designing the model entrusts it with most of its own development, it is the most credible signal of the tool's reliability. For developers, the message is that AI doesn't replace the engineer yet but redefines the role toward supervision, review, and architecture rather than typing every line; the "not reliable enough" argument becomes hard to sustain.

The edition reports that DeepSeek became the most adopted AI provider among US enterprises in Ramp's June 2026 barometer, ahead of OpenAI and Anthropic, driven by price—though Ramp's chief economist Ara Kharazian warns about security risks of Chinese models, leaving US IT departments arbitrating between immediate savings and data sovereignty. Tech and AI stocks fell sharply (SoftBank -6%, Broadcom leading declines, Samsung hit) as investors took profits and reassessed risk after months of gains. The Financial Times reported OpenAI preparing a major ChatGPT overhaul in coming weeks. The "Research" section covers Claude Opus 4.7 rivaling specialized NMR chemistry software and reverse-engineering molecular structures from spectra (a task usually reserved for human chemists); a humorous paper using reductio ad absurdum to critique anthropomorphism applied to LLMs (arguing that if LLMs have human attributes, so does Age of Empires II); DeepSeek V4 Pro surpassing OpenAI's GPT-5.5 Pro on a precision benchmark; and a "training-free" method applying diffusion models to a single image with fewer compute resources (for image editing, inpainting, super-resolution).

Additional briefs include ChatGPT's Lockdown Mode disabling web access, Deep Research, and Agent Mode to limit data exfiltration (blocking only the last step of an attack chain, not the root problem); Perplexity's "Search as Code," letting models write search routines in Python rather than calling rigid APIs (up to 85% fewer token costs and better performance than OpenAI/Anthropic on key benchmarks, with sandboxed filtering/deduplication); Anthropic hiring Clive Chan, the second employee of OpenAI's custom chip program (ex-Tesla Autopilot, OpenAI-Broadcom), as it considers designing its own AI chips amid the IPO race; TechCrunch anticipating an AI price hike ("tokenpocalypse") before the IPOs; a Unitree G1 humanoid kicking a child during a Chinese public demo (not seriously injured), reviving public-space safety debates; the community requesting an official Linux Claude Desktop (489 HN votes); AI creators becoming hard to spot (virtual avatars like Lil Miquela, Imma, Shudu Gram); a Claude Chrome extension automating job applications; and San Diego State University installing 1,300 AI cameras (330 in dormitories) without student consent, raising mass-surveillance concerns.
