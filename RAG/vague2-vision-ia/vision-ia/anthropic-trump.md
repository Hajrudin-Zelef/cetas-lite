## Full summary

This June 17, 2026 edition of the Vision-IA newsletter leads with a paradox: while Anthropic accumulates friction with the Trump administration, its enterprise adoption keeps growing. Data from Ramp suggests the political standoff may actually benefit Anthropic commercially. The latest clash concerns government crackdown on Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5, targeted by Washington for their "sensitive" capabilities. Two sources (TechCrunch, The Rundown AI) confirm the dynamic. The newsletter argues that being in the government's crosshairs can enhance a lab's image of independence—many decision-makers see a provider that doesn't bow to pressure as an asset when model choice involves strategic data. For Anthropic, confrontation with Trump becomes a reputational asset as much as a risk.

The edition reports that Genesis AI (a French startup backed by Eric Schmidt) unveiled Eno, a humanoid robot that intentionally does not resemble a human: no head, no legs, a wheeled mobile base, folding like a beach chair. Its mantra: "humanoid robots don't need to look like humans." It's designed around human capabilities, not appearance, as a generalist robot; its only anatomical concession is hands designed to match the human hand's form and function. France dropped Palantir for a national champion, ChapsVision (per The Guardian), a sovereignty move echoing the Netherlands' GPT-NL announced the same day. The newsletter explains "Loop Engineering" as the next AI buzzword after Prompt, Context, and Harness—Peter (OpenClaw) and Boris (Claude Code) say they no longer write prompts, and Addy Osmani (Google) coined the term; the deeper trend is that AI skill half-life is collapsing.

The "Research" section covers Google claiming the video-generation crown with Gemini Omni Flash (#1 on the Arena leaderboard in both text-to-video and image-to-video); Z AI's GLM-5.2, an open-weights MIT model rivaling best closed systems on code benchmarks, with a 1M-token context window and two effort modes (High, Max)—the closest an open model has come to the top; VoiceDraw, generating architecture diagrams from voice (43 HN points); and MIT Technology Review's six reports on military AI use in decision-making.

Additional briefs include Wired noting that dangerous AI models (with advanced hacking capabilities) will soon be the norm, so the question is how to contain proliferation; Qualcomm's Snapdragon Reality Elite chip (+60% GPU performance for XR, powering Aura glasses); Google DeepMind partnering with the UK government on an AI urban-planning prototype; Databricks growth above 80% ($6.9B annualized revenue) with margins under pressure; Apple reportedly testing camera-equipped AirPods for late 2027 (plus a foldable iPhone); ChatGPT falling below 50% market share for the first time (1.1B monthly users; Gemini 662M, Claude 245M); a Berlin court ruling Google's AI Overviews are not original content (contrasting a stricter Munich decision); Intel starting 18A-P production and nearing an Apple deal; a Claude outage (186 HN points); DeepSeek V4 Pro offering Claude-level performance for 5% of the price; Android 17 with Gemini everywhere; Microsoft moving Copilot Cowork to usage-based billing (considering DeepSeek V4 as a cheap option); China pushing its AI-security vision without the G7; Snap's lighter $2,195 AR glasses; GPT-NL in the Netherlands; AI elder-care at home; Robinhood cutting 10% without blaming AI; and a post on AI amplifying the Dunning-Kruger effect.
