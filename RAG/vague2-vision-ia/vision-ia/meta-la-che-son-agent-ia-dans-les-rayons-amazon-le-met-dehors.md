## Full summary

This September 22, 2026 issue leads with Amazon blocking Meta's AI agent Muse from its site. During the night of Sunday, Amazon cut Muse's access after Meta deployed the agent on Amazon.com without any prior agreement. Muse, launched September 8, lets users write via WhatsApp and have it navigate on their behalf to fill a cart, draft an email, book an appointment, or negotiate a price. Amazon asked Meta to remove the bot; Meta refused, and users now see a warning that access via an unauthorized AI agent violates the site's terms of service. Amazon cites two grievances: the agent does not declare itself as AI while browsing, and it would capture and store customer data, creating a security risk. Meta counters that Muse has no access to passwords or payment methods. Muse became the most-downloaded free app on the US App Store within a week. Amazon has already excluded shopping agents from Perplexity, Google, and OpenAI, and is suing Perplexity over its Comet browser. Paradoxically, the two groups remain partners under a $1B cloud-chips contract signed in April. The core issue is commercial: who controls the customer relationship when software buys for you? Agents that compare, filter, and negotiate bypass storefronts, sponsored recommendations, and advertising—a huge share of Amazon's revenue. Expect major merchants to filter AI agents as methodically as they filter indexing robots, with access negotiated contract by contract.

Alibaba unveiled the Zhenwu V900 AI accelerator at its Apsara conference in Hangzhou, claiming three times the performance of the May-released Zhenwu M890, and pledged over 20 GW of data center capacity by 2032 (stock up ~3% in Hong Kong). The chip has 216 GB memory per card, 1,200 GB/s chip-to-chip interconnect, native FP8 and FP4 support, and is designed for clusters of up to 500,000 cards in the "Apsara Supernode" stack. Mass production and commercialization are set for Q1 2027; current Zhenwu chips already serve 650+ clients in automotive, finance, energy, and industry. Alibaba says Qwen 4 is training, Qwen 4.5 and Qwen 5 are planned, and it targets 5,000–10,000 billion-parameter models.

SpaceXAI launched Grok 4.7 (text-only, same price/speed as Grok 4.6: $2 input / $6 output per M tokens, ~2,100B parameters). Home benchmarks are strong, but independent Artificial Analysis measures an Intelligence Index of 46 versus 53 for Claude Fable 5.1 and GPT-6 Astra, and only 26% on Terminal-Bench 4.0 versus 60% for GPT-6 Astra and 55% for Fable 5.1. Other stories: Apple pays $250M to settle a class action over undelivered AI Siri (25 dollars per eligible iPhone); Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6-Pro tops open-weights rankings (46 on Intelligence Index, MIT license); TypeSafe's Jev decision model is manipulable; Microsoft open-sources RetroChimera for retrosynthesis; and more research/industry briefs (robot-brain "ChatGPT moment" in 2027, French abyssal drones, Amazon antibody design, etc.).

## Key points

- Amazon cut Meta's Muse agent from its site for operating without agreement, not self-identifying as AI, and allegedly storing customer data.
- Meta refused to remove Muse; the dispute is fundamentally about who controls the customer relationship in agentic commerce.
- Alibaba unveiled the Zhenwu V900 accelerator (3x its predecessor) and targets 5,000–10,000B-parameter Qwen models.
- Grok 4.7 launched at unchanged pricing but independent benchmarks fall well short of its marketing claims.
- Apple will pay $250M to settle a class action over the never-delivered AI Siri.
- Xiaomi's MiMo-V2.6-Pro is the best open-weights model under an MIT license.
