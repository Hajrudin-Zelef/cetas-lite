# Pourquoi SpaceX rachète-t-il Cursor pour 60 milliards de dollars ?

## Full summary

This June 16, 2026 edition of the Vision-IA newsletter leads with a surprise acquisition: SpaceX announced it is buying Cursor, the AI-powered code editor beloved by developers, for $60 billion entirely in SpaceX stock—just days after Cursor's own blockbuster IPO. The stated motive is to strengthen SpaceX's AI division, described as struggling, with SpaceX claiming to target a $26 trillion addressable AI market. The story led TechCrunch, The Verge, and Hacker News. The newsletter questions whether a struggling AI division can truly be fixed with tens of billions, or whether this is mainly buying time against fast-moving rivals.

The edition also reports Anthropic being sued over Claude Max usage limits, targeting the commercial conditions and service restrictions imposed on its premium subscribers—the most intensive, highest-paying users. This reflects the broader friction over usage caps in AI subscriptions, where providers juggle shifting quotas to control compute costs; a win for plaintiffs could clarify what customers actually buy. OpenAI spent $34 billion over the past year (up sharply), mainly on infrastructure, compute, and model development, underscoring the industry's cash-burning nature and fueling bubble debate. Facebook launched "AI Mode," a search experience returning generated answers from public Meta posts (not just links), with follow-up questions; it appears alongside "People" and "Marketplace" and raises questions about how users' public content feeds AI.

The "Research" section covers Casey Harrell, paralyzed by ALS, becoming the first long-term "power user" of a brain-computer interface for speech—3,800+ hours over 22 months, largely autonomous, decoded via an algorithm for all 39 English phonemes (published in Nature Medicine). David Sinclair launching human trials of SL-100, an oral drug using chemical reprogramming to reverse aging of the whole body (vs single-organ), targeting the $101M XPrize. Stanford's Digital Economy Lab, using data from 25,000 companies, showing AI has not triggered a general layoff wave despite 40,000 tech layoffs in May 2026 (a two-year record)—AI hits certain profiles and is often a convenient pretext for overstaffed firms. South Korea's record AI optimism: only 16% more worried than enthusiastic, the lowest of 25 countries surveyed by Pew.

Additional briefs include DeepSeek raising 50 billion yuan (~$7.4B) at a $50B valuation, its first external raise; EngineAI's confidential Hong Kong IPO filing and T800 humanoid production (one every 15 minutes, 10,000 units delivering, $1.5B Series B with Luxshare-ICT); The Atlantic revealing millions of songs (Taylor Swift, Bad Bunny) used to train music AI, often unauthorized; cybersecurity experts urging the White House to lift export controls on Anthropic's Fable and Mythos models; the DOJ calling xAI "vital" to national security in the NAACP lawsuit (gas turbine pollution); Big Tech lobbying for a single federal AI regulation; Satya Nadella's "Token Capital" theory (own AI capabilities and private learning loops beat chasing the best model); and a viral fake "AI" run by humans (Mihir Maroju's youraislopbores.me: 25M unique visitors, 280M visits in a month).
