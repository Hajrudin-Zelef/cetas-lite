## Full summary

This August 28, 2026 issue leads with federal judge Rita F. Lin annulling Anthropic's designation as a "supply chain risk," imposed after the company refused to authorize two military uses of Claude. Federal agencies can keep buying and using Claude, and defense suppliers can no longer be forced to cut all commercial ties with Anthropic. The conflict concerned Claude Gov, the government version. Anthropic accepted defense uses but maintained two red lines: mass domestic surveillance and fully autonomous lethal weapons—capable of selecting and striking a target without human validation. The decision annuls Pete Hegseth's February 27 directive and the sanction that took effect March 3, 2026, after five months of proceedings. The judge cited three grounds: retaliation violating the First Amendment, the absence of prior process required by the Fifth Amendment, and a decision deemed "arbitrary and capricious." Agencies are no longer required to abandon Claude; military subcontractors can keep Anthropic among their suppliers. The Pentagon remains free not to buy Claude for its own needs—the decision forbids general exclusion, not requiring the army to become a customer. Anthropic had passed national security checks, per the company's explanation. The government is expected to appeal.

Next, Google DeepMind launched gemini-omni-1.1-flash, a native multimodal Transformer accepting text, images, audio, or video and producing video with sound. Its context window is 1,048,576 tokens, but Google discloses neither parameter count nor independent comparison. It generates a clip, modifies a scene via dialogue, imposes a start/end image, or extends an existing video; it now analyzes the last 10 seconds (versus one before) to preserve characters, decor, and camera movement. Each generation produces a 3–10 second clip at 24 fps; successive 10-second extensions reach 40 seconds total. Drafts in 360p are up to 60% faster and 3x cheaper than 720p renders; the model outputs native 720p with 1080p/4K upscaling. The API costs about $0.10/second in 720p with no free tier, so a 10-second clip costs about $1. It is accessible in AI Studio, the Gemini API, and Flow for AI Plus/Pro/Ultra subscribers; Adobe Firefly, Figma Weave, and Runway plan to integrate it.

128 organizations—including OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Microsoft, AWS, Cisco, and CrowdStrike—signed a letter published August 27 warning that AI-assisted attacks will become much more frequent and sophisticated, with hospitals, water networks, and internet infrastructure as priority targets. It asks governments, vendors, and critical operators to use the current window while defenders still hold an advantage, to fix unpatched software, misconfigurations, excessive permissions, and weak authentication, and to test defenses continuously. The letter contains no financial commitment, timeline, or measurable goal. OpenAI estimates its future Astra model could reach the "Critical" cyber level.

Meta agreed on August 26 in Oakland to modify Facebook and Instagram for under-18s, settling complaints from 48 states, Washington D.C., and territories. Protections activate automatically for at least five years, can only be disabled by a teen with parental consent, and require annual independent audits. Meta admits no fault. Daily use is capped at 2 hours, with blocking between midnight and 6 a.m., notifications muted 10 p.m.–7 a.m. and during school hours, breaks after 60/90 minutes, and a chronological feed option. The amount is $12.1B guaranteed, up to $17.1B over ten years (~$18B per Meta); ~$5.3B depends on TikTok and YouTube adopting comparable protections (neither has committed). Research items cover Hugging Face's $399 Microduck robot, PULSE reconstructing 251 biomarkers from 61 blood tests, a two-drug blood-rejuvenation claim, Claude-associated writing spreading on GitHub (1%→~45% of a corpus), scientific agents solving only 30% of real tasks, a ChatGPT critical-thinking study, Google's double-blind AI evaluation, Photoshop's AI markup, and IBM on AI decision responsibility. Briefs cover Claude Cowork's browser, OpenAI's persistent Codex, Claudeforce/Salesforce, LinkedIn's AI-slop button, Jensen Huang on AGI, Anthropic's Model Hardware Standard, Alphabet's $700B market-cap loss, Nvidia optimizing for Chinese open models, US-China pirate-agent talks, Flock surveillance abuse, a GTA 6 malware scam, Slate Auto, and Nvidia's results and circular investments.
