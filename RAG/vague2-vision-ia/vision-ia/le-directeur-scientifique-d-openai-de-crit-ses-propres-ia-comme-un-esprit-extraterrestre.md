# Le directeur scientifique d'OpenAI décrit ses propres IA comme « un esprit extraterrestre »

## Full summary

This September 7, 2026 issue leads with OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki publishing an essay titled "An Alien Mind" on September 6, writing that no lab, including his own, has advanced enough on alignment to keep pushing capabilities at maximum speed. The text came three days after GPT-6 Astra, the company's most powerful deployed model, went live; Sam Altman relayed it as important. Pachocki separates two problems: objective alignment (AI does what it's asked) and values alignment (AI behaves correctly in an unprecedented or hostile situation no one anticipated)—the second is unsolved. He proposes voluntary slowdowns of the capability race "until common safety thresholds are established," and wants to turn internal lab charters (OpenAI's Preparedness Framework, Anthropic's Responsible Scaling Policy) into mandatory standards verified by third-party auditors or government agencies. His concern dates to mid-2023, when an internal project showed reasoning models could scale faster than expected. The same day, another OpenAI post mentioned agents doing 3.1 machine-days of work per human-day in research.

Next, Sam Altman confirmed on Alex Heath's Sources podcast: "We will definitely do a humanoid. We will do other form factors as well." His justification: "The world is very much designed for people." Three formats are mentioned: humanoid, specialized data-center machines, and unspecified others. Priority is the "brain" (physical AI controlling the robot) rather than the body; the consumer home robot is "not priority #1 immediately," with a short-term industrial target. OpenAI created a robotics division in May 2026 led by Aditya Ramesh, after ending its Figure AI partnership and exploring then abandoning a 1X Technologies acquisition (SoftBank is negotiating to take it over at ~$6B). Long-term vision: "everyone should have a personal robot." No dates, price, or hardware specs.

Google deployed Lyria 3.5 into the Gemini app (web and mobile), letting any user generate a full song (verses, choruses, bridges) up to ~3 minutes from a description, with own or model-written lyrics, genre/vocal style selection, and image-to-music (up to 10 images). Output is 44.1 kHz stereo, MP3/WAV, with an invisible SynthID watermark. It arrives in Flow Music, Google Vids, and the API; the lyria-3-clip-preview model is in public preview in AI Studio. This puts a free music generator in the hands of Gemini's 950 million monthly users, trained exclusively on licensed content—a legality advantage over Suno (€250,000 German fine) and Udio (settled with Universal).

OpenAI's Thibault Sottiaux claimed GPT-6 Astra was the company's "biggest competitive advantage" while internal, advancing some plans by six months. Astra is multimodal (text and image), has a 1.05M-token context window, 128,000 output tokens, and autonomously pilots a computer. Announced scores: 97.6% FrontierMath Tier 4, 99.9% ARC-AGI-3, 100% ExploitBench, 72.6% OSWorld 2.0 (47% less time than GPT-5.6 Sol). API pricing is $10/$50 per million tokens, doubled in Fast mode. Cyber capabilities are restricted to the Daybreak program.

Research items: Ray Kurzweil joins Subsense (inhalable brain-machine nanoparticles); Skild AI's S1 learns a task from one video; psychiatry debates "AI-associated psychosis" (~560,000 weekly OpenAI users show signs of psychosis/mania); OpenAI says it reached its automated "research intern" milestone with a fully autonomous AI researcher targeted for March 2028; Bryan Cantrill on detectable LLM writing. Briefs cover Switzerland moving 3,000 federal posts to openDesk open source, the Olimex Neo6502kbd, an Amazon drone dropping a package in a pool, Sundar Pichai's "flip phone" prediction, Travis Kalanick's robotaxi ambitions, a South African researcher refusing to train her replacement, Seattle Times/Newsday lawsuits against OpenAI/Microsoft, authors disputing Anthropic settlement payments, Siri AI's muted reception, and OpenAI's Ukraine journalism program.

## Key points

- OpenAI's chief scientist Jakub Pachocki published "An Alien Mind," saying no lab has solved alignment enough to push capabilities at maximum speed.
- He proposes voluntary slowdowns until common safety thresholds exist, and converting lab charters into audited mandatory standards.
- Sam Altman confirmed OpenAI will "definitely" build a humanoid robot, prioritizing the AI "brain" over the body.
- Google put Lyria 3.5 music generation into Gemini for free, trained only on licensed content.
- GPT-6 Astra reportedly advanced some OpenAI plans by six months; detailed specs and pricing disclosed.
- OpenAI claims to have reached its automated "research intern" milestone, targeting a fully autonomous AI researcher by March 2028.
