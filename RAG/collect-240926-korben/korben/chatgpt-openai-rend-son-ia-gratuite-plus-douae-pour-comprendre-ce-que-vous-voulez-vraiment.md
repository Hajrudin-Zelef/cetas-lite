# ChatGPT: OpenAI makes its free AI better at understanding what you really want

## Key takeaways AI-generated summary

1. OpenAI has updated GPT-5.5 Instant (the default free model) to better understand the intent behind requests, adapt its responses when you push back, and take geographic context into account for local questions.
2. The improvement includes more natural formatting and fewer endless lists, but it remains an in-house promise from OpenAI to be verified in practice on your everyday questions.
3. You can now view and correct the memories or past conversations that the AI uses to personalize its responses, at no extra cost for all users.

OpenAI has just tweaked ChatGPT's brain. The company has updated GPT-5.5 Instant, the free model that responds by default when you type a question into the app, without you having to activate anything on your end.

The whole point of this update is to better grasp what you really mean. The AI is supposed to understand the intent behind a request, even when poorly worded, and adapt when you push back or challenge its answer, instead of dumbly serving you the same thing again barely reworded.

It also handles geographic context better. If you ask it for a good restaurant or an address, it now takes into account where you are to give you something useful, rather than a generic answer that's valid nowhere in particular.

OpenAI also promises more natural and more coherent formatting, with fewer indigestible walls of text and endless lists. In short, an AI that writes a bit more like a human and a bit less like a spec sheet filled out by the yard.

*Screenshot*

As a reminder, GPT-5.5 Instant has held the role of default model since May. At its launch, OpenAI already announced a big drop in errors, around half as many fabricated claims compared to the previous version, those famous hallucinations where the AI spouts false information with rather disconcerting confidence.

Good news: none of this costs anything. Since it's the free model hooked up by default, the improvement arrives for absolutely everyone, paying subscribers and mere curious visitors who come to ask two or three questions, with no box to tick or option hidden deep in the settings.

OpenAI is also taking the opportunity to give a bit more control over memory. You can now see which memories or past conversations the AI used to personalize a response, and correct or delete what's no longer up to date, so you don't drag around forever some old info that has become false.

One fundamental question remains, hard to verify yourself. Improving context understanding sounds very good on paper, except that these are OpenAI's in-house promises, and it's in practice, on your own everyday questions, that you'll see whether the AI really grasps things better or whether it's mostly PR.

In short, not a revolution, more like a good polish on the AI that hundreds of millions of people use every day without even choosing their model.
