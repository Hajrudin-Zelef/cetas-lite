# An AI chatbot nearly triggered a conflict with China

## Key takeaways AI-generated summary

1. An AI chatbot allegedly wrongly attributed nuclear material to a Chinese ship, without any reliable evidence confirming it, according to testimonies gathered by CNN
2. Armed soldiers were preparing to board and aircraft were already in flight before a verification of the report allowed the operation to be stopped
3. The investigation cites four sources but reveals neither the name of the chatbot nor the true nature of the cargo, while the Pentagon is pushing to give AI access to three million military and civilian personnel

American military aircraft were reportedly already in flight when officials discovered that the intelligence justifying the interception of a Chinese ship had been produced with an AI that had gotten its cargo wrong. The case dates back to spring 2026, in the midst of the war against Iran, and concerns a vessel in the Middle East supposed to be carrying components for a nuclear weapons program.

A special operations analyst had questioned a chatbot about information concerning the ship's manifest, in other words the document describing its cargo. This intelligence came from the U.S. Special Operations Command in the Pacific, based in Hawaii, and the chatbot allegedly combined it with public information and secret intelligence from intercepted signals.

The response allegedly attributed nuclear material to the ship that no reliable evidence allowed to be identified there. This is called a hallucination when the AI produces this kind of false information, but presented as fact, and in this case the error allegedly then benefited from a rather disastrous dressing-up.

The analyst allegedly asked for AI assistance a second time, to format his conclusions in the usual format of an intelligence report before disseminating it. As a result, the recipients received a document consistent with their work habits, with all the credit that can give to a military analysis.

Armed soldiers were preparing to board when the verification of the report allegedly allowed the operation to be stopped. The testimonies gathered thus describe an intervention prepared and then canceled, with a risk of confrontation between Washington and Beijing, without allowing, however, to assert that a boarding would necessarily have triggered a war.

The published investigation cites four sources close to the case, but gives neither the name of the chatbot nor the true nature of the cargo. Impossible therefore to designate a provider, or even to know whether the analyst was using a commercial product or a government tool.

The Pentagon is nevertheless pushing very hard to accelerate the adoption of these software programs. In January, Pete Hegseth, the Secretary of Defense, presented a strategy aimed at giving access to AI models to three million military and civilian personnel, while several officials describe different verification practices depending on the services and tools used.
