# Gemini arrives on Windows, with some limitations

## What to remember AI-generated summary

1. Google launches the official Gemini app for Windows, activatable via Alt + Space, to ask a question without leaving your work software.
2. Installation requires Windows 10 minimum, 8 GB of memory and 200 MB of disk space, with an Internet connection required at all times.
3. Screen context sharing, dictation in applications, and connection to Gemini Spark remain reserved for the Mac version, with no precise timeline for Windows.

Alt + Space is now enough to call Gemini from Windows. Google launched on September 10 the official application of its AI assistant for PC, with a window that opens over the software you are working in, so you can ask a question without going to find the right browser tab.

Gemini can help you write a message, summarize a document, or understand code. You can also send it an image to analyze, then resume your work in the previous application. So you find the uses of the web service, with more direct access from the desktop.

To install it, you need to go through the Gemini for computer page, download GeminiSetup.exe, and sign in to your Google account. Windows 10 or a more recent version, at least 8 GB of memory, and 200 MB available for installation are required. x64 and ARM64 PCs are supported.

Internet remains necessary. Installing this software does not run the AI model on your computer, and the professional or school account must have Gemini access authorized by the administrator. With the same account, Google synchronizes conversation history and memory between desktop, Web, and mobile.

On the usage side, the shortcut can be modified in the settings, which will avoid fighting with another software that already uses Alt + Space. The application is also accessible from the taskbar and its icon in the Windows notification area.

Integration with Google applications makes it possible to search for information in Gmail or Drive to prepare a summary. Image creation with Nano Banana and video creation with Gemini Omni are also among the possibilities highlighted, but some functions require a Google AI subscription and are reserved for adults, with variable availability.

Still missing is part of what makes a desktop assistant interesting. Screen context sharing, dictation in other applications, and connecting local folders to Gemini Spark, the agent responsible for chaining tasks, are still presented as functions of the Mac version.

Google announces their gradual enrichment on Windows, without a precise timeline. As a result, opening Gemini over a spreadsheet does not mean it can automatically read that table: this first version should not be confused with all the on-screen assistance demonstrations offered on macOS. But it's a good start.
