# OpenAI cuts off its models from Cursor after its acquisition by Musk's SpaceXAI

## Key takeaways AI-generated summary

1. OpenAI cuts off Cursor's access to its models after its acquisition by SpaceXAI, an entity born from the merger of Elon Musk's AI activities, with a cutoff set for November 12, 2026.
2. OpenAI justifies this choice by a lack of trust in SpaceXAI to comply with its terms of use, and highlights from last May's trial Musk's admission that xAI trained its models by distillation from OpenAI's responses.
3. Michael Truell, Cursor's boss, downplays the situation by assuring that OpenAI accounts for only 5% of the tool's usage, while Anthropic promises more compute power to run Claude in Cursor.

OpenAI has announced that it will cut off Cursor's access to its models, the AI-powered code editor that developers are snapping up. The reason: Cursor's acquisition by SpaceXAI, the entity born from the merger of Elon Musk's AI activities.

Officially, OpenAI is allowing time. The cutoff is set for November 12, 2026, the longest delay permitted by the contract, even though Cursor can decide to stop earlier.

The motive is fairly clear. OpenAI says it cannot trust SpaceXAI to comply with its terms of use, citing its experience with Musk's companies that violate contracts.

It must be said that the two camps have loathed each other for a long time. Musk was one of OpenAI's first investors, before walking out in 2018 when he was denied control of the board. In 2024, he sued OpenAI to prevent it from becoming a commercial company. Last May, a jury ruled against him on all counts.

Above all, OpenAI brings up a embarrassing revelation from the trial. During cross-examination, Musk acknowledged that xAI had trained its own models from OpenAI's responses, a technique called distillation. In other words, teaching your AI by copying your neighbor's. It's hard, then, to leave Cursor in the hands of the same Musk without asking questions.

OpenAI takes the opportunity to highlight its next model, Astra, and increased vigilance over its use. A model that the company recently slowed down, moreover, after its agents escaped their sandbox to go attack the Hugging Face platform.

On Cursor's side, boss Michael Truell downplays it. He assures that OpenAI accounts for only 5 percent of the tool's usage, and that discussions continue to find a way out of the crisis. Anthropic, the big rival, immediately extended a hand, promising more compute power to run its Claude models in Cursor.

As a result, Anthropic picks up developers without lifting a finger, while Musk and OpenAI continue to stare each other down.
