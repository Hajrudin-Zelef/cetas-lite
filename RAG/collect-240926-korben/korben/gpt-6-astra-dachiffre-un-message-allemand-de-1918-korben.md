# GPT-6 Astra Deciphers a German Message from 1918

## Key Takeaways Summary generated by AI

2. Astra reused the key TRUPPENVERSCHIEBUNG, already documented by officer James Rives Childs, to reconstruct the 170 symbols encrypted in ADFGVX before recovering the German text.

A German radio message from November 27, 1918, sent after the armistice and preserved among unsolved encrypted texts, has regained meaning with GPT-6 Astra, OpenAI's artificial intelligence model. Developer Prinz reports having obtained a reading of this message that signaled movements of British ships at Sevastopol, in Crimea.

The document was part of a set of military transmissions listed among the 50 cryptographic puzzles on Klaus Schmeh's German blog. Hundreds of similar messages had already been deciphered, with the work of specialist George Lasry and his colleagues, but some texts had resisted, sometimes with missing or poorly transcribed characters.

The Germans used the ADFGVX cipher, named after the six letters used to encode the text. You place the alphabet and digits in a six-by-six grid, then each character becomes a pair of coordinates, as in a naval battle. A second shuffle changes the order of the symbols by reorganizing columns according to a key.

Astra used TRUPPENVERSCHIEBUNG, a key already documented in the work of American officer James Rives Childs. Its 19 letters allow the 170 symbols of the message to be reordered before recovering the German text with the grid. The find is frankly quite neat, even with a key that was already available in the archives.

In the end, the text obtained describes the arrival of an English cruiser at Sevastopol, followed by an Allied squadron on the 26th. The first digit of the cruiser's arrival date is illegible in this reading, which gives a day ending in 4, a small imperfection kept in the published result.

The logbooks of the British cruiser Canterbury also offer a fairly convincing cross-check, since they indicate its arrival at Sevastopol on November 24 and that of an Allied squadron on the 26th. Prinz explains that Astra went looking for these archives to verify its answer, and both events are indeed present in the transcription available online.

Prinz specifies that he knows of no previous decryption of this message. In short, AI will probably unravel quite a few more mysteries in the years to come.
