# Firefox integrates Mistral's AI and France gets it before everyone else

## What to remember AI-generated summary

1. Firefox 155 opens the Smart Window to France, the first European country served, before the United Kingdom and Germany by the end of the year
2. Mistral Small 4, under Apache 2.0 license, becomes the recommended model by default in France, while it is only one option among others in the United States and Canada
3. Conversations are not kept by default at Mozilla, and Mistral commits to zero retention, knowing that the feature remains deactivatable

Mozilla has announced a partnership with Mistral, the Parisian start-up that has established itself as the European answer to OpenAI: its Mistral Small 4 model becomes the recommended engine of the Smart Window, the AI-assisted browsing mode that Firefox is rolling out in beta.

The Smart Window, for those who have never come across it, is a separate space from classic browsing where an assistant can summarize the page you are currently reading or retrieve a tab closed a little too quickly.

Until now, it was reserved for the United States and Canada. Firefox 155 opens it to France, the first European country served, with an assistant that finally officially speaks French, before the United Kingdom and Germany by the end of the year.

In our country, Mistral Small 4 will also be recommended by default, while across the Atlantic it simply joins the list of options already in place.

Mozilla says it chose it for its multilingual performance, and the model has another argument that fits well with the Firefox spirit: it is published under Apache 2.0 license, which means that anyone can download it, examine it and run it on their own machine.

Nothing imposed for all that, since the selector keeps other models on hand, including a Google Gemini and the Chinese Qwen, and you can even provide your own API key, the sesame that connects the browser to a third-party AI service.

On the data side, conversations are not kept on Mozilla's servers by default, and Mistral commits to zero retention, in other words nothing is kept once your answer is rendered. And if all that annoys you, the feature can be completely disabled.

It must be said that France is a special market for Firefox, which retains about 12% of the computer installed base there, more than double its global level, thanks to a loyal audience, rather attached to free software, to whom a French AI inevitably speaks more than a Californian service.

For Mistral, it is therefore a public showcase of some 200 million monthly users claimed by Firefox. Not bad.

I admit I will activate it as soon as the update, just to see if a model that speaks French natively does better on our everyday queries.
