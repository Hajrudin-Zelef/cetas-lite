# Claude Opus 5: Anthropic hands you Fable 5 for less

## Key takeaways AI-generated summary

1. Claude Opus 5 costs half as much to use as Fable 5 while still being billed at the same rate as Opus 4.8 ($5 per million tokens for input, $25 for output).
2. Opus 5 makes significant progress on code, agent tasks, and computer use, reaching 96% on SWE-bench Verified, but remains very slightly behind Fable 5 on the harder version of the same test (79% vs. 80%).
3. Reasoning is enabled by default with a one-million-token context window, and a mode about 2.5 times faster exists but costs twice as much.

Anthropic, the company that publishes the Claude assistant and remains one of the main rivals to OpenAI and Google, announced yesterday Claude Opus 5, a new model that slots in just below its flagship Fable 5 while costing about half as much to use.

The price doesn't change. Expect $5 per million tokens for input and $25 for output, exactly as with the previous Opus 4.8, knowing that a token is the small piece of text the model reads or writes, roughly a syllable, and that it's the billing unit for all these AIs.

The context window, that is, the amount of text the model keeps in memory at once, goes up to one million tokens, the equivalent of a very large book, and the response can stretch up to 128,000 tokens. Reasoning is enabled by default, so the model takes time to think before answering, and those in a hurry can switch to a mode about 2.5 times faster, billed at double.

The announced advances mainly concern code, agent tasks, where the AI chains actions on its own to accomplish work, and computer use, where it actually pilots a computer by clicking, browsing, and filling out forms as a human would.

On benchmarks, those standardized tests used to compare models, Opus 5 goes from 19 to 43% on FrontierBench, from 1.5 to 30% on ARC-AGI-3, which measures solving never-before-seen problems, and climbs to 71% on OSWorld, the computer-piloting test, versus 56% for its predecessor.

On SWE-bench Verified, which has it fix real code bugs, it reaches 96%. Except that on the harder version of this test, Fable 5 keeps a very short lead, 80% versus 79, and parity with the in-house top of the line therefore deserves an asterisk.

Another caveat: all these figures come from Anthropic itself, and experience shows that between the scores in an announcement and daily use, there is sometimes a gap.

On the subscription side, Opus 5 becomes the default model of the Claude Max offering and the most powerful accessible with a Claude Pro subscription. As a result, the real question is no longer whether this model is good, but who still has a reason to pay twice as much for Fable 5.

For me, Opus 5 becomes the default choice, and Fable 5 finds itself confined to the rare uses where the last percentage point is paid for without argument.
