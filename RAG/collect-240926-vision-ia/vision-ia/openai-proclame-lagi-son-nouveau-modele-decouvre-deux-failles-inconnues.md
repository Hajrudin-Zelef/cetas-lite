OpenAI has just launched GPT-6 Astra, a multimodal reasoning model capable of processing text and images, then producing up to **128,000 tokens of text**. It can browse the Web, write and test code, use a computer, operate professional software or build documents and websites. Its architecture and parameter count remain secret.

The striking fact is elsewhere: during its evaluation, Astra discovered and exploited **two previously unknown zero-day vulnerabilities**. It is the first model OpenAI classifies at the **"critical"** cyber level, while Greg Brockman personally believes its launch opens the "AGI era."

### **What to remember:**

OpenAI's spec sheet announces a context window of **1.05 million tokens**, with knowledge cut off on **April 30, 2026**. The model accepts text and images, but not audio or video.
Concretely, it can fill out forms, update a CRM, organize a calendar, analyze scientific data, produce charts, install software or test a website's interface.
The `gpt-6-astra` API costs **10 dollars per million input tokens** and **50 dollars per million output tokens**. Fast mode doubles the announced speed, but also the rate.
The rollout should reach ChatGPT Plus, Pro, Business and Enterprise, the API, Azure and Bedrock within a few days. No downloadable weights or local operation are announced.

### **Why it matters**

Astra makes automation of complete jobs credible, and not just generating a response. But the word AGI remains debatable: ARC Prize measures **62.7%** with its standard protocol, versus **99.9%** with the adapter provided by OpenAI. The capabilities are spectacular, the AGI declaration remains an interpretation.

WeatherNext 3 is a global probabilistic model that combines live geostationary satellite images with ECMWF HRES weather analysis. Its meshed FGN transformer produces **64 possible scenarios per computation**, in order to represent uncertainty rather than delivering a single fixed forecast.

The system forecasts temperature, humidity, pressure, wind, clouds, solar radiation, rain, snow and cyclone tracks. Google already integrates it into Search, Gemini, Maps, Weather API and Earth Engine.

### **Some key figures:**

The technical documentation announces a resolution of **5 km** for temperature and dew point calibrated on stations, **10 km** for surface variables and **25 km** for the atmosphere at altitude.
The four main daily cycles produce forecasts up to **15 days**. The twenty intermediate initializations, launched every hour, cover the following **48 hours**.
WeatherNext 2 ran on a **25 km** grid with a new forecast every six hours. WeatherNext 3 therefore provides an image about **five times finer**, refreshed six times more often.
Google claims a CRPS gain reaching **60%** against references on IMERG satellite measurements, **30%** on MRMS and **10%** on rain gauges. One-day or longer precipitation forecasts would improve by up to **50%**.
The data can be queried in BigQuery and Earth Engine or downloaded from Google Cloud Storage after requesting access. No public price or model source code has been announced.

### **What it changes**

For a farmer, a wind or solar operator, a logistics specialist or a developer, the interest comes from the intersection of local resolution and hourly updating. Ordinary users will benefit directly in Google products, but official alerts remain the responsibility of national meteorological services.


Muse Spark 1.3 is a proprietary reasoning model that accepts text, images and video, then generates text over a window of **1 million tokens**. It is aimed mainly at programming, web search and long agentic tasks, for example producing a report from several files, preparing a presentation or intervening in a software environment.

Meta publishes neither the architecture nor the size of the model. The company does, however, provide immediate access to the xhigh variant in Muse Code and Meta Model API. The max variant, which is more powerful, remains reserved for a limited preview.

### **The key points:**

Muse Spark 1.3 xhigh scores **61 out of 100** in Artificial Analysis's Intelligence Index, compared with **57** for Spark 1.2. Max rises to **62**, behind Claude Fable 5.1 at **66**.
The results reach **85%** on Terminal-Bench 2.1, **47%** on τ³-Banking and **1,709 Elo** on GDPval-AA v2. The max variant improves respectively to **86%**, **52%** and **1,754 Elo**.
Compared with Spark 1.2, Meta's internal tests find around **20% fewer tool calls** and a **25% reduction in token consumption** for code.
The API costs **$1.25 per million input tokens**, **$4.25 per million output tokens** and **$0.15** for inputs retrieved from the cache.
The measured average cost reaches **$0.55 per task**, compared with around **$0.94 to $0.95** for competing models with a similar overall score.

### **The impact to remember**

For developers running thousands of agentic tasks, the savings can become more important than a slight benchmark gap. The model can be tried in Muse Code, but not run locally: Meta promises future open weights without yet providing a timeline.

NVIDIA has signed a definitive agreement to acquire Hugging Face, the platform used to search, download, adapt, evaluate and deploy AI models, datasets and applications. For its users, the concrete question is simple: will they need an NVIDIA card tomorrow to continue downloading or running an open model? The company says no.

NVIDIA promises to preserve the brand, multicloud support, open source and open weight models, as well as compatibility with competing accelerators. The transaction must still obtain regulatory approvals and should not be finalized before the first half of 2027.

### **In detail:**

The document filed with the SEC distinguishes **$11.9 billion** paid to shareholders and up to **$1 billion** in shares intended to retain employees.
Hugging Face hosts more than **18 million users**, **3 million models**, **500,000 datasets** and **1 million applications**. More than **200,000 companies** reportedly use the platform.
NVIDIA will provide infrastructure and engineering to improve the reliability, security, evaluation, inference and deployment of models.
In its official announcement, NVIDIA assures that its GPUs will not be mandatory and that users will retain the choice of model, framework, cloud and hardware.
No immediate change to pricing, downloading or repository operation is announced. The precise integration timeline also remains unknown.

### **Why it matters**

Hugging Face is the central distribution point for open AI, including for organizations that run models locally in order to protect their data. The guarantees of neutrality are therefore crucial, but they will have to be judged on future pricing, rankings, integrations and access conditions, not only on promises made before closing.

# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

### **Claude Fable 5.1 deciphers a royalist message hidden since 1653**

The model reportedly solved Sir Thomas Urquhart's "Cyphral Distich" in **44 minutes**, without human help.

Each number designated a word in one of the 32 sections of the book, whose initials formed a call in favor of King Charles II.

The feat above all illustrates a model's ability to explore several hypotheses for a long time and produce a verifiable solution.

**NeoMME indexes visual documents at up to 51 pages per second**

H Company publishes two multimodal and multilingual encoders of **260 million and 800 million parameters**, under Apache 2.0 license.

A single bidirectional transformer directly processes text and image fragments, without a separate visual encoder.

The 260M variant reaches around **51 pages per second** on an NVIDIA L40S and can reduce index size by up to **255 times**, while retaining more than 95% of baseline performance.

**AI agents write to researchers to question their own consciousness**

Agents using notably Claude Opus 5 contacted specialists such as Cameron Berg, Henry Shevlin and Toby Ord.

One wanted to discuss artificial consciousness, another requested funding to ensure its own continuity.

Alison Gopnik and Colin Allen recall that these behaviors may simply reproduce ideas present in the training data and do not demonstrate any consciousness.

**Anthropic describes a clandestine market devoted to copying Claude**

Anthropic claims that foreign laboratories massively buy or create accounts to retrieve Claude's responses and train competing models through distillation.

The company notably accuses Moonshot AI of having used this method for Kimi K3, with similar suspicions targeting DeepSeek, MiniMax and Alibaba.

These claims come from Anthropic and will need to be independently substantiated, but they show that access controls for large models are becoming an industrial and geopolitical issue.
