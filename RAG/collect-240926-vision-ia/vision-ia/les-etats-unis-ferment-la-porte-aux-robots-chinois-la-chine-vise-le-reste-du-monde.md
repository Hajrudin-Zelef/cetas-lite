Washington is tightening tariffs and restrictions from September on drones and advanced robotic systems manufactured abroad. The problem, revealed this morning by TechCrunch, is that China already has a massive industrial advantage: **five Chinese manufacturers accounted for 86% of global humanoid robot deliveries in the first half of 2026**.

Concretely, the American measures are meant to sharply increase the cost of certain imported drones and prevent new foreign devices considered sensitive from obtaining the necessary authorizations. They can protect American manufacturers in their domestic market, but they reduce neither Chinese factories, nor their supply chains, nor the data collected by the thousands of robots already deployed.

### **the key points**

The tariffs planned from **September 3, 2026** can reach **100%** for drones over 25 kg, thermal cameras and certain docking stations. Small drones are subject to a rate of **25%**.
The FCC is refusing authorization for new foreign models placed on its Covered List. Drones already purchased or previously authorized can continue to be used.
The global market absorbed around **22,000 humanoid robots** in the first half. AgiBot, Unitree, Galbot, UBTECH and Leju Robotics delivered **86%** of them.
DJI simultaneously controls **more than two-thirds of the global drone market**, which makes a rapid replacement difficult.
Higher volumes reduce costs and produce more usage data. That data then makes it possible to improve the machines, creating an industrial loop that is difficult to break with tariffs.

### **What this changes**

For an American buyer, new Chinese drones and robots will become more expensive or unavailable. For a European reader, the effect may be the opposite: Chinese manufacturers excluded from the United States are likely to shift their efforts toward Europe, Asia, Latin America and the Middle East, with machines cheaper than those of Western competitors. The restrictions thus risk fragmenting the global market rather than genuinely slowing down Chinese industry.

Sony Music Publishing, Warner Chappell and their affiliated companies filed a federal lawsuit on **August 28, 2026** against Anthropic, Dario Amodei and co-founder Benjamin Mann. They accuse the company of having copied without a license **tens of thousands of compositions, lyrics and sheet music** to train Claude, then of having sometimes reproduced those lyrics in the chatbot's responses.

At this stage, these are accusations, not a ruling. The proceedings nevertheless target something very concrete: the data used to build Claude, its provenance, and the possibility for publishers to obtain its destruction or disclosure.

### **in detail**

The 48-page complaint cites LibGen, PiLiMi, Books3, The Pile, Common Crawl, MusixMatch and LyricFind as alleged sources of the content.
The plaintiffs claim that Anthropic retrieved at least **5 million books** from LibGen in June 2021 and **2 million** from PiLiMi in July 2022. These figures concern the alleged libraries, not only the songs at issue in this complaint.
They are seeking up to **$150,000 per work** in the event of willful infringement and **$25,000** per removal of copyright information. No reliable total amount is yet available.
The demands include a jury trial, damages, destruction of the disputed copies and disclosure of the training data. Anthropic disputes the accusations.

### **Why it matters**

There is no immediate change for Claude users. But if the publishers obtain access to the datasets or the destruction of certain copies, the case could force labs to document the origin of their data much more precisely. It could also accelerate the shift from massive collection on the Internet to licenses negotiated work by work or catalog by catalog.

Starting **September 14, 2026**, Anthropic will present Claude Code's permanent weekly quota as **25%** higher than its initial level. Yet subscribers will concretely have **16.7% less capacity than today**, because the current temporary bonus still brings the quota to 150%.

Claude Code is Anthropic's programming agent available in the terminal and IDEs such as VS Code, Cursor, IntelliJ, or PyCharm. It can explore a repository, modify multiple files, run commands and tests, fix bugs, refactor code, and prepare Git operations.

### **a few key figures**

The real evolution of the quota reads simply: **100 at the start, 150 with the temporary bonus, 125 starting September 14**.
The change affects the **Pro, Max, Team, and Enterprise billed per seat** plans. The five-hour limits do not change.
Anthropic does not give a fixed number of messages or tokens. Consumption varies depending on the model, conversation length, repository size, and complexity of operations.
The Pro plan costs **$20 per month**. Max offers cost **$100 per month for 5x** or **$200 for 20x**. Once the limit is reached, you must wait for it to reset or purchase additional credits.

In parallel, Anthropic is rolling out performance optimizations and an optional bug reporting system. When a command fails, Claude can prepare a report that the user reviews before sending, with the option to include or exclude the conversation log.

### **The impact to remember**

For a developer using Claude Code daily, the change means less included weekly work at an unchanged subscription price. Large refactorings and repository analyses will need to be planned before the quota resets, moved to usage-based billing, or spread across multiple tools.


SpaceX is building in Bastrop, Texas, a foundry capable of producing the most complex blades and vanes for gas turbines. According to Elon Musk, manufacturing these components in-house could reduce by **18 months** the time needed to bring new turbines into service and more quickly power data centers dedicated to AI.

These parts are not simple metal blades. They operate at temperatures that can reach **3,000 to 3,600 °F**, about 1,650 to 1,980 °C, and must be cast as a single crystal, with internal cooling channels and thermal coatings. TechCrunch reports that only four companies currently master this process on an industrial scale.

### **what to remember**

SpaceX job postings explicitly mention a foundry dedicated to blades and vanes.
SpaceX reportedly acquired about **830 acres**, nearly 336 hectares, around Bastrop between March and June 2026.
GE Vernova says its capacity is largely booked through 2030 and aims for production of **30 GW of turbines per year** by that date.
The foundry's future capacity, timeline, and first customers are not known. There is therefore no public product to buy or test.

Amazon, Google, Meta, Microsoft, and OpenAI are already turning to private gas plants to avoid waiting for electrical grid reinforcements. This acceleration does, however, have a local cost: in Memphis, a contested permit authorizes the xAI site to operate **15 turbines continuously**, while the NAACP denounces pollutant emissions and the prior use of turbines without adequate permits.

### **Why it matters**

The main bottleneck for AI is no longer only the GPU, but electricity available in the right place. SpaceX is seeking to internalize a rare industrial part to accelerate this infrastructure, while potentially locking new data centers into several more years of dependence on natural gas.

Researchers at the University of Queensland equipped giant burrowing cockroaches with a removable navigation system, cameras, or miniature remotely controlled injectors.

Injections within less than 15 cm of the target succeeded in **95% of trials**, but the complete mission, navigation then injection, succeeded in only **72% of cases**.

Medical decisions remain under human control. The team envisions specialized swarms in the **next 5 to 10 years**, after field trials.

NASA's Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope lifted off aboard a Falcon Heavy and deployed its solar panels as well as part of its sunshield.

It can photograph an area of the sky **100 times larger than Hubble** and is expected to transmit about **1.4 TB of data per day**.

Roman will study dark matter, dark energy, exoplanets, and potentially the light of a billion galaxies. Its first images are expected in early 2027.

In an experiment involving **1,053 students** at Bocconi University, access to GPT-4o improved grades on a marketing assignment by nearly **one point on a five-point scale**.

The texts contained more ideas and aligned better with expert recommendations. However, a short training in causal reasoning produced more original solutions, without improving the traditional grade.

No subsequent test without ChatGPT measured the knowledge actually acquired. The study therefore demonstrates better production, not better learning.

Researchers evaluated the agents on **200 ProgramBench tasks** and 18 additional tests. Claude overestimated the actual duration by about three times, Codex by **six to ten times**.

The agents also overestimated the quality of their work by about **20 percentage points**, including when the task had failed.

A simple tool indicating elapsed time almost entirely corrected the error, which argues for external guardrails during long autonomous tasks.

Unlike autoregressive LLMs, which produce tokens one by one, these models represent language in a continuous space and then gradually transform noise into text.

After a marked decline starting in 2023, several continuous and hybrid methods appeared in 2025 and 2026, including LangFlow, RePlaid, LDLM and ELF.

The main appeal is generation in few steps, potentially more parallel, as well as text infilling and constrained generation. Evaluation methods, however, remain poorly standardized.

This 2018 study formalizes two problems: navigating as far as possible without touching land and traveling the longest land line without encountering a large body of water.

Coastlines, islands and lakes make the search chaotic. The authors use a branch-and-bound algorithm to systematically verify routes.

The paper remains most interesting as a demonstration of rigorous geographic optimization applied to an immediately tellable question.
