DeepSeek strikes a major blow by **permanently cutting by 75%** the rates of its flagship V4-Pro model. The cost of tokens drops to **$0.0035–0.83 per million**, a level far below that of any competing American model. This dramatic drop sends a clear signal: the AI price war between China and the United States has just crossed a new threshold.

**Key takeaways:**

- The price of V4-Pro tokens is now **10 to 100 times lower** than that of equivalent American models

- The reduction was made possible by the deployment of **Huawei's Ascend 950 supernodes**, which removed compute capacity constraints

- DeepSeek confirms that this reduction is **permanent**, not promotional

- The Chinese company continues to offer frontier-level performance despite American export restrictions

**Why this is a turning point:** This announcement concretely demonstrates that the American embargo on Nvidia GPUs has not achieved its goal. By relying on Huawei chips rather than Western hardware, DeepSeek proves that China has built a functional alternative supply chain. For developers around the world, it's also a windfall: accessing a cutting-edge model at a fraction of the usual cost will reshuffle the deck, particularly for startups and emerging markets.

Google has just unveiled its eighth generation of Tensor Processing Units, with a major innovation: **two distinct architectures** for training and inference. The TPU 8t is dedicated to model training, while the TPU 8i is optimized for inference. A superpod composed of **9,600 chips** reaches the colossal power of **121 ExaFlops**.

**A few key figures:**

- **TPU 8t**: designed to reduce frontier model training cycles **from several months to a few weeks**

- **TPU 8i**: specifically optimized for multi-step AI agents that run continuously

- A superpod of **9,600 chips** delivers **121 ExaFlops** of computing power

- The split training/inference architecture is an industry first at this scale

**What this changes:** By physically separating training and inference hardware, Google addresses a concrete need: autonomous AI agents require continuous, prolonged inference, very different from the one-off requests of a chatbot. Reducing training from months to weeks also means that the pace of model iteration will accelerate even further. Google is laying the hardware foundations here for what the industry already calls "the agentic era" — and sending a clear message to Nvidia on its own turf.

Alibaba enters the race for long-duration AI agents with **Qwen3.7-Max**, a new proprietary model specially optimized for autonomous workflows capable of sustaining execution over **hundreds, even thousands of steps**. To prove its capabilities, the team tested it on a GPU kernel optimization task — work that the model completed in **35 hours of continuous execution** without human intervention.

**In detail:**

- Qwen3.7-Max is designed for **prolonged autonomous agent workflows**, not simple chat

- The flagship demonstration: autonomous optimization of a GPU kernel on a novel hardware architecture for **35 consecutive hours**

- The model maintains its coherence and performance over **hundreds/thousands of reasoning steps**

- Direct positioning against the "agentic" models from Google and OpenAI

**The impact to remember:** Until now, AI models excelled at short tasks — a question, an answer. Qwen3.7-Max marks a paradigm shift: we are now talking about models capable of working for days on a complex problem, autonomously. Combined with Google's TPU 8i and DeepSeek's price war, an ecosystem is taking shape in which AI agents become genuine "digital workers" capable of prolonged missions. China is placing two major players — DeepSeek and Alibaba — in this race.

Pope Leo XIV has published **"Magnifica Humanitas,"** his first encyclical, dedicated to the risks that artificial intelligence poses to humanity. The pontifical document directly addresses the threat of **AI-assisted warfare**, the massive impact on employment and social cohesion, and calls for the creation of **new ethical and legal frameworks** to govern technological development.

**The key points:**

- The encyclical denounces the **lack of protections** in the face of rapid AI adoption and its effects on **human dignity**

- The Pope specifically warns about **autonomous weapons** and AI-assisted warfare

- The text calls for **robust legal and ethical frameworks** at the international level

- Notable fact: during the presentation, the co-founder of Anthropic stated that AI models show **"signs of introspection"**

**Why it matters:** When the Vatican — an institution that weighs on 1.4 billion Catholics — takes such a firm stance on AI, it is a major political and cultural signal. This encyclical comes at a time when legislators around the world are struggling to agree on AI regulation. The rapprochement between the Vatican and Anthropic at this presentation also illustrates a new phenomenon: AI companies are now seeking ethical legitimacy from traditional moral institutions.

**AlphaProof Nexus solves mathematical problems open for decades** — Google DeepMind deployed AlphaProof Nexus to solve **9 open Erdős problems**, including **2 unsolved for 56 years**, all for a few hundred dollars in inference cost. The system uses the **Lean** compiler to formally verify each step of the proofs, a radically different approach from natural language methods. The overall success rate remains 2.5%, but the results obtained are historic.

**ClickUp replaces hundreds of employees with AI agents: what does this say about the future of work?** — The productivity startup ClickUp laid off **several hundred employees** to replace them with **thousands of autonomous AI agents**. This case study illustrates a growing trend in tech: the massive automation of internal operations. TechCrunch analyzes the profound implications of this decision on the transformation of the job market and the long-term viability of this model.

**George Hotz: AI coding agents will be "one of the most costly mistakes" in the industry** — After six months of intensive testing, programmer George Hotz concludes that LLMs produce rapid prototypes but **systematically fail on details**, generating subtle bugs that are increasingly difficult to detect. According to him, the technical debt accumulated by coding agents could become a financial abyss for companies that adopt them massively.
