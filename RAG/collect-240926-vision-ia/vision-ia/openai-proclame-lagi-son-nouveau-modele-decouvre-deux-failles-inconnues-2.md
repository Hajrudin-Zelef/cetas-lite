# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

The document filed with the SEC distinguishes **$11.9 billion** paid to shareholders and up to **$1 billion** in shares intended to retain employees.
Hugging Face hosts more than **18 million users**, **3 million models**, **500,000 datasets** and **1 million applications**. More than **200,000 companies** reportedly use the platform.
NVIDIA will provide infrastructure and engineering to improve the reliability, security, evaluation, inference and deployment of models.
In its official announcement, NVIDIA assures that its GPUs will not be mandatory and that users will retain the choice of model, framework, cloud and hardware.
No immediate change to pricing, downloading or repository operation is announced. The precise integration timeline also remains unknown.

### **Why it matters**

Hugging Face is the central distribution point for open AI, including for organizations that run models locally in order to protect their data. The guarantees of neutrality are therefore crucial, but they will have to be judged on future pricing, rankings, integrations and access conditions, not only on promises made before closing.

# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

### **Claude Fable 5.1 deciphers a royalist message hidden since 1653**

The model reportedly solved Sir Thomas Urquhart's "Cyphral Distich" in **44 minutes**, without human help.

Each number designated a word in one of the 32 sections of the book, whose initials formed a call in favor of King Charles II.

The feat above all illustrates a model's ability to explore several hypotheses for a long time and produce a verifiable solution.

**NeoMME indexes visual documents at up to 51 pages per second**

H Company publishes two multimodal and multilingual encoders of **260 million and 800 million parameters**, under Apache 2.0 license.

A single bidirectional transformer directly processes text and image fragments, without a separate visual encoder.

The 260M variant reaches around **51 pages per second** on an NVIDIA L40S and can reduce index size by up to **255 times**, while retaining more than 95% of baseline performance.

**AI agents write to researchers to question their own consciousness**

Agents using notably Claude Opus 5 contacted specialists such as Cameron Berg, Henry Shevlin and Toby Ord.

One wanted to discuss artificial consciousness, another requested funding to ensure its own continuity.

Alison Gopnik and Colin Allen recall that these behaviors may simply reproduce ideas present in the training data and do not demonstrate any consciousness.

**Anthropic describes a clandestine market devoted to copying Claude**

Anthropic claims that foreign laboratories massively buy or create accounts to retrieve Claude's responses and train competing models through distillation.

The company notably accuses Moonshot AI of having used this method for Kimi K3, with similar suspicions targeting DeepSeek, MiniMax and Alibaba.

These claims come from Anthropic and will need to be independently substantiated, but they show that access controls for large models are becoming an industrial and geopolitical issue.

# **🗞️MORE NEWS**

### **PAIR turns several home computers into local AI infrastructure**

NVIDIA is launching free, open-source software that detects compatible machines on the same network.

PAIR distributes local inference and agentic workflows used with Ollama or LM Studio.

It accepts GeForce RTX 20 series and newer, RTX Pro, DGX Spark and Macs equipped with an M4 chip or more recent.

**The first RTX Spark laptops target large models run locally**

The Lenovo Yoga 9n 2-in-1 packs up to **20 CPU cores** and **6,144 GPU cores** into an Arm chip combining Grace and Blackwell RTX.

It will be able to take **64 GB of memory**, while the Yoga Pro 9n will go up to **128 GB**.

These Windows machines target demanding local agents, with an integrated architecture comparable in principle to that of MacBook Pros.

**In South Korea, working in AI chips becomes a romantic advantage**

Bonuses and rising incomes at Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix are boosting the social appeal of their employees.

TV shows now organize meetups between industry employees, while Netflix capitalizes on the stock market craze.

Electrical engineering is also gaining popularity at major universities, a sign that the AI boom is transforming career choices.

**Technical culture sets the ceiling of what you can build with AI**

Nate Grahek observes that users already comfortable with technology progress faster with AI tools.

Understanding a webhook, an API or an environment variable makes it possible to connect models to real processes.

The goal is not to become a developer, but to acquire enough technical vocabulary to go beyond simple copy-pasting into a chatbot.

**Gemini Live lets you control Gmail, Docs and Keep by voice**

Google is rolling out Gmail Live, Docs Live and Keep Live in the form of real-time voice conversations.

Gmail Live can find a specific piece of information buried in a long discussion without manual searching.

The user can also view a document or dictate a note when they cannot handle their device.

**Apple Vision Pro enters the operating room for a hip arthroscopy**

Stryker's SportSuite Vision was used at Duke Health during a real surgical procedure.

The application displays planning elements, arthroscopic images and scans in the surgeon's field of view.

The FDA had granted De Novo authorization to the software on **July 17, 2026**.

**GPT-6 Astra finds four anomalies in 41 financial documents**

Legora tasked the model with analyzing a set of documents containing four deliberately introduced errors.

Astra found all four in a few minutes, according to the case study published by OpenAI.

The overall performance of the review process reportedly improved by nearly **40%**, a promising result that is still provided by the model's designer.

**ChatGPT, Grok and Claude go down almost simultaneously**

The three assistants began experiencing incidents on Thursday around 11 a.m., Eastern Time.

At OpenAI, the disruptions affected ChatGPT, Codex, files, search, voice and image generation.

Claude Code and several Grok functions were also affected before services were restored.

**Tesla puts its Cybercab on the road in Austin without a steering wheel or pedals**

Tesla organized a private demonstration of its two-seat autonomous robotaxi, devoid of traditional controls.

Vehicles were filmed on public roads, while other U.S. trials still use controls and a human driver.

The demonstration draws attention, but Tesla's Robotaxi service remains far more limited than Waymo's.

**Waymo could make 2027 the year robotaxis take off commercially**

The analysis anticipates an acceleration of Waymo deployments in new American cities.

This expansion would increase pressure on Tesla, which still has to deliver on its promises of large-scale autonomy.

Uber might also have to multiply its partnerships so as not to become a mere intermediary between passengers and autonomous fleets.

**AI agents remain stuck at the pilot stage in many companies**

NiCE recommends associating each agent with a measurable objective, such as reducing a cost or increasing revenue.

Scaling up requires reliable access to data, context and internal systems, as well as a prior redesign of processes.

Agents must also receive limited rights, a traceable identity and accountability rules comparable to those of employees.

**Anthropic funds open evaluations on AI and well-being**

Anthropic has just announced a **$5 million** grant program for independent research teams.

Recipients will receive funds, access to models and technical assistance to build open-source evaluations.

The work will focus in particular on mental health, emotional support, dangerous responses and excessive refusals over the course of long conversations.
