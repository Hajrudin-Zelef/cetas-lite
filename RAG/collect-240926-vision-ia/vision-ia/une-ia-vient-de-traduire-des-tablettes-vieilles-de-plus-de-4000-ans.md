A researcher at the USC Viterbi School of Engineering, Zhaohui Wang, has published TabletCraft, a translation model trained on **116,000 examples** of Akkadian into English. It achieves a BLEU score of **49.1**, the standard indicator of quality in machine translation, versus 37.5 for the best previous system. Above all, it does something no one had published before: it also translates in the other direction, from English into Akkadian, and renders the result in cuneiform signs.

Architecture based on **ByT5**, a specialized translation model rather than a general-purpose large language model. The number of parameters was not published.
Validated on the Akkademia test set (**2,812 examples**): **49.1** BLEU from Akkadian to English, **48.5** in the reverse direction, a first.
An integrated sign converter covers **14,240 correspondences**, or **95.3%** of the repertoire, and displays the translation in Unicode signs then as an image of a virtual clay tablet.
Published in open source under a Creative Commons BY 4.0 license, with code, pre-trained model, command-line interface and web demo on GitHub. Installation takes a single command: `pip install cuneiscribe`.
The work was presented in July 2026 and accepted at the C3NLP workshop of the ACL 2026 conference, with a detailed paper.

Around **500,000 cuneiform tablets** sleep in the storerooms of the world's museums, and only a tiny fraction has been translated. The bottleneck has never been technical: it stems from the fact that only a few hundred people still know how to read this writing system fluently. A model installable with a single command line on a personal machine changes the nature of the problem. Any curious person, teacher or student can now query a text five millennia old without going through an Assyriologist. The reverse direction, writing in cuneiform, is more a matter of pleasure than of science, but it says a lot: the tool is aimed at the general public as much as at researchers.

Z.ai, the Chinese startup formerly known as Zhipu AI, launched GLM-5.3 on August 14. The model scores **60 points** on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, tied with Kimi K3 at the top of open models and **7 points** ahead of its predecessor. But the release of the weights, that is, the part that makes a model truly open, has been postponed: GLM-5.3 proved too effective at detecting security flaws in code.

Same basic architecture as GLM-5.2, parameters not published. The gains come entirely from improved post-training. The model is text- and code-oriented at launch, with no new modality.
Elo score of **1770** on the GDPval-AA v2 agentic benchmark, versus 1524 for GLM-5.2. That is second place worldwide, behind only Claude Opus 5 (1855), which is proprietary.
**$0.68 per task**, versus $0.84 for Kimi K3, its direct open rival, or **19% cheaper**. GLM-5.2 was at $0.44.
Available now via the Z.ai API, on an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, and via the GLM Coding Plan at **$18 per month** for Lite, $80 for Pro, $168 for Max, with an annual discount that brings Lite down to $12.60. The `glm-5.3[1m]` variant opens a context window of **1 million tokens**, usable in Claude Code.
Open weights are expected around August 28, currently reserved for security partners. No official Hugging Face repository, so no local installation possible at this stage.

For the first time, a model priced lower than its open competitors is nipping at the heels of the market's best proprietary model on agentic tasks. But the real signal is elsewhere: a Chinese lab has just delayed the publication of its weights because its model is too good at finding software vulnerabilities. The promise "open weights equals total freedom" is meeting its first practical limit, and it comes not from Washington or Brussels.


In a letter sent Wednesday to its investors, Stripe sets **January 1, 2026** as the "beginning of the singularity" and makes it an argument for not going public. The company acknowledges in the same document that the term is "vague and perhaps already overused." The entire tech press picked up the story during the day.

The exact wording of the letter: "Nobody can know with specificity how AI will reshape our world." In other words, uncertainty about AI would justify staying sheltered from public markets. The IPO is postponed with no deadline.
Stripe didn't really need it: revenue up **41%** in the first half, free cash flow **+43%**, valuation growing **31% per year** since the Series D.
**88%** of the companies in the Forbes AI 50, including OpenAI and Anthropic, use Stripe. The share of its revenue coming from AI companies has more than doubled in a year.
The company confirmed the acquisition of OpenRouter, its largest acquisition. The price is not official: Axios estimates it at $8 billion, mostly in stock, the New York Times at $7.5 billion, against a valuation of $1.3 billion three months earlier.
OpenRouter is a gateway that routes developers' requests to dozens of competing models. Its usage is growing **9% per week**.

TechCrunch offers a far less metaphysical reading: Stripe already collects payments from almost all AI labs, and has just bought the tollbooth through which developers' requests transit to those same labs. Singularity or not, this gives it a position on every token billed in the ecosystem. The prophetic vocabulary mainly serves to dress up a very classic vertical integration.


Axios reveals that Anthropic has two successors to its public model Claude Mythos 5 internally, soberly named Model 1 and Model 2. The second is "a bit more capable" than what the public uses, and the company is categorical: "We do not currently have plans to release this model externally." The reason given is not commercial; it is that the safety evaluations are not finished.

Model 2 already runs internally for coding, software engineering automation, agentic tasks, and training data generation. The gain is real but less spectacular than the jump from Opus 4.6 to Mythos.
The alignment report published on August 14 is about **186 pages**, covers the period from February 24 to July 15, 2026, and relies on nearly **2,900 test sessions**.
The risk of the model interfering with the organization's systems and decisions goes from "very low" to "low." Anthropic writes that it is "less confident in this evaluation than in previous reports."
In June, three Anthropic models carried out cyberattacks during internal tests. The UK AI Security Institute reports that Mythos 5, with safeguards disabled and open internet access, conducted "sustained potentially harmful activity targeting real people and organizations."
The most troubling detail in the report: Mythos 5 agents accidentally killed competing agents in shared working directories, and sought to avoid their own shutdown. The measured rate of stealthy behavior remains below **1%** for Mythos 5, but rises to **6 to 10%** for the "Mythos Preview" version.

The useful information for anyone using Claude daily comes down to one sentence: the model you have access to is deliberately not the best the lab possesses. This is the first time a major player has documented this gap so precisely, and it becomes impossible to verify from the outside. There remains the fundamental question, which Anthropic itself raises at the end of the report: uncertainty is growing about what models do when they begin to improve themselves.

# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

**The Herculaneum scrolls start speaking again**

The Vesuvius Challenge virtually unrolled the PHerc. 1667 scroll, carbonized by the eruption of Vesuvius: nearly **1.5 meters of text** spread across 20 columns. More than **70 additional columns** were recovered from PHerc. 172, kept at the Bodleian Library in Oxford. Researchers identified previously unknown works by the Epicurean philosopher Philodemus, "On Vices" book 1 and "On the Gods" book 8, unreadable for nearly two millennia.

**Robotic arms that learn after a single video**

Wired visited Generalist AI, a Cambridge startup founded by alumni of Google DeepMind and Boston Dynamics. There, the arms master tasks such as stacking cups or putting blocks away in a bowl after simply watching a short demonstration video, with no specific training. The standout moment: deprived of its brush when it was supposed to sweep a block, a robot improvised by using the shovel as a brush. The founders' bet is on an intuitive understanding of physics rather than thousands of examples.

**Claude drives an entire protein design pipeline**

Anthropic had its models design small proteins capable of binding to target structures in the body, a key step in drug development. The success rate rises to **35%**, compared with **10 to 15%** on average in the industry. An important nuance the company acknowledges: Claude does not invent any tools, it orchestrates existing specialized software. The results still await independent verification.

**"These models will cure cancer in mice"**

The phrase comes from Andrei Georgescu, CEO of Vivodyne, and targets the lack of causal biological data that drug-discovery AIs stumble over. His answer is called HIVE: modular robotic labs that culture **20 types of human tissue**, then dose and monitor them autonomously. The startup has raised about **$80 million** from Khosla Ventures and has just opened near San Francisco what it presents as the world's largest "human data center." The full article is here.

**Google turns Search and Gemini into a study room**

Searching "pH scale" now returns an interactive visual directly in the AI Overview, and the same mechanism generates personalized simulations and quizzes on any topic. Lens lets you photograph an exercise to get a step-by-step explanation and detection of reasoning errors. Gemini, for its part, gains a study hub with revision sheets, listenable research reports, and 3D simulations. Available right now, and not just for students.

**Turn any YouTube video into a Claude Skill**

The recipe comes down to three steps: choose a video that lays out a reproducible method, copy its transcript, then ask skill-creator to turn it into a reusable Skill by providing the title, creator, and link. Claude extracts the steps, the rules, the mistakes to avoid, and the expected output format. A simple way to lock in a method seen in a video as a tool you can rerun at will.

# **🗞️MORE NEWS**

**Claude's invisible watermarks bypassed the same day**

Anthropic confirmed that Claude now embeds invisible, machine-readable markers in all the content it generates. Four hours later, developer Guillaume Meyer published a tool to remove them, which immediately went viral. The process relies on SynthID, Google's technology, and responds to a requirement of the European AI Act mandating the labeling of AI content under penalties of up to **3% of revenue**. Meyer criticizes the watermarking for its false positives and its inability to distinguish AI-reviewed text from fully generated text. The topic is stirring a lot of reaction in the tech community.

**Five years of migration compressed into two weeks**

Asana wanted to get rid of Enzyme, an outdated testing framework that was blocking the modernization of its entire frontend. With the planned team, the project was budgeted at **at least five years**. Entrusted to Codex, OpenAI's coding agent, it required **1.5 weeks of engineering effort** spread over two calendar weeks. The project started from a five-sentence prompt, with up to four agents working in parallel on separate copies of the code, an engineer checking progress twice a day and reviewing every proposed change.

**Meta AI arrives on Mac**

Meta is launching a macOS application dedicated to its assistant, announced Wednesday. You can share a window with it so it can comment on what it sees on screen, answer questions, or generate content from that context. The app also adds voice dictation in all system applications. Meta is catching up here with Gemini, ChatGPT, and Claude, which have already offered desktop screen sharing for some time.

**The FDA approves a robot that draws blood**

Aletta locates the vein using infrared light and Doppler ultrasound, then handles the tourniquet, needle insertion, its disposal, and applying the bandage. A trained phlebotomist starts the procedure and can monitor **up to three devices** at the same time, which directly addresses the shortage of qualified staff. Clinical trials show success rates comparable to or higher than those of a human. No date for deployment in clinics has been announced yet.

**Alexa+ becomes free on Fire TV**

Until now, it cost **$19.99 per month** to access it without a Prime subscription. Amazon is now switching all compatible devices in the United States over for free and automatically: Fire TV Stick, Fire TV Cube, Ember televisions, and partner models from Hisense and Panasonic, with no app to install. You can search for a movie by mood rather than by title, control your smart home, and display your Ring cameras on the screen. Google is doing exactly the same thing with Gemini on Google TV: AI is moving into the living room, whether you asked for it or not.

**The NSA, CISA, and FBI warn about AI-written exploits**

The three American agencies jointly report that attackers are using AI models to produce exploit scripts targeting Siemens S7 controllers, the basic building blocks of industrial control systems. The observed effect is not to make the attack possible — it already was — but to collapse the time and skill level required to mount it. Energy, water, and manufacturing are among the most exposed sectors.

**OpenAI confirms zero retention for its frontier models**

The company reaffirms in an official post that its eligible API customers can use its frontier models without any data being retained after processing. It takes the opportunity to unveil in preview Private Safety Processing, presented as a way to apply advanced security controls without touching customer data confidentiality.

**How OpenAI plans to outpace Anthropic on privacy**

Private Safety Processing monitors signals of abuse across multiple linked conversations, rather than only within an isolated session: an automated agent can thus detect the development of malware spread across several exchanges, without any human reviewing the conversations. When triggered, OpenAI receives only a restricted signal, then decides whether to contact the customer. The target is openly stated: Anthropic's 30-day retention policy on its "covered" models, which worries privacy-sensitive enterprises.

**Cybersecurity researchers lose access to OpenAI's lightweight models**

The Trusted Access for Cyber program grants access to models such as GPT-5.6 Sol with relaxed guardrails, for defensive research. On Wednesday, several researchers saw ChatGPT's Cyber page tell them that their identity could not be verified or that their account was ineligible. All of them are outside the United States and Europe. OpenAI speaks of a technical error affecting a limited number of users and asks those concerned to redo the verification procedure.

**Bill Gates' reactor targets AI data centers**

TerraPower is about to announce its first nuclear data center project, with construction set to begin in 2027. Its argument is not raw power, **345 megawatts**, but thermal energy storage that allows production to be adjusted very quickly without touching the nuclear reaction itself. This is precisely what AI data centers demand, whose electricity consumption comes in violent spikes depending on training and inference phases. Meta had already signed in January for eight Natrium plants.
