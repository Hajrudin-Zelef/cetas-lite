OpenAI presented "Jalapeño" on Tuesday at the Hot Chips conference, its very first in-house accelerator, designed with Broadcom. SemiAnalysis's independent tests give it the win against Nvidia's GB200 and GB300: **1.5 to 1.9 times more throughput per kilowatt**, latency **1.7 to 3.6 times lower**, and **700 watts** of consumption versus **1,400 watts** for a GB300. The entire tech press picked up the information during the day.

**What Jalapeño actually does:**

- It's an **inference-only** chip: it runs already-trained models, it doesn't train any. It's optimized for the two real bottlenecks of LLMs, the prefill phase and communication between chips.

- SemiAnalysis benchmarks (InferenceX suite) on **GPT-OSS 120B, DeepSeek R1 670B and Kimi K2.5 1T**, with about **1,400 tokens per second** on GPT-OSS 120B.

- It handles **2.1 to 4.1 times more interactive load** than a GB200 or a GB300, with **50% more HBM memory per watt**.

- It didn't face Vera Rubin directly, but it exceeds the throughput-per-megawatt figures published in July by Nvidia and CoreWeave for Rubin.

- Design started mid-2024, frozen in November 2025. For now only **engineering samples** exist: first units late 2026, volume ramp-up in 2027.

A first-generation chip that beats the market leader almost never happens. "Usually, first-generation chips aren't competitive, and here OpenAI beats Nvidia's Blackwell and even Rubin," summarizes Dylan Patel, head of SemiAnalysis. You'll never buy a Jalapeño, but you'll see its effect: every watt saved on inference is less cost on OpenAI's products. The company is simultaneously maintaining its agreements with Nvidia, AMD and AWS, which mostly looks like a nice negotiating position.

OpenAI announces it has dismantled a network of Russian accounts that used ChatGPT to fuel a Western influence operation, built around an entirely fictitious think tank, the "International Burke Institute." Of the **36 articles** published by this institute between September 2025 and May 2026, **34 were plagiarized**.

**The key points:**

- The IBI website, registered in February 2025, claimed to be based in Israel and cited **Francis Fukuyama and Noam Chomsky** as intellectual endorsements, without the slightest real connection to them.

- The operators used **VPNs** to bypass OpenAI's geographic blocking. ChatGPT was used to write, translate, produce summaries in Russian for a second operator, and above all to erase the linguistic markers that betray a Russian pen.

- The institute published an in-house "sovereignty index," which unsurprisingly ranked Russia ahead of Western democracies.

- Distribution on X, LinkedIn, Facebook, Substack and Telegram, including a German-language channel named "Lahme Ente," hostile to the EU, Ukraine and the German government, with 10,000 to 20,000 subscribers.

- Target countries: Germany, United States, France, Poland, Turkey. OpenAI classifies the operation as **category 3 out of 6** on the Brookings scale: credible infrastructure, tiny real audience.

The cost of manufacturing propaganda has just dropped to zero, but the bottleneck hasn't budged an inch: you still need someone to read it. An operation capable of producing in five languages, with a fake institute, a fake tip and fake intellectual sponsors, ended up with an audience of a confidential blog. The most interesting detail remains this one: it wasn't a state or a social network that blew the final whistle, but the model provider, which sees the prompts go by.


Anthropic merged on Tuesday Claude's memory (the chat) with that of Claude Cowork, its office agent that handles documents, code and tasks. What you talk about in a conversation, the agent already knows at the moment it takes action. The rollout took place the same day, **on the Free, Pro and Max plans**, on web, desktop and mobile.

**In detail:**

- A single memory shared between the two products. Anthropic's example: you prepare a conference in the chat (number of participants, city, speakers), then Cowork drafts the agenda without you having to re-enter anything.

- The memory fills up **as the conversation goes**, and no longer via a summary at the end. You can therefore switch from the chat to the agent mid-way.

- Everything Claude has retained becomes **viewable, editable and deletable, topic by topic**.

- By default, Claude records neither health data, nor ethnic origin, nor gender identity, nor religion, nor political opinions. An "include sensitive topics" setting reactivates them, and the app warns on every sensitive recording. Never stored, however: identity documents, social security numbers, criminal records, immigration status.

- The limit: it's impossible to partition the chat's memory from Cowork's, unless you open two separate accounts. Claude Code, for now, keeps its memory separate. App update required on iOS and Android.

Permanent rebriefing is what makes most agents painful on a daily basis: you spend more time re-explaining the context than retrieving finished work. It's also the right moment to go look closely at this memory setting, because a single memory means a single file on you, which now follows the assistant everywhere. At least it's finally readable and erasable line by line.
