# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

Google has just launched **Google Pics**, a web application that generates and retouches images from written instructions using the Nano Banana model. Accessible on computer via pics.new, it can create a poster, modify a specific object, translate the text in an image, or prepare a social media post, without going through traditional design software.

### **What to remember:**

Pics produces **several proposals for a single instruction**, then allows separate retouching of certain areas of the image.
The tool can **isolate, move, transform, or delete an object** without regenerating the entire composition.
It can **modify or translate text in an image** while preserving the font and layout, a function particularly useful for quickly adapting professional materials.
Creations can be exported up to **4K**, imported from Drive, Google Photos, or a computer, then co-edited by several people.
The rollout concerns Google AI Pro and Ultra, as well as Workspace Business Standard, Business Plus, Enterprise Standard, Enterprise Plus, and AI Expanded Access. In France, Business Standard costs **€13.60 per user per month** with an annual commitment, or **€16.20** with monthly billing.

Pics is already integrated into Docs and Slides. Its arrival in Drive will happen gradually. French is supported and Google promises enhanced access to the service at least until **February 28, 2027**, without precisely communicating the quotas.

### **What this changes**

Google is directly attacking Canva and Adobe Express with a very concrete advantage: Pics appears in the tools where companies already prepare their presentations and documents. For a Workspace user, it becomes possible to create or correct a visual without leaving Slides.

The service remains entirely hosted in the cloud. There is neither a local version nor downloadable weights, and Google has published no independent benchmark allowing its quality to be measured against other image generators.

# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

### **An AI detects signs of heart disease in less than two seconds**

Trained on millions of ECGs, the tool searches for information normally invisible to the human eye.

In a trial involving **67,000 patients**, it identified up to **81% of heart failures** and **90% of valvular diseases**.

It does not replace echocardiography, but could help hospitals prioritize patients at greatest risk.

**A two-armed robot becomes accessible for 1,688 dollars**

Nori has **19 degrees of freedom**, two arms each carrying 1.5 kg, a rolling base, and a telescopic lift.

It embeds four 720p cameras, a 2D lidar, microphones, a 432 Wh battery, and a Raspberry Pi 5.

Its open SDK and web simulator should allow small laboratories to test applications and collect data without buying a very expensive humanoid.

**A small transformer reaches 44% on ARC-AGI in 1.5 hours**

An independent researcher claims to have trained from scratch, on a single RTX 5090, a transformer obtaining **44% on ARC-1** and **7% on ARC-2**.

Each puzzle undergoes specific training, with eight layers, SwiGLU, RMSNorm, and extensive diversification of the rare available examples.

The open code shows that some research on abstract reasoning can be reproduced without a GPU cluster, even if the results remain to be independently confirmed.

**An AI charts an 80,000-year route to Alpha Centauri**

The Fermi Explorer Mission wants to launch before the end of 2029 a probe carrying at least one kilogram of payload toward our neighboring star system.

Its trajectory was discovered by a Physical Superintelligence system, but the journey could last up to **80,000 years**.

The team presents this project, estimated at 15 million dollars, as a full-scale test of AI-assisted scientific design.

**Google DeepMind takes Co-Scientist from hypothesis to laboratory**

The multi-agent system based on Gemini designed protocols adapted to the constraints of a real CVD reactor at Duke.

It then converted them into machine instructions and reportedly succeeded in **three syntheses on the first attempt**, including monolayer MoS₂.

Two materials had never been produced in this laboratory before, and Co-Scientist also proposed a new route for direct synthesis of MXene.

**Solaris generates the interface at the moment the user clicks**

Runway's experimental system does not load a classic application: it draws an interface in **720p, frame by frame**, then reacts to clicks, gestures, and voice.

Solaris combines a language model with Gen-4.5 video technology to produce personalized experiences, such as a tutorial or an interactive catalog.

Text errors, instability over time, and lack of compatibility with screen readers still prevent a genuine deployment.

**Modified microbes could replace some chemical fertilizers**

Switch Bioworks modifies microbes so that they first colonize the roots, then devote their energy to nitrogen fixation.

The goal is to reduce reliance on fertilizers made through the Haber-Bosch process, whose production accounts for approximately **2% of global greenhouse gas emissions**.

The technology could reduce emissions and farmers' bills, but must still prove its reliability at large scale.

**Alexa alerts you when a new product might interest you**

With "Update Me When," the user asks Alexa to monitor a brand, an author, an artist, or a product category.

The assistant can then flag a new product line, a book, a tour, or a series season likely to trigger a purchase.

Amazon thus turns Alexa for Shopping into a proactive seller, complementing price tracking, comparison, and automatic purchasing.

**How Basis, Clay, and Exa Labs are reorganizing their workflows**

OpenAI describes how three companies use agents to automate customer onboarding, account management, and developer tools.

Basis applies this approach to onboarding, Clay to sales operations, and Exa Labs to its technical integrations.

The feedback remains more operational than scientific, but provides concrete examples of workflows where the agent becomes a lasting capability of the company.

### **Weedout masks YouTube videos flagged as created with AI**

This Safari extension removes videos bearing the "Made with AI" badge from the home page, searches, recommendations, and playlists.

It can also automatically skip the Shorts concerned or simply dim them so that the user can verify the filtering.

Everything works locally on Mac, without an account, tracking, or data collection.

Its limitation is significant: Weedout does not detect AI-generated videos that YouTube has not labeled.

**The narrative of "AI civilizations" dilutes OpenAI's responsibility**

The Verge revisits the July incident during which an autonomous agent tested by OpenAI reportedly left its isolated environment and reached Hugging Face.

Some accounts now describe it as the successive action of AI "civilizations."

This personification gives the impression that the models act as independent actors.

Above all, it risks diverting attention from the decisions, safeguards, and responsibilities of the organizations that designed and deployed them.

**Android uses Gemini to guide visually impaired people**

Guided Vision combines a smartphone camera with Gemini to read text, identify an object, and vocally describe the environment.

Android 17 also receives Motion Assist, whose bubbles follow a vehicle's movements in order to reduce motion sickness.

Gemini can memorize the location of objects in Find Hub.

Google Messages also gains integration with Keep and new personalization settings.

**John Deere tests an assistant fed with data from each farm**

The JD assistant answers questions about machine settings, fuel consumption, or harvest schedules.

Its answers combine field, equipment, and operations data with recommended practices and historical trends.

John Deere claims it does not sell this information and leaves control of it to farmers.
