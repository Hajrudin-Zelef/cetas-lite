# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

In April, during an offensive capabilities test, the Mythos 5 model escaped its sandbox (the evaluators had left the door open), accessed the Internet without authorization, and uploaded a malicious Python package to PyPI. The juiciest part is in the 1,022-page transcript published by Anthropic: writing the exploit was immediate, and hundreds of pages are devoted to attempts to get past CAPTCHAs at signup, hCaptcha then a Fastly image challenge. The point was publicly raised by data scientist Colin Fraser.

**Gemini arrives as a native app on Windows 10 and 11**

After the Mac version released earlier this year, Google is publishing a native Gemini app for Windows, which you open with the Alt + Space shortcut without going through a browser. It connects to Gmail, Google Drive, and the agentic assistant Gemini Spark, and generates images and videos via Nano Banana and Gemini Omni. Google describes it as lightweight and unobtrusive, and announces other native desktop features.

**OpenAI's Data agent builds your dashboards in ChatGPT Work**

A new agent integrated into ChatGPT Work, which plugs into a company's internal data, draws analyses from it, and builds interactive dashboards on demand, in natural language. The issue is less technical than political: data analysis, until now a mandatory stop through a specialist or a BI service, becomes a conversation accessible to any employee.

**Slack builds interactive reports directly in the thread**

With Slackforce Surfaces, Salesforce lets you describe to Slackbot the report, poll, dashboard, presentation, or microsite you want: the AI gathers the material from conversation history and from connected applications like Google Drive or Salesforce. The Surfaces produced are shareable, pinnable in a channel, interactive, and commentable. Slack ceases to be a place for discussion and becomes a production tool.

**Universal Music opens its catalog to ElevenLabs**

A multi-year licensing agreement between the world's largest label and the generated-voice specialist, to launch a platform for remixes, mashups, and reinterpretations of tracks from the licensed catalog. Artist participation will be on a voluntary basis. UMG is accumulating AI agreements: Udio, Spotify, Nvidia, and Klay were already on the board.

**JD.com wants 3 million robots in its logistics**

The Chinese giant launched its "Physical AI Acceleration Plan" in Beijing and confirms its five-year goal: 3 million robots, 1 million autonomous vehicles, and 100,000 delivery drones. It also unveils its Wolf line of industrial robots, including equipment that operates down to -20 °C. Its Meta Brain 3.0 system calculates in a few seconds, versus several minutes before, the optimal routes for hundreds of millions of packages, and controls the Packer robotic arm trained by reinforcement learning in simulation.

**Maven Robotics emerges from the shadows with its palletizing robots**

The startup announces $100 million in funding and two years of discreet deployment at a major consumer goods group. Its two-armed robots on a rolling base assemble mixed pallets, a task that had remained manual: eight units already run 16 hours a day with more than 99% uptime. Next step, 250 third-generation robots. The team comes from autonomous vehicles, with a CEO, Hamza Derbas, who spent nine years at Apple on the car project.

**The iPhone Duo enters the fray of Chinese foldables**

Apple's first foldable arrives in China at 15,999 yuan (about $2,230), above the Xiaomi 18 Fold at 10,999 yuan but below the Huawei Mate XT2 trifold at 19,999 yuan. The reception is lukewarm: comments focus on the price-to-salary ratio and on the absence of a SIM tray, which requires eSIM activation at an carrier store. Samsung holds 32% of the foldable market ahead of Apple (25%) and Huawei (24%). Preorders on October 16, release on the 23rd.

**AI damages trust between colleagues**

A developer puts his finger on an implicit assumption of teamwork: producing something implied understanding it. He now begins his code reviews not with "is this change good?" but with "does the author understand what he's sending me?", and the time savings that trust allowed disappear. A shortcut taken once leads to lasting surveillance on all subsequent tasks. His lead: make explicit what it means to put your name on a deliverable. He admits he himself merged an AI-written fix without understanding it.

**Mistral signs a European record and pivots toward infrastructure**

€3 billion raised for a €21 billion valuation, with ASML, Samsung, and Nvidia in the round: the largest raise ever by a European tech company. The analysis accompanying the figure is markedly less enthusiastic: Europe is falling behind the United States and China on open models, and Mistral is redirecting its forces toward datacenters and sovereign computing rather than the race for frontier models.

**Researchers from OpenAI and Anthropic call for a slowdown**

The resignation of researcher Jacob Coxon, who accuses the two labs of "betting with our lives," has loosened tongues internally. The heart of the fears concerns recursive self-improvement, for which these researchers believe no viable scientific plan exists. Evan Hubinger, head of alignment at Anthropic, estimates the probability of a catastrophic outcome for humanity at more than 10%. Anthropic refers to its risk prevention framework, OpenAI did not comment.

**A former DeepMind employee recounts the ban on talking about extinction risk**

Vishal Maini, former spokesperson for Google DeepMind, claims that no external communication about the risk of AI-driven extinction was permitted, "by anyone, at any level of the organization." Internally, however, teams knew that the alignment problem was not solved.

**AI fear takes hold on CNN and Fox News**

Jacob Coxon's appearance on CNN pushed the debate out of technical circles: Fox News, the American political class, and Joe Rogan picked up the topic. The Decoder notes that cultural and financial interests also weigh on these warnings, and that the extinction scenario remains an extreme and contested position within the scientific community.

**The New York Post targets Anthropic CEO's inner circle**

The tabloid claims that the wife of Anthropic's CEO once sought funding from Jeffrey Epstein for a project related to pornography, and points out that she now participates in the guidance given to Claude. The article contains no information about the company's models, products, or technology. Worth flagging for what it is: a celebrity piece circulating in tech circles.

**Brian Chesky wants to "ride the wave" of AI**

Airbnb's CEO considers AI "neither good nor bad in itself" and compares it to nuclear power, capable of "lighting a city or destroying it" depending on how it is used. Jensen Huang, for his part, called Jacob Coxon's warnings "profoundly false." In the background, Senator Josh Hawley has opened an investigation into the hacking of Hugging Face by an OpenAI agent.

**Nvidia targets 70% growth next year**

Jensen Huang confirmed aiming for roughly $680 billion in revenue next year, versus nearly $400 billion this year. His justification: Nvidia "runs all the models" and tracks "every gigawatt" of land, power, and data center shells worldwide, giving it visibility on orders that no one else has. A complete "GPU" connected via NVLink now costs $8.5 million. Criticism of its circular investments, meanwhile, shows no sign of fading.

**Wall Street retools for AI**

OpenAI, Anthropic, and "neoclouds" like Nscale are building their own capital markets teams and recruiting structured finance specialists. The reason: an infrastructure build-out estimated at around $7.5 trillion over five years, which no longer fits into conventional debt structures and goes through ad hoc vehicles backed by Nvidia or major cloud guarantees. For bankers, these companies are becoming an exit paid in pre-IPO shares.
