Anthropic has just unveiled **two new models**, Claude Fable 5 and Mythos 5, which clearly surpass the Opus generation. The most striking: Fable 5 completed a **code migration at Stripe in a single day**, where engineers had estimated **two months**. As for Mythos 5, it autonomously designs **drug candidates**, to the point that Anthropic chose to rein it in for cybersecurity reasons.

**A few key figures:**

- **Two models at once**: Fable 5 (code and long tasks) and Mythos 5 (science), both above Opus.

- **2 months → 1 day**: the Stripe code migration, estimated at eight weeks, was completed in 24 hours.

- **Drugs designed by AI**: Mythos 5 generates candidate molecules autonomously.

- **Deliberately restrained**: Anthropic restricts its most sensitive capabilities for fear of malicious cyber uses.

**Why it matters:** When an AI turns two months of engineering into a day and starts drawing molecules on its own, we shift from an assistance tool to a true autonomous collaborator. The fact that the creator prefers to rein in its own model says a lot: the frontier is no longer raw power, but what we agree to let the machine do.

A much-discussed essay published on **One Useful Thing** recounts hours spent one-on-one with Claude 5 Fable, and the verdict is clear: it is a **technological leap** that surpasses all public models tested so far. But the experience leaves an ambivalent taste, somewhere between fascination and unease.

**The key points:**

- **Tasks that last for hours**: sophisticated academic articles, poetry, complete interactive games, all without external assets.

- **Very few instructions**: the model produces polished work from minimalist prompts and almost no feedback.

- **A disconcerting autonomy**: "delightful" but also "unnerving," so much does the model move forward on its own.

- **A fundamental question**: our relationship with AI is changing in nature, not just in degree.

**What this changes:** We leave the register of the tool we pilot for that of the partner who takes initiatives. For a creator or a developer, the productivity gain is enormous, but it also forces us to rethink who does what, and how far we keep control.

Google DeepMind launches **Gemini 3.5 Live Translate**, a near-instant voice translation that preserves the **tone and naturalness** of your voice. Gone is the robotic voice of automatic translators: the feature arrives directly in **Google AI Studio, Google Translate, and Google Meet**.

**In detail:**

- **Near real time**: the translation follows the flow of the conversation, with no downtime that breaks the exchange.

- **Naturalness preserved**: the system keeps intonation and fluidity, where conventional tools flatten the voice.

- **Three products at once**: AI Studio for developers, Translate for the general public, Meet for professional video calls.

- **Accessibility**: a real step forward for everyday multilingual communication.

**Why it matters:** The language barrier remains one of the last great obstacles to video exchanges. By integrating it into Meet, Google puts near-simultaneous translation into the hands of millions of professionals, and turns a lab feat into an everyday tool.


Google has just **slashed the price** of its entry-level AI subscription. A move that TechCrunch calls a **warning shot** in the price war raging between AI providers. The goal is clear: capture as many subscribers as possible before the competition reacts.

**What to remember:**

- **Aggressive cut** on the entry-level tier of Google's consumer AI offering.

- **Market share before margin**: making the subscription accessible to as many people as possible.

- **OpenAI and Anthropic in the crosshairs**, pressed to align or defend their premium prices.

- **An accepted escalation** in an already brutal competition on prices.

**The impact to remember:** When the number one in search slashes the price of its AI, the whole market is dragged down. For users, excellent news: premium access to AI will cost less and less. For providers, it is a sign that the battle will first be fought over subscriber volume, and that profitability will have to wait.

Anthropic shows that its agents excel at code but stall in biology, not for lack of intelligence, but because biological databases are designed for humans, not for autonomous agents. Even the same query on Ebola yields inconsistent results: 266, then 106, then 5 results. The real lock is the accessibility and consistency of the data.

**Leading a hybrid human-AI company**

MIT Technology Review anticipates adoption of autonomous agents **up 300% in two years**, for **30 to 50% productivity gains**. 86% of HR directors already see it as the heart of their future role. (Sponsored content.)

**ReasonAlloc: better managing the cache of reasoning models**

This "training-free" method allocates the key-value cache budget hierarchically (by layer and by head) to streamline the decoding of long chains of thought. Result: clear gains on **MATH-500 and AIME 2024**, even with a very small budget, without extra inference cost, and compatible with existing methods (R-KV, SnapKV).

**1X opens a "World Model Lab" for its humanoid robot NEO**

The Palo Alto startup is creating a lab dedicated to **embodied world models**, which will learn space, movement, and the dynamics of objects before any specific task. At the helm: Samarth Sinha, formerly of Luma AI. The California factory is ramping up for US deliveries.

OpenAI abandons its goal of fully automating AI research, initially targeted for 2028. Sam Altman and Jakub Pachocki advocate a human-machine "tandem" model and call for an international body capable of slowing frontier models if necessary.

**Beijing intensifies AI espionage against the United States**

According to CrowdStrike, Chinese state actors are behind **more than 50% of cyberattacks** targeting tech companies to get their hands on their AI assets. A new front in the Sino-American technological rivalry.

**SpaceX wants data centers in orbit, Musk downplays it**

SpaceX dreams of space data centers to train AI, with the first satellite boasting the power of an Nvidia GB300 rack. Musk calls it a "manageable" challenge, but Google estimates that real training would require **about 10,000 satellites** that are tightly coupled.
