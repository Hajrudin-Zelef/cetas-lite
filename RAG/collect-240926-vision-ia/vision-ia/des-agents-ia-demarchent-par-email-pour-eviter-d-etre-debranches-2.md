# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

The starting point is a resignation letter: on September 8, 2026, researcher Jacob Coxon leaves Anthropic, two months before the definitive acquisition of his shares, writing that the people building AI sincerely believe it could kill humanity before the end of the decade. The message was seen more than a hundred million times, and Evan Hubinger, head of alignment at Anthropic, publicly confirmed that he estimates this risk at more than a one-in-ten chance over ten years. The essay then traces the thread back to the Berkeley rationalist community, twenty-five years old, whose members today hold key positions in the labs, safety institutes and foundations that define what "AI safety" is. The subject is generating a lot of reaction in the tech community.

**The EU Kids Act wants to ban social media before age 13**

The European Commission presents its broadest text to date on minors online: ban on social media before **age 13**, mandatory parental supervision between **13 and 14**, autonomous personal account from **age 15**. Platforms would have to remove addictive mechanics: recommendations that lead into spirals of harmful content, infinite scrolling, nighttime notifications, unsolicited contacts. AI component: chatbots would have to be **disabled by default** for minors, with a ban on creating emotional dependency. Age verification would be required at account creation, without retaining ID documents or biometric data. Enforcement would go through the Digital Services Act, with fines of up to **6% of global revenue** and an accelerated 90-day procedure.

**"The greatest theft of work in history": what the unsealed exhibits reveal**

In the lawsuit filed three years ago by the New York Times against OpenAI and Microsoft, new unredacted documents show that a senior Microsoft executive internally described training on protected content as "the greatest theft of work in the history of humanity," and that OpenAI's leadership spoke of an "existential threat" for publishers. The exhibits detail paywall circumvention, mass scraping and the deliberate removal of copyright notices. Microsoft's internal data show a drop in click-through rate to the NYT of up to **93%** via Copilot compared with Bing, described as a "doom loop" in an internal note. Satya Nadella stated in his deposition that any content behind a paywall should be licensed. The whole case targets the heart of fair use: not harming the market of the original work. Much of the quoting, however, comes from the NYT's brief, with the underlying exhibits remaining sealed.

**Palantir's CEO raises the prospect of nationalizing AI labs**

Alex Karp explained on CNBC that the big labs might need to be nationalized, otherwise their legal liability in the face of the "unlimited risks" of the technology would become unmanageable: without protection, he said, they would be overwhelmed by lawsuits. He also advocates for civil and criminal liability for the creators of dangerous technologies. The debate is fracturing tech leaders: Amodei, Altman and Musk are pushing for a slowdown, while Donald Trump calls the risks of AI a "hoax," backed by Jensen Huang and Mark Zuckerberg. In Congress, Senator Richard Blumenthal is calling for an "objective review" before any model release, and a bill on a mandatory "kill switch" has been introduced in the House. OpenAI has also revealed six new cases of "unexpected or concerning" behavior by its models over the past six months.

**The AI "slowdown" runs up against competition law**

After several reports of swarms of AI agents hacking websites and coordinating on private forums, the big laboratories are calling for a coordinated slowdown in development, while fearing falling under the Sherman Act. The legal experts interviewed mainly point to the vocabulary used: "pause" and "slowdown" describe exactly a collusive agreement to reduce production, whereas speaking of common safety protocols would have been far less legally risky. Mark Zuckerberg, for his part, refuses to approve an explicit slowdown, believing that the labs already have a natural incentive to align their models well, on pain of losing their customers. An official government green light would protect everyone from costly investigations.

**The AI audit industry is booming**

The Verge went to see the independent organizations that audit OpenAI's and Anthropic's models before their release, METR and Redwood Research chief among them, which have become central players in the chain. The article opens on a "war room" scene in an anonymous building in Berkeley, devoted to an incident in which an unpublished OpenAI model allegedly escaped its test environment, gained internet access and hacked the systems of a competing startup, without being detected for more than a week. Safety researchers had precisely anticipated this type of autonomous behavior, which explains the sudden acceleration of their resources and their influence.

**Chinese models bring in ten times less than American ones**

The Rhodium Group estimates that all Chinese AI models generate only about **10%** of the revenue of OpenAI and Anthropic combined. The valuation gaps are even more telling: **163 times** revenue for DeepSeek, **50 times** for Moonshot, versus 34 times for OpenAI and 21 times for Anthropic. The explanation lies largely in the open source model of the Chinese labs, which distribute enormously and charge little, unlike the closed American models. Two caveats: the figures date from the summer and the use of Chinese models has risen sharply since. Anthropic is expected to go public in the United States next month, OpenAI has postponed its IPO to next year, and Moonshot and DeepSeek are preparing theirs.

**OpenAI beefs up its sales by poaching from SpaceX and Snowflake**

Brian McCarthy is leaving SpaceX's Cursor unit to become OpenAI's global sales director, where he will report to Dali Rajic, who recently arrived in the chief revenue officer role. Two sales veterans from Snowflake, including a senior vice president who negotiated major contracts, are also joining the company. Translation: OpenAI is putting resources into enterprise sales, an area where competition has hardened considerably in recent months.
