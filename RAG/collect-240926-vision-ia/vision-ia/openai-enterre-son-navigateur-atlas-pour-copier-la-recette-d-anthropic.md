# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

<!-- source: https://vision-ia.beehiiv.com/p/fin-openai -->

OpenAI has just announced the closure of **ChatGPT Atlas**, its in-house browser launched with great fanfare in October 2025. Less than a year later, the tool will be shut down on **August 9, 2026**. The reason is stated outright by the company: stop side projects, the famous "side quests," to refocus on productivity and catch up with Anthropic, which has become the reference for AI-assisted work.

Key takeaways:

- Atlas closes permanently on **August 9, 2026**, barely **ten months** after its launch.

- OpenAI merges Atlas, ChatGPT, and Codex into a single desktop application called **ChatGPT Work**.

- ChatGPT Work is presented as "an agent capable of acting in your applications and your files, staying on a project for hours, and transforming an objective into finished work."

- The company acknowledges wanting to cut back its peripheral projects to concentrate its forces on a single front: the work of professionals.

**What this changes:** seeing the industry leader kill one of its flagship products to imitate a competitor's "productivity-first" strategy is a rare reversal of fortune. The message sent to users is crystal clear: the future of consumer AI is no longer played out in a smart browser, but in agents capable of executing work on your behalf, from the first click to the finished result. OpenAI is no longer trying to occupy the field everywhere; it is choosing its battles, and it is choosing them where Anthropic has gotten ahead of it.

The era of the "all-inclusive" AI plan is coming to an end. Anthropic is preparing to charge for access to **Claude Fable 5**, its best consumer model, on a usage basis: concretely, additional fees for subscribers who want the top tier. The cause of this tightening comes down to three letters and a number: GPT-5.6 Sol, OpenAI's newcomer, which is reigniting a brutal price war.

A few key figures:

- **GPT-5.6 Sol** scores **59 points** on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, just **one point** behind Claude Fable 5.

- It costs **$1.04 per task**, roughly **three times cheaper** than Anthropic's flagship model.

- In agentic coding, Sol outpaces **all** its competitors, which further intensifies the pricing pressure.

- Result: Anthropic abandons the unlimited plan and switches Fable 5 to usage-based pricing.

**The impact to remember:** when a rival reaches 98% of your performance for a third of the price, maintaining an unlimited subscription becomes economically untenable. For users, the news has two faces. On one hand, the best of Claude may cost more for those who use it intensively. On the other, this fierce competition drives overall prices down and makes cutting-edge AI more affordable elsewhere. Today's good deal may well be found on the challenger's side, not the leader's.

ByteDance, TikTok's parent company, is launching **Seedream 5.0 Pro**, an image generation model that claims to no longer merely draw, but to "understand design." According to the announcement, this is the first Chinese version to come so close to the benchmark level held by **OpenAI Images 2.0**.

In detail:

- Marked progress on **text rendering**, **structure**, and **alignment**, long the Achilles' heel of image generators.

- Convincing results on **infographics** and professional visuals, not just pretty illustrations.

- A **precision editing** feature with layer separation, as in real design software.

- A frontal, openly claimed positioning against Western frontier models.

**Why it matters:** image generation has for years stumbled over two stubborn pitfalls, readable text and clean layout. If Seedream keeps its promises, producing a complete infographic or a fully composed poster in seconds becomes realistic for anyone. And once again, China demonstrates that it is no longer content to follow: it is closing its gap with American leaders at a speed that is starting to make one dizzy.

Meta is in turn entering programming AI with **Muse Spark 1.1**, a tool capable of generating code, fixing bugs, and automating code migrations at large scale. According to TechCrunch, the company is aiming directly at large organizations and their heaviest technical tasks.

The essential points:

- Muse Spark 1.1 handles **agentic workloads** and **massive code migrations**, exactly the type of automation that companies are demanding.

- Meta arrives in an already **very crowded** market, facing **Cursor**, **GitHub Copilot**, and **Windsurf**.

- The launch was **picked up by 6 sources** within hours, a sign of the attention the topic attracts.

- The direction is clearly enterprise, with a central promise: productivity and automation.

**The context:** AI that writes code has become the most contested battlefield in the industry, and seeing Meta join in confirms that software development automation is worth billions. Even without being a developer, the effects will be felt: software designed faster, updates delivered continuously, and growing pressure on technical jobs. When the five biggest names in tech are fighting on the same ground, it means the next great transformation of work is being decided there.

# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

**Humanoid robots perform their first surgery on live pigs****.** Researchers at **UC San Diego** had teleoperated humanoids perform two real operations, including a **gallbladder removal**. Notably, the second procedure was carried out by a duo of robots **with no human surgeon** at the table. Nicknamed "Surgie" (1.50 m, 27 kg), the machines handle standard surgical instruments. Work published in **Nature**.

**Anthropic discovers the "J-space," the hidden place where Claude really thinks****.** By building a tool called the **Jacobian lens**, Anthropic uncovered a hidden zone inside Claude Opus 4.6, the "J-space," which contains the words the model is about to say before it even formulates them. The discovery confirms in black and white that a model can do something entirely different from what it claims to be doing. An interactive demo is available with Neuronpedia.

**An OpenAI AI beats all humans at AtCoder****.** At the world finals of the **AtCoder 2026** competitive programming contest, an OpenAI system solved **all five problems** in the Algorithm division, two of which were deemed exceptionally difficult, crushing the best programmers on the planet. A new milestone for machine algorithmic reasoning.

**Microsoft opens Aurora 1.5, its weather forecasting model****.** Microsoft is updating **Aurora 1.5**, its foundation model for Earth systems, with **22 additional weather variables**, hourly forecasts, and probabilistic ensembles. The model is **open source** on GitHub, with checkpoints on Hugging Face, enough to help researchers and developers anticipate climate and energy risks.

**Ollama raises $65 million and reaches 9 million users****.** The open-source tool that lets you run AI models directly on your own PC raises **$65 million**. With **176,000 stars** on GitHub and **9 million users**, Ollama has become the gateway to local AI, with no cloud and no subscription.

**Four nuclear microreactors reach criticality in the United States****.** Four American companies (Antares Nuclear, Valar Atomics, Deployable Energy, Aalo Atomics) have reached **criticality** with their microreactors, surpassing the goal of three set by the federal program. A caveat, however: criticality is a technical test, not proof that these reactors will soon power the grid with electricity.



# **🗞️MORE NEWS**

**Databricks adopts GLM 5.2, the Chinese open source model that matches Opus for 34% less****.** Databricks tested the Chinese model **GLM 5.2** on its own codebase of several million lines. Verdict: coding quality equivalent to **Claude Opus 4.8**, but at **$1.28 per task versus $1.94**, or **34% cheaper**. The company is making it its default daily coding engine. The lesson it draws: no single vendor dominates on its own, and it's better to build your own benchmarks than to trust public rankings.

**FableCut, the video editor controllable by AI agents****.** Spotted on Hacker News (**89 points**), FableCut is a video editor that runs directly in the browser, **with no external dependencies**, and designed to be controlled automatically by AI agents. An open-source project that hints at fully automated video editing, from script to export.

**Zhipu AI considers its own chip after the explosion of GLM-5.2****.** The Chinese lab **Zhipu AI** is studying the design of a custom **ASIC** chip after usage of its GLM-5.2 model **multiplied by 27 in a single week**. The idea: merge software and hardware to drive down costs against Nvidia GPUs. The partner has not been chosen and the project would take more than two years.

**A company wakes up to a $500 million token bill****.** The AI bill is exploding. **Uber** exhausted its 2026 AI budget as early as April, Microsoft revoked licenses granted a few months earlier, and one company accumulated a bill of **$500 million** for failing to set usage limits. The paradox: the price per million tokens has dropped **67% in a year** (from $18.40 to $6.07), but volumes are climbing even faster.

**Ello, an AI tutor that responds to children in real time****.** Presented on Hacker News, **Ello** is an AI tutor that teaches reading, mathematics, and English to children aged **4 to 9**. Its technical feat: responding at the speed of a conversation thanks to a custom architecture, while checking every exchange for safety, without ever breaking the pedagogical thread.

**Google labels ads created with AI****.** Google is adding a **"created or modified with AI"** notice in its "My Ad Center" for ads shown on **Search, Discover, and YouTube**. The label is automatic for ads designed with Google's own tools, and manual for those created elsewhere. One more step toward the traceability of generated content.

**The 1X Neo robot gets ultra-fast hands****.** The domestic humanoid robot **1X Neo** has been equipped with new tactile hands whose fingers are noticeably faster, a real leap in dexterity for accomplishing household chores. Home robotics is advancing, one precise gesture at a time, and approaching the moment when it will become truly useful in daily life.

**Robots, Tesla, and rescue helicopters: the grand bazaar of the UN AI summit****.** The UN's **"AI for Good"** summit mixed spectacular demonstrations (robot dogs, autonomous vehicles, rescue helicopters) with debates on global governance. The uncomfortable question remained in the background: can international institutions regulate a technology that is already moving faster than politics?

**Claude Reflect: Anthropic launches its "Spotify Wrapped" for AI****.** Anthropic is rolling out **Reflect**, a feature that shows Claude users an analysis of their usage (topics discussed, types of tasks, habits) over **1, 3, 6, or 12 months**. Directly inspired by Spotify Wrapped, it mainly helps users become aware of the place AI has ended up occupying in their daily lives.

**GPT-5.6 becomes the default model for Microsoft 365 Copilot****.** OpenAI announces that **GPT-5.6** now powers **Microsoft 365 Copilot** in Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Chat, and Cowork. The payoff: better-performing responses for hundreds of millions of users of the office suite, and a strengthened anchor for the partnership between OpenAI and Microsoft.

**A £30 AI blood test to avoid uterine cancer exams****.** The British NHS is rolling out a machine-learning-powered blood test that analyzes **30 markers** to assess the risk of uterine cancer before any invasive exam. In a trial involving **16,481 patients**, it shows a sensitivity of **99.1%**. Cost: **£30**, enough to spare biopsies and unpleasant exams for many low-risk women.

**Fidji Simo permanently leaves OpenAI's leadership****.** OpenAI's number two, **Fidji Simo**, is leaving her position after a medical leave longer than expected. The departure comes at a delicate moment: the company is considering an IPO and is chasing Anthropic in the enterprise market, with a void now at the top of its org chart.

**Gradium, the Parisian voice startup, raises 100 million backed by Nvidia****.** The French young company **Gradium**, specializing in AI voice, raises **100 million dollars** in seed funding, with the support of **Nvidia**. It plans to open an office in Silicon Valley to recruit talent there. An encouraging signal for European technological sovereignty, even if the magnet remains American.

**The New York Times accuses OpenAI of hiding evidence****.** In its copyright infringement lawsuit, the **New York Times** claims that OpenAI concealed tools and datasets used to identify protected articles in ChatGPT's responses. The newspaper is now calling for sanctions, escalating one of the most closely watched lawsuits in the entire AI industry.

**Microsoft beefs up its security patches thanks to AI****.** Microsoft now uses AI to spot vulnerabilities earlier in development, which will inflate the number of patches delivered with each **Windows 11** update. A direct response to the rise of automated attack tools, themselves also AI-powered. Cybersecurity is becoming a race between machines.

**Meta will launch production of its own AI chips in September****.** Meta will begin production of its in-house AI chips in **September 2026**, with a modular approach to adapt to a field that evolves at breakneck speed. The goal is twofold: reduce its dependence on external suppliers and regain control over its infrastructure costs, which are dizzying today.

**Three AI IPOs weigh more than 25 years of tech exits****.** The anticipated IPOs of **Anthropic, OpenAI, and SpaceX** are expected to generate more value than all exits of US venture-capital-backed startups since 2000. A single figure that sums up the disproportionate scale the sector has taken on in just a few years.

**Anduril's CEO considers an IPO in the middle of a "hype cycle" risky****.** Valued at **61 billion dollars**, Anduril is among the most expensive private startups in the world. Yet its CEO warns against a rushed IPO amid euphoria, preferring to wait in order to target a more solid valuation over the long term.
