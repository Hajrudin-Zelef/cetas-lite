# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

<!-- source: https://vision-ia.beehiiv.com/p/space-x-datacenter -->

Every week, a new AI tool makes a skill obsolete. Those who know how to use these tools save time, money, and clients. Those who don't watch others do it.

SpaceX has just opened the doors of its Colossus data center to AI companies seeking computing power. The first major contract, signed with the open source startup **Reflection**, could reach **$6.3 billion**. And Elon Musk's company has already convinced some big names, since **Anthropic, Google, and Cursor** are among its new clients.

**Key takeaways:**

- The contract with **Reflection** is valued at **$150 million per month**, from July 1, 2026, to 2029, totaling up to **$6.3 billion** (TechCrunch)

- Reflection gains access to the latest **Nvidia GB300** chips hosted in **Colossus 2**, near Memphis (Tennessee)

- **Anthropic, Google, and Cursor** have also reserved capacity on the platform

- SpaceX is transforming into a computing infrastructure provider, a market currently under extreme pressure

**Why it matters:** computing capacity has become the nerve center of the AI war, and seeing SpaceX join the very exclusive club of compute providers, alongside Microsoft, Amazon, or CoreWeave, says a lot about the gap between demand and supply. For open source labs like Reflection, it's the assurance of accessing cutting-edge hardware without having to build their own data centers, a strategic advantage that until now was reserved for giants funded with billions.

It's the video announcement of the moment. At Volcano Engine's FORCE conference, ByteDance unveiled **five new AI models**, including the star **Seedance 2.5**, capable of generating videos of **more than 30 seconds** in a single take. Enough to challenge tools like Sora.

**The essential points:**

- **Seedance 2.5** crosses the **30-second** threshold, a milestone that video generators had struggled to exceed until now

- ByteDance presented **five new models** at its **FORCE 2026** conference

- Commercial launch expected **early July 2026**

- The model positions itself as a direct competitor to market benchmarks, starting with **Sora**

**What it changes:** duration is the Achilles' heel of AI-generated video. Most tools cap out at around 5 to 10 seconds, which seriously limits narrative uses and forces creators to stitch dozens of clips together end to end. Crossing the 30-second mark opens the door to sequences that are genuinely usable for creators, editors, and brands. It's also a clear signal: ByteDance, TikTok's parent company sitting on a treasure trove of video data, intends to throw its full weight onto this terrain. The AI video battle, long dominated by American players, is taking a distinctly more international turn.

Japanese company **Sakana AI** unveils **Fugu**, a multi-agent orchestration system that provides access to a whole pool of models via a **single OpenAI-compatible endpoint**. Its goal: free companies from dependence on a single provider, and protect them from geopolitical uncertainties like export controls.

**In detail:**

- Fugu routes requests internally and, when needed, assembles a team of expert models (selection, delegation, verification, synthesis)

- Two tiers available: **Fugu standard** (low latency, coding) and **Fugu Ultra** (complex analyses, paper reproduction, patents)

- The agent pool is **entirely interchangeable**, making it possible to bypass a restricted or degraded provider on the fly

- Nearly **500 users** tested the system in beta, notably to automate full cybersecurity evaluation cycles

- Fugu Ultra claims performance comparable to cutting-edge closed models like **Fable 5** and **Mythos Preview**

**Why it matters:** vendor lock-in is a growing concern among companies. When a model disappears overnight due to a political decision, as shown by recent export controls targeting certain Anthropic models, an entire production chain can collapse. Fugu turns this dependency into a simple adjustment variable: if a provider becomes inaccessible, traffic is redirected without the end user noticing. A promise of resilience that speaks directly to IT leaders wary of the sector's volatility.


An internet user recreated the atmosphere of a crowded **World of Warcraft** server by filling it with **1,800 AI bots**, each connected to the **DeepSeek** API. The result: a chat channel teeming with life like in the good old days, for only **€43 per month**.

**A few figures:**

- **1,800 autonomous characters**, each with its own personality

- Total cost of about **€43 per month**, the price of a dinner, thanks to **DeepSeek**'s cut-rate pricing

- The bots chat, reminisce about old raids, and banter like real players

- The post garnered **1,100 upvotes** and dominated its forum's rankings

**The impact to remember:** beyond the nostalgic nod, the experiment illustrates a real economic shift. Running 1,800 conversational agents continuously would have cost a small fortune just two years ago. Today, the bill fits in a single note. When conversational intelligence becomes nearly free, this kind of whimsy comes within reach of any tinkerer, and we begin to glimpse what virtual worlds entirely populated by AI will look like.

**OpenAI launches GPT-5.5-Cyber, surpassing Anthropic's Mythos in cybersecurity**

OpenAI expands its **Daybreak** initiative with a specialized model, **GPT-5.5-Cyber**, and an updated Codex Security plugin. According to the company, the model outperforms Anthropic's **Mythos** on cybersecurity benchmarks. Above all, the approach pivots from simple vulnerability detection to their **automatic correction**, with a network of more than 25 security firms and several partner governments.

**Neural cellular automata with free particles**

A stunning interactive demonstration: autonomous particles, guided by rules learned by a neural network, move freely and form complex morphologies. Each particle follows a simple rule, but the whole self-organizes and even manages to regenerate after damage. A fine example of emergent behavior (44 points on Hacker News).

**Public trust in AI is collapsing**

According to a **Pew Research** poll, only **16%** of Americans think AI will have a positive impact on society within 20 years, compared with **40%** who anticipate a negative impact. Those under 30 are the most skeptical (14%). At the same time, a UN report predicts that data center electricity consumption will nearly double, to **945 TWh** by 2030, equivalent to Japan's consumption.

**Google DeepMind and A24 partner for AI cinema**

Google DeepMind and the studio **A24** are forming a long-term research partnership, accompanied by an investment of about **$75 million** from Google in the studio. Goal: explore how generative AI, and tools like Veo, can transform the creative process in cinema.

**A three-armed robot that cuts salmon into sashimi**

A Norwegian team has developed the **Sashimi-Bot**, equipped with three coordinated arms to position the fish, slice it with a knife and plate the slices with chopsticks. A **GelSight** tactile sensor allows it to "feel" the resistance of the flesh and adjust its cutting force, a concrete demonstration of AI robotics applied to a high-precision manual task.
