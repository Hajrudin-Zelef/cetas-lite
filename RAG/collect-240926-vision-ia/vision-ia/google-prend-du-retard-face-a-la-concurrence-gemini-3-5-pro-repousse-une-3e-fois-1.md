Every week, a new AI tool makes a skill obsolete. Those who know how to use these tools save time, money and clients. Those who don't, watch others do it.

According to Bloomberg, Google has once again postponed **Gemini 3.5 Pro**, its flagship model, for lack of results up to par, especially in **coding**. This is the **third consecutive delay** of a model promised for June at the I/O conference in May. Meanwhile, the AI pioneer is quietly being overtaken by Meta, xAI and the best Chinese models.

**Key takeaways:**

- Announced for **June 2026** at I/O, Gemini 3.5 Pro is still not here by mid-July. The latest stable version remains **Gemini 3.1 Pro**, released in February 2026.

- At the end of June, Google **retrained** the model with new code data: results deemed **"disappointing"** internally (Bloomberg cites ten current or former employees), rebuilt version unreliable and prone to hallucinations.

- Rivals, meanwhile, are moving ahead: OpenAI released **GPT-5.6 Sol** (88.8 on Terminal Bench 2.1), Meta launched **Muse Spark 1.1**, and Chinese Moonshot AI unveiled **Kimi K3** the same day, now **No. 1 on the Frontend Code Arena**, ahead of Claude Fable 5.

- Google is considering a **"stopgap"** (interim) release and is testing several models with its partners. Internally, **75%** of new code is already written by AI, compared with 50% at the end of 2025.

**What this changes:** Google invented the Transformer and is home to DeepMind, but it is no longer the benchmark in the race. Seeing a pioneer postpone its flagship model three times while China takes the lead in a coding ranking is a sign that technological lead can no longer be decreed: it is lost as quickly as it was gained.

Agility Robotics is opening a **5,600 m²** (60,000 sq ft) factory in **Fremont**, California, a few kilometers from the site where Tesla wants to build its Optimus. The difference comes down to one sentence: unlike Tesla, Agility **already** has a robot that works for real customers and brings in money.

**In detail:**

- **Digit**, Agility's humanoid: **1.75 m**, **65 kg**, 28 degrees of freedom, **16 kg** payload, **8 h** battery life, list price around **$250,000**.

- Already deployed at **Amazon, GXO, Schaeffler and Toyota**. At GXO, Digit has moved more than **100,000 bins** in real-world conditions. The company claims **$300M** in signed orders and more than **30 customers** in discussions.

- Agility is preparing its stock market listing via a **SPAC merger** (Churchill Capital Corp XI, **$2.5B** valuation, $620M raised), backed by **Amazon and Nvidia**: it would be the first pure-play humanoid listed in the United States.

- The **Digit v5** version, expected this fall, will for the first time be able to detect humans and work alongside them without a separate zone.

**The contrast:** Tesla promises, Musk talks about the "greatest product in history" and targets **1 million** Optimus per year, but still shows **no** quantified customer deployment. Agility, more discreet, is already cashing in. Its technical bet also goes against the all-generative trend: AI is used to program tasks and scale up, but **safety** remains outside the control of generative models ("we don't want to be creative with our safety stack," summarizes co-founder Damion Shelton).

Google is launching **Gemini Omni Flash** in **Google Vids**, a model it presents as **"the video equivalent of Nano Banana."** Concretely: you edit an existing video (filmed or AI-generated) by typing instructions, and you can generate an **avatar** that has your face and voice.

**The key points:**

- **Conversational editing**: change the lighting, erase a cluttered background, modify a product's color, a character's appearance, the camera angle or the voiceover, all with a simple prompt, without reshooting a single second.

- **Personal avatars**: you record your face and voice, you type a text, and the avatar "speaks" it in your place. Intended uses: social clips, customer welcome messages, ad storyboards.

- **The limits**: generation capped at **10 seconds** at launch (compared with 60 for OpenAI's Sora), invisible **SynthID** watermark applied by default, avatars reserved for **18 and over** in English only.

- **Availability**: **Google AI Plus, Pro and Ultra** subscribers and **Workspace** customers, plus **free access on YouTube Shorts**. An API is promised "in the coming weeks." Google Vids already claims more than **7 million** monthly users.

**Why it matters:** for a creator or freelancer, this means refreshing a video without bringing the camera back out, testing ten variants of an ad in one afternoon, or recording a client message without putting on makeup. Google is attacking **Sora** and **Runway** head-on in the professional arena, where AI video becomes a production tool rather than a gadget.

Starting **July 20**, Anthropic is scaling back access to **Claude Fable 5** and pushing its subscribers toward usage-based billing. On Reddit, users are calling it a model **"put in a cage,"** and one of them says they burned **$100 in credits in 9 minutes** on a single agent workflow.

**What changes on July 20:**

- The **Max** and **Team Premium** plans keep Fable 5, but at **50%** of the usual weekly limits, which are themselves cut by **a third** the same day.

- **Pro** and **Team Standard** subscribers lose included access: a **one-time $100 credit**, then billing at API rates.

- Those rates sting: **$10 per million tokens** for input, **$50** for output — **double Opus 4.8** and the highest price Anthropic has ever charged in general availability (with a 90% discount on cached tokens).

- A reminder of what Fable 5 is: the "5th generation" model launched on June 9, built for **long tasks**, multi-day coding, autonomous agents, analysis and PDF reading, with a **1 million token** context window.

**The context:** Anthropic cites demand that is "hard to manage" and the cost of its server capacity. But the timing betrays the real pressure: OpenAI's **GPT-5.6 Sol** reportedly offers comparable performance for about **a third of the price**, and Chinese models are slashing rates. A sign of the strain on compute, Anthropic is even reportedly in talks to **rent Meta's capacity** for up to $10 billion. The result: the most capable model on the market also becomes the most expensive and least accessible, at the risk of driving away the community that adopted it.
