# **🗞️MORE NEWS**

### **LibreOffice breaks all its records by promising the absence of AI**

Version 26.8, released on August 26, was downloaded more than a million times in a week, an absolute record for the free office suite, and that's without counting updates coming through Linux repositories. The Document Foundation assumed the reason in a post titled "Yes, the absence of AI is now a feature": no generative AI by default, because no offering on the market respects its five principles (execution controlled by the user, no content sent without authorization, zero telemetry, no dependence on a single vendor, no compromise on file formats). The foundation isn't closing the door, however: community plugins remain recommended for anyone who wants to add some. It takes the opportunity to take a swipe at competitors who use AI to justify price increases while keeping documents within their own infrastructure.

**Meta launches Muse, its personal AI agent, accessible from WhatsApp**

Available right now on iOS, Android, the Muse.ai website and via a simple message in WhatsApp, with the brand's AI glasses announced as the next step. The agent sends emails, books trips, sells a car or buys online via Stripe's Link infrastructure, with single-use card numbers and guaranteed free returns. Each user runs in their own isolated virtual machine, a "Secure VM" architecture meant to separate untrusted data coming from the web from the part of the agent that executes actions. Tested internally under the code name "Hatch," Muse is Meta Superintelligence Labs' late response to OpenClaw and Instinct. Free with limits, paid subscription for intensive use.

**Adobe brings its generators directly into the Premiere timeline**

The new Generative Media tool lets you select an empty gap on a video or audio track and generate the missing clip, video, sound effect, music or ambience, without leaving the project or opening a third-party application. The clips produced are contextual, fully editable, and several models are offered to choose from. The generators themselves already existed: the novelty is that they stop being an excursion and become an editing gesture.

**Arm federates 80 partners around a common standard for robotics**

Total Design for Physical AI brings together AWS, Hugging Face, Siemens, Unitree Robotics, Qwen, NXP and QNX to standardize the design of robotic systems and embedded AI, from mining to agriculture to transportation. The accompanying Robotics Capability Framework mirrors the SAE levels of autonomous driving with a scale of capabilities, from the purely reactive robot to the one that learns on its own. Each tier sets concrete constraints: latency, compute placement, memory, power consumption, determinism and safety standards. The goal is to move industrial robotics out of the prototype stage.

**Robots walked a runway at IFA in Berlin**

The trade show organized a real fashion show, a format borrowed from fashion weeks, with humanoids, wheeled machines and quadrupeds that walked, posed and performed figures in front of the audience. Eleven brands, the vast majority Chinese, each sent their flagship model for a solo walk, including the heavy T800 from EngineAI and its full-body movements. The demonstrations highlighted agility, sensor progress and interaction capabilities, not autonomy.

**Meta stops rating its engineers on their AI consumption**

Dashboards and token counters are disappearing from performance reviews, replaced by the quality, speed and complexity of the work, according to an internal memo from executives Maher Saba and Santosh Janardhan. The criterion had spawned "tokenmaxxing": employees burning tokens en masse to rank well in internal leaderboards. The internal usage bill is heading toward several billion dollars in 2026, which is pushing Meta to institute budgets and a centralized dashboard starting in 2027.

**In China, micro-drama studios are buying faces for $75 to $200**

About 95% of the 128,000 micro-dramas released in the first quarter were produced with generative AI, according to CCTV. Unable to freely draw on real faces after several "face theft" scandals, studios now sign image licensing contracts with extras, students and secondary actors, paid 500 to 1,500 yuan for a year of use. The stars, however, refuse these contracts. Some studios outright manufacture their own fully synthetic actors to cut costs even further.

**Wispr launches Notetaker, a meeting minutes assistant**

The maker of the dictation tool Wispr Flow offers an assistant that joins and records Google Meet, Teams or Slack Huddles meetings with a single button, including those not listed on the calendar. The summaries rely on personal context: participants from the calendar, a custom jargon dictionary, connected tools. It plugs into Claude, ChatGPT and Cursor via MCP, and imports the history from Granola or Otter in one click.

**Inworld TTS-2: first voice audible in under 100 ms**

The speech synthesis engine renders its first audio segment in under 100 ms at P99, on dedicated inference infrastructure. Its particularity: actor direction is given in plain English, by instruction, rather than in technical parameters. The stated target is high-volume voice applications that until now had to arbitrate between quality, latency and cost. Five hours of generation are offered for testing.

**Chrome moves to an update every two weeks**

The cycle shifts from four to two weeks starting with Chrome 153, on desktop, iOS, and Android. The goal is to reduce the "N-day" window, that delay between the publication of a patch and its actual arrival for the user, while automated tools drive up both the volume of flaws detected and the speed of attacks that exploit them. Mozilla, Microsoft, and Brave have already adopted this pace. An accepted side effect: the browser's AI features arrive faster, in the face of Comet, Dia, Opera Neon, and ChatGPT Atlas.

**The data side of the Navier-Stokes affair deserves to be read separately**

Tristan Buckmaster had deposited all of his drafts into Codex. OpenAI assured him that the model did not consult user data, but did not answer his question about training. The company denies all of the accusations, including that of having threatened the mathematician after his refusal to set aside his co-author employed at Anthropic. For anyone who pours confidential working documents into an assistant, contracts, client files, or reports in progress, the question remains exactly as stated.

**Hackers steal Claude tokens from paying subscribers**

Grant De Swardt, an independent consultant in the United Kingdom on a Max 20x plan, saw his quota go from 45% to 55% while he was not working and had unplugged all of his integrations. Anthropic attributed the incident to a compromised session key that had been used to generate unauthorized Claude Code OAuth tokens, then suspended the account and partially refunded. Other cases are surfacing from Reddit and GitHub, including accounts maxed out at 100% or upgraded without consent. Support tracks total consumption but not the per-task detail, which can let a theft go unnoticed for months.

**Google will "degrade" its search engine in Europe to avoid fines**

The company is changing the display of its results in the European Union in order to escape part of the 890 million euros in fines imposed this summer under the Digital Markets Act. The new pages will place a specialized comparison site at the top (Expedia, Hotels.com), then two others, above the merchants' own sites, while the hotels, flights, and restaurants carousel will lose features such as real-time prices. Google itself calls the change the largest quality drop in the history of its engine and predicts that local businesses will pay the price for the benefit of intermediaries. No rollout timeline has been communicated.

**ASML locks in TSMC, Samsung, and Intel while Huawei tries to break free**

The Dutch company has convinced the three major foundries to switch to larger photomasks, which should increase the throughput of its most recent EUV machines by 40%. On the other side, Huawei is coordinating the Chinese response with equipment manufacturer Yuliangsheng and its own suppliers, to reduce a dependency that remains the main sticking point for China's chip industry.

**Heavy Claude users sue Anthropic**

An expanded class action reproaches the company for having misleadingly presented the real usage limits of its high-end Max plan. The complaint is brought by Monica Vaca and Kati Daffan, two former Federal Trade Commission lawyers who served during the Lina Khan era. It is one of the first attempts to have an AI company judged on its pricing practices, and the irony is not lost on anyone: Anthropic had precisely prioritized these heavy consumers, even at the cost of cutting off access to popular applications like OpenClaw.

**The first trailer for "Artificial," the Sam Altman biopic, is chilling**

Andrew Garfield plays the OpenAI boss under the direction of Luca Guadagnino, in a tone that is closer to a horror thriller than a portrait. The screenplay covers the five days of November 2023, Altman's brutal ousting and then his return. Amazon MGM dropped the project after having invested massively in OpenAI, Netflix, A24, and Focus declined, and it is ultimately Neon that will distribute it, in theaters on December 25, barely three years after the events.

**Patagonia attracts AI data centers, with no opposition for now**

Cold climate, hydroelectricity, wind power, Vaca Muerta shale gas, and a lot of empty land: Argentine Patagonia checks every box. Pampa Energía plans up to 500 MW in Neuquén province, with a grid connection priced at 900 million dollars, Polish company Green Capital wants to start at 300 MW in Chubut with 3,000 MW in its sights, and OpenAI has announced a project with Sur Energy that has still not been signed. The absence of local opposition is explained mainly by the absence of precedent in the country, but Mapuche families already in conflict with energy companies live near the Loma de la Lata site.

**Starship 40, recovered intact, will take months to return to Texas**

SpaceX hoisted its upper stage onto the semi-submersible ship Forte near Christmas Island, after 24 days spent at sea. It is the first Starship to survive its splashdown intact, which offers engineers a real specimen to examine rather than simple telemetry. Launched on July 24 from Starbase, it had deployed 20 Starlink V3 satellites in a suborbital flight of about 65 minutes. The return, probably via the Cape of Good Hope, will take several months, with an arrival mentioned in Brownsville on October 8.

**The foldable iPhone would be called "Duo" and cost 2,000 dollars**

According to Mark Gurman (Bloomberg), Apple's first foldable would start at $2,000 for 256 GB and climb to nearly $3,000 for 2 TB. On the agenda: A20 Pro chip, C2 modem, dual 48 MP sensor, magnetic hinge, side-by-side multitasking and Apple Pencil compatibility, for an October release, shortly after the iPhone 18. The 18 Pro and Pro Max models would increase by $100 in the process.

**OpenAI publishes its argument on AI and work**

A reflection piece on how more capable and cheaper models expand what individuals and businesses can accomplish, with the idea that falling costs make growth more accessible. No product or model announcement: it is an economic positioning, to be read as such.

**Cognition, the publisher of Devin, raises $2 billion**

The round, led by Andreessen Horowitz, Accel, Founders Fund, General Catalyst and Avenir, values the startup at $48 billion, nearly double what it was four months ago. Annualized revenue has gone from $492 to $900 million since May, with a projection of $4 to $5 billion by the end of 2026. More interesting than the amount: Cognition now trains its own model from open source alternatives to reduce its costly dependence on OpenAI and Anthropic, with the rental of its Nvidia cluster potentially pushing its cash consumption to $800 million this year.

**Marvell is up 241% in a year, its CEO explains why**

Matt Murphy attributes the stock's surge, versus 6.6% for its rival Broadcom, to a decade of trust built with cloud giants, to whom the company supplies custom chips for AI. Marvell describes itself as the "Switzerland" of the sector, a neutral supplier to the four main American hyperscalers, with partnerships signed with Nvidia in March and Google in August. Its data center segment is expected to grow by 60% in fiscal 2027 according to FactSet.
