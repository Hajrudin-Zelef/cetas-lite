# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

**DARPA has an AI fly an F-16 in a real flight.** An F-16 converted into an autonomous testbed (**VENOM** program) flew under the control of an AI agent, without touching the aircraft's base software. The pilot can **take back control with a simple switch**, a safeguard of the system. These flights prepare the future AIR program, which aims for fleets of drones coordinated by humans. A hot topic in the tech community: **219 points** on Hacker News.

**Johnson & Johnson breaks a 25-year monopoly on robotic surgery.** The FDA authorizes **Ottava**, the first surgical robot whose **four arms fold into the operating table** instead of surrounding the patient. It is approved for common abdominal procedures (gallbladder, appendectomy, gastric bypass, hernia) and ends the near-monopoly of Intuitive Surgical's **da Vinci** system.

**Nvidia opens a medical physics simulator to train healthcare robots.** Called **Medical Physics Simulation**, this **open source** extension of Isaac for Healthcare generates through simulation the physical sensations (contact, force, soft tissue) that a surgical robot needs in order to learn. By combining classical physics and AI generation (Cosmos-H Dreams), it recreates at scale on GPU rare clinical cases that are difficult to obtain in real life.

**Flux 3 generates videos with native sound up to 20 seconds long.** Black Forest Labs launches **Flux 3**, a multimodal model that learns from images, video, and audio, and generates for the first time at BFL **video with integrated sound**, up to **20 seconds**. Its internal tests place it just ahead of **Seedance 2.0**, with no independent validation for now. The company is already testing Flux 3 on robotics tasks, with a "world model" in its sights.

**How AI helps design tomorrow's medicines.** AstraZeneca applies AI to every stage of designing **biologics** (therapeutic proteins), through a "design, measure, learn" cycle that **computationally prioritizes** candidate molecules before laboratory tests. The payoff: fewer failures, and the promise of multi-target treatments for diseases considered out of reach until now. (Article labeled "sponsored.")

**No, Kimi K3 probably does not owe its performance to stealing Fable.** A White House adviser and the Treasury Secretary accuse China's **Moonshot** of having distilled Anthropic's **Fable** model to build **Kimi K3**, on chips banned from export. Except that several researchers (Snorkel AI, Allen Institute) consider the explanation implausible: **two weeks** after Fable's release, distillation alone is not enough to explain that level. The debate fuels a possible ban on Chinese open-weight models.

**Etched's AI chip doubles its valuation to $10.3 billion.** Founded by three Harvard alumni, Etched designs chips **specialized for inference**, with components dedicated to the prefill and decode phases meant to speed up computations at lower cost. Its systems target all types of models (Mixture of Experts such as DeepSeek or Qwen, non-transformer architectures such as Mamba). A **$300 million** raise led by Sequoia, with Peter Thiel and Andrej Karpathy participating.


# **🗞️MORE NEWS**

**Why Chinese open-source AI could become more powerful than ever.** Perplexity CEO Aravind Srinivas believes export controls create only about a **12-month** gap between open-source and frontier models. Worse, he says, this pressure could push China to excel at the physical layer of AI (data centers, energy, workforce), where it already has a head start.

**US and China jointly back open-source AI at APEC.** The **21 APEC economies**, including Washington and Beijing, signed the "Chengdu declaration" in favor of open-source AI, subject to "strong security guarantees." A turning point: open source, long libertarian, is moving closer to state oversight, as Chinese models (DeepSeek, GLM 5.2) nibble away at closed models.

**Moonshot allegedly bypassed the Nvidia chip embargo via Thailand.** A White House official accuses Moonshot of obtaining servers with **Nvidia GB300** chips by going through Thailand. The reason: **Kimi K3**, an open-source model with **2.8 trillion parameters** that reportedly beats Fable 5 and GPT-5.6 Sol on some benchmarks. Congress is meanwhile examining a bill to extend controls to remote cloud access.

**Palmier Pro, the open-source macOS video editor built for AI.** This open-source video editing tool for macOS integrates AI generation (video, image, sound, subtitles) and includes a **local MCP server** to let an agent like Claude or Codex drive the timeline. Goal: automate the repetitive work (transitions, multicam editing, cutting into short formats). **140 points** on Hacker News.

**Nvidia is sending its GPUs to the Moon.** Startup Lunar Outpost will equip its next lunar rover with **Nvidia Jetson** chips to drive its lidar, which would make it the very first GPU placed on the Moon's surface. The real challenge isn't computing but hardware survival: radiation, extreme temperature swings, and minimal power consumption during the lunar night.

**ChatGPT gives worse health advice if you don't pay.** OpenAI is rolling out "Health in ChatGPT" in the United States, connected to Apple Health and medical records. Already **300 million people** ask health questions every week, but quality depends on the subscription: paying users get **GPT-5.6 Sol**, free users get the less accurate GPT-5.5 Instant. Two-tier healthcare.

**A portable device detects when your body is burning fat.** Researchers have developed a **breathalyzer** that measures acetone to tell whether the body is in ketosis. Tested on 12 people, with results close to lab analyses, and a companion app to make the measurement more reliable. Targeted applications: metabolic tracking and the effectiveness of GLP-1 treatments.

**Gemini nears a billion monthly users.** Google's assistant reaches **950 million monthly users**, a figure that has tripled in a year. ChatGPT keeps the lead with 1 billion, but its market share drops **below 50%** for the first time, while Gemini climbs to **27.7%** (Sensor Tower). The assistant battle is tightening significantly.

**Italy unveils Gene.01, a humanoid with sensory skin.** The robot from Generative Bionics is covered with **artificial skin** that detects touch, force, proximity, and temperature, a safety layer for working side by side with humans. Developed in **six months**, it is already starting with welding tasks in Fincantieri's shipyards.

**Beni, the all-terrain camera robot that follows you everywhere.** This small robot films solo skateboarding, running, or children by following you at up to **nearly 29 km/h** (17.9 mph), with jumps of up to 25 cm. Automatic multi-angle cinematic tracking and stabilized **4K HDR** video, with no operator. Funding currently underway on Kickstarter.

**Alexa Plus finally handles complex smart-home instructions.** The update (in preview) connects the assistant to third-party devices (Bosch, iRobot, Yale Home, Whirlpool, Eufy...) and **automatically routes** each request to the right device. A concrete example: choosing the right wash cycle from a simple natural-language sentence.

**Asia's biggest IPO of the year is shaking Chinese stocks.** CXMT, China's leading memory chipmaker, lists in Shanghai on July 27 after raising **$8.6 billion**. The deal is soaking up so much liquidity that the STAR 50 index has fallen nearly **20%** this quarter. A sign of semiconductor fever on the Chinese side.

**Reddit threatens to cut off Google's access to its data.** Reddit is reportedly considering not renewing its **$60 million-per-year** deal that lets Google train its AIs on its posts. The reason: Google's AI Overviews, which capture the answers and **dry up the traffic** sent back to Reddit. The stock fell 8% on the news.
