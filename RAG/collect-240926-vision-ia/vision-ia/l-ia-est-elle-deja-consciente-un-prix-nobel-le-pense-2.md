# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

According to AXA's Mind Health 2026 report, **more than 60%** of respondents turn to AI for psychological support. A figure that says a lot about the trivialization of chatbots as mental well-being tools, and about the profound evolution of our relationship to managing emotions.


# **🗞️MORE NEWS**

**Anthropic extends Project Glasswing to 150 partners to hunt critical flaws**

Anthropic is accelerating its Glasswing program by opening it to **150 new partners in more than 15 countries**. Thanks to Claude Mythos Preview, these players are scanning critical infrastructure in search of vulnerabilities: **more than 10,000 serious flaws** have already been identified. In parallel, Anthropic is commercializing Claude Security, a solution dedicated to remediation. A ramp-up that confirms the laboratory's ambition in the cybersecurity field.

**Anthropic's IPO marks generative AI's transition to the stage of enterprise utility**

By filing its IPO prospectus, Anthropic marks a turning point: generative AI is leaving the phase of unrestrained research to become a **stable and predictable enterprise utility**. The operation will force the company to align pricing, schedules and contracts with the expectations of corporate clients. For public investors, it is a **first opportunity to invest directly** in a creator of cutting-edge models. The challenge: reconciling colossal GPU expenses with the quarterly profitability that markets demand.

**Google will let sites opt out of AI search results**

Google will offer publishers an option to **voluntarily remove their content** from AI-boosted search results, without losing their place in classic results. A direct response to concerns from media outlets and creators, who denounce the exploitation of their content by automatically generated summaries. It remains to be seen how many will take the step, at the risk of sacrificing part of their visibility.

**OpenAI launches six Codex tools for skilled professions**

OpenAI is deploying **six new Codex tools** tailored to specific professions: data analytics, design, sales, creative production, stock investing and investment banking. Each comes with its own plugins, instructions and business context to bring Codex closer to the expertise of a professional in the field. An expansion that pushes generative AI well beyond simple code, toward the automation of expert tasks.

**Apple pushes back its AI glasses to late 2027**

Apple has delayed by a year, to **late 2027**, the launch of its connected glasses (project N50), slowed by development difficulties. Designed as lightweight AI companions **without a screen**, they will rely on a camera, a microphone and an on-device assistant, in a spirit close to the Ray-Ban Meta. A new delay that leaves the field open to Meta in this emerging market.

**Perplexity's CEO unveils his key metric for winning the AI race**

For Aravind Srinivas, head of Perplexity, the winner of the AI race will be judged on the **"value generated per watt and per user."** In other words, energy efficiency would matter more than the raw power of models. A vision that places sobriety at the heart of the strategy, at a time when the electricity bill for data centers is exploding.

**Google Phone detects scammers who clone your contacts using AI**

Google is adding fraudulent call detection to its Phone app that uses AI to impersonate your contacts. When an unknown number pretends to be a loved one, the app **automatically flags** the suspicious call. The feature is part of the June 2026 Android drop, packed with new security features. A welcome protection as voice-cloning scams multiply.

**Amazon hit with class action over Ring's facial recognition**

Amazon faces a class action targeting the Familiar Faces feature of its Ring cameras, accused of **storing passersby's faces without their consent**. The case reignites the debate over data protection and the transparency of consumer-facing facial recognition. Depending on its outcome, it could set a precedent for the entire connected camera industry.

**ChatGPT becomes a job search platform with a résumé editor**

OpenAI is turning ChatGPT into a true career tool: the assistant now integrates listings from **Indeed, Upwork, and Appcast** and lets users **create and tailor their résumé** directly in the app for each application. Launched first in the United States, the feature positions ChatGPT as a direct competitor to LinkedIn in the job market.

**Holo3.1, fast and 100% local computer agents**

Holo3.1 unveils a new generation of agents capable of executing computer tasks **directly on the machine**, without going through the cloud. The payoff: reduced latency, better performance for "computer use," and **enhanced privacy**, since data never leaves the device. An appealing approach for sensitive or offline use cases.

**Warren Buffett invests $10 billion in Alphabet's AI infrastructure**

Alphabet is raising **$80 billion** to finance its AI infrastructure, including **$10 billion** in private funds contributed by Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway. The group plans up to **$190 billion** in capital expenditures in 2026. That the Oracle of Omaha, long reluctant about tech, is betting big on AI speaks volumes about the scale of the infrastructure race.

**Microsoft launches the Surface RTX Spark Dev Box, a mini-PC for local AI**

Microsoft unveils the Surface RTX Spark Dev Box, a mini desktop computer powered by **Nvidia RTX Spark** chips (Arm architecture). Optimized for local and sustained AI workloads, it packs **128 GB of RAM** and 100 W active cooling in a compact form factor reminiscent of an Xbox. A machine designed for developers who want to run models locally.

**AI agents are "rehumanizing" healthcare**

Facing a projected shortage of **11 million healthcare workers by 2030**, **68% of hospitals** are already deploying AI agents to automate administrative work, support medical teams, and triage patients. Unlike older digital tools, these agents handle nuanced situations and make decisions autonomously, easing clinicians' mental load and improving quality of care.

**BYD will cover damages in the event of an accident caused by its autonomous system**

BYD becomes the **first automaker in the world** to assume full financial responsibility for accidents caused by its God's Eye 5.0 autonomous driving system. The coverage includes **repairs, third-party damage, and injuries**, provided the Urban Navigate on Autopilot is used legally in China. With more than **3.15 million vehicles** concerned, it's a strong bet on the reliability of its technology—and an unprecedented sales pitch.

**HPE jumps 19%, its best session ever**

Buoyed by exceptional quarterly results, HPE saw its stock climb **19%**, an all-time record. CEO Antonio Neri believes the company is "ideally positioned" to benefit from the AI wave and demand for cloud servers. Another tech stock fueled by the market's insatiable appetite for everything related to artificial intelligence.
