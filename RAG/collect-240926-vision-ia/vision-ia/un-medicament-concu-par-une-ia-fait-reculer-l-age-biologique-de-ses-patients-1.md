Insilico Medicine let its AI do all the work: find the target in the body, then design the molecule capable of binding to it. The result is called rentosertib, and a study published in *Nature Biotechnology* claims that treated patients saw their biological age recede by **3 to 4 years on average**, and **up to 6 years** according to the most sensitive measure.

Concretely, rentosertib is not an "anti-aging drug." It's a treatment for **idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis**, a disease that gradually stiffens the lungs. Insilico's platform works in two stages: a first system identifies the proteins involved in the disease and designates **TNIK** as the target, a second analyzes its structure and generates from scratch the molecules capable of blocking it. Total duration of the process: about **18 months**, whereas classical discovery of a candidate is counted in years.

**What to remember:**

- The trial included only **42 patients**, followed for **12 weeks** with Olink proteomic analyses

- **Six independent biological clocks** (including ProtAge, OrganAge and PAC, developed by Harvard, Oxford, Peking University and Insilico) all conclude the same rejuvenation, visible **as early as the fourth week**

- Optimal dose: **30 mg twice a day**. Forced vital capacity, the reference measure of lung function, also improves proportionally to the dose

- Rentosertib is already in **phase III**, the last stage before possible approval, but for pulmonary fibrosis, not for aging

- No testing on healthy people at this stage, nor price, nor commercialization date

It's the first candidate whose target *and* molecule come out of an AI to show an effect on aging in humans. The usual stars of longevity, rapamycin and metformin, are drugs repurposed for a use they weren't intended for. Here the anti-aging effect is a secondary benefit, observed by surprise, on a molecule created for something else. Keep that in mind before getting excited: 42 sick patients is not proof, it's a lead.

For six weeks, from July 13 to August 24, ChatGPT Plus subscribers could use Codex and ChatGPT Work without an hourly cap. That's over: OpenAI has restored a **rolling 5-hour window**, and users are discovering it mostly in practice, when their session stops dead in the middle of work.

The mechanism is twofold. You're blocked as soon as you reach **either the 5-hour limit or your weekly quota**, whichever comes first. Then you have to wait for the reset or buy additional credits. Immediate side effect, and it's what annoys people most: the famous quota "resets" lose most of their value, since you hit the 5-hour barrier anyway.

**Who is affected:**

- **Plus ($19/month)** and **Business Standard**: the limit applies

- **Pro ($100 and $200/month)**: exempt, but only "for the coming months" according to OpenAI

- **Enterprise and Edu**: unaffected, they run on a separate credit-based infrastructure

- OpenAI has never published the exact number of messages or tasks allowed in a 5-hour window

The justification came from Thibault Sottiaux, an engineer at OpenAI, on X: the 5-hour window **"smooths the load on compute"** and allows keeping a generous weekly allowance. In short, it targets burst usage, the kind that burned through an entire week's quota in a single marathon session.

**What this changes for you**

If you're a Plus subscriber and run Codex on long tasks, plan to break up your work. The topic is generating a lot of reaction in the tech community, between those who find the measure reasonable for spreading the load and those who see it as the sector's classic mechanics: very generous access to build loyalty, then a tightening once habits are established.


Figure has an unusual problem for a robotics company: its robots are not limited by their motors or joints, but by the lack of data and computing power to train their brain. The cloud provider **Nscale** has just signed a multi-year agreement to deliver enough to last several years.

This brain is called **Helix**. It's the AI system that drives the Figure 02 and 03 humanoids, and that learns by watching: since the launch of the **Index** initiative in August, Figure has accumulated **16 million videos**, ingested at a rate of 30 to 35 minutes of video per second. It's these mountains of images that must now be transformed into gestures.

**In detail:**

- Up to **100,000 Nvidia GPUs**, on the **Vera Rubin** platform, the generation succeeding Blackwell

- First servers powered on in the **second half of 2027**, in **Barstow, Texas**

- Nscale becomes Figure's preferred compute provider and takes a stake in the company, in an initial agreement worth 3.5 billion dollars, expandable beyond 6

- Figure has raised approximately **1.9 billion dollars** since its beginnings, for a valuation of **39 billion** in September 2025

We see mega compute contracts passing by every week, but they almost always serve to train text models. This one finances the training of a model that will have to **move a body in the real world**, with the constraints of balance, grasping, and safety that this implies. It's the bet that humanoid robotics is hitting the same wall as LLMs in 2020, that of scale, and that it can be overcome the same way. The answer comes in 2028, when the first models trained in Barstow are released.
