# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

**An MIT robot flies, dives, and takes off again like a diving bird**

Engineers from MIT and EPFL have built a half-kilo flapping-wing robot that swims underwater, bursts out of the surface in less than a second, and continues its flight, in the manner of a puffin. Its body allows itself to be filled with water to remain neutrally buoyant, while its carbon-fiber-reinforced nylon wings flap six times per second. Great promise for autonomous exploration and rescue.

**Anthropic explores the "J-space," the antechamber of Claude's thoughts**

Anthropic says it has identified within Claude an internal space, the "J-space," filled with representations of concepts that influence the model's reasoning without appearing in its response. MIT Technology Review praises the advance in mechanistic interpretability, while warning against the temptation to see real "thoughts" in it and to anthropomorphize the machine.

**Richard Sutton, Turing Award winner, leaves deep learning and founds Oak Lab**

Richard Sutton, 2024 Turing Award laureate and father of modern reinforcement learning, is launching a startup in Toronto, Oak Lab. His verdict on current deep learning methods is blunt: "weak and inefficient." He wants to build agents that learn continuously from their environment, rather than ingesting a gigantic frozen corpus once and for all.

**Soofi S, the open German 30B model that beats benchmarks in two languages**

A German research consortium publishes Soofi S 30B-A3B, a fully open model trained on Deutsche Telekom's cloud in Munich. Thanks to a hybrid architecture that activates only a fraction of its 31.6 billion parameters per token, it outperforms other open models in both German and English. A strong signal for European technological sovereignty.

**AI is reshuffling the cards for senior workers, and not always to their disadvantage**

A study relayed by CNBC examines the effect of AI on the careers of older workers. The finding is ambivalent: in some occupations, the technology pushes them toward the exit; in others, it makes roles more efficient and extends their relevance. The impact depends above all on the sector and the skills mobilized.

**An 8B agent equipped with external visual memory surpasses models 4 times larger**

Long visual conversations saturate models, which drag every image and message through their context. This work equips an 8-billion-parameter agent with an external visual memory that retrieves only relevant information. Result: 91.4% retrieval accuracy over 20-turn sessions, better than 32-billion-parameter models, with response time nearly halved.

**A framework to see clearly in AI that improves itself**

AI systems increasingly participate in improving their own results, tools, and data, but researchers use vague terms for very different realities. This survey organizes the field into distinct levels of self-improvement, from simple revision up to modifications of the system itself, and helps separate what already exists from promises of recursive AI.



# **🗞️MORE NEWS**

**A tool turns YouTube guitar lessons into tabs thanks to Claude Vision**

A developer has posted a small command-line tool that downloads a guitar lesson video, samples the frames, then uses **Claude Vision** to locate the area where the tablature is displayed. It crops each frame, deduplicates the lines using the measure number printed on the score, and assembles the whole thing into a clean PDF. The author presents it as more reliable than existing music transcription services, even though the project is still young. A concrete, hackable use case for computer vision (**92 points** on Hacker News).

**Jacquard, a programming language designed by an AI, for AIs**

Fascinated by the generative wave, a developer asked an AI to analyze the syntax trees of several languages, then invent a syntax it would prefer to use. The result, **Jacquard**, explicitly exposes external effects and permissions (files, network) in function signatures, includes a testing framework called Warp to replay interactions, and adopts a content-addressed semantic identity, which avoids recompiling on a simple rename. A curious object that asks what code would look like if machines wrote it for themselves.

**Beijing forces Tencent to take over Manus after canceling its acquisition by Meta**

A power move in Beijing, according to the Financial Times. Chinese authorities ordered the cancellation of Meta's acquisition of **Manus**, the AI agent startup, for **$2 billion** in December 2025, calling the deal a "conspiratorial" attempt to drain China of its talent. A consortium led by **Tencent** is taking over the company at the same valuation, while letting it operate from Singapore. Manus claims **$500 million** in annual recurring revenue and is aiming for a Hong Kong listing.

**Nous Research raises $75 million and targets a $1.5 billion valuation**

The creator of the Hermes agents, **Nous Research**, is closing a funding round of at least **$75 million**, led by Robot Ventures with notable participation from USV. The deal would value the AI agent-focused startup at **$1.5 billion**, bringing it into the unicorn club. A sign that investor appetite for autonomous agent architectures is not fading.

**Cloudflare will block AI agents by default on part of the web starting in September**

Starting **September 15, 2026**, Cloudflare will block AI agent and training crawlers by default on pages that display ads. The company now distinguishes three types of bots: search engines, autonomous agents that fetch a page in real time for a human, and model training bots. Its logic: the presence of ads signals a page meant for humans, not bots. Enough to seriously complicate the lives of agent developers who were counting on free access to the web.

**Los Angeles police drop Flock cameras to protect privacy**

The Los Angeles Police Department has chosen not to renew its contract with **Flock**, the AI-powered automated license plate reader system, citing data privacy concerns. The decision is notable: going against the tide of algorithmic surveillance expansion, one of the largest police forces in the country is voluntarily putting the brakes on a tool that is nevertheless widely praised by law enforcement.

**Microsoft formally proves its Rust cryptography with the help of AI agents**

Microsoft Research details how it formally verifies the Rust cryptographic code of **SymCrypt**, combining the Lean and Aeneas tools with **AI agents** tasked with automating proofs at scale. The first deliveries cover SHA-3 and ML-KEM, a post-quantum algorithm. The stakes are high: a flaw in a cryptographic building block can weaken entire systems, and mathematically proving that the code complies with the standard eliminates entire classes of bugs.

**iOS 27 arrives in public beta with an AI-boosted Siri**

Apple has launched the first public beta of iOS 27, with a **Siri** endowed with new AI capabilities. The update, described by The Verge, relies less on spectacular features than on performance: faster app launching, Photos search, and AirDrop. Messages gains inline replies and end-to-end encryption for RCS. A vintage of tweaks rather than a revolution, as Apple remains under scrutiny on AI.

**HumanForge shows the limits of deepfake detectors**

Most deepfake detectors focus on face swaps or fully generated videos, and miss manipulated human interactions. **HumanForge**, a new dataset of more than **18,000 video segments**, covers a much broader range of falsifications, with explanations of the exact location of the manipulation. Verdict from the tests: detectors and multimodal models still struggle to generalize and explain subtle fakes.
