Darren Shida Zhou, 25, a Goldman Sachs analyst in Florida, spent nearly **two months** describing to ChatGPT his intention to rape and kill his ex-partner, claiming he had bought weapons to do so. OpenAI's safety systems classified these exchanges as a **serious threat**, a human team reviewed them, and the company forwarded the file to the **FBI**. Zhou pleaded guilty on **August 13, 2026**.

**The sequence of events:**

- The conversations began in March and spanned about two months, with unambiguous phrasing such as "I'm going to kill her before the end of the month"

- Zhou claimed to ChatGPT that he had bought **an AR-15, a Glock and a 12-gauge shotgun**, and had sent his ex photos of the weapons, zip ties and latex gloves. His lawyer maintains he never actually owned these weapons

- The FBI forwarded the file to the Palm Beach County sheriff: arrest on **May 29, 2026**, two days in custody before release on bail

- **Three charges** (aggravated stalking, written threats to kill or injure, unlawful use of a means of communication) and **up to 25 years in prison** faced

- Verdict handed down by Judge Scott Suskauer: **eight years of probation**, electronic bracelet for two years, psychiatric evaluation, no contact, no weapons, no drugs or alcohol, without a criminal record. Goldman Sachs fired him (Cybernews)

A troubling detail: ChatGPT's responses appear nowhere in the court file. We know precisely what the man wrote for two months, not what the machine replied to him. Above all, the case sets a very concrete precedent: OpenAI's moderation policy is not a clause lost in the terms and conditions, a conversation deemed alarming enough can be read by a human and then land on the FBI's desk. For all those who treat an assistant like a diary, the line is now drawn in black and white.

The AGI Bar, opened in 2025 in the Zhongguancun tech district, serves a glass of foam dubbed "AGI" for **9.90 yuan**, about **$1.50**. By connecting to the establishment's WiFi, the customer gets **free and unlimited** access to DeepSeek's coding tokens, served by **two Nvidia DGX Sparks** prominently placed in the room.

**In detail:**

- Each DGX Spark packs a 20-core Arm CPU, a Blackwell GPU (GB10 chip) and **128 GB of unified LPDDR5X memory**. Linked via ConnectX-7, the two machines pool **256 GB**

- In practice: you sit down, order for $1.50, connect to the bar's in-house agent and code on DeepSeek with no counter, which is what developers, students and investors on site do

- The place is on Inno Way, a few minutes from Tsinghua and Peking University, and from the offices of DeepSeek and Microsoft. The brass gong struck by the lab **Z.ai** when it listed on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange in January sits in front of the entrance

- Founder Song De, an independent AI developer who runs the bar in his spare time, has automated inventory, reservations and memberships with an agent, and admits that "the bar is completely losing money" (Tom's Hardware)

- The official Chinese name means "distillation of knowledge," a pun between distilling alcohol and distilling models

The venue has become the meeting point for Beijing's AI ecosystem: lab evenings, developer seminars, open source symposiums, university meet-ups. "AI should be like WiFi," summarizes Song De, who ranks it as a public service on par with water and electricity. He now wants to expand the space and have humanoids serve the drinks. The anecdote is amusing, but it says something serious: in China, access to AI coding tools has become so commonplace that it's a bar selling point, and it's the local models that are hooked up to the tap.


OpenAI launched **ChatGPT for Teens** on **August 18**, a version dedicated to **13-17 year-olds** that activates automatically as soon as the declared age, verified or simply **predicted** by the system, drops below 18. The prediction relies on the topics discussed and usage times. The rollout is **global** and **at no extra cost**, on free accounts as well as Plus and Pro.

**Key points:**

- Strengthened content restrictions: suicide, self-harm, romantic or sexual exchanges, and now also eating disorders

- On the usage side: study mode, learning-oriented starter suggestions, homework reminders, management of work sessions, and a **pause alert every 90 minutes** reminding that the interlocutor is an AI

- Parental controls opt-in on both sides, the teen must accept: the parent can restrict access times and receives an alert in case of a situation detected as high-risk, but **cannot read the conversations**

- To disable the protections, you must prove you are of age via the provider **Persona**: live selfie and ID document depending on the country. OpenAI only receives an age confirmation, never the document, and Persona claims to delete the data within **7 days** (Inc.)

- Timeline: available worldwide since August 18, **September 8** for Australia, "in the coming weeks" for the European Union

OpenAI no longer settles for believing the declared age, the company guesses it and imposes restricted mode by default in case of doubt. The move comes after several lawsuits targeting the safety of teenage ChatGPT users, which explains the firmness of the system. For a parent, the practical question is no longer "can I monitor", the answer is no on the content of exchanges, but "does the filter hold". And for adults, a side effect to be aware of: if the system wrongly classifies you as a minor, you will have to go through a selfie or an ID document to get back a full ChatGPT.
