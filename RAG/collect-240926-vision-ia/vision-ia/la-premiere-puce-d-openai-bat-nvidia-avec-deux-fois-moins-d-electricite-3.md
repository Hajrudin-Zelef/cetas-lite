# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

After several weeks of a purely weekly quota, OpenAI restored on August 25 a usage limit per 5-hour block on Codex and ChatGPT Work for Plus accounts. The official argument from Thibault Sottiaux, head of engineering for Codex: smooth the load on the servers, and prevent an occasional user from emptying their weekly quota in a single session without realizing it. The Pro plans at $100 and $200 remain exempt for the coming months. The subject is generating a lot of reaction among heavy users.

**Google buys 34 years of Spirit Airlines data, flight attendants protest**

The American low-cost airline went bankrupt in the spring, but its archives are still worth something: Google won the auction at $10 million (against $7.5 for Mercor) for 34 years of invoices, flight operations, wifi sales, personnel files, and crew schedules. Google assures that customer data is excluded and that no personal information will be transmitted to it. The AFA union, which represents 5,500 former crew members, has filed an objection: employee records are being transferred without consent, and pretty much nothing in American law prohibits it.

**Meta launches Hatch, its paid agent at up to $199.99 per month**

According to internal documents obtained by The Information, Meta will release in the coming weeks Hatch, the paid consumer version of its OpenClaw agent, with tiered pricing that climbs up to $199.99 per month. The agent connects to DoorDash, Etsy, Reddit, Yelp, or Outlook and offers a customizable dashboard, from fitness tracking to travel planning. A new model dubbed Watermelon is expected in October. Meta is also preparing a platform on WhatsApp allowing third-party agents to be connected to it.

**Waymo will launch its robotaxis in Munich in 2027**

The Alphabet subsidiary is starting phased testing in Munich in the coming weeks, with a public opening planned for late 2027. It is its third city outside the United States after London and Tokyo, and the first in the European Union. Waymo already operates in 11 American cities, has announced 19 more, and claims nearly 221 million miles driven in autonomous mode. The first real European regulatory test of the consumer robotaxi.

**Einride deploys 500 Tesla Semis for Amazon**

The Swedish company Einride will run 500 Tesla Semi electric trucks driven by its Saga AI optimization platform, for Amazon and other North American customers. It is the largest deployment of Tesla Semi in the world, third-party financed, spread over 24 months starting in September 2026 and distributed across five American states. The operation triples the electric fleet deployed by Einride, whose platform already claims more than 19 million optimized electric miles.

**BlackBerry, which has quietly become a robotics player**

The former king of the keyboard smartphone now makes its living with QNX, its embedded system present in 275 million vehicles, and its CEO John Giamatteo tells CNBC that robotics is one of its most dynamic segments, on an order book of about $950 million. The target is not the humanoid but industrial physical AI: warehouses, autonomous forklifts, medical instruments. An expanded partnership with Nvidia was announced in April to bring QNX into these markets.

**A voice bracelet for seniors born from an MIT AgeLab study**

Having come to take part in an MIT AgeLab study on technologies to assist the elderly, Don Yansen mostly heard participants recount how unusable smartphones and smartwatches are for them. He founded Kiwi Health in October 2024 to offer a screenless, voice-controlled bracelet whose AI is trained to understand vocal variations related to age. The device measures health parameters, detects falls, and automatically alerts caregivers.

**Applying has become so easy that no one can recruit anymore**

Three years ago, Andrew Stockwell, head of HR at Vendr, received a few dozen applications per listing, a hundred on good days. Today, recruiters take in more than a thousand, boosted by generated resumes, optimized cover letters, and auto-fill extensions. The result is paradoxical: everything looks the same, much is exaggerated or fake, and spotting a good profile becomes almost impossible. Platforms like Greenhouse are now considering deliberately reintroducing friction into the process.

**Is agentic AI just automation in disguise?**

A consultant applies a simple test to the agent trend: does the system, during execution, make a decision that no one had anticipated at the outset? He then walks through a detailed use case, a maintenance agent on an Airbus A320 that diagnoses a fault, queries documentary databases, and recommends a repair, only to find that the whole thing fits into a classic flowchart. A salutary read before signing an "agentic" quote.

**Successful agent projects are the ones that rein in their agents**

Gartner predicts that more than 40% of ongoing agentic AI projects will be abandoned by 2028, weighed down by costs, unclear return on investment, and insufficient risk controls. The McKinsey 2026 survey points in the same direction, with average maturity in AI governance at only 2.3 out of 4, and 30% of organizations at a level deemed sufficient. The underlying reason is mechanical: the more autonomous an agent is, the less traceable its decisions are after the fact, which becomes disqualifying in financial compliance or industrial quality control.

**OpenAI loses its head of data centers**

Chris Malone, who had been leading OpenAI's data center strategy since March 2025, left the company last week, with no official reason given. Having previously worked at Meta and then Google, he arrived shortly after the launch of the Stargate project. According to Business Insider, this is the thirteenth senior executive departure at OpenAI in 2026, following those of former COO Brad Lightcap and chief product officer Fidji Simo. Three other executives continue to oversee the infrastructure portfolio, now under the responsibility of Sachin Katti.

**SpaceX announces a $100 billion spaceport in Louisiana**

The site, located in Vermilion Parish, is to become the primary launch base for Starship and "support thousands of launches per year" according to the Louisiana economic development agency. Musk promises "probably 10,000 exciting jobs." The announcement comes as SpaceX faces lawsuits and environmental fines at its Texas site in Boca Chica, which promises lively local debates.

**Stability AI raises $76 million from the entertainment industry**

The company behind Stable Diffusion closes a $76 million Series B, bringing its total funding to $232 million. The interest of the round lies less in the amount than in its composition: Universal Music Group, Sony Music Group, Warner Music Group and Electronic Arts, with which Stability already has content licensing agreements, alongside AMD Ventures and Pacific Alliance Ventures. After years of lawsuits between rights holders and image generators, the majors are moving from the plaintiffs' camp to the shareholders' camp. The funds will go to the creative production suite for image, music and video.
