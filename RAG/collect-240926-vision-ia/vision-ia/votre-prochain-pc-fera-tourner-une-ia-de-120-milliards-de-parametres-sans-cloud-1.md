Nvidia has just lifted the veil on RTX Spark, a consumer chip capable of running AI models with **120 billion parameters** directly on a personal computer, with no dependence on the cloud. The announcement goes beyond silicon: Lenovo, HP, Microsoft, and Adobe have already joined the venture, a sign that local AI is moving from geek fantasy to industrial reality.

**Key takeaways:**

- RTX Spark delivers **1 PFLOP** of compute at FP4 precision and packs **128 GB** of unified memory

- The GB10 chip runs models of up to **120 billion parameters** entirely locally

- Microsoft is re-architecting Windows, while Adobe is overhauling Photoshop and Premiere to leverage the chip

- Lenovo and HP are assembling the first PCs equipped with RTX Spark, expected to reach consumers

**Why it matters:** Until now, running a large model meant renting costly cloud servers and staying connected at all times, with all the privacy questions that raises. By bringing that power back to the user's machine, Nvidia makes heavy AI private, instant, and offline. For creators, it means generating images and videos without a subscription or latency; for the industry, it's a possible rebalancing of the power dynamic between the personal PC and hyperscalers. If the promise holds, 2026 could be the year AI leaves the data centers and returns to our desks.

OpenAI is integrating Codex, its coding agent, into the very heart of ChatGPT, and pairing it with three new features clearly designed for knowledge workers rather than developers. The message is clear: the autonomous agent is no longer a gadget for engineers, it's a mainstream feature.

**The essential points:**

- Codex is rolling out in ChatGPT over the coming weeks, for all users

- Three additions: plugins based on your role, inline annotations, and **Sites** to create a complete web app in a single prompt

- **5 million weekly users** already use Codex, with growth driven by non-developers

- A head-on positioning against Anthropic Cowork

**What it changes:** By making agent capabilities a standard part of its consumer tools, OpenAI is accelerating an underlying trend: the commoditization of the AI agent. Tomorrow, delegating a complete task to an AI, coding a page, assembling a file, automating a workflow, will become as ordinary as asking a question. For users, it's a considerable gain in power; for the competition, Anthropic first and foremost, it's the obligation to move ever faster. The battle of assistants is now being fought on the field of agents.

OpenAI is rolling out Dreaming, a new memory system that allows ChatGPT to durably retain your preferences and maintain a coherent context from one conversation to the next. Gone is the amnesiac assistant that starts from scratch with every exchange.

**A few key points:**

- ChatGPT benefits from significantly improved memory

- Better retention of each user's preferences and habits

- Context stays fresh and coherent across successive conversations

- Result: more personalized and more relevant assistance over time

**Why it matters:** Memory is the missing link between a handy chatbot and a true personal assistant. By learning who you are over the course of exchanges, ChatGPT stops being a tool that you reconfigure every time and becomes a companion that knows you. It's also a powerful retention lever: the more the AI knows about you, the more costly it becomes to go elsewhere. A strategy that competitors will have to imitate, with the privacy questions that come with it.


Ideogram and Reve are simultaneously launching two models that break with the classic text prompt in favor of an approach based on **layouts**, giving creators unprecedented control over the composition of their images. Ideogram 4.0 also establishes itself as the best open source model of the moment on the Design Arena ranking.

**In detail:**

- Ideogram 4.0 and Reve 2.0 adopt a layout-centric approach rather than one based solely on text

- An iterative and agentic process that multiplies creative checkpoints

- Ideogram 4.0 dominates open source models and excels in text rendering and typography

- Enough to rival the closed models of OpenAI and Google, targeting professional designers

**The key takeaway:** Generating an image is no longer just about finding the perfect prompt and hoping for the best. By going through layout, these models make visual creation more predictable and closer to professional design tools. For graphic designers, it's the promise of an AI that can finally be controlled down to the pixel, rather than a creative lottery. A fine sign of maturity for image generation as a whole.

xAI updates its Grok Imagine model to version 1.5, now capable of transforming a static image into a cinematic video up to 720p from a simple text prompt. Users can assemble multiple generated clips to compose longer scenes. An advance that brings xAI closer to the leaders in generative video.

SemiAnalysis shows how AI generates very real economic value, but one that is invisible to GDP and traditional indices. The reason: 41% of US services GDP is measured through wages and hours worked, so productivity gains linked to AI evaporate from the statistics. A blind spot the author considers comparable to the Industrial Revolution.

Richard Sutton, Turing Award winner and father of reinforcement learning, argues alongside Banafsheh Rafiee for a new path: enactive AI, where intelligence emerges from an agent that interacts with its environment and learns from the consequences of its actions, rather than predicting patterns in labeled data. Four pillars structure it: real experience, perception-action loop, autonomy, and embodiment.

Flourish, a startup backed by Jeff Bezos, raises **$500 million** at a **$2.5 billion** valuation. Its bet: rebuilding AI by studying the brain's true neurological mechanisms rather than imitating current architectures, in the hope of decoding the "fundamental algorithm" of cognition. A very long-term, and very high-risk, project.

Anthropic publishes a report on its advances in recursive self-improvement, meaning a model's ability to iteratively improve itself. Fundamental research to anticipate how future systems could evolve and optimize themselves without human intervention. A topic as fascinating as it is sensitive, touching the heart of AI safety questions.
