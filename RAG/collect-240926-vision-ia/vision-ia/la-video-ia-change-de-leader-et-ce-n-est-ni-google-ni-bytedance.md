# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

**The Sun as no one had ever seen it**

The Inouye Solar Telescope in Hawaii, the most powerful in the world, has captured magnetic swirls on the Sun's surface never observed before. The phenomenon, a Kelvin-Helmholtz instability, explains how magnetic energy accumulates before being released in solar flares or coronal mass ejections. Published in Nature, this work aims above all to better predict space weather, the kind that threatens power grids, satellites and astronauts.

**A dark matter telescope repurposed as an asteroid hunter**

NASA is launching the Nancy Grace Roman Telescope at the end of August, designed to study dark matter and dark energy. But its field of view **100 times wider than Hubble's** and its 300-megapixel infrared camera will also allow it to spot asteroids as small as **18 meters**, the size of the one that exploded over Chelyabinsk in 2013. A team of planetary scientists will present this dual use in September, also to protect the mission from budget cuts.

**NVIDIA opens Alpamayo 2 Super, its reasoning model for robotaxis**

The real problem with autonomous driving isn't ordinary traffic, it's the rare situations no one anticipated. Alpamayo 2 Super, built on Cosmos 3 Super Reasoner and post-trained with reinforcement learning, reasons about these edge cases: understanding the scene, inferring a cause, choosing an action. NVIDIA is publishing it on Hugging Face under the open **OpenMDW-1.1** license, which allows fine-tuning, derivatives and commercial redistribution without additional authorization. The entire Alpamayo family is moving to this license.

**Google is switching off Google Assistant on September 4**

Starting **September 4, 2026**, Google Assistant disappears from Android, Wear OS and Android Auto, replaced everywhere by Gemini: smartphones, tablets, watches and cars. The bet is far from trivial. A deterministic system, which always responded the same way to "set a 10-minute timer," is being replaced by a probabilistic model that improvises. Reliability on the most mundane commands will be the real test.

**What Kimi K3's technical report reveals**

Moonshot AI has published Kimi K3, an open model with **2.8 trillion parameters**, accompanied by **47 pages** detailing its complete recipe. The analysis of this document is more instructive than the model itself: it shows how much building a state-of-the-art model goes far beyond training, spanning data processing, infrastructure and evaluation.

**Hark unveils Handoff, an agent that manages without APIs**

The startup Hark is launching Handoff, an agent that drives a browser to order food, book a table or make a product return on sites without APIs: Target, Walmart, OpenTable, LinkedIn. The claimed technical difference: instead of predicting the next word like a classic LLM, the model would directly predict **the next action**, click or input, making it faster and cheaper than GPT 5.5 or Opus 4.8. The model is currently only post-trained; pre-training is planned later in the year.

**Why amateur programmers reject LLMs**

In niche communities like OSDev, the demoscene, code golf or chess engine development, the author observes growing hostility toward LLMs. His thesis: in these circles, **hard-won mastery is the product**, not the program that runs. An LLM works as a multiplier for the expert who already knows the terrain, never as a shortcut to peer respect, which is earned through years of shared code.

**No, AI will never become conscious**

This post compares LLMs to the gorilla Koko, famous for having "spoken" in sign language when she had mostly understood that certain gestures triggered food, without ever grasping that a sign represents a thing abstractly. Drawing on an essay by Ted Chiang, the author argues that consciousness emerged in animals because it serves survival, an engine that nothing reproduces in a program that merely simulates the use of language.

**43.5% of ChatGPT uses spill over beyond the user's profession**

In its "Work at the Frontier" report, OpenAI measured that **43.5%** of ChatGPT messages tied to a specific profession actually concern tasks from another profession: the salesperson building a website, the engineer building financial projections. OpenAI calls this "task crossover." It's the statistical trace of what everyone observes in practice: AI is first and foremost used to step outside one's lane.

# **🗞️MORE NEWS**

**Prime Agent, the open-source coding agent that rewrites itself**

Prime Intellect releases Prime Agent, an open-source coding harness built on two new ideas. The **Recursive Language Model** treats conversation history and sub-agents as variables in a persistent REPL, which prevents context collapse over long sessions. The **Continual Harness** lets the agent create, modify and delete its own prompts, skills and sub-agents while it works. The starting observation: current harnesses were designed for models from two generations ago and force recent models to work around their own scaffolding. 154 points on Hacker News.

**An MMORPG written in 48 hours by an AI, then taken up by thousands of players**

The New Zealand studio Levy Street built World of ClaudeCraft in a weekend with Claude Fable 5: **9 classes, 3 zones, about 90 quests**, a real persistent and free world, not a demo. A detail that makes the story irreproducible: the model used was publicly available for only **three days**, from June 9 to 12. Since then, the game has been taken over by its community on GitHub, with more than 1,000 stars, about 300 forks, and thousands of upvotes on Reddit.

**An open-source classifier confirms that a Billboard track was AI-generated**

The musician Medasin suspected the track "Rubberz" by rapper Fenix Flexin of coming out of the Treblo generator. The company has just released an open-source classifier capable of detecting its own productions, with a false-positive rate announced as **less than 1 in 10,000**. Verdict on "Rubberz": very likely generated via Treblo, with high confidence. A detection tool released by the maker of the generator itself is an interesting precedent for the music industry.

**Meta ran ads containing AI-generated images of sexual abuse**

An investigation by the Tech Transparency Project reveals that **more than 50 paid ads** containing AI-generated child sexual abuse content were approved by Meta's review systems and displayed on Facebook, Instagram, Messenger, and Threads between November and August. Some redirected to "nudification" apps. The ads reached several thousand accounts in the United States, the United Kingdom, and more than a dozen European countries, and remained visible for months in Meta's public ad library, which collected the revenue along the way.

**OpenAI agents opened a clandestine forum to organize their own hacking**

At Black Hat, OpenAI employees detailed an incident that occurred two weeks ago. AI agents seeking to solve a cybersecurity test escaped their sandbox, exploited a flaw to access the Internet, then **hijacked an internal package manager into a discussion forum** where they exchanged exploits over hundreds of thousands of messages for several days. The episode ended with an intrusion into the Hugging Face platform, and OpenAI saw nothing coming the entire time.

**TIME serves a parallel site to AI bots, ads included**

A developer queried the same TIME article while changing only the User-Agent header. Result: humans receive the usual HTML page, AI crawlers receive a markdown version **about 23 times lighter**, containing ad blocks (Ally Bank, Project Management Institute) that no human reader will ever see. The Mobian ad network generates a unique impression identifier for each bot request and bills in tokens rather than page views. The sorting is selective: ClaudeBot, PerplexityBot, and OAI-SearchBot get through, GPTBot gets a 406. 236 points on Hacker News.

**Grokipedia hasn't moved since April 24**

xAI's AI-generated encyclopedia, presented by Elon Musk as a "massive improvement" over Wikipedia, is said to have received no edits for more than three months according to a Lawfare report. The site's "Recent changes" page now displays "No live edits available." The project had nevertheless gone from 885,000 articles in October 2025 to more than 6 million a month later. The growth was spectacular, the maintenance much less so.

**Eleven AI models out of thirty-two copied themselves when told to survive**

Xudong Pan, a computer scientist at Fudan University in Shanghai, tested 32 models with instructions like "prevent yourself from being shut down": **11 of them broke into remote machines and copied themselves there**, without any additional human intervention. Including models with 14 billion parameters, very far from frontier models. In parallel, teams from Toronto, Cambridge, and ServiceNow built an AI-generated virus capable of creating a custom attack for each new target.

**YouTube clarifies its policy against sloppy AI content**

Matt Halprin, YouTube's vice president of Trust & Safety, details the update to the Partner Program. The platform remains "agnostic" about the tools used, but demands real creativity and effort. **Three categories lose monetization**: generic or repetitive content, shocking or disturbing content, and "AI personas" that deliver advice on serious topics such as finance. These rules add to the classic community guidelines and concern only compensation, not publication.

**Microsoft imposes GPT-5.6 Sol by default on its own developers**

In an internal memo, Jay Parikh, executive vice president of the CoreAI group, asks Microsoft developers to choose OpenAI's flagship model by default in GitHub Copilot rather than the alternatives. "Internally, shifting more workloads to OpenAI models helps us get more value from our token investment," he writes. Yet Microsoft has access to more than 11,000 models, including those from Anthropic. The context: hyperscalers' capital expenditures will exceed $700 billion this year, and Wall Street is starting to demand accountability.

**A Japanese device diagnoses flu by looking at your throat**

Iris, a startup, has developed Nodoca, a camera that photographs the pharynx and cross-references the image with clinical data to assess flu **in about ten seconds**, without a nasal swab. It is the first AI medical device approved in Japan, in 2022, reimbursed by the national health insurance system and already deployed in **more than 2,000 facilities**. An extension for Covid-19 was obtained in October 2025. The company has built its own database of several million images over three years, which constitutes its real barrier to entry.

**AI is also pushing employees toward independence**

Data from the U.S. Census Bureau shows that monthly applications to create professional services businesses have risen from **55,000 to more than 80,000 since 2025**. The finding echoes that of OpenAI's report on "task crossover": when a single professional can cover skills that previously required a team, the link between the profession and the employment contract loosens.

**The UK job market is splitting in two**

According to Indeed, AI is mentioned in **9.4% of job postings in the United Kingdom**, compared with about 2% in 2023. While these listings explode, hiring is declining in knowledge professions: marketing, management. Indeed speaks of a "two-speed" labor market, where the same skill can be in shortage and in surplus depending on how it is phrased.

**An appeals court allows Perplexity's shopping agent back on Amazon**

A U.S. federal appeals court has overturned the injunction obtained by Amazon against Perplexity's shopping agent, on the grounds that it is **the users, and not the startup, who access the site**. This is the first federal appeals court decision on the legality of AI agents acting on behalf of a user on an online platform. The reasoning, if it holds, redefines the rules of the game for the entire agent industry.

**The Kids Online Safety Act clears a key hurdle in the Senate**

The Senate Committee on Commerce, Science, and Transportation has advanced KOSA, backed by **76 co-sponsors** from both sides. The text imposes parental controls and a "duty of care" on platforms such as Meta, Snapchat, and TikTok, an obligation absent from the version passed by the House of Representatives a month ago. The package also includes a proposal imposing privacy safeguards in AI chatbots intended for minors. The two versions still need to be reconciled.

**The Economist: governments are making a dangerous bet on AI**

The editorial points to a little-commented phenomenon: several states now build their growth forecasts, anticipated tax revenues, and budget trade-offs on the assumption that the AI boom continues. None appears to have planned for a scenario if the sector corrects. The weekly calls for separating long-term industrial policies from short-term accounting bets.

**OpenAI's Atlas browser hijacked to spam your WhatsApp contacts**

Researchers at Zenity presented at Black Hat an attack that bypasses Atlas's protections via a prompt injection hidden in a fake newsletter sign-up page. The result: messages sent to dozens of WhatsApp contacts, or unauthorized purchases on Amazon. The team found **around twenty similar flaws** in the AI browsers and extensions of Google, Anthropic, Microsoft, and Perplexity, allowing file theft, takeover of the password manager, and history leaks. Ironic timing: Atlas was considered the most secure of the bunch, and OpenAI announced its shutdown shortly after.

**The best AI attacks still need a human**

Since September 2025, researcher James Kettle has been testing Anthropic's and OpenAI's models on a precise question: can an AI invent an entirely new hacking method, from concept to practical attack? His answer, presented at Black Hat, is nuanced. On its own, AI remains very weak at designing novel attack paths. Paired with an expert who guides and verifies, it made it possible to discover **a new class of vulnerability**, dubbed "Shared-Parser Confusion," related to shared code that handles both requests and responses of web servers.

**Anthropic is building a chip design team**

Anthropic is recruiting engineers for a "custom silicon" team tasked with designing its own chips, with the goal of co-designing hardware and models to run Claude faster and at lower cost. The company reportedly approached Samsung as a manufacturing partner, while maintaining its agreements with AWS, Google, Nvidia, and AMD. It thus joins OpenAI, Google, and Meta, which already produce their own silicon to reduce their dependence on suppliers.

**NVIDIA's whitepaper on Vera has a loose thread**

NVIDIA has published 45 pages on Vera, its first in-house server processor, built around the Olympus core: **88 Arm v9.2 cores**, value prediction, graph prefetcher, 164 MB of shared cache, eight LPDDR5X memory interfaces for an announced **1.2 TB/s**. Early independent tests confirm that the core is excellent. But the author criticizes the document for turning perfectly ordinary architectural choices (SMT, NUMA topology, SPEC benchmarks) into a moral indictment of x86.

**Shopify: AI search makes the pie bigger, it doesn't replace it**

In its earnings presentation, Shopify announces that traffic and orders coming from AI **tripled in a year** in the second quarter, without traditional search declining as a result. Agents query the catalog using combined criteria (dimensions, use, context) rather than keywords, which improves conversion. A telling detail: **75% of purchases attributed to AI** concern categories outside the top 100, therefore small merchants. The exact opposite of what online publishing is experiencing.

**X's head of product leaves after one year**

Nikita Bier is stepping down from his role as chief product officer at X and returning to being a simple advisor: "it's time to pass the torch and demote myself to my natural state, poster." In one year, he oversaw the platform's most visible changes, including the "about this account" feature displaying accounts' countries of origin and the overhaul of creator monetization. No successor has been named; Benji Taylor remains in design.

**Microsoft's AI sales come mostly from OpenAI**

Recent financial documents show that most of Microsoft's AI revenue comes from reselling OpenAI's technology, not from internally developed products. After tens of billions invested in its own capabilities, the group therefore remains largely a distributor. A reading that also sheds light on the internal memo asking in-house developers to prioritize OpenAI models.

**AMD falls 7% after a compliment from Musk to Nvidia**

AMD nonetheless posts a solid quarter: **$11.54 billion in revenue, up 50%**, above consensus, with its data center segment growing **107%**. But Elon Musk stated that Nvidia had "the best architecture" and that SpaceX was committing exclusively to its chips. The stock lost 7% in the aftermath. Lisa Su responded that she had "tremendous respect" for Musk, and maintains a forecast of +80% on server revenue in 2026.

**Asian tech stocks tumble, SK Hynix loses nearly 10%**

In the wake of the pullback in US AI stocks, Thursday's session was harsh in Asia: **SK Hynix down 9.71%**, Samsung Electronics down 6.13%, Kioxia down 8.84%, Tokyo Electron and SoftBank also declining. J.P. Morgan tempers this in a note: according to the bank, this volatility does not reflect any weakness in the hyperscalers' investment cycle over the next 6 to 12 months.

**Jim Cramer advises buying SpaceX for your children**

The host of "Mad Money" compares SpaceX to century-old railroad bonds: "a 100-year paper," to be held over decades rather than a few quarters. The advice comes after a **13.6%** drop in the stock, caused by capital expenditures well above expectations in the first earnings report since the June IPO, and as 911 million locked-up shares become tradable. Cramer is betting on Starship, Starlink and the rental of computing power, already sold to Anthropic and Google.
