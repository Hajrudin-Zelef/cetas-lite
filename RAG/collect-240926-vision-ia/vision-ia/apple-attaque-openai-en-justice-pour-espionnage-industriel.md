Apple has just filed a lawsuit against OpenAI, its former hardware chief **Tang Tan**, and its **io devices** division, accusing the trio of turning an aggressive recruiting campaign into a veritable **pipeline for stealing trade secrets**. According to the complaint, **more than 400 former Apple employees** now work at the lab. And Cupertino isn't just seeking damages: the company is demanding a **complete redesign of OpenAI's still-unreleased device**.

Key takeaways:

- Apple names **Tang Tan**, its former hardware chief who moved to OpenAI, as well as the **io devices** unit in charge of the lab's future connected object.

- **400 former Apple employees and more** have since joined OpenAI, a flow Apple describes as systematic and organized, not as simple turnover.

- Apple claims it warned OpenAI via a **letter in February** and received **no response**.

- The complaint demands a **redesign of OpenAI's secret device**, a far heavier request than financial compensation.

- Worth reading: the text of the Apple v. OpenAI complaint.

What it changes: this is the first major legal showdown in which a hardware giant directly attacks an AI lab on its own turf—that of devices. If a court actually ordered a redesign of OpenAI's device, it could delay the lab's highly anticipated "device." Beyond that, the case turns Silicon Valley's talent war—until now settled with checks—into a battlefield for lawyers.

Nine months after assembling a team in Paris, **Rémi Cadène**, a former scientist on Tesla's **Optimus** program, unveils **Northstar**, a humanoid robot designed first for European factories and warehouses, and ultimately for the home. Behind him, a new startup, **UMA**, and a clearly open source DNA.

Key points:

- The founder worked on Tesla's **Autopilot** and **Optimus**, before leading robotics at **Hugging Face**, where he created **LeRobot**, a benchmark open source library.

- Northstar swaps the human face for a **neutral visor** and clothes its joints in a **soft shell**: a deliberate choice of a "clothed machine" rather than a disturbing fake human.

- Priority target: European **factories and warehouses**, before an extension to residential use.

- A proudly European positioning, at a time when American and Chinese humanoids monopolize attention.

Why it matters: Europe finally has a credible humanoid project, led by an engineer who has seen Tesla's robotics up close and who bets on openness rather than secrecy. The choice of a deliberately non-anthropomorphic robot is also a bet on our relationship with machines: reassuring by owning up to the fact that it's a tool, not a companion disguised as a human.

Anthropic is beefing up **Claude Code**: its development environment now includes an **integrated browser** that allows the AI to open web pages, read them, and interact with them, without leaving the tool. The topic was **picked up by six sources** within hours (The Decoder, Towards Data Science, Hacker News, GitHub), a sign that the tech community sees a real milestone here.

In detail:

- The agent can now **view, click, and enter text** on external sites directly from Claude Code.

- **Sensitive write actions** (purchases, account creation) go through **safety classifiers** and require your **explicit validation**.

- The goal: to allow the AI to accomplish real tasks online, not just generate code on its own.

The impact to remember: with this browser, Claude Code ceases to be a simple coding assistant and becomes a true **web agent**, capable of fetching information, testing an interface, or following a procedure on a site. The safeguard (human validation for any purchase or account creation) sums up the central tension of agentic AI: the more useful you make it, the more you have to fence in what it's allowed to do on its own. For developers as well as the curious, it's a very concrete glimpse of what AI-assisted work will look like: delegating no longer lines of code, but actions.

Apple's lawsuit against OpenAI sparked fireworks elsewhere: **Elon Musk** and **Sam Altman** once again publicly clashed on **X**. But according to Altman, if Musk is going back on the attack, it's mainly because of the **release of a new OpenAI model** this week, not really because of the legal case.

The sequence of events:

- The clash erupts in the wake of **Apple's lawsuit** against OpenAI.

- **Altman** accuses **Musk** of being "obsessed again" with him because of a recent **OpenAI model launch**.

- A new salvo in a rivalry that has been simmering since Musk left the OpenAI board, which he nevertheless co-founded.

The context: behind the theater, a real signal. OpenAI's release cadence continues to irritate its rivals, and this recurring feud serves as a proxy for a more serious question: who sets the tempo in AI. We will remain cautious about an episode that is as much soap opera as news.

# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

**SensorFM: Google's health AI that learns from your sensors** (The Decoder, BREAKING). Google Research unveils a foundational model trained on **more than a trillion minutes** of data from **5 million users** of Fitbit and Pixel Watch. It beats existing benchmarks on **34 of the 35** health and behavior tasks tested, and could one day power a homegrown AI health coach, even though Google has not yet announced any integration.

**GPT-5.6 beats Claude/Fable-5 at coding, for half the cost** (Rohan Paul). On the **DeepSWE** benchmark, which tests coding agents on real open-source repositories with automated verification, GPT-5.6 reaches **72 to 73% for $8.40 per task**, versus **70% for $13 to $22** for Fable-5. That is a performance/cost ratio **1.5 to 2.6 times** better, on one of the most realistic tests of the moment.

**A device revives the eyes of deceased donors** (MIT Technology Review, via The Rundown). A team in Barcelona has built a box that circulates an oxygen-rich liquid through the eye, keeping harvested eyes **viable and light-sensitive for hours** after death, whereas an untreated eye degrades in less than 24 hours. Enough to remove one of the major obstacles to **whole-eye transplantation**.

**AI and quantum computers team up to design new drugs** (Wired). Researchers are combining generative AI and **quantum computing** to generate new **therapeutic peptides**, with a stated goal: **rare diseases** and underserved populations. A still modest demonstration, but one that sketches what quantum could bring to treatment discovery.

**41% of long LinkedIn posts may be written by AI** (The Decoder). According to a Pangram analysis conducted across five platforms, **one in four long posts** on social media is entirely AI-generated, and **LinkedIn leads the pack with 41%**. The network accounts for only a third of the posts scanned but concentrates nearly **two-thirds** of the detected AI content, and the measurement is considered conservative.

**AI boosts researchers' careers but homogenizes science** (IEEE Spectrum, 146 points on Hacker News). A study shows that AI improves productivity and researchers' trajectories, but **concentrates research on the same promising topics**, to the detriment of unconventional avenues. The risk: a homogenization of scientific discovery, with less innovation on the margins of dominant trends.

**Giving AIs a "human-like" memory** (arXiv, Shanghai University). Fast models (linear attention, state-space) compress everything they read into a **single internal state**, which makes them "forgetful" over long contexts: like reading a detective novel with **a single Post-it note**. The authors propose a mechanism inspired by human memory to overcome this limitation revealed by "needle-in-a-haystack" tests.

**DeepMind wants to frame task delegation to AIs** (arXiv, Google DeepMind). The framework treats delegation as a series of decisions: should the task be entrusted, how should it be explained, how should the work then be verified. Instead of rigid rules that break at the first unexpected event, the researchers envision a **market where agents bid** on tasks, under strict supervision and **cryptographic proofs**.



# **🗞️MORE NEWS**

**Open source models may have only 6 months left before regulation** (Interconnects). The White House is reportedly considering executive orders to regulate the most powerful open source models, with a possible **ban on those exceeding the level of GPT-5.5 or Claude Opus 4.8**. The argument is geopolitical: Chinese models like **DeepSeek** now dominate open weights, and Washington wants to regain control. Lacking an economic champion to defend open source, the ecosystem could falter within the **next six months**.

**The robot "gone mad" that panicked the web was just a scripted demo** (via Superhuman AI). For days, millions of people believed that a humanoid robot filmed in **Indonesia** was attacking office employees, arms raised in a combat stance. The reality is far less dramatic: **every movement was preprogrammed**, a choreography intended to demonstrate its mobility. The episode says a lot about our fascination, and our distrust, toward robot autonomy.

**Waze switches to Gemini for natural-language voice commands** (The Verge, BREAKING). Google is integrating its **Gemini** assistant into Waze: users can now **report an incident by voice**, suggest a map correction, or search for a destination in everyday language ("find an open cafe"). It is one of the most concrete applications of Gemini in a mass-market tool used heavily every day.

**Caestro wants you to "hire" AI employees who learn on the job** (via Superhuman AI). The platform offers **autonomous AI agents** that you recruit with a real job description, with nothing to configure. They integrate with **Slack or email**, accumulate company memory, and have each of their actions **validated by a human manager** that you designate. Founded by former employees of **Snowflake, Goldman Sachs, and Microsoft**, the startup is betting on AIs that "learn the ropes" like a new colleague.

**The Apple car fiasco secretly gave birth to its AI chips** (The Verge). Apple's autonomous car project (Project Titan) never came to fruition, but it reportedly gave rise to the **Neural Engine**, the core of AI processing in all of its current chips. Originally designed for computer vision (Face ID), this component became the **backbone of the M-series chips** and on-device AI. A fine example of a technological failure that ends up paying off, elsewhere.

**The revolt against AI data centers is only just beginning** (The Verge). Everywhere, local communities are opposing the construction of giant **data centers**, heavy consumers of electricity and land. The Verge recalls the precedent of Apple's data center in **Ireland in 2015** to shed light on a growing resistance, as these installations weigh on **local power grids**.

**Microsoft replaces OpenAI and Anthropic with its own models in Excel and Outlook** (via Rohan Paul). To lighten the **Copilot** bill, Microsoft is gradually shifting Excel and Outlook to its **in-house models**, at the expense of paid calls to external labs. **Mustafa Suleyman**, the group's AI chief, admits he wants to "reduce then eliminate" this dependency, even at the cost of straining a long-standing partnership with OpenAI.
