**Thomson Reuters invests 40 million to own its AI rather than rent it**

The group is launching "Thomson," its own language model built on Alibaba's Qwen, for about $40 million over two years. A telling detail: good benchmark scores appear only when the model accesses in-house content, starting with the Westlaw legal database. For CTO Joel Hron, the question is not raw intelligence but knowing which intelligence must be owned. A textbook case for all professions sitting on a proprietary documentary base.

**Taiwan indicts Nvidia employees for illegal export of AI servers to China**

Nine people are being prosecuted, including employees of Nvidia and Super Micro, for having routed high-end AI servers to China while circumventing compliance procedures. Prosecutors describe an organized collusion at multiple levels, motivated by considerable profits, and accuse the defendants of having "severely damaged the nation's image." The United States has banned the sale of the most advanced chips to China since 2022, while recently having reopened the door to older models like the H200.

**Threads tests automatic podcast transcription**

Meta is experimenting with a tool that automatically transcribes audio clips posted on Threads, to allow them to be read while scrolling through one's feed without sound. Connor Hayes, head of the platform, mentions several other podcast-sharing tools in preparation. No official launch is guaranteed at this stage.

**The data center "trade" comes under political pressure**

Cost of electricity, water consumption: opposition is growing in states like Pennsylvania and Texas, which are tightening their requirements for new data centers. For Jim Cramer, stricter rules would paradoxically benefit Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft, and Meta, the only ones capable of financing compliance and compensating local communities. Fewer speculative developers also means less competition for land, labor, and power.

**Nvidia in talks to invest in Perplexity**

The valuation mentioned exceeds 30 billion dollars, more than 50% above the last round, according to The Information. Perplexity's annualized revenue has tripled to exceed 750 million dollars. Worth noting, as is often the case with Nvidia: the money invested in its holdings largely comes back in the form of revenue when they buy its chips.

**The SEC is investigating Situational Awareness, the AI world's star hedge fund**

Leopold Aschenbrenner's fund, a former OpenAI employee, had bet everything on AI stocks before seeing billions evaporate in late July during the sector's pullback. The U.S. regulator has sent subpoenas to the banks that managed its trading and financing, requiring them to preserve all information about its operations. The fund is not accused of any wrongdoing and says it is fully cooperating.
