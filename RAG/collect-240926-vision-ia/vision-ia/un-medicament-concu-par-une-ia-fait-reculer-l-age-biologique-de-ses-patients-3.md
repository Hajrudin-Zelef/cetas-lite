# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

According to Similarweb, ChatGPT has regained ground and once again captures more than half of AI chatbot web traffic, putting an end to Gemini's comeback. The twelve-month picture nevertheless tells the opposite story: OpenAI's share was **73.3%** a year ago. Over the same period, **Gemini doubled** its own and **Claude multiplied its share by nearly five**. To be handled with caution, these figures measure only web visits: neither mobile apps nor desktop clients are counted, and that is precisely where daily usage is shifting.

**Tesla unveils a Cybercab motor without the slightest rare earth**

Presented on September 3 in Austin, the Cybercab's motor does entirely without rare earths, neodymium included, while being **18% more compact and 25% lighter**. Elon Musk describes a design "extremely difficult" to achieve. The stakes go far beyond automobiles: rare-earth magnets are everywhere in chip manufacturing, from lithography machines to wafer manipulators, and **China controls about 70% of global refining**. Tesla is in the habit of generalizing its motor innovations across its entire lineup, as it did with the Model 3 design. Analyst Gary Black nonetheless judges the launch overall disappointing.

**Alibaba releases Qwen-Drive 1.0, a single model for the entire car**

Alibaba's research arm publishes a model that brings together in a single system environmental perception, traffic question-and-answer, and route planning. The stated goal is ambitious: a single model to manage both the cabin and driving. The most interesting research result is elsewhere, in a limitation the researchers themselves document: text-image models **do not spontaneously understand three-dimensional space**, that spatial awareness must be explicitly trained. Another acknowledged downside: the explanations the model gives for its decisions do not always correspond to the maneuver it actually executes.

**Voicebox wants to replace customer review forms with voice**

You scan a QR code or hold your phone near an NFC chip, you speak for a few seconds, and that's it: the message is automatically transcribed and sent to the company with a sentiment analysis. Its CEO Karan Gupta believes speech recognition is finally fast and accurate enough for this type of use. The startup has just launched a **directory** that will make these voice reviews publicly viewable, in the manner of Google Maps. Airports are already using the service. Wired sees in it a symptom of the wave of voice-centered software, alongside Wispr and Granola.

**How ChatGPT wiped Nairobi's homework industry off the map**

At the sector's peak, at the start of the decade, **at least 40,000 people** in Nairobi made a living writing essays and dissertations for American and British students, in medicine, computer science, or engineering, sometimes directly using their clients' university credentials. Teresios Bundi, 34, wrote more than 2,500 of them in twelve years, billed at 40 to 70 dollars apiece. The launch of ChatGPT in 2022 caused prices and then orders to collapse, with a contagion effect on transcription, data annotation, and content moderation for Meta. Scarcely anything remains but the "humanizer" trade, which consists of reworking AI-generated texts to fool detectors. Oxford professor Mark Graham anticipates the same shock elsewhere, among bankers, accountants, engineers, and architects.

**The Economist: the jobs apocalypse is postponed, a boom is coming**

The dominant discourse for three years has heralded massive job destruction. The data The Economist compiles tell another story: the heralded destruction does not show up in the figures, and what is being observed today is instead an AI-related hiring boom. The debate is obviously not closed, particularly on the sectoral distribution of these creations, but the article has the merit of putting data up against a prediction that circulates mostly as a conviction.

**UBS will require AI skills for hiring starting in 2027**

The Swiss bank will make AI proficiency a **mandatory qualification** for its graduates and interns in Global Banking and Markets starting in 2027. It will not be about checking a box on a résumé: candidates will have to demonstrate in interviews how they concretely use AI to improve their results and efficiency. Santander is also looking for advanced AI users. In the background, Morgan Stanley anticipates the disappearance of more than **200,000 banking jobs in Europe** within five years.

**An a16z partner sweeps away the fear of a "permanent underclass"**

Interviewed on Lenny Rachitsky's podcast, Anish Acharya, a partner at Andreessen Horowitz, calls a "dark fantasy" the idea that AI would create a caste of workers permanently left behind. His argument: the current boom is far less centralized than previous ones, with **around twenty relevant players at each layer** of the technology stack. He cites the market for coding agents, where Claude Code, Codex, Lovable, Replit, and Wabi compete for ground without any one sweeping the pot, and notes that job postings are holding up in occupations nonetheless reputed to be threatened, such as radiology. Sam Altman made comparable remarks in July. In San Francisco, the most tangible effect of the boom remains for now the surge in rents.

**Bernie Sanders wants to ban superintelligence by law**

Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Greg Casar are preparing a text that would **permanently ban** the development and deployment of artificial superintelligence, and temporarily suspend work on advanced AI while waiting for a new federal regulator to set its rules. The project provides for the creation of an AI agency at cabinet level, endowed with the power to monitor cutting-edge systems, impose a model review procedure, and have capabilities deemed dangerous removed. Sanction envisaged for companies in violation: **outright dissolution**.

**Trump floods Truth Social with AI-generated images**

The American president spent **more than ten hours** on Sunday publishing dozens of AI-generated images depicting him as a hero vanquishing Iran and claiming territories, without ever flagging their synthetic nature. All of it accompanied by unsubstantiated claims about the Iranian economy and the hundreds of billions of dollars he says he has brought back to the United States, illustrated with infographics with no verifiable source. In the background, controversy is growing around Truth API, a service billed at $100,000 per month that senators have asked the SEC to investigate.

**Moonshot AI prepares its IPO in Hong Kong**

The creator of the **Kimi** assistant has filed a confidential IPO application in Hong Kong, with a target of raising $3 billion, according to Reuters sources. The Chinese company would be valued at around $50 billion in a funding round still underway, which would make it one of the largest IPOs in the sector. The timeline and financial terms remain subject to regulatory approval and market conditions.

**Nvidia could finance half of Thinking Machines' next round**

Mira Murati's startup is reportedly negotiating a raise of $5 to $6 billion on a pre-investment valuation of at least $40 billion, according to The Information. **Nvidia would contribute about half of the round**, with Accel expected to lead it. Thinking Machines Lab is reportedly already generating at least a few hundred million dollars in annualized revenue. Notably, the valuation under discussion is lower than the more than $50 billion mentioned in earlier negotiations, and nothing is finalized.
