Anthropic spent almost the entire week fighting to get its models back online. The cause: an order from the Trump administration demanding it cut access to **all foreign nationals**, including those on American soil, and even down to the company's own employees. Unable to filter precisely, Anthropic had to block **Fable 5 and Mythos 5 for absolutely everyone.**

According to The Verge, this is **the first time US export controls have been used to restrict access to an AI model** in this way. "To my knowledge, this is the first time US export controls have been used to control access to an AI model in this way," summarizes one expert cited.
The order is so broad that it **even blocks Anthropic employees based in the United States**, a situation the company calls unusual.
To justify itself, the government invokes "national security authorities," **without ever detailing the legal basis** for the decision.
The story has been picked up by **4 newsrooms** (The Verge, Ars Technica, Wired, Natural 20 AI): the entire tech press is talking about it.
Anthropic published its own __clarification__ on access to Fable and Mythos.

Why it matters: for the first time, an AI model is being treated as a strategic technology, subject to the same regime as armaments or advanced semiconductors. If the precedent holds, every American lab will have to rethink who can technically access its models, and users and developers outside the United States could find their access cut overnight, without notice or clear recourse.

A thunderclap in the talent war: **Noam Shazeer**, VP of Engineering at Google and co-lead of Gemini, is joining OpenAI. Co-author of the founding paper **"Attention Is All You Need"** that gave rise to Transformers, he had nonetheless only returned to Google in 2024, as part of a **$2.7 billion** deal around Character.AI.

Shazeer wasn't running just anything: he **co-led Gemini**, Google's flagship in-house model.
His return to Google was very recent (2024) and had been costly, via the Character.AI deal at **$2.7 billion.**
This is the **second recruiting earthquake of the year** in AI, after Karpathy's move to Anthropic.
The information is confirmed by **3 sources** (CNBC Tech, The Decoder, Hacker News).

What it changes: star transfers are no longer anecdotal, they are redrawing the map of brains at the top of the industry. Losing one of Gemini's two architects to the direct competitor, and moreover one of the inventors of the architecture underlying all of today's generative AI, is a worrying signal for Google at the most tense moment in the model race. For OpenAI, it's the opposite: a prize capture that further strengthens its R&D and its aura among the best researchers.

Meeting in **Évian**, France, the G7 did something unprecedented: devoting an **entire working lunch to artificial intelligence.** Around the table were the leaders of the seven largest democracies, but also, for the first time, **the CEOs of the largest AI companies** (OpenAI, Google, Anthropic) who came to debate regulation directly.

The stated framework for the discussions: a "safe, fast, and effective" deployment of AI.
This is the **first time AI bosses have sat alongside heads of state** on this subject.
According to the account relayed by AI Secret, the exchanges were **eclipsed by Washington's decision** to block foreign access to its most powerful models (see the Anthropic case above).
On the European side, concrete guarantees ("checks") are being demanded in response to the American approach.

The context: AI is officially shifting into the top-tier diplomatic arena, on the same level as climate or trade. When regulators and industry bosses negotiate in the same room, the rules of the game for the entire sector can evolve very quickly, and Europe is clearly seeking not to let the United States set the tempo. It remains to be seen whether this first lunch will lead to a common framework or just a display.


Anthropic is tightening the links between two of its tools: its **design assistant** and its coding agent **Claude Code.** The two now work much better together, with the goal of streamlining the passage from mockup to code.

The improvement concerns **interoperability** between the design assistant and Claude Code.
The idea: a more continuous design-to-code flow, without a break between visual conception and technical implementation.
The announcement is relayed by **2 sources** (Engadget AI, The Rundown AI).

The takeaway: the news may seem minor next to the day's geopolitical jolts, but it illustrates a deeper shift. Anthropic no longer sells isolated building blocks; the company is building an **integrated ecosystem** where its tools talk to each other. For product teams, bringing design and code together in a single AI-driven chain potentially means fewer back-and-forths between designers and developers, and a prototype that becomes a working application faster. It's also the classic strategy of large platforms: once several tools are interlocked, it becomes much harder to leave just one of them.

**__Predicting a model's failures before it even launches__** (The Decoder) Researchers at OpenAI propose a method to estimate **how often a model will make mistakes once deployed.** The approach aims to fill the blind spots of conventional safety tests, which don't always capture real-world failures in production. This could allow for more precise evaluation of a model before putting it in the public's hands.

**__AMIE, Google's medical AI, matches primary care physicians__** (Google Research) Google announces, backed by a study published in **Nature**, that its conversational system **AMIE** matches primary care physicians in the **management of complex diseases**, not just one-off diagnosis. The system can hold a dialogue, follow a patient over time, and adjust their care. One more step toward concrete clinical uses.

**__A neural network... made of goats in Age of Empires II__** (The Decoder) A Microsoft researcher built a working neural network using **goats, bridges, and ice ramps** in the game's map editor. Behind the joke lies a serious critique: his analysis of **315 papers** shows that more than half already assume LLMs have human traits **before even running the experiment.** The lesson: the medium (a chat interface or goats) doesn't change the math, but it does change the researcher's perception.

**__Amazon: useful quantum computers within 5 to 7 years__** (CNBC) An Amazon AI executive argues that quantum computing will reach **commercial usefulness in 5 to 7 years.** Amazon thus joins an increasingly contested race against **Microsoft, Google, and IBM.** Take this with a grain of salt given the track record on quantum timelines, which are often optimistic.
