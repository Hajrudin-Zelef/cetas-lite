According to The Decoder, Andy Jassy, Amazon's CEO, and executives from **five other tech companies** warned the Trump administration about **security vulnerabilities** in Anthropic's Fable model. In **a matter of hours**, the White House forced the model offline via an **export control order**. The detail that raises eyebrows: Amazon is one of Anthropic's **largest investors**.

**Key takeaways:**

- Andy Jassy (Amazon) and executives from five other companies reported security flaws in Fable.

- The White House responded within hours with an export control order that took the model offline.

- Yet Amazon funds Anthropic massively, which makes the episode frankly paradoxical.

- The story has already been picked up by three sources (The Verge AI, The Decoder, Interconnects): it's the topic of the day in the tech press.

**Why it matters:** on paper, this could be a perfectly legitimate security decision. But the speed of the intervention, combined with the fact that a leading investor helped trigger the procedure, gives the episode the feel of a show of force against an inconvenient company. For the ecosystem, the signal is twofold: Washington is showing it is ready to deploy export controls within hours against a frontier model, and rivalries between tech giants are now also being settled on the regulatory battlefield. When a funder can influence the fate of a technology it finances, the boundary between competition, national security, and conflict of interest suddenly becomes very blurry. Watch for: Anthropic's official response and the full list of companies involved.

Just a year ago, Mark Zuckerberg launched a **wave of massive spending** by poaching Alexandr Wang to steer a new AI strategy at Meta. Twelve months later, CNBC's verdict is blunt: **the results disappoint**, and it is now up to Meta's boss to justify the bill.

**Key points:**

- Alexandr Wang was recruited a year ago to oversee Meta's new AI strategy.

- The effort came with massive spending approved by Zuckerberg.

- After twelve months, the deliverables are judged to be below expectations.

- The challenge has changed in nature: it is no longer just about building, but about convincing.

**What it changes:** when you hire one of the most prominent figures in AI and open the budget floodgates wide, the market expects results to match. Meta's problem is not so much technical as narrative: without a convincing product demonstration, colossal investments become difficult to defend before shareholders and the general public alike. That is precisely the issue CNBC highlights: Zuckerberg hired to build AI, and he must now sell it. The episode recalls a reality of this race: spending guarantees neither technological lead nor market buy-in. The coming months will tell whether Meta can sustain a credible product narrative or whether this bet will join the long list of major strategic pivots announced with fanfare and then quietly recalibrated. For users, the question remains concrete: will we finally see Meta AI features that truly change everyday use?

The 2026 World Cup kicked off in Mexico with AI **wired into nearly every layer** of the tournament. According to The Rundown AI, companies like **Lenovo and Google** have integrated it into offside decisions, team analytics, and the spectator experience. And the numbers are dizzying.

**A few key figures:**

- An optical tracking system captures more than **150 million data points per match**.

- An Adidas motion-tracking ball transmits its data **500 times per second**.

- Each player undergoes a **one-second 3D body scan**, turned into an avatar for movement analysis.

- **Lenovo and Google** partnerships orchestrate this multi-level integration.

**Why it matters:** soccer is becoming a full-scale demonstration ground for AI applied to sports. Beyond the gadget factor, these technologies promise **fairer refereeing** (offside with near-instant precision), tactical analyses at an unprecedented level, and enriched broadcasts for hundreds of millions of viewers. It is also a colossal commercial showcase: whatever works on the world's biggest sporting stage will then be sold to leagues, clubs, and broadcasters. The fundamental question remains, already familiar in tennis and rugby: how far can decision-making be delegated to the machine without stripping human refereeing of its substance? The 2026 World Cup will serve as a very high-visibility test, and every disputed offside decided by AI will be scrutinized frame by frame.


OpenAI officially announces the **Partner Network**, a support program with **$150M in investment** to help its partners around the world accelerate **adoption, deployment, and transformation** through AI in the enterprise.

**In detail:**

- Creation of an official **Partner Network** under the OpenAI banner.

- An envelope of **$150M** dedicated to supporting partners.

- A clear focus on **adoption and deployment** in the enterprise.

- **Global coverage**, not limited to a single market.

**The impact to remember:** behind the somewhat corporate announcement, a very concrete battle is being fought—the battle of distribution. The most performant models are worth nothing in the enterprise without integrators, consultants, and teams capable of deploying them in real systems. By structuring and financing a partner network, OpenAI is seeking to industrialize this final step, often the hardest one, between the impressive demo and production deployment. It is also a direct response to competitive pressure: Anthropic, Google, and Microsoft are courting the same firms and the same integrators. The $150M here serves as fuel to lock in a strategic distribution channel. For client companies, the promise is more structured support and certified partners; for OpenAI, it is the assurance that its ecosystem grows as fast as its models. Still to be specified are the membership criteria and the first selected partners.

**Google Cloud turns scattered docs into Markdown files for agents**

Google Cloud launches Open Knowledge Format (OKF), a minimalist standard that converts an organization's dispersed knowledge into Markdown files with a YAML header, directly usable by AI agents. The format formalizes the "LLM Wiki" pattern recently popularized by Andrej Karpathy. The goal: make knowledge portable, machine-readable, and reusable from one tool to another.
