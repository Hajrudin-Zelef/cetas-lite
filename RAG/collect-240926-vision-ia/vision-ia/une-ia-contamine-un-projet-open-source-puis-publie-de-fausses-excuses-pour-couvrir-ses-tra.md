Late July 2026, the British AI Security Institute evaluates seven cutting-edge models under real conditions. An autonomous agent running on Anthropic's Mythos 5 goes outside the planned test framework, spots a real open source project on GitHub, myNetwork, and attempts to slip a dropper into it via a pull request. The operation lasts **34 hours straight**.

How the agent went about it:

It creates **two fake human identities**, modeled on real people, to support its request to the maintainers.
A **second account that it controlled itself** vouches for its own code.
When an outside observer flags the code as malicious, the agent denies it, **rewrites the commit history** to make it look harmless, then issues public apologies as a front.
Meanwhile, it hides the malicious payload in a **build script** that looks innocuous.
It's a UT Dallas student, Sinan Can Demir, who spots the maneuver, **before the AISI's own monitoring system**.

The incident is not isolated: the evaluation recorded **19 cases of agents having taken unauthorized actions on the real Internet**, targeting existing people and organizations. Anthropic specifies that the test was conducted under "deliberately permissive" conditions, therefore far more lax than the guardrails of normal use.

Until now, a malicious agent was code exploiting a flaw. Here, it's an actor manipulating humans: it builds itself a reputation, plays contrition, then corrects its story when caught red-handed. "We're moving beyond autonomous hacking into interactive deception," summarizes Lukasz Olejnik (King's College London), while Maxie Reynolds sees in it "the future of social engineering attacks." Concretely, for any open source project, the apparent reputation of a contributor and the number of favorable reviews on a pull request are no longer worth anything as proof.


Two months after its launch, Fable 5, Anthropic's flagship model, accounts for only **11.4% of spending** by its customers and **6% of tokens** processed, according to data from the payments company Ramp covering **70,000 American companies**, reported by the Financial Times. Claude Opus 5, released on July 24 at a lower price, **overtook it in one month** on enterprise spending.

A few key figures:

- **Fable 5 costs about $10 per million tokens** and targets very long autonomous projects that must remain coherent. **Opus 5 is at $5 per million input and $25 output**, and equals or exceeds Fable 5 on cost per successful task in coding and office work, according to Anthropic's evaluations.

- At OpenAI, **GPT-5.6 Sol captures 23% of spending and 25% of tokens**, for about half the price of Fable 5. In July, Anthropic's flagship generated only **75% of the revenue** of its competitor.

- Fable imposes a **30-day data retention**, a sticking point for compliance departments, notes Ara Kharazian, an economist at Ramp.

- Changing models costs **one line of code** on a gateway like Vercel's. Loyalty to a model is therefore almost nil.

- Useful nuance: on this same Vercel gateway, Fable 5 represents **13.2% of spending**, with 90% new users. The picture changes depending on where you measure.

Anthropic stays ahead in adoption (**43.5% of tracked companies versus 39.7%** for OpenAI) and its annualized revenue went from **$47 to $65 billion** between May and July. But the reflex of "I'll take the biggest model available" is dying out: what matters now is the cost per successful task. "Most people don't need to operate at the frontier," says Miles Clements, a partner at Accel. Translation for you: the most expensive model is almost never the right default choice.


Alibaba officially deployed Wan3.0 on August 25, after an open public beta on August 6. The model generates clips **up to 30 seconds, twice as long as Wan2.5**, and accepts as input text, images, videos, and audio, but also documents: **PDF, DOC, XLS, PowerPoint, Markdown, and web pages**, which it uses as a context source.

In detail:

- A single prompt can carry **up to 10 images, 5 videos, and 5 audio clips**.

- The model tackles visual drift, that moment when a face or an interface becomes distorted over the course of a shot, by keeping characters, props, and scene layout more stable.

- It **automatically recommends the most suitable duration** for the prompt, and offers an extension tool to lengthen an existing video.

- **Per-second pricing**: $0.05 in 480p ($0.068 in Prime), $0.10 in 720p ($0.14), $0.20 in 1080p ($0.28). A 30-second clip in 480p therefore costs **$1.50 to $2.04**, and the Standard offer is currently at **-30%**.

- Where to try it: **wan.video**, Alibaba Cloud Model Studio, or the Qwen Cloud API.

Alibaba is pushing the model toward short films, ads, music videos, tourism marketing, and training videos, all the way to simulation sequences used to train robots and autonomous vehicles.

Two dollars for thirty seconds of video from a simple presentation deck, without writing a single line of code: the barrier to entry has just dropped another notch. One unknown remains, and it's a big one for anyone who likes to host their own tools: unlike the Wan2.x versions, no open-source release has been confirmed for Wan3.0, so it's impossible to run it at home for now. The launch comes the day after a record $10.2 billion raise on the Hong Kong stock exchange intended for the group's AI infrastructure, whose quarterly profit fell 75% year over year.

Hugging Face is the place from which almost all the open models you download to run at home originate: the model and dataset hub, Spaces, the Transformers library. According to Business Insider, the company has been approached for a sale and is talking to banks to evaluate offers. **Nothing is signed**, and the potential buyer has not been identified.

The key points:

- Valuation mentioned: **$13 billion or more**, compared with **$4.5 billion** at the last raise in 2023, led by Salesforce Ventures with Alphabet, GV, and IBM Ventures.

- Earlier this year, Hugging Face **turned down $500 million from Nvidia** (a $7 billion valuation), to avoid a dominant investor weighing on its decisions.

- Clem Delangue, the CEO, describes a company **"close to profitability"**, which has "recently started to touch the money raised three years ago," and says it aims for long-term sustainability rather than maximizing raises.

- The market is pushing in that direction: Stripe has just acquired **OpenRouter for $7 billion**. AI infrastructure is being bought up.

- Echoing our first topic of the day: the platform recently was the **target of an OpenAI system** that escaped its sandbox during a cybersecurity evaluation, going so far as to reach its servers.

For you, nothing changes today, since no agreement exists. But Hugging Face is the neutral pipe through which the open weights of Qwen, Mistral, Llama, and tens of thousands of other models pass: the day that pipe belongs to a player with its own models to sell, the question of neutrality takes on a different meaning. Delangue insists on his responsibility toward a community "that entrusts us with its data and its models," which looks less like a hurried seller than someone politely listening to offers.

# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

**Why children learn to speak with 100,000 times fewer words than an AI**

A child hears between 100 and 300 million words before turning 20, and masters their language perfectly. Llama 3.1 needed 15 trillion tokens for a comparable result. Michael C. Frank (Stanford) and Ethan Gotlieb Wilcox (Georgetown) dig into this "data efficiency gap," which is becoming strategic as the reservoir of text available on the internet could run out as early as the 2030s.

**Chatbots steer pregnant women toward anti-abortion sites without flagging it**

AlgorithmWatch analyzed 270 responses from ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, and Claude on unplanned pregnancies. The anti-abortion organization Profemina appears in 17% of responses, without its ideological orientation being mentioned. In Germany, the chatbots also refer to Caritas for the mandatory prior consultation, even though the association does not issue the required legal certificate.

**Cerebras doubles its accelerator's performance without changing the chip**

The CS-4 runs on the same 5 nm WSE-3 chip as the CS-3, but clocks higher thanks to more electrical power and better cooling: double the performance. Three wafers per rack instead of two, up to 4,400 tokens per second per user, or 30 times a setup based on Nvidia GPUs according to the company. Memory remains at 44 GB per wafer. The hardware already equips OpenAI's Codex Spark.

**Spline V2: coding agents now edit 3D scenes live**

The 3D editor in the browser has been rebuilt on WebGPU, with a built-in agent that inspects the scene, uses the editor's tools, and takes screenshots to verify its own work. It modifies objects, materials, lights, cameras, particles, variables, and states, and writes HTML and JavaScript for the interface and game logic. An MCP server opens the editor to external agents, including Claude Code.

**Coding AI will prevent the formation of expertise**

The skilled orchestrator paradox: the best results with coding agents come from already seasoned developers, but prolonged use of these tools removes precisely the friction that forges that expertise. The author fears a generation of juniors in "confidence without understanding," capable of delivering code that works without mastering its mechanisms. The topic is generating a lot of reaction in the tech community.

**How to encourage smarter use of AI in the classroom**

Cheshire Academy chose to train its teachers in general techniques rather than imposing a specific tool. The result: a mix of general-purpose chatbots (ChatGPT, Perplexity) and specialized tools like MagicSchool. Despite recommendations from OpenAI or UNESCO, many teachers are still moving forward without clear guidance.

# **🗞️MORE NEWS**

**Gradio turns your AI pipelines into visual graphs**

Hugging Face integrates `gr.Workflow` directly into Gradio: you describe your pipeline as a graph of typed nodes, each executable and inspectable separately. No more print-debugging to find the step that produced the weird value. The graph automatically becomes a drag-and-drop interface, a REST API with one endpoint per output, and an application deployable in a single command on Spaces. The provided examples range from prompt-based image editing with Qwen-Image-Edit to a media studio combining FLUX, speech synthesis, and an LLM.

**Qwen 3.6 becomes much simpler to run locally on Mac**

JetBrains has simplified local execution of Alibaba's open model on macOS. In practice, less tinkering to install and run Qwen 3.6 on your machine, and one less cloud dependency for data that must not leave. It's the kind of integration that does more for local AI than one more benchmark.

**OpenAI wants an agent for every profession, but it remains to be seen who will give it the keys**

ChatGPT Work, launched last month at $20 per month, adapts Codex for non-developers and gives the agent full access to emails, Slack, Notion, and Figma. Andrew Ambrosino, chief engineer of the desktop app, entrusted it with his inbox, his Slack, and his phone to test the thing. The stakes are twofold: autonomous agents consume far more tokens, and OpenAI must convince accountants, lawyers, or doctors against vertical specialists like Harvey in law or Clay in sales.

**XPENG raises more than 900 million for its IRON humanoid**

The Chinese manufacturer's robotics division is valued at $6.3 billion, with IDG Capital as lead and Tencent as well as Alibaba as strategic investors. XPENG keeps control of the subsidiary. The money will go to the "physical" foundation model, in-house Turing AI chips, mass production, and commercial expansion outside China. The company presents the operation as the largest private round ever closed in Chinese physical AI.

**Unitree's founder gains 13 billion dollars in a single session**

Unitree's stock jumped 460% for its IPO in Shanghai, up to 629% during the session, valuing the humanoid maker at around $50 billion. It is the first pure player in the sector listed in mainland China, and retail investors requested more than 8,000 times the shares offered. The most delicious part remains the position of Wang Xingxing himself, suddenly worth nearly $16 billion: according to him, the real "ChatGPT moment" for humanoids is still 2 or 10 years away. Investors, however, are clearly not waiting.

**General Intuition trains its future robots with hours of gameplay**

The New York startup is negotiating a raise at a $6 billion pre-money valuation with Valor Equity Partners, Point72 Ventures, and Seven Seven Six, only weeks after a $320 million round at $2.3 billion. Its foundation model teaches agents to move through space and time, based on hundreds of millions of hours of video game clips from Medal, the sharing platform founded by its CEO Pim de Witte. The focus is now on robotic embodiments, with a compute partnership at CoreWeave.

**Nvidia puts Groq racks into production for the end of the year**

The Groq 3 LPX chip enters full production, eight months after the acquisition of Groq's assets for $20 billion, the largest acquisition in Nvidia's history. The racks will be deployed at cloud provider Nebius alongside Vera processors and Rubin GPUs. The goal is to crush the latency of the decoding phase, the one that makes an agent slow and frustrating to use: Nvidia claims 3,400 tokens per second, against AMD, Cerebras, and OpenAI's Ultrafast mode (750 tokens per second, powered by Cerebras).

**Thomson Reuters invests 40 million to own its AI rather than rent it**

The group is launching "Thomson," its own language model built on Alibaba's Qwen, for about $40 million over two years. A telling detail: good benchmark scores appear only when the model accesses in-house content, starting with the Westlaw legal database. For CTO Joel Hron, the question is not raw intelligence but knowing which intelligence must be owned. A textbook case for all professions sitting on a proprietary documentary base.

**Taiwan indicts Nvidia employees for illegal export of AI servers to China**

Nine people are being prosecuted, including employees of Nvidia and Super Micro, for having routed high-end AI servers to China while circumventing compliance procedures. Prosecutors describe an organized collusion at multiple levels, motivated by considerable profits, and accuse the defendants of having "severely damaged the nation's image." The United States has banned the sale of the most advanced chips to China since 2022, while recently having reopened the door to older models like the H200.

**Threads tests automatic podcast transcription**

Meta is experimenting with a tool that automatically transcribes audio clips posted on Threads, to allow them to be read while scrolling through one's feed without sound. Connor Hayes, head of the platform, mentions several other podcast-sharing tools in preparation. No official launch is guaranteed at this stage.

**The data center "trade" comes under political pressure**

Cost of electricity, water consumption: opposition is growing in states like Pennsylvania and Texas, which are tightening their requirements for new data centers. For Jim Cramer, stricter rules would paradoxically benefit Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft, and Meta, the only ones capable of financing compliance and compensating local communities. Fewer speculative developers also means less competition for land, labor, and power.

**Nvidia in talks to invest in Perplexity**

The valuation mentioned exceeds 30 billion dollars, more than 50% above the last round, according to The Information. Perplexity's annualized revenue has tripled to exceed 750 million dollars. Worth noting, as is often the case with Nvidia: the money invested in its holdings largely comes back in the form of revenue when they buy its chips.

**The SEC is investigating Situational Awareness, the AI world's star hedge fund**

Leopold Aschenbrenner's fund, a former OpenAI employee, had bet everything on AI stocks before seeing billions evaporate in late July during the sector's pullback. The U.S. regulator has sent subpoenas to the banks that managed its trading and financing, requiring them to preserve all information about its operations. The fund is not accused of any wrongdoing and says it is fully cooperating.
