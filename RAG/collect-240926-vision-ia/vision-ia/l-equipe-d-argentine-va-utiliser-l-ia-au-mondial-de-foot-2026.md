# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

**DiffusionGemma: Google generates text from noise, not word by word**

Google unveils **DiffusionGemma**, an open-source model with **26 billion parameters** that produces text by diffusion, in the manner of image generators, rather than token by token. The result: about **4 times faster** than comparable autoregressive models (nearly 1000 tokens/s on an H100 GPU), at the cost of quality that still lags behind. Google presents it as an experimental tool intended for developers.

**An astrophysicist simulates black holes with Codex**

Astrophysicist Chi-kwan Chan relies on **Codex** to write the code for highly complex black hole simulations. Goal: to probe the physics of extreme conditions and put Einstein's **general relativity** predictions to the test. A fine illustration of AI as a copilot for fundamental research.

**AI maps the melting of Svalbard's glaciers**

Researchers at Friedrich-Alexander University (Germany) trained a model to identify glacier calving fronts with **a single labeled image** per glacier. The margin of error drops from more than **1 kilometer to 68.7 meters**, on par with a human expert. The method now monitors each month the **145 glaciers** of Svalbard, in Norway.

**Perplexity measures what AI agents change in knowledge work**

Perplexity and **Harvard Business School** compared AI agents and classic search on **10,000 identical queries**. Verdict: the agents (Computer platform) handle far more complex work, but take **26 minutes** on average versus **33 seconds** for a simple search. The whole trade-off lies there, between full autonomy and speed.

**When memory makes AI models worse**

A new study shows that memory tools built into models can, paradoxically, **degrade their performance**. It also points to a risk of **sycophancy**, where the model ends up approving its user a little too readily. Enough to fuel reflection on the design of LLMs and agents.

OpenAI commits to the European **Code of Practice** on transparency for AI-generated content. The company promises to advance **provenance** standards and develop tools to detect machine-created content, in the service of a more trustworthy ecosystem.

**Graviton5: AWS's new chip doubles down**

Amazon launches **Graviton5**, its in-house cloud processor: **192 cores** (twice as many as Graviton4), **3 nm** process, **+25%** performance per core and an L3 cache five times larger. Optimized for machine learning and agentic AI, the chip powers more than 350 types of EC2 instances.

**OpenAI wants its largest data center, financed by Nvidia**

OpenAI is negotiating the lease of a **10-gigawatt** data center in Ohio, its most ambitious infrastructure project to date. **Nvidia** could foot the bill. A fresh illustration of the frantic race for computing power among AI giants.

Developed with Google, **ArchIQ** already handles **90%** of drive-thru orders without human intervention, in both English and Spanish, and recognizes regulars. Deployed in 5 American restaurants, it has processed more than **a million** transactions. A winning comeback after the failed test conducted with IBM in 2024.

Independent musicians are suing Google, accusing it of training its **Lyria 3** model on their tracks posted on YouTube without authorization. Google defends itself by invoking the **broad license** accepted by creators when uploading. A case that could set a legal precedent on copyright and AI.

**Palantir takes a swipe at the big AI labs**
