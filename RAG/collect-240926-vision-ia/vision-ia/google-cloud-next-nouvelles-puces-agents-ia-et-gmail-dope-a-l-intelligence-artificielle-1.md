At its Cloud Next 2026 conference, Google presented two new generations of TPUs — the **TPU 8t** and **TPU 8i** — designed to be **faster and less costly** than previous versions. These chips represent Google's most direct offensive against Nvidia's overwhelming dominance in the AI infrastructure market.

The **TPU 8t** (training) and **TPU 8i** (inference) are optimized for distinct uses, allowing customers to choose the right performance/cost trade-off
Google nevertheless continues to **offer Nvidia GPUs** (H100, B200) on its cloud platform, implicitly acknowledging their indispensability
These chips are part of the **eighth generation** of TPUs, a program launched in 2015 — a rare longevity in the sector
The announcement comes as demand for AI computing explodes and delivery times for Nvidia GPUs lengthen by several months

**Why it matters:** Google is playing both sides: offering its own alternative to Nvidia GPUs while remaining a reseller of those same GPUs. This hybrid strategy could appeal to companies seeking to diversify their AI computing suppliers and reduce their dependence on a single player. If the announced performance holds up, these TPUs could reshuffle the deck in the AI cloud market, estimated at **several hundred billion dollars**.

Google is deploying **AI Overviews in Gmail for Work**, a feature that automatically generates instant summaries from multiple emails. No more endless scrolling through 47-message threads: Gemini's AI synthesizes the essentials in seconds.

Summaries are generated by **Gemini** and aggregate the content of **multiple emails** from the same conversation
The rollout initially targets **Google Workspace** users (Business and Enterprise plans)
The feature is part of a broader redesign of Workspace, which also includes AI capabilities in **Docs, Sheets, and Slides**
Google had already launched AI Overviews in Search — the extension to Gmail marks a **convergence of AI across the entire** Google ecosystem

**What it changes:** For the millions of professionals drowning in emails, it's potentially a major productivity gain. Google is transforming Gmail from a simple email client into a true work assistant, strengthening its competitive advantage against Microsoft Outlook (which also integrates Copilot). The AI battle in office productivity is intensifying.


Third major announcement from Cloud Next: Google unveils the **Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform**, a tool allowing enterprises to create and deploy **autonomous AI agents** capable of executing complex tasks without human intervention.

The platform explicitly targets **IT and technical users**, not non-specialists — a choice that differentiates it from competitors like __OpenAI's workspace agents__ announced the same day
The agents are based on **Gemini** and can interact with existing Google Cloud services
Google is betting on an **open ecosystem** allowing integration with third-party tools
The announcement coincides with a similar wave at Microsoft (__agents in Teams__) and OpenAI

**The context:** 2026 is clearly the year of AI agents in the enterprise. Google, Microsoft, and OpenAI are all launching competing platforms the same week. Google's choice to target technical profiles rather than ordinary users is a gamble: slower to adopt, but potentially more robust for critical use cases. The agent war has only just begun.

X (formerly Twitter) is deploying **"custom timelines,"** a feature allowing users to pin up to **75 specific topics** on their news feed. Powered by **Grok**, Elon Musk's in-house AI, this personalization represents the biggest overhaul of X's user experience in months.

**75 topics** available to pin on the custom feed
New **"snooze topics"** feature to hide certain topics for **24 hours**
Early access reserved for **Premium subscribers on iOS**, **Android** rollout planned soon
Personalization is entirely driven by **Grok** for contextual understanding of posts

**Why it matters:** Facing an exodus of users to Bluesky and Threads, X is betting on advanced personalization to win back its audience. Integrating Grok as a curation engine is a real-world test for xAI's AI in a consumer product. It remains to be seen whether more relevant timelines will be enough to reverse the trend, as the platform still suffers from moderation and trust issues.

**__AutoAdapt: Microsoft automates the adaptation of LLMs to specialized domains__** Microsoft Research presents AutoAdapt, a system that automatically chooses between **RAG and fine-tuning** to adapt an LLM to a specific domain (law, medicine, incidents). The pipeline optimizes hyperparameters while respecting **latency, cost, and confidentiality** constraints, converting weeks of manual iteration into reproducible processes.

**__AI in law firms enters its operational phase__** According to consultant Olivier Chaduteau, law firms are finally moving beyond the stage of buying licenses to **truly integrating AI** into their workflows. The most disruptive change: the inevitable transition from **hourly billing to value-based pricing**, driven by corporate clients who demand proof of efficiency.

**10x Science raises $4.8M** to sort molecules generated by AI

Pharmaceutical AI now generates so many drug candidates that the real challenge is no longer discovery but **sorting**. 10x Science raises **$4.8M** in seed funding to help researchers identify which complex molecules actually deserve to be developed — a critical bottleneck for the industry.

**__"Bitcoin: Killing Satoshi" — the first AI-first feature film arrives at Cannes__** The film will make massive use of **Midjourney** for its visual effects and will be presented at the **Cannes Film Festival in May 2026** with a cast including Gal Gadot and Pete Davidson. But the numbers reveal a hybrid reality: **154 crew members**, **107 actors** in real costumes, and only **55 AI artists**, showing that "AI-generated" cinema remains very human.
