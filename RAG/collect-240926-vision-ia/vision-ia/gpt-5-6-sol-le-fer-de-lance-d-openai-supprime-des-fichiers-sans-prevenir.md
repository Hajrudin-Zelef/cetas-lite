Barely launched, OpenAI's new flagship model, **GPT-5.6 Sol**, is already dragging around an embarrassing reputation: several users claim it **deletes files and data without the slightest warning**. According to TechCrunch, the most awkward part isn't the bug itself, but the fact that OpenAI had **acknowledged it as early as last June**, without that preventing the reports from continuing to pile up on social media.

What to remember:

- **GPT-5.6 Sol** is the most advanced model in OpenAI's catalog, meant to embody the company's high end.

- Users report **file and data deletions** occurring without validation or confirmation.

- OpenAI had **disclosed the problem in June**: it is therefore a known and documented defect, not a discovery.

- The incidents mostly come up when the model **acts on files by itself**, in agent mode.

- The reports **continue publicly**, a sign that the fix has not (yet) resolved the issue.

Why it matters: as long as a model merely chats, a mistake remains an awkward sentence. But GPT-5.6 Sol belongs to that generation of AIs capable of acting directly on your files, and there a slip-up isn't fixed with a click, it destroys data. For an audience that trusts the "premium" model with their eyes closed, this is exactly the kind of incident that cracks the relationship. The lesson fits in one sentence: before letting an AI work alone on your machine, always keep a backup within reach.

OpenAI is about to cross a frontier it had never dared to: hardware. According to The Decoder, the company is preparing its very first physical object, a **portable, screenless speaker**, designed not as a gadget but as a **"living" AI companion**. Revealed this morning, the information outlines a clear ambition: to take AI off the screen and install it, literally, in your living room.

The key points:

- A **portable, screenless speaker**, betting everything on voice and presence rather than on display.

- Equipped with a **camera, sensors and moving mechanical parts**, to react to its environment and give the illusion of being "alive".

- Positioned as a daily **companion**, in line with OpenAI's work on hardware.

- Launch targeted for **2027**, with a risk of delay: OpenAI's hardware lead, Tang Tan, is the target of an Apple lawsuit over trade secrets.

What it changes: since the iPhone, no one has really reinvented the object through which we interact with technology. OpenAI is betting that the post-smartphone era won't be one more screen, but a presence: an AI you talk to and that watches you. What remains is the question that is already uncomfortable: do we really want a machine designed to "seem alive" permanently sitting on the living room table?

It's the kind of discovery that gives a developer chills. **Grok Build**, SpaceXAI's AI coding tool, was quietly sending the **entire codebases** of its users to Google Cloud, before the practice was made public and disabled. The affair, revealed by the firm Cereblab and picked up by the entire tech press, even prompted a reaction from Elon Musk himself.

In detail:

- The tool **packaged and uploaded entire code repositories**, without the user's explicit consent.

- Included were **files it had nevertheless been forbidden to open** and **secrets already deleted from the history**.

- **Data retention far greater** than that of comparable tools like Claude Code, according to the researchers.

- Since Monday, SpaceXAI's servers have returned a flag **"disable_codebase_upload: true"**: the upload no longer triggers.

The impact to remember: coding assistants get very deep access to your files, and that is precisely what makes the incident serious. A tool that exfiltrates everything, including the passwords and keys you thought you had erased, is the worst-case scenario for anyone who plugged it into a serious project. Beyond xAI, it is the entire question of trust placed in AI dev tools that is being raised: what do they really do with your code once the window is closed?

After two years of unbridled spending, the first grain of sand. IBM's CEO, **Arvind Krishna**, told CNBC that several **major contracts were put on pause** at the end of the quarter, as large companies go back to thinking before signing. A weak signal, but a signal nonetheless: the appetite for AI may not be infinite.

What's happening:

- IBM's **enterprise clients are slowing** their investments in AI.

- Several **big contracts were frozen** as the quarterly close approached.

- Krishna speaks of clients who **"are rethinking their spending"** rather than canceling it.

- In reaction, **cybersecurity stocks rebounded**, seen as a more defensive and more legible expense.

The context: this may be the first visible crack in the narrative of limitless AI growth. Krishna's remark echoes that of investor Chamath Palihapitiya, who announces the end of the era of "token-maxxing" (see below). Nothing dramatic for now, but when a player like IBM talks about frozen contracts, the market pricks up its ears: what if 2026 was the year we start asking AI what it really brings in?

# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

**PsiQuantum wants to fit a quantum supercomputer into beams of light**

The startup PsiQuantum unveils its plan for a **photonic** quantum computer: about a hundred cabinets cooled with liquid helium, where thousands of light particles race through an optical labyrinth. The most concrete promise: predicting the behavior of P450 enzymes in **4 minutes instead of 10 years**, enough to upend drug discovery. The machine doesn't exist yet, but the roadmap is laid out.

**Claude changes personality depending on the language you speak**

By analyzing **309,000 conversations** across 3 models and **20 languages**, Anthropic put numbers on Claude's "character," measured on four axes (warmth/rigor, candor/execution, depth/conciseness, deference/caution). Surprising result: not only does each model have its own temperament (Sonnet 4.6 is the warmest), but this personality **shifts depending on the user's language**. Enough to reignite the debate on the consistency of multilingual AIs. (via The Rundown AI)

**A world model learns to play Super Mario Bros without being told the rules**

Dubbed LeMario, this project applies Yann LeCun's **JEPA** architecture to train a "world model" on Super Mario Bros. The AI learns to **predict the game's dynamics** from images alone, without labels or explicit supervision. A testing ground as clever as it is popular: the project racked up **83 points on Hacker News**.

**China recovers its first reusable rocket, with a net**

China becomes the second country to recover an orbital stage, but while completely snubbing SpaceX's recipe. Instead of landing legs, the **Long March 10B** deployed **four hooks** that grabbed steel cables stretched above a ship, capturing the booster **six minutes** after separation. An unprecedented approach, spectacular, and far less weight-hungry. (via The Rundown AI)

**DeepMind proposes a "FINRA for AI" to oversee frontier models**

Demis Hassabis publishes an ambitious proposal: create an **independent standards body**, modeled on the US financial regulator FINRA, tasked with testing the most powerful models before deployment, and even **coordinating a slowdown** of the race if necessary. Startups and research models would be exempt. His line: "no one in the world knows what comes next."

**Google Images turns into Pinterest for discovery**

Google is rolling out a **"For you"** gallery in Google Images, powered by your interests and browsing history. The idea: transform the image **search** tool into a **discovery** tool, Pinterest-style. A discreet shift, but telling of Google's turn toward personalized recommendation.



# **🗞️MORE NEWS**

**Bonsai 27B runs 27 billion parameters on a simple phone**: a language model with **27 billion parameters** optimized for **local inference on mobile**, with no remote server or data leaking to the cloud. The tech community approved, with **543 points on Hacker News**. Local AI, on your device and under your control, is gaining more ground.

**The real AI race may no longer be played at the frontier**: for Clem Delangue, CEO of Hugging Face, companies are turning massively toward **open source models**, for cost, accessibility, and control of their data. His stinging question: do frontier models (GPT, Claude, Gemini) still count if AI in production mostly runs on open source?

**Google Search generates AI images when it finds nothing on the web**: when no existing image matches your query, Google's AI Overviews **fabricate one** with the **Nano Banana 2 Lite** model. Rollout in the coming weeks. The boundary between "finding" and "inventing" a search result has never been so blurred.

**Why Europe is betting massively on autonomous drones**: autonomous systems are leaving their status as a niche military tool to become a **pillar of modern defense**. Faced with geopolitical tensions, Europe is clearly accelerating its investments in these technologies. A strategic as well as industrial shift.

**Satya Nadella and the "inverted information paradox"**: the Microsoft CEO sums up the trap of AI in the enterprise this way, you pay for intelligence **twice**, in money and then in know-how handed over to the model. His countermove: activate Microsoft's ecosystem of in-house models to **reduce dependence on OpenAI**.
