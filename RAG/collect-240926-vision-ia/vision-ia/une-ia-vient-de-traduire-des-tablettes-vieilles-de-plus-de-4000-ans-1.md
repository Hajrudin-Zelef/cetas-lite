A researcher at the USC Viterbi School of Engineering, Zhaohui Wang, has published TabletCraft, a translation model trained on **116,000 examples** of Akkadian into English. It achieves a BLEU score of **49.1**, the standard indicator of quality in machine translation, versus 37.5 for the best previous system. Above all, it does something no one had published before: it also translates in the other direction, from English into Akkadian, and renders the result in cuneiform signs.

Architecture based on **ByT5**, a specialized translation model rather than a general-purpose large language model. The number of parameters was not published.
Validated on the Akkademia test set (**2,812 examples**): **49.1** BLEU from Akkadian to English, **48.5** in the reverse direction, a first.
An integrated sign converter covers **14,240 correspondences**, or **95.3%** of the repertoire, and displays the translation in Unicode signs then as an image of a virtual clay tablet.
Published in open source under a Creative Commons BY 4.0 license, with code, pre-trained model, command-line interface and web demo on GitHub. Installation takes a single command: `pip install cuneiscribe`.
The work was presented in July 2026 and accepted at the C3NLP workshop of the ACL 2026 conference, with a detailed paper.

Around **500,000 cuneiform tablets** sleep in the storerooms of the world's museums, and only a tiny fraction has been translated. The bottleneck has never been technical: it stems from the fact that only a few hundred people still know how to read this writing system fluently. A model installable with a single command line on a personal machine changes the nature of the problem. Any curious person, teacher or student can now query a text five millennia old without going through an Assyriologist. The reverse direction, writing in cuneiform, is more a matter of pleasure than of science, but it says a lot: the tool is aimed at the general public as much as at researchers.

Z.ai, the Chinese startup formerly known as Zhipu AI, launched GLM-5.3 on August 14. The model scores **60 points** on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, tied with Kimi K3 at the top of open models and **7 points** ahead of its predecessor. But the release of the weights, that is, the part that makes a model truly open, has been postponed: GLM-5.3 proved too effective at detecting security flaws in code.

Same basic architecture as GLM-5.2, parameters not published. The gains come entirely from improved post-training. The model is text- and code-oriented at launch, with no new modality.
Elo score of **1770** on the GDPval-AA v2 agentic benchmark, versus 1524 for GLM-5.2. That is second place worldwide, behind only Claude Opus 5 (1855), which is proprietary.
**$0.68 per task**, versus $0.84 for Kimi K3, its direct open rival, or **19% cheaper**. GLM-5.2 was at $0.44.
Available now via the Z.ai API, on an OpenAI-compatible endpoint, and via the GLM Coding Plan at **$18 per month** for Lite, $80 for Pro, $168 for Max, with an annual discount that brings Lite down to $12.60. The `glm-5.3[1m]` variant opens a context window of **1 million tokens**, usable in Claude Code.
Open weights are expected around August 28, currently reserved for security partners. No official Hugging Face repository, so no local installation possible at this stage.

In a letter sent Wednesday to its investors, Stripe sets **January 1, 2026** as the "beginning of the singularity" and makes it an argument for not going public. The company acknowledges in the same document that the term is "vague and perhaps already overused." The entire tech press picked up the story during the day.

The exact wording of the letter: "Nobody can know with specificity how AI will reshape our world." In other words, uncertainty about AI would justify staying sheltered from public markets. The IPO is postponed with no deadline.
Stripe didn't really need it: revenue up **41%** in the first half, free cash flow **+43%**, valuation growing **31% per year** since the Series D.
**88%** of the companies in the Forbes AI 50, including OpenAI and Anthropic, use Stripe. The share of its revenue coming from AI companies has more than doubled in a year.
The company confirmed the acquisition of OpenRouter, its largest acquisition. The price is not official: Axios estimates it at $8 billion, mostly in stock, the New York Times at $7.5 billion, against a valuation of $1.3 billion three months earlier.
OpenRouter is a gateway that routes developers' requests to dozens of competing models. Its usage is growing **9% per week**.

TechCrunch offers a far less metaphysical reading: Stripe already collects payments from almost all AI labs, and has just bought the tollbooth through which developers' requests transit to those same labs. Singularity or not, this gives it a position on every token billed in the ecosystem. The prophetic vocabulary mainly serves to dress up a very classic vertical integration.
