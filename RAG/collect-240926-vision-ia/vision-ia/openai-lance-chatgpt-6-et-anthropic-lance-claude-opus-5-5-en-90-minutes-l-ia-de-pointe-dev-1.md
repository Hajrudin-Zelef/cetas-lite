OpenAI launched yesterday **GPT-6 Sol** and **GPT-6 Luna**, **only 90 minutes** after the release of Claude Opus 5.5. These two models are faster and more affordable versions of its flagship model **GPT-6 Astra**. API prices drop by **about 50%**, and Sol reportedly makes **half as many errors** as its predecessor. At least six media outlets covered the announcement, from TechCrunch to CNBC.

**What to remember:**

- **Two models, two uses**: Sol handles complex tasks, especially programming. Luna handles repetitive, high-volume office tasks: summarizing documents, extracting information, answering quick questions.

- **Prices**: Sol costs **$2 / $10** per million tokens for input / output, versus $4 / $20 for GPT-5.6 Sol. Luna drops to **$0.10 / $0.50**, versus $0.20 / $1.20 previously. OpenAI attributes this drop to progress in caching and inference (gHacks).

- **The same base as Astra**: both models were trained with the same methods, which gives them improvements in factual accuracy, code, autonomous computer use, and alignment.

- **Where to try them**: Sol is available in ChatGPT Work, in Codex (paid accounts), and via the API. Luna is also coming to the desktop app and for **Free and Go accounts**. The rollout has been gradual since September 22 (TechCrunch).

**What this changes**

Even with a free account, you benefit from some of Astra's improvements via Luna. For companies automating tasks, the cost is simply cut in half. Releasing these models on the same day as Anthropic is no coincidence: the price war between the two giants directly benefits users.

Anthropic launches **Claude Opus 5.5**, less than **two months** after Opus 5 (July 24). According to the company, it's the most performant model it has ever tested. It beats **Fable 5.1** on several benchmarks, even though the latter is bigger and **150% more expensive** via the API. It even succeeds at tasks where Fable failed, while its output price drops from **$25 to $20** per million tokens.

**The key points:**

- **The spec sheet**: it's a multimodal model (text and image) with a context window of **one million tokens**. Anthropic presents it as the best on the market in programming and intellectual work: analysis, writing, synthesis.

- **Prices**: **$4** per million tokens for input (-20%), $20 for output, and a prompt cache at **$0.20** (-60%). Since the model needs fewer tokens to finish a job, a task costs in practice **about 40% less**.

- **Performance**: on Terminal-Bench 4.0, which measures the ability to accomplish computer tasks autonomously, the score climbs to **66.4%**, versus 52.3% for the previous generation (VentureBeat).

- **More readable answers**: the model uses less jargon and gives the important information at the start of its response.

- **And next**: **Sonnet 5.5** and **Haiku 5.5** arrive "in the coming weeks," with comparable improvements.

**The context**

This is the first model since Dario Amodei took a stance in favor of deliberately slowing the progression of AI capabilities: "Fully addressing the risks still requires even more caution," he wrote this month. With its capabilities in biology and cybersecurity judged comparable to those of Mythos, Opus 5.5 is subject to the same safeguards as Fable and was evaluated before its release by METR and Frontier Design. Anthropic is therefore slowing the race for capabilities, but not the race on prices.

Tuesday morning, before the **UN General Assembly**, Donald Trump announced that the United States would henceforth call artificial intelligence "**super intelligence**" (or "SI"). His reason: the word "artificial" would give the impression of **fake** intelligence. According to Axios, the new term is to apply to official documents.

**In detail:**

- **The context**: the announcement came in the middle of a speech that also targeted Iran, "globalists," and the fight against climate change. The Verge notes that this option was not even included in the poll published by Trump a few days earlier on the subject.

- **No legal effect**: at the time of publication, no decree or federal directive had been confirmed. The statement remains verbal, with no budget or timeline (The Hill).

- **The substance of the speech**: Trump "totally" rejects international initiatives for global AI control and claims a "massive" American lead over China.

- **A vocabulary problem**: in AI, "superintelligence" already refers to **hypothetical** systems that would surpass humans in almost every domain. Applying it to current chatbots mixes today's technology with speculation about the future.

- **A habit**: this renaming joins the Gulf of Mexico, Lake Superior, the Kennedy Center, and Mount Denali.

**Why it matters**

For you, nothing changes: ChatGPT remains ChatGPT and no standard is modified. But the word carries weight in the debate: calling current chatbots "super intelligence" blurs the benchmarks at the very moment when Sam Altman and Dario Amodei are asking the Security Council to slow down.

AstroForge, a startup that wants to exploit asteroid resources, has developed **Solo**, an in-house AI model based on the transformer architecture, tasked with piloting its spacecraft alone. The first fully autonomous flight, **Autonomy-1**, is planned for **2027** on Stoke Space's very first rocket, in a mission supported by **NASA**. Its CEO does not even plan to include a radio capable of receiving orders from Earth.

**A few key figures:**

- **2,500 sensors**: this is the training base for Solo's global intelligence layer. It complements classic control algorithms and models specialized by subsystem (power, navigation).

- **200 million dollars**: the estimated cost of a network of five ground antennas. "Do I build my own network, or do I replace it with a model?" summarizes CEO Matthew Gialich.

- **100 operators** per eight-hour shift: this is what NASA mobilized for the OSIRIS-REx mission. A startup does not have those resources.

- **56 million dollars** raised, and two prototypes that experienced anomalies. In 2025, AstroForge lost contact with the Odin probe, for lack of available antennas.
