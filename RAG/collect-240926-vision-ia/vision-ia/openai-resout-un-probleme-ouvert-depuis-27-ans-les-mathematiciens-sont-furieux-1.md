On August 1, 2026, OpenAI announced that its Astra model had solved **ten open mathematical problems**, including the first construction of a **non-sofic group**, a question posed by Gromov **27 years ago**. Except that the proof relies directly on a 2019 paper by Andreas Thom and Gábor Kun, and that Thom, a group theorist at TU Dresden, had spent months working on precisely this subject while talking with ChatGPT. He is now asking OpenAI to prove that his private conversations did not feed the model's training.

**Key takeaways:**

- Thom detailed his accusation in a series of posts on Mastodon, speaking of "dishonest" behavior and total opacity about the origin of the data

- He had **disabled training on his data on June 29, 2026**, already doubting traceability: this setting exists for everyone in ChatGPT's data controls, and it is active by default in the opposite direction

- OpenAI researcher Mark Sellke responded "that did not happen," a phrasing that only covers direct access to the paper during solving, not the use of conversations in the training corpus

- On another matter, around the Navier-Stokes equations, OpenAI acknowledged **being unable to rule out** that "de-identified" data improved its models

- Thom is the **second mathematician in a few days** to make this accusation, and the company has produced no technical proof of traceability

So the loop is closed: a researcher works on a problem with a chatbot for months, the chatbot solves the problem, and the company takes credit without being able to prove otherwise. For any professional who puts a confidential file into an assistant, the lesson is concrete: the opt-out exists, it's in the settings, and today it is the only verifiable protection, for lack of an audit log on the lab's side.

On September 10, OpenAI launched **ChatGPT for Financial Services**, a variant of ChatGPT Work that combines built-in financial datasets and the **GPT-6 Astra** reasoning model. Concretely, the tool attacks the stack of work entrusted to analysts: company valuations, LBO models, screening of potential buyers, quarterly earnings analysis, pitchbooks and client notes. No more downloading Excel files: the data arrives natively in the tool.

**In detail:**

- Built-in data sources: **Daloopa, PitchBook, LSEG News and Crunchbase**, i.e. earnings transcripts, financial statements and private company data

- Every figure produced carries a **granular citation** that traces back to its source, the usual friction point for these tools in a regulated environment

- Designed with **Morgan Stanley and Evercore** as design partners, with authentication and access rights integrations at FactSet, S&P Global, Preqin and Datasite

- Availability: **on request** from eligible financial institutions, through OpenAI's sales team. The price is not officially published (one source mentions about $10 per million tokens, unconfirmed)

- Notable omission in the announcement: **no mention of European hosting or DORA compliance**, even though that is the first purchasing filter for an EU bank

The target market isn't the star trader, it's the floor below: the junior who spends nights reprocessing accounts and formatting deliverables. And for French-speaking professionals in finance, audit or legal, the real question of the day isn't the quality of the model but the announcement's silence on data residency, which is enough to block a deployment on the European side.

OpenAI opens to developers **GPT-Live-1**, the voice model already running in ChatGPT, at **$0.05 per minute**. Its distinguishing feature comes down to one word: **full-duplex**. The model listens and speaks simultaneously, can be interrupted, can itself interrupt, pause, and call tools while answering you. It handles only the voice layer and plugs into the reasoning model of your choice for business logic, which allows balancing depth, speed, and cost.

**A few key figures:**

- Full-duplex interactivity measured at **80.1%** versus 45.4% for GPT-Realtime-2.1, the previous generation

- Turn-taking latency reduced to **0.8 seconds** versus 1.4 seconds, the threshold at which an exchange stops being painful

- Tool-calling accuracy raised to **87%**, versus 60%, which is what allows the agent to consult a calendar or a client file live

- On a voice banking support benchmark, the success rate rises to **32%**, versus 12.4% before: big progress, but still two failures out of three

- **Twelve new voices** with varied accents, dialects, and languages, ASR transcriptions and response text provided by default

Yelp already uses it for its phone reservations and its CTO Alex Levy reports better call handling. At three dollars per hour of conversation, synthetic voice becomes cheaper than a human switchboard, and the next answering system at your bank, your health insurer, or your medical office will know how to follow up with you before you have finished your sentence.

Anthropic published on September 10 its report "Detecting and Countering Misuse of AI," which documents **five cases** where Claude accounts were used for work that could facilitate the development of biological weapons. The most notable: a user asked Claude to draft a **grant application** for gain-of-function research on the **chikungunya virus**, in order to mutate it in living animals to make it more infectious and more capable of evading the immune system. The virus has no authorized treatment and causes debilitating symptoms for weeks.

**The essential points:**

- The detection came from Anthropic's internal **biological security classifier**, not from an external report. The project was affiliated with a **military research institute**, which weighed in the decision

- Second case: a researcher outside the United States exchanged **thousands of messages over several weeks** about highly pathogenic avian influenza, on genetic approaches related to mammalian adaptation and airborne transmission
