Microsoft announces **Majorana 2**, a quantum chip that could mark a turning point. Its qubits are **1,000 times more reliable** than the previous generation and retain their quantum state for **20 seconds**, whereas the industry caps out at a few microseconds. But the real story is elsewhere: this breakthrough was orchestrated by **Microsoft Discovery**, the group's agentic AI platform dedicated to R&D, which today reaches general availability.

**Key takeaways:**

- Topological qubits **1,000x more reliable**, with a lifespan of **20 seconds** (versus microseconds elsewhere)

- **Microsoft Discovery** reaches general availability: an agentic AI platform dedicated to scientific research

- Agents automate qubit measurements (from several weeks to an automated process) and drive manufacturing workflows

- Ability to synthesize decades of siloed data and spot correlations invisible to human researchers

- Roadmap: a commercially scalable quantum computer targeted for **2029**

**What this changes:** We're entering an era where AI agents no longer merely assist researchers—they conduct entire portions of the scientific process, from measurement to manufacturing. Microsoft claims its agents compressed weeks of work into automated routines, synthesized decades of scattered data, and uncovered correlations that humans would have missed. If the approach spreads beyond quantum, into materials, chemistry, or pharma, the entire tempo of scientific discovery could accelerate dramatically.

**Emmanuel Macron** invites **Sam Altman**, CEO of **OpenAI**, to take part in the **G7** summit. Behind the protocol gesture lies a clear ambition: to tie the American AI giants to French interests and strengthen national capabilities in a global race where Europe struggles to exist. The president is multiplying signals toward Silicon Valley to position France as Europe's gateway to AI.

**Key points:**

- **Sam Altman** invited to the G7 as a leading figure in the AI sector

- Macron seeks to bring tech giants closer to French diplomatic stakes

- A deliberate government strategy to boost national AI capabilities

- France wants to carry weight against the American and Chinese blocs

- An agenda that extends announcements of investments and data centers on French soil

**The context:** For Paris, wooing OpenAI amounts to trying to exist on the global AI map, where **Mistral** remains the sole French champion against the giants. The diplomatic operation aims as much to attract capital and infrastructure as to avoid a technological falling behind. One fundamental question remains: how far is European sovereignty compatible with growing dependence on American models and chips? Macron's bet is that cooperation is better than isolation.

**Meta** is rolling out its AI agents via **Meta One**, a paid subscription offering tailored for businesses. It's **Mark Zuckerberg**'s latest move to diversify revenue still almost entirely dependent on targeted advertising. With this B2B offering, Meta is stepping out of its consumer comfort zone to hunt on the turf of Microsoft, Google, and OpenAI.

**In detail:**

- New AI agents integrated into the **Meta One** offering

- A paid subscription that directly targets the B2B market

- A diversification strategy beyond advertising

- A head-on positioning in enterprise AI against the sector's leaders

**The impact to remember:** Meta built an empire by making its services free for users, funded by ads. Charging businesses directly for AI agents marks both a cultural and economic shift. The message is clear: direct monetization of AI is becoming the new front for the giants, and even the king of advertising no longer wants to depend on a single revenue stream. The challenge remains convincing businesses to entrust their workflows to agents bearing the Meta name.


**Nous Research** releases **Hermes Desktop**, an **open source** AI agent application under the **MIT license**, designed to run on any device and any platform. The stated ambition: democratize access to quality autonomous agents, without going through the closed, paid ecosystems of the big labs. The project follows in the line of the open models that built Nous Research's reputation.

**A few key points:**

- **Hermes Desktop**: a multi-platform, open source AI agent

- Open source under **MIT license**, freely modifiable and redistributable

- Designed to run on all devices and platforms

- An initiative to bring autonomous agents out of proprietary silos

**Why it matters:** The timing is striking. At the very moment Meta is locking its agents behind a subscription, Nous Research is betting on total openness. For developers, researchers, and tinkerers, it's the promise of a local, inspectable, controllable, and free agent—a true counter-model to commercial offerings. Open source is establishing itself as the main counterforce against the concentration of AI in a few hands. It remains to be seen whether Hermes Desktop will hold up against proprietary agents on the performance front.

**Hyper (YC S26): an "enterprise brain" for AI agents**

Hyper ingests a company's scattered data (Slack, Docs, Email, Calendar) to build a structured knowledge graph accessible to agents. The idea: give agents persistent context between sessions, whereas MCP starts from scratch every time. The system keeps facts up to date, detects outdated information, and captures even style preferences and the context of past decisions.

**OpenAI beefs up GPT-Rosalind for life sciences**

OpenAI is equipping GPT-Rosalind, its biology-specialized model, with new capabilities: enhanced biological reasoning, medicinal chemistry expertise, advanced genomic analysis, and optimized experimental workflows. Enough to accelerate life sciences research and, eventually, drug discovery.

**Ideogram 4.0 arrives in open-weight with native 2K resolution**

Ideogram moves to an open-weight model with its 4.0 version: native 2K resolution, bounding box control, and significantly improved text rendering. It climbs to first place among all open models on the DesignArena leaderboard, surpassed only by OpenAI's and Google's closed systems.

**Amazon: "ground truth is a process, not a dataset"**

Amazon proposes a method for evaluating long-context AI summaries, which static benchmarks can no longer judge. Its "audit-then-score" approach has benchmarks audited by the models themselves, because PhD experts achieve no more than **60.8%** accuracy on these complex factual tasks. A challenge to the traditional ground truth model.
