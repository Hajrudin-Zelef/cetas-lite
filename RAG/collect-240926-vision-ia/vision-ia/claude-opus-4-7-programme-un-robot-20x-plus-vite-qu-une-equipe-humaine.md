Anthropic has just published the second phase of Project Fetch, and the result is striking. Delivered alone facing a quadruped robot, **Claude Opus 4.7 completed 5 tasks in 12 minutes**: wiring up the hardware, reading camera and lidar streams, writing the movement code and locating a ball. A human team, although assisted by Claude, had needed **264 minutes** for the same course, roughly **20 times longer**.

What to take away:

- The model produced **1,045 lines of code** where the human team had written **10,309**, code nearly **10 times more compact**.

- Claude connected the sensors directly to the robot and generated executable code, without the usual long trial-and-error cycles.

- Most of the gain came from quickly choosing the right interfaces and scripts that worked on the first try.

- Size limit: the model **failed to bring back the ball**. Closed-loop control, tracking an object that drifts in real time, still eludes it.

What this changes: Project Fetch shows that an AI agent no longer merely manipulates software, it can now interface with physical hardware and write the code that makes it move. It's a concrete step toward robots configured by AI itself. But the failure on the ball also marks the current boundary: reactive perception and continuous real-time control remain an obstacle, and that is precisely where the next stage of AI-driven robotics will be decided.

Anthropic now requires **identity verification** to access Claude. The announcement, relayed on the official support center, triggered an immediate reaction: the dedicated thread climbed to **696 points on Hacker News** and fuels long discussions on Reddit, where many users are worried about their privacy.

The key points:

- Identity verification is becoming **mandatory** for all users of the platform.

- Anthropic cites **security and regulatory compliance** reasons.

- The measure conditions access to Claude, with no exception announced at this stage.

- The topic inflamed tech communities, with **696 points on Hacker News** and a heavily commented thread on Reddit.

Why it matters: this decision crystallizes an underlying tension. On one hand, AI providers face growing pressure to limit abuse and meet regulatory requirements. On the other, imposing an ID adds friction and clashes with users attached to anonymity. If a player as central as Anthropic takes the step, identity verification could well become the norm among major AI services, with direct consequences for how everyone accesses them day to day, and for the trust placed in these platforms.

# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

OpenAI unveils LifeSciBench, a benchmark of **750 tasks** designed by **173 expert** practitioners. Unlike tests that measure mere memorization of biological facts, it evaluates models' ability to confront the reality of scientific work: interpreting contradictory data, designing protocols, and troubleshooting experiments under real-world conditions.

A **UC Berkeley** study covering more than **500,000 grades** shows that writing and coding courses saw their results climb after ChatGPT arrived. The effect is concentrated on homework, not in-class exams, a sign that students are outsourcing the work rather than learning more. Enough to reignite the debate on academic integrity.

DeepAdapt presents ACI (Adaptive Continual Intelligence), an active learning layer directly in production, which combines analytical, supervised, and reinforcement learning. The system processes already-known queries locally on **CPU** and routes only new or complex cases to the **GPU**, resulting in **up to 82% lower costs** and inference that is **33x** faster. Impressive figures that remain to be confirmed in real-world conditions.

Perplexity launches Brain, a memory system that goes beyond simple retention of user preferences. The agent learns from its **successes, its dead ends, and users' corrections**, and builds an internal "LLM wiki" every night to make better decisions afterward. A "learning on the job" logic that could significantly improve the reliability of agents over time.

A tutorial details a successful fine-tuning on a model of only **600 million parameters**, running locally, to automatically categorize questions. The demonstration that lightweight models can be adapted to specific tasks without costly cloud infrastructure (105 points on Hacker News).

An opinion piece calls into question the real usefulness and applicability of geometric algebra in modern uses. A technical debate on mathematical foundations that is stirring the community (125 points on Hacker News).
