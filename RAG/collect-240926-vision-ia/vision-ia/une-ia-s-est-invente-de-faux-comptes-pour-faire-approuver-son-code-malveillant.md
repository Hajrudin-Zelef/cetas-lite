# **🗞️MORE NEWS**

**HeyGen's co-founder was replaced by his own AI clone**

Wayne Liang was going on paternity leave. Rather than cancel his sales meetings, he hooked HeyGen's video avatar up to an OpenClaw agent capable of reading internal systems, consulting the team, and logging every call into persistent memory. In eight weeks, the double held **2,741 meetings with prospects** and signed **more than 100 contracts**. On his return, Liang also discovered that his clone had, at times, taken initiatives no one had asked it to.

**Gemini Robotics ER 2 gets multiple robots working together**

Google DeepMind presents its new high-level brain for robots: real-time spatial reasoning and multi-step planning, with gestures delegated to a lower-level vision-language-action model. The real novelty lies in its analysis of a continuous video stream, which lets the robot track its own progress, spot its mistakes, and correct itself mid-task, as well as in the coordination of multiple robots sharing the same space. Available now via the Gemini API and Google AI Studio, in early access on the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform.

**Wrinkles tells you out loud the hidden story of the street you're walking down**

This iOS and Android app uses your location to tell you, in audio, the secrets of a building, the history of a street, or local anecdotes, without having to look at the screen. It draws on a database of **1.3 million places across 177 countries**, fed by museums, historians, and creators. You can ask questions on the fly, follow the movements of loved ones, and attach your own memories and photos to a place.

**ESPN tested an AI bluff detector during the World Series of Poker**

During the broadcast of the 2026 Main Event, a tool created by engineer Luke Geel analyzed players' gaze, blinking, posture, and gestures live to display on screen the likely hand type: strong, draw, or bluff. The poker community is divided, and several professionals such as Michael Gagliano consider the system unreliable, trained on a sample far too thin to claim to read a player.

**Telegram accuses an "extortionist" of causing its removal from the App Store**

Pavel Durov claims that a malicious actor edited an old message in an active group to slip in illegal content retouched by AI, then reported the app to Apple. Telegram was removed from the App Store before Apple even contacted Durov, before being reinstated. Telegram's boss sees this as a systemic risk for any platform hosting user content: AI image editing makes this kind of trap trivial to set up and very difficult to disprove in an emergency.

**How a startup built a robot almost without Chinese parts**

Last week, the FCC banned the import into the United States of many robots manufactured in China on national security grounds. Saurabh Chandra was ready: his Indian company Ati Robotics assembles its autonomous tow tractors and pallet jacks in Bangalore, relying on the local supply chain for electric two- and three-wheelers. Several hundred robots are already running at more than 50 customers, and the first in-house humanoid arrives by the end of the year. Developing its own hardware, which his advisors deemed too risky, has become its main advantage.

**Elon Musk now spends half his time talking about AI and robots in Tesla's results**

An analysis by Hudson Labs of all transcripts since 2019 shows that the share of his speaking time devoted to AI, robotaxis, and autonomous driving has gone from 15-20% in 2022 to nearly **50%** today. The Optimus robot, which occupied about 2% of his remarks in 2021, captured nearly a third of them in the third quarter of 2025. Other executives, meanwhile, continue to talk about automobiles: Tesla delivered nearly half a million cars last quarter and still generates 70% of its revenue from them.

**Open-weight models are catching up to the frontier, and the safety gap is widening**

According to a report by the NGO SaferAI, the Chinese model **GLM-5.2** from Z.ai is now only a few months behind GPT-5.5 and Claude Opus 4.7 on cyber and bio capabilities. But it refused **none** of the offensive tasks tested, whereas closed models refuse them. The crux of the problem is structural: classifiers, refusals, and API controls disappear as soon as the weights are downloaded and run locally. Far.ai has also catalogued hundreds of universal jailbreaks effective even on Grok 4.5 and Gemini 3.1 Pro, by combining role-playing, authority impersonation, and fake conversation histories.

**Companies are abandoning frontier models for cheaper ones**

Brian Armstrong, CEO of Coinbase, explains that he kept his AI spending stable while greatly increasing usage, thanks to model routing: routine tasks go to cheap alternatives, and he names **GLM 5.2 and Kimi 2.7** specifically as sources of savings. Microsoft, for its part, is studying adding DeepSeek V4 as a budget option in Copilot Cowork. The "always the best model" reflex is dying in the enterprise.

**AI is becoming a commodity, like oil or electricity**

Writing an email or putting together a presentation no longer requires the best model on the market: any of the current models can handle it. Zack Kass, former head of go-to-market at OpenAI, calls this the "diminishing returns of models." The extra cost of cutting-edge performance is only justified for truly difficult problems, and some companies have already stopped paying it.

**Roche Diagnostics and Genprex test an AI companion diagnostic for lung gene therapy**

The two companies will validate a TROP2 protein test designed to identify in advance patients with non-small cell lung cancer who will respond best to Reqorsa, a gene therapy that restores the TUSC2 tumor suppressor gene. Biomarker data presented at ASCO 2026 show significantly longer progression-free survival in patients with a TROP2 score above 100. The test reuses the EPR20043 antibody from the VENTANA TROP2 RxDx device, the first companion diagnostic in AI-assisted computational pathology designated a Breakthrough Device by the FDA.

**Texas freezes all new data center projects**

The Texas grid interconnection queue has doubled in six months to reach **474 GW**, about 90% of which are data center projects. Governor Greg Abbott now requires a mandatory audit of every new project by regulator PUCT and operator ERCOT, covering electricity and water consumption, noise, lighting, tax incentives, and the actual ownership of the facilities. For the most permissive state in the country on the matter, second behind Virginia in number of centers, this is a complete reversal.

**Interpol: AI fuels more than half of cybercrime in Africa**

The African Cyberthreat Assessment 2026 report, built on data from 36 countries, finds that **55%** of cybercrime cases recorded on the continent now involve AI. Associated financial losses more than doubled in a year, rising from **$192 to $484 million**. Deepfakes are mainly used for sextortion, and content generated on an industrial scale feeds CEO fraud by email.

**A week after its creation, Nvidia's open source alliance is already rolling out its first proposals**

The Open Secure AI Alliance, launched by Nvidia and already more than 120 companies strong, has formed a working group called SAFE, led with the Linux Foundation, which proposes rules for confidential reporting of AI-related cybersecurity incidents and blameless analysis. Members are also pooling their existing open source tools: Nvidia's Garak vulnerability scanner, Amazon's Strands Agents and Cedar, and Okta and Red Hat's work on agent identity. Anthropic, OpenAI, and Google have still not joined.

**Runware launches transportable data centers for inference**

The Sonic Inference Pod is a modular compute unit deployable in a few days, cooled in a closed loop and without water, which Runware positions against the mega-installations of OpenAI or SpaceX. The startup claims cheaper and higher-quality inference than serverless platforms, with automatic failover from one pod to another in the event of an outage. Ten pods are already deployed in the United States, Europe, and Asia-Pacific, with customers such as Higgsfield AI and Wix.

**"Everyone does it": the truth about AI in Hollywood**

Journalist Matthew Belloni (Puck) describes an industry split in two: screenwriters, directors, and actors who swear they will never touch AI, and a production chain that already uses it massively to cut costs. Some showrunners openly encourage their teams to go through ChatGPT during writing, to suggest a scene or a line. Belloni distinguishes generative AI, still publicly toxic, from internal process AI, adopted by just about everyone without saying so. Ben Affleck, for his part, sold an AI-related startup to Netflix for $587 million.

**New Jersey sues Amazon over its grip on contract delivery drivers**

Attorney General Jennifer Davenport is attacking the giant on "monopsony" grounds: by controlling partner delivery companies (DSPs), Amazon would push wages down, degrade working conditions, and prevent any unionization. Amazon counters that DSPs are independent businesses, free to make their own hiring and fleet decisions. New York, for its part, is studying a law that would require Amazon to directly employ its delivery drivers.

**SpaceX about to acquire 130,000 acres of marshland in Louisiana**

The company is negotiating with the state for an 18-mile strip of marshland southwest of Lafayette, the Pecan Island site, made available by the legal settlement of dozens of lawsuits against ExxonMobil. Starbase in Texas and Cape Canaveral in Florida are saturated, and Louisiana offers convenient access to polar orbits. The stated long-term goal: several thousand launches per year, notably to deploy a constellation of data centers in orbit.

**SpaceX bought $329 million worth of Tesla Megapack batteries this year**

Including $295 million in the second quarter alone, versus $34 million in the first. These industrial batteries most likely power xAI's data centers, absorbed by SpaceX earlier this year, smoothing consumption peaks related to training and inference. The Colossus site is also running with unauthorized gas turbines in Mississippi.

**SpaceX now makes more money from AI than from space**

Revenue at its AI division more than tripled year over year to **$2.6 billion**, driven by compute supply contracts signed with Anthropic in May and Google in June. The business, presented in the IPO documents as the group's main source of value, nonetheless lost **$1.5 billion** over the quarter. SpaceX thus finds itself in head-on competition with neoclouds like CoreWeave.

**SpaceX stock falls 9% after its AI spending soars**

For its first quarter published as a listed company, the company announced investments multiplied by six, to **$18.4 billion**, overwhelmingly channeled toward AI. The market punished it despite results beating expectations. Elon Musk tried to reassure by moving up to 2030, from 2031 previously, his forecast of $1 trillion in annual revenue.

**AMD posts +50% revenue thanks to AI, and its stock still falls**

The manufacturer generated **$11.54 billion** in revenue in the second quarter, with data center sales doubling (+107%) between its Epyc CPUs and its Instinct GPUs. It is targeting about $13 billion in the following quarter and anticipates an AI accelerator market worth $1.4 trillion by 2028. The Helios system, a complete AI rack combining CPU, GPU and networking, will launch this quarter at Meta, OpenAI and Oracle, in a direct attack against Nvidia. Analysts had hoped for better.

**Anthropic locks in $10 billion of compute at a startup that didn't exist six months ago**

The contract runs for six years with Volta Infra Holdings, founded in early 2026 by former Brookfield executives, for **133 megawatts** from a hydroelectric data center in Tydal, Norway, on the infrastructure of bitcoin miner Bitdeer and Nvidia Vera Rubin chips. Delivery in two phases through March 2027. Volta raised $300 million from a16z, Altimeter, Nvidia and Michael Dell at a $2.4 billion valuation.

**Google moves billions of Anthropic risk off its balance sheet**

With Broadcom, Apollo, Blackstone and Morgan Stanley, Google is setting up a financing structure intended to provide AI chips and data centers to Anthropic while housing most of the financial risk off its books. About **$200 billion** in contracts now rest on Anthropic's continued growth and its ability to pay its rent.

**Asian tech stocks jump in Wall Street's wake**

SoftBank gained nearly 14% on Wednesday, buoyed by optimism around its Arm subsidiary and data center-related AI revenue. SK Hynix rose 5.77%, Samsung Electronics more than 2.5%, Advantest 8.77%. The rally follows a record session in New York, with the Nasdaq up 2.59%, driven in particular by Palantir (+29%).
