# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

OpenAI is rolling out a **Lockdown Mode** in ChatGPT that disables web access, Deep Research and Agent Mode to limit data exfiltration. The feature only blocks the last step of an attack chain, without addressing the underlying problem. But it adds a welcome layer of protection against a security threat that remains, to this day, unresolved.

**Perplexity has AI write its own search pipelines**

With "Search as Code," **Perplexity** lets its models write their search routines directly in Python rather than calling rigid APIs. The result: up to **85% lower token costs** and performance superior to OpenAI and Anthropic on key benchmarks. Filtering and deduplication are automated in a secure sandbox, an approach that could inspire the entire industry.

**Anthropic hires an OpenAI chip engineer ahead of IPOs**

**Clive Chan**, the second employee of OpenAI's custom chip program, is joining **Anthropic**. He brings his ASIC experience, notably on Tesla's Autopilot and the OpenAI-Broadcom partnership. Anthropic is reportedly considering designing its own AI chips to gain independence, in a context of an IPO race between the two rivals.

**Toward a token crisis? An AI price hike expected before the IPOs**

TechCrunch anticipates a rise in AI service prices, as the major players prepare their stock market listings and seek to improve profitability. This "tokenpocalypse" would hit directly users and businesses dependent on APIs. A reminder that today's cheap AI is not necessarily tomorrow's.

**A Unitree G1 humanoid robot strikes a child during a demo in China**

Sporting a clown wig, a **Unitree G1** robot kicked a child during a public demonstration, a scene that went viral. The child was not seriously injured, but the incident reignites the debate over the safety of humanoids deployed in crowded public spaces. The question of control and regulation of these powerful machines has never seemed so concrete.

**The community calls for an official Claude Desktop version for Linux**

A GitHub issue asking Anthropic for an official Linux version of **Claude Desktop** garnered **489 votes** on Hacker News. Currently reserved for macOS and Windows, the application leaves out a large base of Linux developers. The strong traction shows real demand, which the company would be well advised to heed.

**AI creators are becoming increasingly hard to spot**

Virtual avatars like Lil Miquela, Imma or Shudu Gram were once recognizable at first glance. They are now gaining in realism to the point of blending in among real creators on social media. This evolution raises fundamental questions about the authenticity and transparency of AI-generated content, at a time when the boundary between human and synthetic is fading.

**A Claude extension to automate your job search**

This Chrome extension relies on **Claude** to analyze your resume, identify the most relevant job listings and automate part of the applications. The idea will appeal to those overwhelmed by listings, even if entrusting your job search to an automated agent calls for caution. A good example, in any case, of the wave of practical AI tools sweeping over daily life.

**SDSU installs 1,300 AI cameras without telling its students**

San Diego State University has deployed **1,300 AI-powered cameras**, including **330 directly in dormitories**, without student consent or prior notification. The case raises serious questions about mass surveillance and privacy in educational settings. When AI makes its way into bedrooms, the ethical debate ceases to be theoretical.
