John Jumper, co-creator of AlphaFold and winner of the Nobel Prize in Chemistry, announced on X his departure from Google DeepMind after **nine years** to join Anthropic. According to The Rundown, this is the **second major departure from Google in a single week**, after that of Noam Shazeer, co-lead of Gemini, who left for OpenAI. One after another, two of the lab's most renowned minds see their names move to the competition.

**Key takeaways:**

- **Nine years** at Google DeepMind before switching to Anthropic

- Co-creator of **AlphaFold**, the AI for predicting protein structure that earned him the **Nobel Prize in Chemistry** alongside Demis Hassabis

- Departure announced just days after that of **Noam Shazeer** (Gemini) to OpenAI

- Anthropic, long perceived as a lab focused on safety and text, is now recruiting a signature name in scientific research

**What this changes:**

Recruiting a Nobel laureate specializing in life sciences suggests that Anthropic wants to push Claude beyond code and conversation, toward the life sciences, DeepMind's historic hunting ground. For Google, losing two heavyweights in seven days sends a worrying signal about its ability to retain its talent, while the AI battle is now played as much on brains as on chips. The lab war has clearly tipped toward poaching.

A developer, Taha K. (aka lordx64), published **Qwable-v1** on Hugging Face, a model distilled from Claude Fable-5. The peculiarity: it uses **none of Anthropic's weights**. It was trained on Fable-5's **tool-use traces**, in other words the way the model calls tools and reasons through code. As a result, it spread like wildfire through the open-weight community.

**Key points:**

- Distillation of Claude Fable-5's **tool-use behavior**, without ever touching the original weights

- Viral among open-weight developers: thousands of downloads, **more than 10,000** for the quantized versions alone

- A legal gray area: the weights are protected, but the **agentic traces** can be imitated

- Publicly available on Hugging Face, signed Taha K. (lordx64)

**Why it matters:**

Until now, protecting a model mostly meant protecting its weights. Qwable-v1 shows that a good part of a proprietary model's agentic "personality" can be reproduced simply by observing how it behaves. For Anthropic and the other labs betting on closed models, this is a new exposure surface: know-how leaks through usage, not only through source code. The question of ownership of behaviors, and no longer just of data, is only just beginning.

Mistral launches **OCR 4**, the new generation of its optical character recognition model. The announcement propelled the topic to **464 points** on Hacker News during the day, a sign that automated document reading remains a highly contested field. The French champion continues to expand a suite of specialized tools rather than betting everything on a single generalist model.

**In detail:**

- **OCR 4**, an improved version of Mistral's text recognition model

- Designed to extract text from images and scanned documents

- **464 points** on Hacker News on launch day, the technical community is buzzing

- A new building block in the Mistral ecosystem, which is multiplying specialized models

**The impact to remember:**

OCR is one of the most immediately useful AI building blocks: digitizing invoices, contracts, archives, or poorly scanned PDFs to make them usable and queryable. By refining this layer, Mistral is targeting concrete enterprise use cases, where extraction reliability matters more than the raw size of the model. For the European ecosystem, it is also a way to keep sovereign document-processing tools in the face of American giants, in a use case where document confidentiality is often critical.

**GPT-5 solves a 3-year-old immunological enigma****.**

OpenAI recounts how GPT-5 Pro helped researchers unravel a mystery about the behavior of **T cells** that had remained unanswered for three years. The lab sees it as a concrete demonstration of AI as a research accelerator, with potentially direct implications for **cancer** and **autoimmune diseases**.

**OpenAI wants to build global standards for advanced AI****.**

OpenAI announces its support for evaluation frameworks, safety best practices, and international cooperation via the **Appia Foundation**. The stated goal: to coordinate the development of the most advanced systems around shared norms rather than a scattered race.

**An ultrasound bracelet gives a robotic hand unprecedented dexterity****.**

Researchers from MIT and the University of Southern California have designed an ultrasound sensor bracelet that reads the movements of the wrist's muscles and tendons in real time. An AI algorithm translates these images into precise finger positions, allowing a robotic hand to play the piano or manipulate objects in 3D.

**Fitbit Air: Google's AI health coach****.**

Google integrates Google Health Coach into the Fitbit Air. The assistant analyzes sleep, heart rate, heart rate variability, and environment in real time to deliver personalized recovery and training recommendations. The Verge notes a coach that is sometimes a bit too alarmist about its tester's fitness level.

**PlasmoSniff diagnoses pneumonia by blowing into a tube****.**

Researchers at MIT have developed a portable sensor that detects pneumonia from breath. Thanks to custom-designed nanoparticles and spectroscopy, the test identifies infection biomarkers in about **10 minutes**, without a lab or heavy equipment.

**Omio reorganizes its entire dev workflow around OpenAI models****.**

The travel platform Omio (3,000+ suppliers, 47 countries) integrated ChatGPT and then Codex into its entire engineering cycle: research, architecture, code, testing, reviews.
