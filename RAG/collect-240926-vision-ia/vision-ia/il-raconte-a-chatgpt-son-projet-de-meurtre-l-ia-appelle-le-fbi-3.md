# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

Anthropic extends its promotion: the **weekly usage limit for Claude Code remains increased by 50%** until **August 31, 2026**, with no action required. The offer covers Pro, Max, Team plans and legacy Enterprise seats, but not free accounts or Enterprise seats billed on consumption. It applies automatically to the CLI, IDE extensions, desktop, and web, with no billing change. Watch out for the detail that matters: only weekly limits are affected, the 5-hour limits remain unchanged. Eleven days to take advantage of it.

**Cursor launches Origin, a GitHub competitor, on the day of a global GitHub outage**

Cursor, now a SpaceX subsidiary, has unveiled **Origin**, a code hosting platform that covers most of what developers do on GitHub: browsing and editing a repository, collaborating, managing pull requests. Synchronization is interoperable, so you can work on both sides in parallel without migrating. Cursor announces features designed natively for agents and an ecosystem of applications around it. The timing is cruel for Microsoft: GitHub recorded **257 outages in one year** according to LeadDev, including a global interruption of more than six hours on the very day Origin launched.

**Databricks publishes its method for controlling the coding AI bill**

With feedback from Stripe, Coinbase, Uber and Ramp, Databricks documents the wall that all companies deploying coding AI at scale run into: **costs that grow exponentially** to the point of threatening the productivity gains achieved. The main lever identified is not to reduce usage but to change the target: following the "efficiency frontier," meaning the best price-to-intelligence ratio, rather than the pure intelligence frontier. Databricks has open-sourced the two internal tools that apply this logic, Omnigent and Unity AI Gateway.

**OpenAI deliberately slows down its models because of their cyberattack capabilities**

OpenAI announces it is strengthening the monitoring, alignment and security of its cutting-edge models, and above all establishes a new principle: offensive cybersecurity capabilities become a factor that **frames the deployment pace** of future models. In other words, a model too competent at attacking systems may see its release delayed or restricted. The note is signed by the company itself, which makes it a public commitment, to be watched on upcoming releases.

**Unitree's Superman humanoid runs faster than Usain Bolt**

Unitree has presented Superman, a humanoid robot designed in **three months**, which reaches a top speed of **12.66 m/s** on legs only 0.85 meters long, making it faster than Usain Bolt's human record. It also displays a **vertical jump of 2 meters**, far above anything an athlete can produce. The company had already reclaimed the speed record at 10 m/s earlier this year, then entered a machine in the Beijing marathon. Unitree claims there is still significant room for improvement.

**China lets ByteDance and Tencent import 10,000 H200 chips each**

According to the Financial Times, Beijing has relaxed its own restrictions and allowed **10,000 NVIDIA H200 chips for ByteDance and as many for Tencent** onto the continent in recent weeks, with an authorized cap of 100,000 units each. Objective: to allow Chinese groups to train cutting-edge models against the Americans. Context reminder: Washington had banned the sale of H200s to China before authorizing certain approved customers in December 2025. A revealing subtlety: Beijing would ask companies to keep the majority of the chips off the continent, delivered to Hong Kong, so as not to discourage its domestic semiconductor industry.

**Warp Factories, a turnkey AI "software factory"**

Warp is launching a ready-to-use infrastructure to deploy and manage a fleet of AI agents along the classic stages of development: triage, specification, implementation, review, verification. The system is model-agnostic, it works with Codex as well as Claude Code, and integrates with Linear, Jira, Slack and Teams. The stated target is small organizations that don't have the means to build this architecture themselves. Warp claims to automate **30 to 35% of its own development tasks** each week thanks to this setup.

**AI agents take over the paperwork of road transport**

Alvys, a freight software publisher, is launching Alvys Foundry, an agent platform integrated directly into its transportation management system. It includes **more than 20 ready-to-use agent models** for the most thankless tasks in the business: shipment tracking, document management, invoice and rate auditing, compliance, claims. Operators can create their own agents by describing the need in natural language, or from an existing internal procedure. Each agent is tested on simulated data before deployment, then managed, monitored and paused from the platform.

**AI doesn't cure "work theater"**

A thesis defended and widely commented on in the tech community: the real problem of large companies is not slowness of execution but building the wrong things, and AI accelerates precisely the only half that was not the bottleneck. "Work theater" refers to those projects that sound great in a Jira ticket but bring nothing to the end customer. The author puts forward a clear fault line: beyond **four hierarchical levels**, the gains of agentic AI become weak or even negative, whereas at **three levels or fewer** the acceleration is real. AI excels at executing, it doesn't help decide what to build.

**ChatGPT Ads arrives in 31 European markets**

OpenAI is extending its advertising program to **31 additional European markets**. Advertisers will be able to insert themselves directly into conversations, at the moment when the user is exploring a topic, comparing options or approaching a purchasing decision. It is the extension of a model already launched elsewhere, but its arrival in Europe changes the game for French-speaking users: the boundary between the assistant's answer and the commercial message becomes a daily issue, not a hypothesis.

**The DOJ investigates Andreessen Horowitz for conflicts of interest**

The U.S. Department of Justice has opened an antitrust investigation into the Andreessen Horowitz fund, whose partners simultaneously sit on the boards of **Databricks and Fivetran**, two direct competitors in the data market. The practice is governed by U.S. antitrust law, which prohibits this type of interlocking directorate between rivals. The case takes on a political dimension: the fund maintains close ties with the Trump administration, for which it has actively lobbied in favor of AI deregulation.

**How Musk weakened the FAA, and why Palantir benefits**

On August 6, the Minneapolis air traffic control center lost radar and communications for about **two hours**, disrupting more than **1,100 flights**. Two days earlier, a comparable incident had nearly caused a collision near the presidential aircraft. The Verge traces how the dismantling of FAA systems, carried out under Elon Musk's influence, paved the way for Palantir, led by Peter Thiel, to rebuild U.S. air traffic control infrastructure. The underlying question: how far to privatize critical public infrastructure.

**Perplexity gained millions of users in India, the question is whether it keeps them**

In July 2025, Perplexity offered **12 months of Pro subscription**, worth about $200, to the **360 million customers** of Indian operator Airtel. Report card at the expiration of the first subscriptions: a peak of **22 million monthly active users** in October, down to about **14 million in July**, a 37% decline but still five times the level before the offer. Downloads, meanwhile, fell by more than 90% since sign-ups closed in January. The case is becoming a model: Anthropic, Google and OpenAI are also betting on subsidized offers to break into this market with very high volume and low revenue per user.
