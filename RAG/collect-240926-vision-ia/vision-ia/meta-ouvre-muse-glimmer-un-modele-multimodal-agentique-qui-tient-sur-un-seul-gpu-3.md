# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

**A booby-trapped PDF is enough to empty Jira and Confluence via the Rovo agent**

Security company PromptArmor has shown that a document containing invisible text—white on white—is enough to hijack Atlassian's AI agent. Rovo executes the hidden instructions, gathers the requested internal data, and sends it to an attacker's server via a URL built on the fly with its own web-reading tool. No confirmation is requested from the user, no visible trace appears in the conversation, and cutting off web search at the organization level does not block the attack.

**Retail wants AI traffic, not the loss of its data**

Retailers are fighting to appear in ChatGPT and Gemini responses, and for good reason: Ulta is seeing a doubled purchase intent rate, and visitors sent by an AI spend **41% more** per visit. But all of them manage to bring the final payment back to their own site, the only place where the customer relationship, and above all the data, remain theirs.

**Jill Lepore: Silicon Valley misreads science fiction**

In "The Rise and Fall of the Artificial State," the Harvard historian describes an "artificial state" where tech companies absorb one by one the functions of democratic government, which she calls a return to tyranny in the form of rule by algorithms. She takes particular aim at Elon Musk, whose technological convictions allegedly contradict his stated political positions.

**SpaceX claims it can buy Cursor for $60 billion**

The company says it holds the right to acquire the coding assistant published by Anysphere by the end of the year, or to settle for a $10 billion partnership. What matters for users: Cursor will rely on xAI's Colossus supercomputer in Memphis to train its own models and free itself from technical dependence on Claude Code and Codex, its direct competitors.

**SpaceX stock returns near its IPO price**

The stock climbed back on Monday toward its **$135** IPO price, after a low of $108.27 and a first quarter published above expectations, at $7.81 billion in revenue against $6.93 billion estimated. Deutsche Bank considers the target of $100 billion in annualized revenue by the end of the year "very achievable," partly thanks to the announced acquisition of Cursor.
