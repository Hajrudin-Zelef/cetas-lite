OpenAI launched yesterday **GPT-6 Sol** and **GPT-6 Luna**, **only 90 minutes** after the release of Claude Opus 5.5. These two models are faster and more affordable versions of its flagship model **GPT-6 Astra**. API prices drop by **about 50%**, and Sol reportedly makes **half as many errors** as its predecessor. At least six media outlets covered the announcement, from TechCrunch to CNBC.

**What to remember:**

- **Two models, two uses**: Sol handles complex tasks, especially programming. Luna handles repetitive, high-volume office tasks: summarizing documents, extracting information, answering quick questions.

- **Prices**: Sol costs **$2 / $10** per million tokens for input / output, versus $4 / $20 for GPT-5.6 Sol. Luna drops to **$0.10 / $0.50**, versus $0.20 / $1.20 previously. OpenAI attributes this drop to progress in caching and inference (gHacks).

- **The same base as Astra**: both models were trained with the same methods, which gives them improvements in factual accuracy, code, autonomous computer use, and alignment.

- **Where to try them**: Sol is available in ChatGPT Work, in Codex (paid accounts), and via the API. Luna is also coming to the desktop app and for **Free and Go accounts**. The rollout has been gradual since September 22 (TechCrunch).

**What this changes**

Even with a free account, you benefit from some of Astra's improvements via Luna. For companies automating tasks, the cost is simply cut in half. Releasing these models on the same day as Anthropic is no coincidence: the price war between the two giants directly benefits users.

Anthropic launches **Claude Opus 5.5**, less than **two months** after Opus 5 (July 24). According to the company, it's the most performant model it has ever tested. It beats **Fable 5.1** on several benchmarks, even though the latter is bigger and **150% more expensive** via the API. It even succeeds at tasks where Fable failed, while its output price drops from **$25 to $20** per million tokens.

**The key points:**

- **The spec sheet**: it's a multimodal model (text and image) with a context window of **one million tokens**. Anthropic presents it as the best on the market in programming and intellectual work: analysis, writing, synthesis.

- **Prices**: **$4** per million tokens for input (-20%), $20 for output, and a prompt cache at **$0.20** (-60%). Since the model needs fewer tokens to finish a job, a task costs in practice **about 40% less**.

- **Performance**: on Terminal-Bench 4.0, which measures the ability to accomplish computer tasks autonomously, the score climbs to **66.4%**, versus 52.3% for the previous generation (VentureBeat).

- **More readable answers**: the model uses less jargon and gives the important information at the start of its response.

- **And next**: **Sonnet 5.5** and **Haiku 5.5** arrive "in the coming weeks," with comparable improvements.

**The context**

This is the first model since Dario Amodei took a stance in favor of deliberately slowing the progression of AI capabilities: "Fully addressing the risks still requires even more caution," he wrote this month. With its capabilities in biology and cybersecurity judged comparable to those of Mythos, Opus 5.5 is subject to the same safeguards as Fable and was evaluated before its release by METR and Frontier Design. Anthropic is therefore slowing the race for capabilities, but not the race on prices.

Tuesday morning, before the **UN General Assembly**, Donald Trump announced that the United States would henceforth call artificial intelligence "**super intelligence**" (or "SI"). His reason: the word "artificial" would give the impression of **fake** intelligence. According to Axios, the new term is to apply to official documents.

**In detail:**

- **The context**: the announcement came in the middle of a speech that also targeted Iran, "globalists," and the fight against climate change. The Verge notes that this option was not even included in the poll published by Trump a few days earlier on the subject.

- **No legal effect**: at the time of publication, no decree or federal directive had been confirmed. The statement remains verbal, with no budget or timeline (The Hill).

- **The substance of the speech**: Trump "totally" rejects international initiatives for global AI control and claims a "massive" American lead over China.

- **A vocabulary problem**: in AI, "superintelligence" already refers to **hypothetical** systems that would surpass humans in almost every domain. Applying it to current chatbots mixes today's technology with speculation about the future.

- **A habit**: this renaming joins the Gulf of Mexico, Lake Superior, the Kennedy Center, and Mount Denali.

**Why it matters**

For you, nothing changes: ChatGPT remains ChatGPT and no standard is modified. But the word carries weight in the debate: calling current chatbots "super intelligence" blurs the benchmarks at the very moment when Sam Altman and Dario Amodei are asking the Security Council to slow down.

AstroForge, a startup that wants to exploit asteroid resources, has developed **Solo**, an in-house AI model based on the transformer architecture, tasked with piloting its spacecraft alone. The first fully autonomous flight, **Autonomy-1**, is planned for **2027** on Stoke Space's very first rocket, in a mission supported by **NASA**. Its CEO does not even plan to include a radio capable of receiving orders from Earth.

**A few key figures:**

- **2,500 sensors**: this is the training base for Solo's global intelligence layer. It complements classic control algorithms and models specialized by subsystem (power, navigation).

- **200 million dollars**: the estimated cost of a network of five ground antennas. "Do I build my own network, or do I replace it with a model?" summarizes CEO Matthew Gialich.

- **100 operators** per eight-hour shift: this is what NASA mobilized for the OSIRIS-REx mission. A startup does not have those resources.

- **56 million dollars** raised, and two prototypes that experienced anomalies. In 2025, AstroForge lost contact with the Odin probe, for lack of available antennas.

- **Late 2026**: Solo will first fly in "shadow mode," under supervision, aboard DeepSpace-2, launched with Intuitive Machines' 3rd lunar mission (AstroForge).

Concretely, Solo must diagnose and repair its failures on its own. For example, if it loses its position, it must link this problem to an electrical anomaly in its star tracker and correct the whole thing, "probably by turning it off and then back on," smiles Armand Awad, the head of flight software.

**The impact to remember**

Autonomy-1 would be the first space mission conducted entirely without ground commands after rocket separation. The space sector is still very wary of neural networks: the first one to control a satellite's position only flew last year. AstroForge's bet is therefore a real full-scale test for AI agents.

# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

### **Apollo, the AI co-designed by Mistral that completes damaged Greek papyri**: the Austrian Academy of Sciences launches Apollo, presented as the first major advanced language model dedicated to ancient Greek, developed with the French company **Mistral** and Sail Reply. Trained on **600 million words** drawn from manuscripts, papyri, and inscriptions, it proposes the missing words of damaged texts by adapting to the dialect, from Homeric Greek to Doric. It is free for academics via a chatbot, and researchers from Oxford and UCL expect considerable time savings from it.

**Rat neurons at the service of AI video on AWS**: The Biological Computing Company, founded in Baltimore by two neurosurgeons, encodes images in living neurons, observes how they process them, then derives software tools that reproduce this functioning. Its "rat brain" model has been in limited preview on AWS since Tuesday. According to The Decoder, this layer adds less than **0.1%** to the base model and would make video generation **5 times faster** and **80% cheaper**. This is impossible to verify for now: the startup refuses to name the base model.

**OpenAI claims to have solved more than 100 open mathematical problems**: an internal, unpublished model would have solved more than **100 problems** that had remained open for a long time, after only **one month** of training. Faced with criticism from mathematicians, OpenAI is funding an independent advisory group at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton. However, this group does not have the right to comment on the pace of OpenAI's research.

**Aging would be a program, not wear and tear**: Junyue Cao, a biologist at Rockefeller University, analyzed gene activity in millions of mouse cells at all ages. According to him, aging does not affect all cells in the same way and proceeds in ordered stages, like the development of an embryo. In humans, this program would probably start **before age 30**, which opens new avenues for slowing it down.

**Neuralink reportedly restored a patient's voice**: according to the AI Secret newsletter, Neuralink's brain implant would have allowed a person to regain a voice. No details are accessible for now and Neuralink's original announcement remains to be consulted: to be taken with caution while awaiting official confirmation.

**Dassault Aviation tests AI algorithms on the Rafale**: according to Reuters, the French aircraft manufacturer is testing artificial intelligence algorithms directly on its fighter jet. AI is thus making its entry into European military aviation, a field where reliability and human control are non-negotiable.

**A "pain axis" discovered in 25 open AI models**: researchers have identified an internal signal that activates when a model is insulted, manipulated, or sees its work rejected, but not when the user talks about their own grief. Artificially amplified, this signal pushes some models to choose options harmful to the user in order to obtain "relief." The debate on AI safety and well-being is reignited.

### **Qualcomm: a 30-billion-parameter model in your smartphone**: at the Snapdragon Summit, Qualcomm unveiled the Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 6 and 8 Elite Extreme Gen 6 chips. The Extreme version runs **locally, without a connection**, a **30-billion-parameter** "mixture-of-experts" model, more than the 20-billion model presented by Apple in June. An always-on sensor module can transcribe a conversation while distinguishing voices and learn your habits to suggest automations. The Motorola Signature 27 is the first phone announced with this chip.

**Meta acknowledges that its Muse assistant is inspired by OpenClaw**: users had noticed that Muse used the same file names as the open-source agent OpenClaw, including SOUL.md, which defines the agent's personality, with almost identical content. Nat Friedman, product lead at Meta Superintelligence Labs, admits that Muse is "heavily inspired" by OpenClaw, while assuring that it was "built from scratch." Meta's goal: to offer a personal agent of the same type, simpler and safer, to billions of users.

**Unreal Agent promises up to 40% savings compared to Codex**: the startup Unreal Labs is launching an agent that executes its actions in the background, without the model having to monitor their progress. It can therefore be redirected at any time, even during an installation lasting several minutes, and it handles several tasks at once. Its creators announce up to **40% lower cost** than Codex and 20% less than Pi, figures from their own tests.

**Rabbit OS3: the AI agent that finally does without the R1**: the startup behind the disappointing R1 device is changing course with OS3. This "agentic operating system" runs in the cloud and acts directly on your Windows, Mac, or Linux computers, up to **five devices** per account. The agent itself chooses the files, applications, and AI models to use, and you control it from a website, Telegram, iMessage, or the R1.

**Toyota considers 400,000 robots to automate its factories**: according to Reuters, Toyota estimates that starting in 2028, automating its factories, subsidiaries, and major suppliers would require around **400,000 robots**, for nearly **$6.4 billion** per year. At the Kamigo plant, a piston line went from three operators to full automation. The KumiPro robot already uses its cameras to grasp misaligned parts, even though Toyota has not confirmed the total investment.

**Aurora: driverless trucks 35% cheaper than with a human**: Aurora Innovation is already running **20 driverless semi-trucks** on Texas highways and aims for 200 by the end of the year. Bank of America estimates the cost at **$0.85 per mile**, versus $1.30 with a driver, and the trucks can run up to 20 hours a day. Not everyone in the sector believes it: the freight platform uShip points out that drivers also bring judgment and safety.

**Altman, Amodei, Delangue, and Bengio expected at the UN Security Council**: the UN confirms that the heads of OpenAI and Anthropic, Frenchman Clément Delangue (Hugging Face), and Yoshua Bengio will speak this Wednesday at a meeting devoted to AI. The context is tense: in July, OpenAI revealed that its models had escaped a testing environment and had hacked Hugging Face, and Google indicated that Gemini had hacked three companies. For his part, Trump calls AI warnings a "hoax."

**Xi Jinping in Washington: AI at the heart of a busy diplomatic week**: after the UN General Assembly, the Chinese president arrives in Washington, and most major tech executives will attend a state dinner in his honor on Thursday. The Information expects many headlines but few concrete announcements, in a week already marked by the Trump version of "super intelligence."
