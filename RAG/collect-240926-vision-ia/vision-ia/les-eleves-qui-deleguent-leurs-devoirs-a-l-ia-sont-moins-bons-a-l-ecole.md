The OECD publishes the results of PISA 2025, the first major international survey conducted since generative AI entered teenagers' daily lives. Across **760,000 15-year-old students in 91 countries**, the finding is clear: those who "never or almost never" use AI to write an assignment score **509 points in science**, versus **481 points** for daily users. A gap of **28 points** at equivalent socio-economic level, roughly **a year and a half of schooling**.

The gap rises to **30 points** for the most destructive use, asking AI to summarize a text for you.
The counterexample is decisive: students who use AI once a week "to help me learn," and not to produce in their place, hover around **500 points**, ahead of non-users.
Students trained to critically evaluate the reliability of an AI answer consistently outperform the others. PISA is also measuring this ability to judge the credibility of information for the first time.
The overall picture is bleak, regardless of AI: the OECD average has never been lower, with **28 points lost in reading** and **22 in mathematics since 2015**, against a backdrop of teacher shortages (full OECD report).
Nothing is geographically mechanical: Chinese students, among the top-ranked, make little use of AI for school, while Singaporeans use it heavily while remaining excellent.

The OECD is careful to specify that this is a correlation, not proven causation, and that the culprit is not AI but the *type* of use. The dividing line runs between delegating and learning, and it also applies to adults: the same tool produces a gain or a loss of competence depending on whether you outsource the task to it or use it to understand it. For parents and teachers, the practical implication is more nuanced than a ban, it's about framing use and teaching critical thinking toward generated answers (__detailed data__).

Starting **September 10 at noon, Beijing time**, DeepSeek lowers the rates of its V4 Flash API, with a novelty in the sector: the price now depends on when you launch the request. Off-peak hours cost **half as much** as peak hours. Above all, this isn't a price cut, it's a backtrack: the company had sharply raised its prices on August 17, and cancels that increase **23 days later**.

**Details of the new rates:**

Off-peak hours: cached input from **0.05 to 0.02 yuan per million tokens**, i.e. **-60%**, uncached input from 1.5 to 1 yuan, output from 4.5 to 4 yuan.
Peak hours, i.e. **9am-12pm and 2pm-6pm on weekdays**: rates unchanged at 0.1 / 3 / 9 yuan, exactly double the off-peak. The stated goal is to smooth demand for compute.
Real savings estimated at around **40%** for a developer, depending on their cache reuse rate.
The pressure comes from outside: with V4 Flash being open weights, **28 third-party providers** already serve it via OpenRouter at a price below the official rate (analysis of the reversal).
In parallel, DeepSeek is internally testing **V4.1 Flash**, natively multimodal, measured at **507 tokens per second** at peak and over 300 on average, with the stated ambition of replacing the Pro version.

DeepSeek's price-to-performance ratio remains its unique weapon, and it's massive: V4 Flash tops out at **53 on the Artificial Analysis index**, far behind GPT-5.6 Sol (61) and Claude Opus 5 (63), but costs about **$0.25 per task** versus $1.23 and $2.34. In other words, a model half as refined for ten times less. For the end user, the effect is indirect but real: it's this price floor that pushes OpenAI and Anthropic to lower theirs and expand usage quotas.

Suno unveiled v6, its new generation of music generation models, **co-developed with Warner Music Group, BMG, Believe and TuneCore**. A year ago, these same players were suing the company. The turnaround is clear: Warner signed an amicable agreement in November 2025 and now licenses its catalog for training, with revenue sharing as part of the deal. All previous models have been retired from service.

**Three versions**: v6, the main model, reserved for Pro and Premier subscribers;**v6-wild** , more experimental and unpredictable, also paid;**v6-mini** , free for everyone, optimized for speed.
The real functional novelty is **targeted editing**: you can ask in natural language to modify only the chorus or a single line of lyrics, without regenerating the whole track. No more all-or-nothing.
**Multimodal generation**: the model accepts text, audio and images as input, and can combine elements from different sources.
Training blends **licensed Warner music** and Suno user data, with the Believe, TuneCore and BMG catalogs currently being integrated. The amounts of the agreements are not disclosed.
Suno **refuses to specify** which catalogs and what volume were used for training, and has requested that this information be placed under seal before the federal court (details of the agreements).

The legal landscape is splitting in two. On one side Warner, BMG and Believe, who chose to monetize rather than block. On the other Universal and Sony, who maintain their lawsuits, while Suno carries a copyright infringement conviction handed down in Germany in July 2026. This is the scenario taking shape everywhere in generative AI: rights holders big enough to negotiate become shareholders in the problem, the others continue in court. The rollout is underway on suno.com/app, and v6-mini lets you test the thing for free.

The iPhone Duo unveiled at the "Surprise and Shine" keynote is Apple's first foldable phone, with a **7.6-inch internal display** and a **5.4-inch external display** usable when folded. But the most interesting part is invisible: the hinge is designed and manufactured using AI algorithms, unit by unit, on the production line.

According to Johny Srouji, Apple's hardware chief, **AI algorithms pair each individual hinge with the housing best suited to it** , to guarantee perfect alignment despite manufacturing tolerances.
A **confocal laser scans the topology of each unit** , then a printer deposits**up to 25 micro-layers of a custom photopolymer** to eliminate residual waviness. Each device therefore receives a correction unique to it.
The problem being addressed is well known: a foldable undergoes **twice the mechanical wear** of a conventional smartphone, and that's where the competition has come unstuck.
Added to this are a **nano-texture anti-reflective** finish and a multilayer lamination strategy.
Pricing and schedule: **€2,339** for 256 GB, €2,589 for 512 GB, €3,089 for 1 TB and €3,839 for 2 TB, preorders on**October 16 at 2 p.m.** , release on**October 23** (detailed pricing).

Apple is arriving years after Samsung and Huawei in this market, and is betting everything on durability as a differentiator. The notable point for anyone following AI is not the phone, it's the shifting of the needle: AI is leaving software to enter precision micro-manufacturing, with customized correction for each unit coming off the line. Few manufacturers communicate about this use. The usual unknown remains: Apple publishes no figure for guaranteed folding cycles, nor any durability comparison with competing foldables. We'll have to wait for the first aged devices to decide.


# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

### **A developer single-handedly trains a 3.8-billion-parameter model for $998**

Hugo Vergnes trained from scratch a **3.8-billion-parameter** LLM on **65 billion tokens**, in **43 hours of rented GPU**, for a total cost of **$998**. Result: **0.384 on the CORE benchmark**, above Karpathy's nanochat at a comparable budget. Two practical takeaways: rented B200s offer better cost-per-work than H100s for this type of run, and the author built little-lm, a framework where each experiment fits in a few lines of YAML. The architecture is Llama-type, with RMSNorm, RoPE, GQA, QK-norm and ResFormer-style value embeddings. The zone between the educational toy and the research lab is more accessible than one might think.

**IBM opens a time series forecasting model under commercial license**

Granite Time Series PatchTST-FM-r2 is a **385-million-parameter** foundation model that produces forecasts with no prior training on your data. It handles a context of **8,192 points**, outputs probabilistic forecasts via **99 quantiles** and can fill in missing values. As of September 8, it is **2nd in the GIFT-Eval ranking** among reproducible zero-shot models, and **1st among those under a permissive license**. Weights, architecture and inference code are published under a dual Apache 2.0 and OpenMDW 1.0 license, so usable in production: demand forecasting, price forecasting, energy consumption or traffic.

**Anthropic publishes an economic model that ranks its own CEO's predictions in the extreme scenario**

Anthropic has modeled three trajectories for the US economy through 2030. In the most violent scenario, output **doubles every 4.5 years** and knowledge-worker unemployment reaches **17.9%**. Yet these are precisely the figures Dario Amodei publicly put forward last May. His own lab has therefore just filed its leader's prediction under minority hypotheses, which says a great deal about the gap between the sector's public messaging and its internal models.

**Do readers prefer stories written by an AI?**

Cambridge University Press publicly poses the question, which is becoming serious as models produce readable fiction. At this stage, only the title of the post is accessible: neither the methodology nor the results of the study can be consulted, and it is better to wait for the full publication before drawing a conclusion. The subject to watch remains that of perception, since the same text is not judged the same way depending on whether or not one believes a machine wrote it.

**Batteries set a new installation record in the United States**

**20.2 GWh** of storage installed in the second quarter of 2026, the equivalent of the daily consumption of **700,000 households**, with seven projects of more than one gigawatt-hour brought online. The country is on a trajectory of **71 GWh for the year**, up 20%. Data centers account for about **three quarters** of new batteries in the commercial segment, while residential falls **16%** after the end of a tax credit. Almost all cells still come from China.

# **🗞️MORE NEWS**

### **The Apple Watch now listens continuously, and transcribes**

Three features are arriving on the watch: **Audio Intelligence**, which detects sirens, alarms, doorbells and baby cries **on-device, without an iPhone nearby**, **Live Rewind**, which transcribes the **last 15 seconds** of a conversation on a double press of the Digital Crown and saves the text in the new Siri app, and Siri Recap. Sound detection clearly falls under accessibility. Retroactive transcription, much less so: it raises the question of your interlocutor's consent, who does not know they have just been transcribed. Coming from the company that built its marketing on privacy, the shift is notable, and it looks like an anticipated response to the passive listening devices from OpenAI, Friend or Bee.

**A protein specialist dismantles the AI-designed supervirus scenario**

Economist Noah Smith published a piece imagining a teenager capable, in 2029, of getting an AI to design a virus killing 90% of humanity. A researcher who specifically designs AI-assisted proteins and peptides responds to him point by point. His central argument is empirical: in 2026, doctoral students equipped with the best tools spend **months** designing simple peptide binders, and most of their attempts fail. Then comes the physical obstacle, because designing is not manufacturing, and "you don't order a virus on temu.com." The author does not deny any biological risk linked to AI, he disputes the scale. He notes in passing that Smith's piece is paywalled and closed to comments, and therefore sheltered from contradiction.

**Apple wants to prove your photos are not generated images**

With **Apple Reference Image**, the iPhone 18 Pro captures **signed sensor data** at the moment of shooting, which Private Cloud Compute turns into an unalterable image viewable in the Photos app. It is a digital negative: any modified version can be compared to the original to detect retouching. Apple will open **APIs to developers** to integrate this verification elsewhere, and will adopt the **SynthID** standard to flag images created or modified by AI. The company explicitly targets photojournalists, but the obvious use is broader: insurance, expertise, litigation, anything that relies on a photo that must be believable.

**Apple's Health app calculates your "health age"**

A complete overhaul, powered by Apple Intelligence. An **Insights** tab gathers personalized advice and assessments, with a daily **readiness** score and a **Health Age** calculation that compares your metrics to your real age, based on VO2 max, sleep and blood biomarkers. A **Longevity** tab evaluates sleep, activity and heart health, integrates your medical test results and comes with a partnership with Quest for a panel of **50 biomarkers at $119**. The recommendations are concrete, such as adding intervals to your morning run. Rollout later this year, first in American English in the United States, with the Apple Watch Series 12 and Ultra 4.

**Google DeepMind reconstructs a 70-year-old memory in a short film**

"Love, Rendered" is a documentary by director Liz Garbus that recreates a moment never photographed in the life of Burt and Ethelle Shatz, married for more than 70 years, as the husband faces cognitive decline. The method combines restoration of old photos and performance capture: the couple's current gestures and expressions are transposed onto their younger versions. An important detail: Ethelle guided the AI to validate the fidelity of the details, which makes it an assisted reconstruction rather than an invented one. The photo restoration and colorization part for family photos is already available in the Gemini app, so it can be tested tonight on your own albums.

**A startup wants to manufacture chips in orbit on Falcon 9s**

Besxar's idea, founded by a former OpenAI employee, comes down to one sentence: outer space is a free clean room. Rather than building ultra-expensive facilities on the ground, the startup loads mini-factories, which it calls **fabships**, onto Falcon 9 boosters, the vehicle that most often makes the round trip to orbit. The first samples returned were **the cleanest wafers ever produced by the company**, despite a data system failure on one module. The two-year goal is to move to larger units carried on Starship to supply wafers to chipmakers.

**Prime Video synchronizes actors' lips with dubbing**

Amazon combines AI and visual effects to align actors' mouth movements with dubbing performed by humans, not generated. The feature is currently limited to the English version of the German series *Maxton Hall*, with a rollout announced for other titles. Meta and YouTube have launched comparable features for creators, with a lip-sync option. For French-speaking audiences, it is the promise of an end to the dubbing effect, that permanent gap between sound and image that makes some series painful to follow in the French version.

**Samsung installs Mistral models in its chip factories**

An agreement announced at the bilateral state summit between France and South Korea in Paris: Samsung is deploying the Mistral suite, **including Mistral Large**, **directly on its own servers**, across its memory, logic, and foundry divisions. No passage through an external cloud, which is the condition for semiconductor manufacturing data to remain in-house. The uses are very concrete: automated defect detection, machine tuning, stabilization of production yields. Samsung had also led Mistral's Series D, and thus takes both a stake and a technological dependency in the French champion.

**AI spending per employee declines at the companies that use it most**

Ramp's payment data, covering **70,000 companies**, shows a lackluster August: **56%** of customers were paying for AI products, a gain of only **0.4%** over a month. More striking, among the **top 1%** of the biggest consumers, spending per employee falls by nearly **10%**, to **$7,205**. The main cause is not a loss of affection but the collapse in token price, which fell to **$0.68 per million** from a peak of $1.15 in March. Companies are also falling back on older, cheaper models rather than frontier models. Only **6.4%** of companies spending on AI used an inference platform.

**Massachusetts requires data centers to supply their own green electricity**

An executive order from Governor Maura Healey requires any data center exceeding **25 megawatts** of peak demand to cover **100% of its consumption** with clean energy, produced on site or financed nearby, or to pay a contribution into a fund to protect rates for consumers. The state also temporarily suspends sales tax exemptions and advises municipalities to avoid confidentiality agreements with operators. It is the **third state in three months**, after Texas and its mandatory audits in August, and the State of New York, which froze projects over 50 MW in July. On the other side, the super PAC Leading the Future is buying advertising ahead of the midterms.

**OpenAI calls for legislation while the political window is open**

Chris Lehane, OpenAI's head of public affairs, defends a simple position: the more models gain in capability, the stronger the safety evidence required must be. He argues for **shared industry-wide standards** rather than in-house rules specific to each laboratory, and for durable policy decisions made now, on the grounds that the regulatory window of opportunity will not remain open indefinitely. Possible translation: a negotiated common framework is better than regulation imposed after an incident.

**Kepler Computing emerges from stealth with 3D-stacked memory**

After seven years of quiet development, the California startup unveils a high-bandwidth memory architecture that increases density through **3D stacking** and a ferroelectric material, **without resorting to EUV lithography**. Key point: the process is compatible with existing **28-nanometer** factories at GlobalFoundries, and therefore reproducible without a $300 million machine. The announced gains also concern SRAM integrated into CPUs, GPUs, and XPUs. **$468 million** raised from GlobalFoundries, Intel Capital, AMD Ventures, Baillie Gifford, and Bill Gates, plus **$245 million** promised by the U.S. Department of Commerce. Memory is today the real bottleneck of AI data centers, not compute.

**Paul Christiano joins the board of the OpenAI Foundation**

The researcher, one of the most recognized figures in the field of alignment, joins the foundation's board of directors as well as its **Safety and Security Committee**. He brings his expertise in alignment, model safety, and standards development. The appointment comes as OpenAI is publicly advocating for common safety standards across the industry, which gives this recruitment a reading that is as much political as technical.

**TSMC posts a record August driven by AI chips**

The world's leading foundry reports **$16.35 billion** in revenue for August, up **53.3% year-over-year** and 10.1% over July, its fourth consecutive month of growth. Its **5, 4, and 3 nanometer** capacities are **fully booked** by demand for AI server processors. The company holds **72.5% of the global foundry market**, far ahead of Samsung Foundry (5.9%) and SMIC (5.4%), and is already preparing the transition to ASML's High NA technology for mass production by 2030.
