SoftBank plans to build AI data centers in France with a total capacity of **5 GW**, which would constitute its **largest investment in AI infrastructure in Europe**. The Japanese group mentions an envelope of **75 billion euros**, including **45 billion** deployed by 2031 across three sites concentrated in Normandy. Enough to make France one of the nerve centers of AI computing on the continent, if everything goes as announced.

**A few key figures:**

- **75 billion euros** announced in total for AI data centers in France

- A targeted capacity of **5 GW**, representing a significant share of European AI computing

- **45 billion euros** of operational installations expected by **2031**

- Three sites grouped in **Normandy**

- SoftBank's most ambitious European bet in AI infrastructure to date

**Why it matters:** If the project materializes, France would rise to the rank of major AI computing hub in Europe, with direct consequences for digital sovereignty, local employment, and the country's attractiveness to labs. One major caveat remains: SoftBank is known for announcing spectacular megaprojects that then struggle to materialize. The media outlet points this out bluntly. Before crying revolution, it's better to wait for the first shovels in Normandy.

Nvidia has just launched its **first publicly accessible humanoid robotics platform**, building on the robots of Chinese startup **Unitree**. A partnership that formalizes the chip giant's entry into humanoid robotics, at the precise moment when Unitree is preparing its **stock market listing**. A sign that the sector is moving from lab demo to a business in its own right.

**Key takeaways:**

- Nvidia deploys its first public humanoid system based on **Unitree**'s hardware

- **Unitree**, a young Chinese startup, is establishing itself as a key player in affordable robotics

- The startup is reportedly aiming for an **IPO**, reflecting growing investor appetite for AI robotics

- An unprecedented **US / China** axis for Nvidia, as robotics becomes a strategic battleground

**What it changes:** Nvidia is no longer content to sell the "brains" (its GPUs) of robots; it now provides the complete platform that makes them work. This could dramatically accelerate the adoption of humanoids by giving manufacturers a ready-made base to build on. And the choice of a Chinese partner, in the midst of Sino-American technological rivalry, says a lot about who is really leading the race for consumer robotics hardware today.

Jensen Huang unveiled a **chip based on the ARM architecture** intended for laptops, marking **Nvidia**'s official entry into the **consumer PC market**. The chip will equip new laptops from **Microsoft, Dell, HP, and ASUS**, an alliance that immediately places Nvidia at the heart of the Windows ecosystem.

**The essential points:**

- Nvidia's first **ARM** chip designed for consumer PCs

- Partnerships with **Microsoft, Dell, HP, and ASUS** from launch

- A strategic diversification beyond data center and gaming **GPUs**

- Nvidia moves directly onto the turf of **Intel, AMD, and Qualcomm**

**The impact to remember:** By betting on ARM for PCs, Nvidia is attacking a market historically locked down by Intel and AMD's x86, with embedded AI as its main selling point. For users, this promises a new generation of Windows laptops designed from the ground up to run AI locally. And for the industry, it's renewed competition that could shift the lines, both in performance and in prices.


Google is making Nano Banana 2 (**Gemini 3.1 Flash Image**) and Nano Banana Pro (**Gemini 3 Pro Image**) **generally available**, its two models specialized in image generation and editing. The new feature that changes everything: these models now accept **video files** as prompt input, not just text or still images.

**In detail:**

- **Nano Banana 2** (Gemini 3.1 Flash Image) and **Nano Banana Pro** (Gemini 3 Pro Image) are generally available

- The models now accept **video** as prompt input

- They deeply analyze **subjects, actions, and visual styles** of a sequence

- All of it is integrated into **Google Cloud**'s enterprise cloud infrastructure

**Why it matters:** Being able to feed video into an image model opens the door to significantly richer creative workflows: extracting a style, reproducing a precise action, spinning a scene into multiple variations. For creators, agencies, and studios, it's one more step toward visual production pipelines almost entirely driven by AI, directly on enterprise cloud infrastructure.


# 🧠 **RESEARCH**

**A universal model of life: Biohub maps proteins**: Biohub (a Chan Zuckerberg initiative, in Meta's orbit) releases Evolutionary Scale Models to predict and design proteins. Its flagship model, ESMFold2, trained on **2.8 billion protein sequences**, delivers SOTA results in structure prediction. The payoff: highly concrete applications against cancer and immune diseases.

**3,000 tokens/s per user on standard GPUs**: Kog AI announces an inference optimization reaching **3,000 tokens/s per user** on standard datacenter GPUs, or **10 to 30 times** more than the usual 100 to 300 tokens/s. The method exploits a hidden inefficiency in the GPU's token generation. Demonstrated on a 2B model, it should scale to large MoE models.

**DeepSWE: the code benchmark that exposes the real gaps between models**: Datacurve launches DeepSWE, a "long-horizon" software engineering benchmark built on novel tasks rather than public GitHub issues, to avoid training contamination. The results are damning: **GPT-5.5 at 70%**, GPT-5.4 at 56%, Claude Opus 4.7 at 54%. Differences that older tests completely masked.

**NVIDIA Cosmos 3, an open model for physical AI**: NVIDIA releases Cosmos 3, an omni-directional open source model capable of reasoning and acting for robotics and physical AI. It generates and understands videos to simulate the behavior of autonomous agents, a rarity at this scale in open source. A valuable asset for training and controlling robots without starting from scratch.

**Apple rebuilds Siri on Google Gemini**: Apple is completely overhauling Siri by basing it on Google Gemini, giving it generative capabilities comparable to ChatGPT. The new version integrates with the Dynamic Island, accessible with a swipe, supports AI-powered web search and third-party agents, all while leveraging on-device data. Enough to transform a long-struggling assistant into a credible competitor, across hundreds of millions of devices.

**Blue Origin's New Glenn rocket explodes on the ground**: Blue Origin's New Glenn launcher exploded during an engine test at Cape Canaveral, destroying the booster as well as the company's only operational launch pad. The incident deals a direct blow to NASA's Artemis program, in which Blue Origin plays a key role. A serious setback for Jeff Bezos's lunar ambitions, who will have to rebuild before he can fly.

**Epicure: three AIs that cook by recipes or by chemistry**: Kaikaku.AI launches Epicure, three models trained on **4.14 million recipes** and the FlavorDB database. Depending on whether they learn culinary associations or chemical relationships between ingredients, their recommendations differ markedly. The surprise: the purely chemical model outperforms the "recipe" models at ranking tastes and nutritional values, without even accessing that data.

**The Atlas robot learns soccer by watching videos**: Boston Dynamics unveils a series of five videos in which Atlas learns soccer from scratch, by analyzing gameplay footage. The highlight: the execution of the "Ghost Rabona," one of the sport's most difficult moves, requiring formidable balance and coordination. The company plans to showcase Atlas and its quadruped Spot at the 2026 World Cup.

**Anthropic bans AI during its job interviews**: To assess candidates' true thinking, Anthropic bans AI tools during its interviews, with up to **5 rounds** covering skills, values, and ethical reasoning. Salaries climb as high as **$850,000**, and paid preparatory coaching ($4,600) is offered. An acknowledged irony for an AI lab that asks its future employees to think... without AI.

**Bonsai Image 4B: image generation that fits on your machine**: This new **4-billion-parameter** model relies on 1-bit quantization to run directly on-device, without a cloud connection. The payoff: a drastic reduction in size and latency, and execution possible on both personal PCs and edge devices. Its strong engagement on HackerNews (**357 upvotes**) confirms the community's appetite for decentralized, accessible AI.

**How Turkey industrialized hair transplants thanks to machine learning**: Turkey's hair transplant industry, worth **over a billion dollars**, relies on specialized engines and machine learning algorithms to optimize its procedures. A textbook case of AI applied to a very concrete medical sector, where technological innovation serves as a decisive competitive advantage in an ultra-competitive market.

**Building a complete game with Codex in a single prompt**: This practical guide shows how to leverage Codex's "goals" feature to build a functional browser game, without repeated manual follow-ups.
