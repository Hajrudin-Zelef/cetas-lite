Anthropic has just gone on the offensive. In a letter obtained by CNBC, the company accuses Chinese giant Alibaba of conducting **"the largest known distillation attack to date"** against its models. Translation: Alibaba allegedly queried Claude massively to siphon off its expertise and copy it into its own systems, all without paying the bill for years of research.

**Key takeaways:**

- The letter, revealed by CNBC, denounces **the largest distillation operation ever recorded** against Anthropic

- Distillation consists of querying a model a very large number of times to **replicate its behavior** in a competing model, at lower cost

- Anthropic calls the maneuver an **illicit campaign** and outright theft of intellectual property

- The targeted model is **Claude**, the company's flagship product and main competitive advantage

**What this changes:**

Beyond the standoff between two heavyweights, the affair hits a sensitive point: if a cutting-edge model can be copied simply by querying it en masse, then the technological barriers that labs thought insurmountable are far more fragile than they appear. It also adds a new front to the Sino-American tech rivalry, this time on the terrain of intellectual property. Expect to see the major labs tighten access to their APIs and, perhaps, bring these disputes before the courts.

OpenAI no longer wants to depend solely on others to run its models. The company has just presented **Jalapeño**, its very first in-house processor, designed with **Broadcom** and intended exclusively for inference, that is, running models day to day. According to The Decoder, the chip is due to operate **at large scale by the end of 2026**. A sign of the scale of the announcement: five major tech outlets covered it the same day.

**The essential points:**

- **Jalapeño** is OpenAI's first custom chip, co-developed with **Broadcom**

- It is optimized for **inference** (running models), not for training

- Large-scale deployment expected by **the end of 2026**

- Stated goal: reduce dependence on **NVIDIA**, which currently equips most AI datacenters

- Announcement picked up by **5 sources** (TechCrunch AI, The Verge AI, CNBC Tech, Hacker News)

**The impact to remember:**

It's the same bet Apple made with its in-house chips: by designing its own silicon, OpenAI seeks to control its costs, timelines, and performance, rather than enduring NVIDIA's prices and shortages. If Jalapeño lives up to its promises, the entire economic balance of generative AI could be redrawn, and it could be a serious thorn in the side of the chip leader.

**Talos: Microsoft's AI that reopens rare disease files**

Microsoft Research is releasing Talos, an open-source tool that automatically reanalyzes genomic data as scientific advances are made. Deployed on nearly **5,000 undiagnosed patients**, it enabled **241 new diagnoses** (an additional yield of **5.1%**), with a minimal false-positive rate. Better still: in 90% of cases, it leaves on average only **1.3 genetic variants to examine** per patient, which makes human review sustainable month after month.

**A self-taught researcher claims to have deciphered Linear A using AI**

Tom Di Mino, a self-taught engineer, says he has cracked **Linear A**, the **3,500-year-old** Minoan script that has resisted linguists for 70 years. Alone and in five months, he used Python scripts driven by AI agents to comb through digitized corpora, producing a **lexicon of 408 entries** and **40 phonetic values**. Caution, however: linguists at Cambridge and Rutgers have not yet validated these results, which the author himself describes as "proposed."

**Microsoft's "quantum breakthrough" challenged over simple Python errors**

A researcher is casting doubt on Microsoft's recent quantum announcement: according to him, the Python code used to validate the result contains **basic methodological errors**. Enough to revive the debate over reproducibility and rigor in quantum research at the major players. The topic is generating reactions in the tech community, with **166 points on Hacker News**.
