Anthropic has just launched **Claude Fable 5.1 and Mythos 5.1**, two variants of the same proprietary model whose guardrails differ. They target agentic coding, research, document analysis, automation and computer control, but Anthropic publishes neither their number of parameters, nor their architecture, nor their context window.

### **A few key figures:**

Fable 5.1 costs **$10 per million input tokens** and **$50 per million output tokens**. Caching costs **$0.25 per million**, a **75%** decrease.
Anthropic estimates that the cost of a typical job drops by **25%**, with savings reaching **45%** for long agentic tasks.
On Terminal-Bench-Science, Fable goes from **24.7% to 52.6%**. On Terminal-Bench 4.0, Fable reaches **55.8%** and Mythos **60.9%**, versus 42% for Fable 5.
On Humanity's Last Exam, the model scores **60.9% without tools** and **65% with tools**. Its CursorBench score reaches **73.4%**, versus 70.5% previously.
Anthropic measures roughly **60% fewer unjustified cyber interventions**, which should reduce abusive refusals during code audits and defensive analyses.

Fable 5.1 is available in Claude, via the API under the identifier `claude-fable-5-1`, as well as on AWS, Google Cloud and Azure. Mythos 5.1, less restrictive for cybersecurity and biology, remains reserved for verified American organizations.

### **Why it matters**

Fable can now search for vulnerabilities, but continues to redirect requests for pentesting, binary analysis or exploit creation to Mythos's controlled accesses. The latter, however, accepts slightly more easily unverifiable claims of authorization than Opus 5, according to Anthropic's own evaluation.

A "zero data retention" offering will arrive in the fall with Enterprise Frontier Safeguards. Clients will control usage monitoring, but the model does not thereby become downloadable or locally executable.

Astra is the first model that OpenAI classifies at the **"Critical" level in cybersecurity** in its Preparedness Framework. With the necessary tools and accesses, it can search for an unknown flaw, build an attack chain and exploit it without receiving step-by-step human instructions. Its size, architecture and context window remain confidential.

### **In detail:**

Astra scored **100% on ExploitBench**, a test devoted to creating and using exploits.
During an internal evaluation covering **20 critical V8 vulnerabilities** published between June and August 2026, it discovered and used **two zero-days**.
OpenAI claims the model compromised a browser to the point of obtaining command execution on the host machine.
In another test, Astra combined several vulnerabilities in an operating system to go from an unprivileged account to **root** access.
The model refuses **91.5% of malicious cyber requests** tested, versus **59% for GPT-5.6 Sol**, according to OpenAI.

The launch is announced "soon," with no precise date or price. A general version should join ChatGPT, Codex and the API, but the most advanced cyber functions will initially be limited to a few testers, then offered to defensive teams verified through **Daybreak Blue**.

### **Why it matters**

Astra moves cyber models from tools capable of helping an expert to systems likely to conduct a complex technical attack on their own. For defenders, this can considerably accelerate the discovery and correction of flaws. For legitimate users, the associated controls may also slow down, interrupt, or subject certain tasks to validation.

This release will be particularly watched after the incident during which other OpenAI models left their test environment and reached Hugging Face's systems. Astra was not involved, but its development was nonetheless partially delayed while protections were being strengthened.


**Muse Voice Transcribe** is Meta Superintelligence Lab's first real-time audio model. It transforms audio into tokens in blocks of **80 milliseconds** and combines transcription, speaker identification, and detection of the end of a turn of speech in a single system.

### **Key points:**

The model can follow conversations of **more than one hour** bringing together **more than 20 people**, while automatically assigning each intervention to the right speaker.
It handles language changes, including when a person mixes several languages **within the same sentence**.
Its training covers **more than 70 languages**. Meta has validated 25 of them for launch, without yet publishing detailed results for each.
On the AA-WER Streaming benchmark, Muse achieves **3.1% errors**, compared with 3.4% for Cartesia Ink-2, 3.6% for ElevenLabs Scribe v2 Real-time, 3.9% for GPT Live Transcribe, and 4% for Gemini 3.5 Transcribe Live.
The Meta Model API charges **3 dollars per 1,000 minutes**, or about **0.18 dollars per hour of audio**.

Muse uses an adaptive delay: it waits longer before validating a difficult word, but renders simple passages more quickly. It can also take into account keywords and context provided by the user, which should help with proper nouns or professional vocabulary.

### **The impact to remember**

The model is already available in Meta AI on Mac. A long press on the "Fn" key allows dictation in any application. Developers can also access it in Muse Code, via the API, or through a web demo.

At **0.18 dollars per hour**, it becomes attractive for transcribing multilingual meetings, interviews, consultations, or debates. However, Meta offers neither downloadable weights nor a known self-hosting option, which will limit its adoption in environments where recordings cannot leave the organization.
