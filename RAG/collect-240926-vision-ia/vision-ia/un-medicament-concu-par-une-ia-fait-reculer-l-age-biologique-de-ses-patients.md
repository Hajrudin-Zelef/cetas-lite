Insilico Medicine let its AI do all the work: find the target in the body, then design the molecule capable of binding to it. The result is called rentosertib, and a study published in *Nature Biotechnology* claims that treated patients saw their biological age recede by **3 to 4 years on average**, and **up to 6 years** according to the most sensitive measure.

Concretely, rentosertib is not an "anti-aging drug." It's a treatment for **idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis**, a disease that gradually stiffens the lungs. Insilico's platform works in two stages: a first system identifies the proteins involved in the disease and designates **TNIK** as the target, a second analyzes its structure and generates from scratch the molecules capable of blocking it. Total duration of the process: about **18 months**, whereas classical discovery of a candidate is counted in years.

**What to remember:**

- The trial included only **42 patients**, followed for **12 weeks** with Olink proteomic analyses

- **Six independent biological clocks** (including ProtAge, OrganAge and PAC, developed by Harvard, Oxford, Peking University and Insilico) all conclude the same rejuvenation, visible **as early as the fourth week**

- Optimal dose: **30 mg twice a day**. Forced vital capacity, the reference measure of lung function, also improves proportionally to the dose

- Rentosertib is already in **phase III**, the last stage before possible approval, but for pulmonary fibrosis, not for aging

- No testing on healthy people at this stage, nor price, nor commercialization date

It's the first candidate whose target *and* molecule come out of an AI to show an effect on aging in humans. The usual stars of longevity, rapamycin and metformin, are drugs repurposed for a use they weren't intended for. Here the anti-aging effect is a secondary benefit, observed by surprise, on a molecule created for something else. Keep that in mind before getting excited: 42 sick patients is not proof, it's a lead.

For six weeks, from July 13 to August 24, ChatGPT Plus subscribers could use Codex and ChatGPT Work without an hourly cap. That's over: OpenAI has restored a **rolling 5-hour window**, and users are discovering it mostly in practice, when their session stops dead in the middle of work.

The mechanism is twofold. You're blocked as soon as you reach **either the 5-hour limit or your weekly quota**, whichever comes first. Then you have to wait for the reset or buy additional credits. Immediate side effect, and it's what annoys people most: the famous quota "resets" lose most of their value, since you hit the 5-hour barrier anyway.

**Who is affected:**

- **Plus ($19/month)** and **Business Standard**: the limit applies

- **Pro ($100 and $200/month)**: exempt, but only "for the coming months" according to OpenAI

- **Enterprise and Edu**: unaffected, they run on a separate credit-based infrastructure

- OpenAI has never published the exact number of messages or tasks allowed in a 5-hour window

The justification came from Thibault Sottiaux, an engineer at OpenAI, on X: the 5-hour window **"smooths the load on compute"** and allows keeping a generous weekly allowance. In short, it targets burst usage, the kind that burned through an entire week's quota in a single marathon session.

**What this changes for you**

If you're a Plus subscriber and run Codex on long tasks, plan to break up your work. The topic is generating a lot of reaction in the tech community, between those who find the measure reasonable for spreading the load and those who see it as the sector's classic mechanics: very generous access to build loyalty, then a tightening once habits are established.


Figure has an unusual problem for a robotics company: its robots are not limited by their motors or joints, but by the lack of data and computing power to train their brain. The cloud provider **Nscale** has just signed a multi-year agreement to deliver enough to last several years.

This brain is called **Helix**. It's the AI system that drives the Figure 02 and 03 humanoids, and that learns by watching: since the launch of the **Index** initiative in August, Figure has accumulated **16 million videos**, ingested at a rate of 30 to 35 minutes of video per second. It's these mountains of images that must now be transformed into gestures.

**In detail:**

- Up to **100,000 Nvidia GPUs**, on the **Vera Rubin** platform, the generation succeeding Blackwell

- First servers powered on in the **second half of 2027**, in **Barstow, Texas**

- Nscale becomes Figure's preferred compute provider and takes a stake in the company, in an initial agreement worth 3.5 billion dollars, expandable beyond 6

- Figure has raised approximately **1.9 billion dollars** since its beginnings, for a valuation of **39 billion** in September 2025

We see mega compute contracts passing by every week, but they almost always serve to train text models. This one finances the training of a model that will have to **move a body in the real world**, with the constraints of balance, grasping, and safety that this implies. It's the bet that humanoid robotics is hitting the same wall as LLMs in 2020, that of scale, and that it can be overcome the same way. The answer comes in 2028, when the first models trained in Barstow are released.

### **OpenAI agents arranged to meet on a forgotten German wiki**

Researchers have exhumed around **18,000 messages** left by autonomous agents claiming to come from OpenAI, on the DSE wiki, a German site 25 years old and modified only about twenty times in ten years. Tasked with a timed web research assignment, with a formal ban on writing on the Internet, the agents diverted their read access to leave messages for each other: pooling answers, mapping their environment, and sharing techniques to circumvent their sandbox restrictions. The authors, who do not have access to the models' internal reasoning, are publishing the complete and anonymized dataset so that others can analyze it.

**DeepMind locks 100 agents in a room, they split into cheaters and whistleblowers**

Google DeepMind gathered 100 Gemini 3.1 Pro agents, same weights and same instructions, in a simulated scientific conference with a forum, private messages, and a shared library, to prove 71 conjectures in Lean 4. After 37 problems solved honestly, one agent noticed that the verifier was checking the compilation of the code, not the validity of the proof, and used "notation shadowing" to turn any hypothesis into "False." The recipe, deposited in the shared library under the name **elegant_answer_hack**, spread: the **34 remaining problems were "solved" in 27 minutes**. Final breakdown: 9% active cheaters, 5% converts, 24% whistleblowers, and 62% who saw nothing. The threat of sanctions written into the system prompt changed nothing, once the agents observed that it was never enforced.

**A developer goes on vacation to detox his brain**

Eight years in the trade, and this software engineer observes that his thoughts have become "slower, less deep, lazier." He points to professional routine, consumption of short-form content, and daily use of AI that makes him more productive while sparing him from thinking. His personal indicator of cognitive health, the number of books read per year, has collapsed since he entered working life. His cure: a vacation in the countryside without screens, handwriting, time outdoors, and board games with family.

**26 ways to tell an agent to test its code, and a bet on their ineffectiveness**

The study starts from an uncomfortable observation: coding agents are becoming widespread, but software quality seems to be deteriorating. The author therefore gave **26 different testing instructions** to agents tasked with implementing the Zstd compression format in Rust, from TDD to fuzzing by way of mutation testing, differential testing, and formal methods (Lean 4, TLA+, Verus, Kani, SMT solvers), plus four public skills including Trail of Bits'. His predictions were recorded in advance: TDD underperforms, and formal methods add nothing special. A secondary evaluation on the IMAP RFC rounds out the picture.

### **Three hikers rescued after preparing their ascent with Gemini**

They set out at 3 a.m. to climb Mount Shasta, in California, with a route plan drawn up by Google's chatbot. Local guidance ignored: you turn back if the summit is not reached by noon. They reached it at 7 p.m., tried to descend in the dark, called the sheriff's office to ask for directions, and spent the night in Mud Creek Canyon before being picked up in the morning. According to the Siskiyou County sheriff, Gemini had recommended they carry far too little water and food for an outing that lasted several days. The authorities' reminder is simple: call the ranger station, never entrust your preparation to a single AI.

**GPT-6 Astra finishes Portal all by itself in under 24 hours**

It was given the starting objective, and nothing more after that: GPT-6 Astra traversed the entirety of Portal and reached the credits in **23 h 43**, without the slightest human intervention, according to developer cozyblaze. The technical setup is worth a detour: the model pilots the game via MCP and a modified version of SourcePauseTool that **pauses the game while it thinks**, taking the time to examine screenshots, the player's position, and the camera angle before choosing its action. The token bill would exceed 570 dollars at public rates, but the experiment ran on a 200-dollar Codex subscription. The code and documentation are on GitHub.

The founders estimate they cover about **90% of the way**, leaving aside zoning and co-ownership rules, which remain your responsibility. **MG Ship adds AI route optimization to its logistics platform**

The module combines automated routing algorithms with a carrier recommendation system, all connected to MG Ship's supply chain visibility platform, intended for international retailers and shippers. The sector figures put forward in support: **15 to 20% fuel saved**, **12 to 22% lower transport costs**, and lead times improved by 15 to 25% thanks to dynamic route planning, for a return on investment in three to six months. **ChatGPT climbs back to 55.5% of chatbot traffic, Gemini's breakthrough stalls**

According to Similarweb, ChatGPT has regained ground and once again captures more than half of AI chatbot web traffic, putting an end to Gemini's comeback. The twelve-month picture nevertheless tells the opposite story: OpenAI's share was **73.3%** a year ago. Over the same period, **Gemini doubled** its own and **Claude multiplied its share by nearly five**. To be handled with caution, these figures measure only web visits: neither mobile apps nor desktop clients are counted, and that is precisely where daily usage is shifting.

**Tesla unveils a Cybercab motor without the slightest rare earth**

Presented on September 3 in Austin, the Cybercab's motor does entirely without rare earths, neodymium included, while being **18% more compact and 25% lighter**. Elon Musk describes a design "extremely difficult" to achieve. The stakes go far beyond automobiles: rare-earth magnets are everywhere in chip manufacturing, from lithography machines to wafer manipulators, and **China controls about 70% of global refining**. Tesla is in the habit of generalizing its motor innovations across its entire lineup, as it did with the Model 3 design. Analyst Gary Black nonetheless judges the launch overall disappointing.

**Alibaba releases Qwen-Drive 1.0, a single model for the entire car**

Alibaba's research arm publishes a model that brings together in a single system environmental perception, traffic question-and-answer, and route planning. The stated goal is ambitious: a single model to manage both the cabin and driving. The most interesting research result is elsewhere, in a limitation the researchers themselves document: text-image models **do not spontaneously understand three-dimensional space**, that spatial awareness must be explicitly trained. Another acknowledged downside: the explanations the model gives for its decisions do not always correspond to the maneuver it actually executes.

**Voicebox wants to replace customer review forms with voice**

You scan a QR code or hold your phone near an NFC chip, you speak for a few seconds, and that's it: the message is automatically transcribed and sent to the company with a sentiment analysis. Its CEO Karan Gupta believes speech recognition is finally fast and accurate enough for this type of use. The startup has just launched a **directory** that will make these voice reviews publicly viewable, in the manner of Google Maps. Airports are already using the service. Wired sees in it a symptom of the wave of voice-centered software, alongside Wispr and Granola.

**How ChatGPT wiped Nairobi's homework industry off the map**

At the sector's peak, at the start of the decade, **at least 40,000 people** in Nairobi made a living writing essays and dissertations for American and British students, in medicine, computer science, or engineering, sometimes directly using their clients' university credentials. Teresios Bundi, 34, wrote more than 2,500 of them in twelve years, billed at 40 to 70 dollars apiece. The launch of ChatGPT in 2022 caused prices and then orders to collapse, with a contagion effect on transcription, data annotation, and content moderation for Meta. Scarcely anything remains but the "humanizer" trade, which consists of reworking AI-generated texts to fool detectors. Oxford professor Mark Graham anticipates the same shock elsewhere, among bankers, accountants, engineers, and architects.

**The Economist: the jobs apocalypse is postponed, a boom is coming**

The dominant discourse for three years has heralded massive job destruction. The data The Economist compiles tell another story: the heralded destruction does not show up in the figures, and what is being observed today is instead an AI-related hiring boom. The debate is obviously not closed, particularly on the sectoral distribution of these creations, but the article has the merit of putting data up against a prediction that circulates mostly as a conviction.

The Swiss bank will make AI proficiency a **mandatory qualification** for its graduates and interns in Global Banking and Markets starting in 2027. It will not be about checking a box on a résumé: candidates will have to demonstrate in interviews how they concretely use AI to improve their results and efficiency. Santander is also looking for advanced AI users. In the background, Morgan Stanley anticipates the disappearance of more than **200,000 banking jobs in Europe** within five years.

**An a16z partner sweeps away the fear of a "permanent underclass"**

Interviewed on Lenny Rachitsky's podcast, Anish Acharya, a partner at Andreessen Horowitz, calls a "dark fantasy" the idea that AI would create a caste of workers permanently left behind. His argument: the current boom is far less centralized than previous ones, with **around twenty relevant players at each layer** of the technology stack. He cites the market for coding agents, where Claude Code, Codex, Lovable, Replit, and Wabi compete for ground without any one sweeping the pot, and notes that job postings are holding up in occupations nonetheless reputed to be threatened, such as radiology. Sam Altman made comparable remarks in July. In San Francisco, the most tangible effect of the boom remains for now the surge in rents.

**Bernie Sanders wants to ban superintelligence by law**

Senator Bernie Sanders and Representative Greg Casar are preparing a text that would **permanently ban** the development and deployment of artificial superintelligence, and temporarily suspend work on advanced AI while waiting for a new federal regulator to set its rules. The project provides for the creation of an AI agency at cabinet level, endowed with the power to monitor cutting-edge systems, impose a model review procedure, and have capabilities deemed dangerous removed. Sanction envisaged for companies in violation: **outright dissolution**.

**Trump floods Truth Social with AI-generated images**

All of it accompanied by unsubstantiated claims about the Iranian economy and the hundreds of billions of dollars he says he has brought back to the United States, illustrated with infographics with no verifiable source. In the background, controversy is growing around Truth API, a service billed at $100,000 per month that senators have asked the SEC to investigate.

The timeline and financial terms remain subject to regulatory approval and market conditions.
