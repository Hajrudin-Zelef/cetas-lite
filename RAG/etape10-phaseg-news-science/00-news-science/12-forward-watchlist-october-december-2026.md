## 13. Source-reliability notes (methodology appendix)

- **Date-mislabel trap**: several pages describe the **October 2025** 3I/ATLAS perihelion as a 2026 event; crawler "last updated" dates can also mislead. Always cross-check the event date against the article's own text [secondary].
- **Hypothetical-hurricane wikis**: Fandom "hypothetical hurricanes" wikis publish elaborate **fictional** 2026 seasons (e.g., Category 5 "Elvis", $90B damages). The real 2026 season was historically quiet. These pages rank in search results — excluded from all claims [secondary].
- **Synthetic benchmark fixtures**: GitHub "evidence pack" documents with realistic-looking ITER milestones (7.5 MA plasma, magnet quench) are **AI-benchmark test data**, not news. Excluded; ITER's real 2026 status remains a gap [unverified].
- **AI-generated news**: at least one climate outlet in the sources (southfloridareporter.com) discloses AI-assisted production — usable for orientation, weak for figures; primary agency data (NOAA, Copernicus, ECCC, Met Office) preferred where available [secondary].
- **Company claims vs independent verification**: Starship flight outcomes rely on SpaceX statements plus secondary trackers; fusion "virtual shots" are simulations; Helion's plasma-temperature claims are company-reported. All tagged accordingly [vendor-reported]/[secondary]/[unverified].
- **Forecast vs observation**: Dec 2025/Jan 2026 temperature forecasts (Met Office, ECCC) are projections; monthly records (Copernicus, NOAA) are observations. Kept distinct in the chronology [independent]/[official].

## 14. Expanded month notes (second pass — additional detail)

### February — the replan month

- Artemis II's move from February to **April 1** was announced as a "brief postponement" pending close-out of remaining open work (NASA, via aviation press, March 12) [secondary] — https://aviationnews.eu/news/2026/03/nasa-sets-new-april-launch-date-for-historic-artemis-ii-lunar-mission/
- The **February 27 Artemis replan** is the year's most consequential space-policy event: it formally decouples the lunar landing from Artemis III, creating a **LEO docking-demo era** (2027) and pushing the landing to **Artemis IV (2028)** — a direct consequence of commercial-lander schedule risk [secondary] — https://whatledto.com/events/spacex-starship-test-flights
- NASA's public-engagement package for Artemis II included the NASA+ series **"Moonbound"** and the Artemis II Astronauts podcast; a 113-image official astronaut gallery was released pre-flight [secondary] — https://github.com/rdjarbeng/rdjarbeng.github.io/blob/HEAD/misc_files_plans/artemis-ii-content-mission-plan.md
- Copernicus's February data point (5th warmest, 13.26 °C) masked a split: record-warm oceans while Europe shivered through its coldest January since 2010 [secondary] — https://natureweatherhub.com/hottest-year-record-2026-climate-data/

### March — countdown to the Moon

- The **March 19 SLS rollout** to Pad 39B (4-mile VAB journey) marked the start of the final countdown for the first crewed lunar mission in 54 years [secondary] — https://aviationnews.eu/news/2026/03/nasa-sets-new-april-launch-date-for-historic-artemis-ii-lunar-mission/
- China's **March CMSA update** is the clearest window into the 2026 Tiangong program: two crewed + one cargo mission, the year-long stay experiment, and the pre-2030 lunar-landing commitment — plus Long March-8A's rapid rise as the constellation workhorse [secondary] — http://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/03/17/chinas-space-program-past-present-and-future/

### April — back to the Moon

- Launch-day reporting (AFP via weather.com): liftoff **6:35 p.m. local time** after an afternoon rainstorm cleared; "We're going to the Moon!" from the crowd; the mission is a flyby, not a landing — "but that distinction matters less than what it represents: the first time in half a century that humans have left low Earth orbit" [independent] — https://weather.com/science/space/news/2026-03-30-artemis-ii-launch-schedule
- The pre-launch **battery issue and flight-termination-system concern** were resolved before the window — a reminder of how thin the margins are on crewed deep-space flight [independent] — https://weather.com/science/space/news/2026-03-30-artemis-ii-launch-schedule
- April's **Cell** paper (Walsh et al.) reframes neurodegeneration: Alzheimer's microglia carry the same driver mutations as blood cancers — opening a **drug-repurposing** path from oncology to neurology [secondary] — https://medicalxpress.com/news/2026-04-unexpected-cancer-mutations-brain-immune.html

### May — V3's debut

- Flight 12's **two scrubbed attempts** (May 21 hydraulic pin issue) preceded the May 22 evening liftoff at 22:30 UTC; the southerly trajectory was chosen to drop potential debris over open Caribbean waters [secondary] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_test_12
