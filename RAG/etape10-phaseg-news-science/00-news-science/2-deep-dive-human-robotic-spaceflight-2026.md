## 2. Deep dive — Human & robotic spaceflight 2026

### 2.1 Artemis: the year the program changed shape

- The defining spaceflight story of 2026 is a **double event**: the triumphant **Artemis II crewed lunar flyby** (April 1, 2026) and the **program replan** (announced ~February 27, 2026) that redefined Artemis III as a **2027 Earth-orbit rendezvous-and-docking demo** and pushed the crewed lunar landing to **Artemis IV (2028)** [secondary] — https://whatledto.com/events/spacex-starship-test-flights
- **Artemis II mission profile**: SLS Block 1 + Orion lift off from LC-39B at 6:35 p.m. EDT April 1, 2026; ~24-hour high-elliptical Earth orbit for ECLSS checkout and the Rendezvous Proximity Operations Demonstration (RPOD) with the spent upper stage; translunar injection; lunar flyby; ~10-day total duration; Pacific splashdown [official][secondary] — https://www.nasa.gov/image-detail/artemis-ii-launch-20/ ; https://www.karmactive.com/nasa-artemis-ii-moon-mission-april-2026-launch/
- **Crew**: Reid Wiseman (commander), Victor Glover (pilot), Christina Koch (mission specialist), Jeremy Hansen (CSA, mission specialist) — first crew beyond low Earth orbit since Apollo 17 [official] — https://www.nasa.gov/image-detail/artemis-ii-launch-20/
- **Launch-day details**: rollout March 19, 2026; 2-hour window opening 6:24 p.m. EDT; 80% favorable weather; pre-launch resolution of a battery issue and a flight-termination-system concern [secondary] — https://www.karmactive.com/nasa-artemis-ii-moon-mission-april-2026-launch/ ; https://weather.com/science/space/news/2026-03-30-artemis-ii-launch-schedule
- **Why the replan matters**: the February 2026 decision acknowledges commercial-lander readiness as the pacing item. Artemis III's 2027 LEO demo will test Orion docking with **Blue Origin and SpaceX landers**; the landing itself waits on orbital refueling and lander milestones [secondary] — https://whatledto.com/events/spacex-starship-test-flights
- **Artemis III crew** was reportedly announced June 9, 2026 (Bresnik, Parmitano, Rubio, Douglas) — single weak source; treat as [unverified] — https://github.com/a2uicatalog/a2ui/blob/HEAD/examples/artemis.roadmap.md
- **Gap**: post-flight Artemis II heat-shield/reentry assessments and official NASA splashdown imagery packages were not captured by cutoff.

### 2.2 SpaceX Starship in 2026: V3's rough debut

- **Starship V3** (Block 3, 124 m tall, 33 **Raptor 3** engines, ~18M lbf thrust, ~100 t to LEO) flew for the first time on **Flight 12 (May 22, 2026)** from the new **Pad 2** at Starbase — after a May 21 scrub on a tower-arm hydraulic pin [secondary] — https://www.globalsecurity.org/space/systems/space-x-starship-012.htm ; https://biggo.com/news/202605251550_SpaceX_Starship_V3_First_Flight
- **Flight 12 outcome**: upper stage succeeded — first payload-bay opening in space (20 Starlink V3 mass simulators + 2 "Dodger Dog" inspection satellites that imaged Ship 39's heat shield from orbit — a rapid-reusability enabler); booster failed its return after engine-ignition failures post-flip, hard splashdown in the Gulf of Mexico [secondary] — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_test_12 ; https://www.tesery.com/blogs/news/a-goal-for-humanity-the-full-story-of-spacexs-epic-starship-flight-12
- **Flight 13 (~July 24, 2026)**: first deployment of **real Starlink V3 satellites**; ship reportedly splashed down intact — but a separate analysis (Sept 2026) said SpaceX had **not announced a firm Flight 13 date** and only expected it before end of summer 2026. **Conflict preserved**: either the flight happened in July or the tracker's date is wrong; FAA-window coverage leans July but is not authoritative [secondary + conflict] — https://whatledto.com/events/spacex-starship-test-flights ; https://www.urdupure.com/blog/spacex-starship-2026-why-the-next-launch-matters-more-than-another-test-flight
- **Flight 14**: Ship 41 static-fired August 20, 2026; FAA listed a **September 18** primary window; planned as the **first orbital and revenue-generating** Starship mission; ship catch deferred. Outcome by cutoff unknown [secondary] — https://whatledto.com/events/spacex-starship-test-flights
- **Artemis linkage**: NASA OIG had warned Starship delays threaten the 2028 landing target; Flights 12–13 are treated as critical-path milestones for orbital refueling and lander development [secondary] — https://www.tesery.com/blogs/news/a-goal-for-humanity-the-full-story-of-spacexs-epic-starship-flight-12
- **Industrialization signal**: SpaceX preparing a **fuel pipeline** at Starbase (Reuters "Starpipe" reporting) — trucking propellant is becoming the bottleneck, a sign the company expects launch cadence to rise sharply [secondary] — https://www.urdupure.com/blog/spacex-starship-2026-why-the-next-launch-matters-more-than-another-test-flight

### 2.3 China: the five-mission year

- CNSA's announced **2026 manifest** (December 2025 plan; statuses as of cutoff):
  - **Chang'e-7** (planned August 2026): robotic lunar-south-pole mission — orbiter + lander + rover + mini-hopping probe, targeting a permanently illuminated peak near **Shackleton crater** for water-ice prospecting [secondary] — https://dataconomy.com/2025/12/26/one-year-in-tiangong-china-to-test-long-duration-space-stay-in-2026/
  - **Tianwen-2**: launched May 2025; rendezvous with asteroid **2016 HO3** planned **July 2026**, ~100 g sample via three methods, Earth return November 2027, then onward to comet 311P/PANSTARRS (2034) [secondary] — https://dataconomy.com/2025/12/26/one-year-in-tiangong-china-to-test-long-duration-space-stay-in-2026/
  - **Tiangong**: continuous crewed presence via **Shenzhou-23 and Shenzhou-24**; one Shenzhou-22/23 crew member to attempt a **>1-year stay**; possible first Hong Kong/Macao astronaut flight [secondary] — http://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/03/17/chinas-space-program-past-present-and-future/
  - **Mengzhou-1** uncrewed test flight (mid-2026): China's next-generation crew vehicle for future lunar missions [secondary] — https://dataconomy.com/2025/12/26/one-year-in-tiangong-china-to-test-long-duration-space-stay-in-2026/
  - **Xuntian** space telescope (planned late 2026): 2-meter mirror, field of view **300x Hubble's**, co-orbiting with Tiangong for astronaut servicing [secondary] — https://dataconomy.com/2025/12/26/one-year-in-tiangong-china-to-test-long-duration-space-stay-in-2026/
- **March 2026 CMSA update** confirms two crewed + one cargo station missions for 2026 and the year-long experiment; Long March-8A becoming a core vehicle for internet-constellation deployment; Hainan commercial launch site expanding [secondary] — http://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/03/17/chinas-space-program-past-present-and-future/
- **Crewed lunar landing goal**: before 2030, reaffirmed [secondary] — http://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/03/17/chinas-space-program-past-present-and-future/
- **Gaps**: actual launch/arrival outcomes for Chang'e-7, Tianwen-2's asteroid rendezvous, Mengzhou-1, and Xuntian were **not verifiable by cutoff** — flagged [unverified].
