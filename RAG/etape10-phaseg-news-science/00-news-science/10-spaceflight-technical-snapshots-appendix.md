# 10. Spaceflight technical snapshots (appendix)

## 10. Spaceflight technical snapshots (appendix)

### 10.1 Artemis II mission profile in detail

- **Launch window**: opened 6:24 p.m. EDT, April 1, 2026; liftoff **6:35 p.m. EDT** from LC-39B [official][secondary] — https://www.nasa.gov/image-detail/artemis-ii-launch-20/ ; https://www.karmactive.com/nasa-artemis-ii-moon-mission-april-2026-launch/
- **Phase 1 — Earth orbit (~24 h)**: high-elliptical orbit for ECLSS checkout (CO₂ scrubbing, thermal regulation) and the **Rendezvous Proximity Operations Demonstration (RPOD)** — crew manually flies Orion near the spent upper stage [secondary] — https://www.karmactive.com/nasa-artemis-ii-moon-mission-april-2026-launch/
- **Phase 2 — journey to the Moon**: translunar coast, far-side flyby, free-return trajectory; **Phase 3 — return**: high-speed "skip" reentry profile (the same profile whose uneven heat-shield ablation was observed on Artemis I in 2022 and studied before Artemis II) [secondary] — https://www.karmactive.com/nasa-artemis-ii-moon-mission-april-2026-launch/ ; https://www.space.com/space-exploration/artemis/nasa-delays-artemis-2-moon-mission-to-april-2026-artemis-3-lunar-landing-to-mid-2027?ref=lite.verity.news
- **Splashdown**: Pacific Ocean off San Diego after ~10 days (reported April 10; official time not captured) [secondary] — https://github.com/a2uicatalog/a2ui/blob/HEAD/examples/artemis.roadmap.md
- **Program lineage**: Artemis I (uncrewed, Nov–Dec 2022) → Artemis II (crewed flyby, Apr 2026) → Artemis III (2027 LEO docking demo) → Artemis IV (2028 landing target) [secondary] — https://whatledto.com/events/spacex-starship-test-flights

### 10.2 Starship V1 → V3 evolution (2023–2026)

- **V1**: 120 m, ~20 t to LEO, 33× Raptor 1, ~17M lbf; first flight April 2023 (failed) [secondary] — https://biggo.com/news/202605251550_SpaceX_Starship_V3_First_Flight
- **V2**: 120 m, ~35 t to LEO, 33× Raptor 2, ~17M lbf; first flight 2025 (failed) [secondary] — https://biggo.com/news/202605251550_SpaceX_Starship_V3_First_Flight
- **V3**: **124 m**, ~100 t to LEO, 33× **Raptor 3**, ~18M lbf; first flight May 22, 2026 (mostly successful) [secondary] — https://biggo.com/news/202605251550_SpaceX_Starship_V3_First_Flight
- **Flight-test arc**: Flight 1 (Apr 2023, destroyed) → Flight 2 (Nov 2023) → Flight 5 (Oct 2024, first tower catch) → Flight 7 (Jan 2025, ship lost) → Flight 10 (Aug 2025, all objectives, first payload deployment) → Flight 12 (May 2026, V3 debut) → Flight 13 (~Jul 2026, Starlink V3) → Flight 14 (Sept 18 window, first orbital/revenue mission) [secondary] — https://whatledto.com/events/spacex-starship-test-flights
- **Recurring failure mode** (independent analysis): post-flip booster engine-ignition failures on Flights 7, 8, 9, and 12 — attitude rotation → incomplete thrust vector → lost control authority [secondary] — https://github.com/ericrenone/orbital-cordic-dual-mode-geometry-native-hardware-for-the-orbital-ai-era

### 10.3 China's human spaceflight posture in 2026

- Tiangong in **steady continuous operation**; the program's 2026 plan pairs routine crew rotation with a **year-long stay experiment** — a stepping stone toward deep-space endurance [secondary] — http://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/03/17/chinas-space-program-past-present-and-future/
- **Mengzhou-1** (next-generation crew vehicle) uncrewed test: the architectural bridge from LEO operations to the **pre-2030 crewed lunar landing** [secondary] — https://dataconomy.com/2025/12/26/one-year-in-tiangong-china-to-test-long-duration-space-stay-in-2026/
- **Chang'e-7's** south-pole water-ice survey directly feeds ILRS (International Lunar Research Station) site selection — with Chang'e-8 reserved for in-situ resource-use demos [secondary] — http://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/03/17/chinas-space-program-past-present-and-future/
- **Tianwen-2** (May 2025 launch) is China's first asteroid sample-return; the 2016 HO3 rendezvous (July 2026 plan) would make China the second nation to return asteroid samples [secondary] — https://dataconomy.com/2025/12/26/one-year-in-tiangong-china-to-test-long-duration-space-stay-in-2026/

### 10.4 Missions that watched the interstellar visitor (context, 2025 events)

- Although **3I/ATLAS's** Mars flyby (Oct 1–7, 2025) and perihelion (Oct 29–31, 2025) fall outside this phase's window, the observation campaign is documented for reference: ESA's **Mars Express** and **ExoMars TGO**, NASA's **MRO/HiRISE**, **MAVEN** (UV), rovers **Perseverance** and **Curiosity**, solar observatories **PUNCH/STEREO/SOHO**, and asteroid missions **Lucy** and **Psyche** all observed it; NASA associate administrator Amit Kshatriya publicly confirmed "this object is a comet" against alien-tech rumors [secondary] — https://www.sciencealert.com/nasa-reveals-our-closest-look-yet-at-interstellar-comet-3i-atlas
- **Why it matters for 2026**: Rubin Observatory expects **5–50 interstellar objects** over its survey — the 3I/ATLAS campaign is the template for future ISO rapid response [secondary] — https://therockinghorsecompany.com/article/vera-rubin-observatory-hunting-5-50-interstellar-comets-like-3i-atlas-in-the-next-decade
