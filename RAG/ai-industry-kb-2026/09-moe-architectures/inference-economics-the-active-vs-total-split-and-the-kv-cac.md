# Inference economics: the active-vs-total split and the KV-cache nuance

- **2023 — Mixtral 8x7B economic precedent.** 46.7B total / 12.9B active, Apache 2.0. Proved open-weight MoE could rival dense LLaMA 2 70B-class capability at ~6× the inference speed — the first public demonstration that sparse activation converts directly into $/token advantage. (Release lineage detail in sibling part §9a; economics treatment here.)
- **2024-12 — DeepSeek-V3 reference training recipe.** 671B total / 37B active. Documented production recipe: 16-way pipeline × 64-way expert × ZeRO-1 data parallel across 2048 H800 GPUs; DualPipe eliminating pipeline bubbles; DeepEP all-to-all with compute-communication overlap; FP8 mixed precision in both forward and backward passes.
- **The cleanest public anchor for MoE vs dense training cost** comes from DeepSeekMoE's published ratios: DeepSeekMoE 16B matched LLaMA2 7B with ~40% of the compute; DeepSeekMoE 145B matched DeepSeek 67B with 28.5% of the compute — roughly **2.5–3.5× compute savings at matched capability**, still the rule-of-thumb anchor used in 2026 analyses.
- **The FLOPs-vs-memory decoupling** is the structural fact behind the anchor: per-token training FLOPs scale with ACTIVE parameters, while optimizer states, gradients, and all-reduce/all-to-all communication scale with TOTAL parameters. A 428B/23B model trains at roughly dense-20B-class FLOPs per token (plus routing overhead) but requires dense-428B-class memory provisioning (optimizer states ~8× weights for Adam-style) plus EP dispatch bandwidth — TB-scale per layer at 64-way EP for V3-class models.
- **2025-12-16 — MiMo-V2-Flash pretraining scale.** Trained on 27T tokens in FP8 mixed precision — a dated datapoint for the "pretraining token budgets" series: 27T (MiMo-V2-Flash, 2025-12), 30T (GLM-5.3-Flash multimodal corpus, 2026-08), 33T/32T (DeepSeek-V4-Pro/Flash, 2026-04-24).
- **2026-02 — Unsloth MoE pipeline launch.** Headline "12x faster MoE training" [VENDOR-REPORTED, HARDWARE-SPECIFIC — top multipliers attributed to B200-class hardware in Unsloth's own MoE post; no independent reproduction found]. **2026-05-06 — Unsloth×NVIDIA collaboration blog** decomposes gains into auditable components: packed-sequence metadata caching +43.3% forward / +5.8% backward / +14.3% per batch on Qwen3-14B QLoRA SFT; double-buffered checkpoint reload +8.4% (8B) / +6.7% (14B) / +4.6% (32B); GPT-OSS MoE routing via bincount +23% forward / +13% backward in the targeted routing path.
- **2026-07 — intermediate Unsloth datapoint.** Studio v0.1.481-beta release notes claim "MoE training 3–5x faster" [VENDOR-REPORTED] — sits between the February "12x" headline and the measured component numbers; record all three with dates rather than choosing one. **2026-09-09 — MoE kernel correctness fix** (unsloth-zoo-staging PR #887): down-LoRA row-order bug in the Triton grouped-GEMM MoE forward silently added deltas to the wrong tokens; companion hardening on dtype-aware MoE backend selection (`torch._grouped_mm` is bf16-only). Evidence that Unsloth's MoE training path was still under active correctness maintenance in September 2026.
- **2026-07-27 — Kimi K3 training innovations** (primary technical report): per-head Muon optimization; Attention Residuals (AttnRes, ~25% higher training efficiency); SiTU-GLU activation with bounded activations (‖f(x)‖∞ ≤ 100) stabilizing low-precision training; RMSNorm before up-projection; reported ~2.5× scaling efficiency over Kimi K2.5 at ~3× the parameter count.
- **The fine-granularity bandwidth correction (2026).** MoE Parallel Folding (arXiv 2504.14960v2) finds fine-grained MoE has **lower training efficiency than coarse-grained MoE across tested parallelism strategies**: more experts and more active experts per token raise dispatch communication volume, shrink GEMM efficiency, increase activation memory, and widen model-parallel groups. The "fine granularity is bandwidth-free" formulation is [UNVERIFIED / CONTRADICTED AS STATED]. Bandwidth is preserved only by co-designed mitigations: DeepEP-class dispatch libraries, latent-space routing (Kimi K3's Stable LatentMoE projecting the 7,168-wide hidden state into a 3,584-wide latent space, roughly halving routed-expert activation traffic and compute), topology-aware parallelism, and single-all-reduce balancing (Kimi K3's Quantile Balancing).
- **Stability consensus (2026):** MoE training stability rated "medium" vs dense "high" (community tutorial consensus) — routing collapse, expert imbalance, EP communication instability. Standard mitigations by late 2026: DeepSeek-V3-style dynamic routing bias (no gradient interference), expert-choice routing (balanced by construction), Stable LatentMoE, QB/quantile balancing, per-head Muon. The classic auxiliary loss (L = α·N·Σ f_i·P_i) is falling out of production use; ~23% degradation from interference gradients cited in 2026 analyses.
- **Post-training, not architecture, differentiates in 2026.** Same-family controlled comparison: Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (35B/3B) at 73.4% SWE-bench Verified vs Gemma 4 26B-A4B (25.2B/3.8B) at 17.4% — similar active compute, the gap entirely training data, RL, and tool-use fine-tuning. GLM-5.3's gains over 5.2 came from post-training on an identical 753B base. DeepSeek-V4 reconstruction [PARTIALLY VERIFIED, secondary sources]: 256/384 routed experts, 6 active per token, first three blocks with deterministic token-ID hash routing instead of learned routing, retained aux-loss-free balancing plus a small sequence-wise balance loss, routing affinity changed from sigmoid to sqrt(softplus), routed expert weights reportedly deployed in FP4.
- **Fine-tuning complexity:** higher than dense — expert-specialization-aware methods (ESFT: score expert affinity on task data, fine-tune only relevant experts per layer; reported storage ↓ up to 90%, training time ↓ up to 30% vs full FT, and the finding that finer-grained experts are MORE advantageous for ESFT); shared experts partially mitigate by isolating common knowledge. Framework division of labor by late 2026 (training-systems side, mechanics in §10): Unsloth for 1–2 GPU PEFT, Axolotl for multi-GPU FSDP2/EP YAML workflows (ScatterMoE/SonicMoE LoRA, `quantize_moe_experts`, DeepEP-based EP, NVFP4 MoE LoRA, July 2026), torchtune for hackable DTensor research (FSDP2 + custom expert-parallel plan + loss parallel + context parallel), DeepSpeed for max scale (AutoEP blog 2026-02-10; MoE all-reduce penalties of 3–12 s in problematic layouts).
