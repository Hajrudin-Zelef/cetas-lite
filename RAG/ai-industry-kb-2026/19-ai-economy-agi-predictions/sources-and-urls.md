# Sources and URLs

1. **Treat the 2026 economic record as three evidence classes, never merged**: (a) *attributed forecasts* (AGI dates, agent-economy market numbers); (b) *survey/executive-belief figures* (ROI aggregation, adoption percentages); (c) *measured facts* (Sora shutdown dates, revenue run-rates, MCP download counts). The RAG must never let a class-(a) figure answer a question about realized value.
2. **The agent-economy gap is the central unresolved tension**: "1.3B agents by 2028" forecasts coexist with Gartner's 40%+ project-cancellation forecast, the 2026 benchmark trust crisis, and concrete agent-caused safety incidents (March 2026 Unit 42 campaigns; April "Comment and Control," CVSS 9.4). Forecaster consensus and measured evidence point in opposite directions.
3. **The Sora–Kling divergence is the most important 2026 datapoint for AI economics**: the only consumer video product that died was the one with the worst reported unit economics; survivors monetize B2B/API (Kling, Seedance), aggregate others' models (Firefly), or gate behind expensive subscriptions (Genie at $249.99/mo). Cross-ref: §1-5 for model capabilities; this section's economics are about *viability*, not capability.
4. **The capex story moved from "train bigger" to "serve cheaper"**: CES 2026's inference-spend inversion forecast, Sora's compute reallocation, and agentic-video-understanding efficiency gains (−88% tokens, Google AVU Sept 2026) all point the same direction. [DIRECTIONAL]
5. **Narrative-aware reading is a methodology, not cynicism**: the audit identified a repeating three-step cycle (event-narrative loop → attention market → softening cycle) that lets a reader date *when* a forecast was made and *why then*. A 2026 AGI-timeline claim from Davos-week and one from the Sept audit month are different evidence objects.
6. **Labor-displacement predictions from labs are position-taking, not measurement**: the May 2026 walkbacks (Altman, Amodei, Dario) all followed real job numbers; the Newslaundry op-ed's attention-market mechanism explains both the loud Jan–Feb framing and the quiet May retreat. For the RAG: attribute every jobs claim to its speaker, date, and the economic numbers available at that date.
7. **Coding-agent revenues are the closest thing to a measured agent-economy datapoint**: Devin $492M→$900M run-rate in four months and Cursor ~$4B annualized (both vendor-reported, unaudited) are actual money changing hands for agentic products — keep them labeled [VENDOR] and keep them in the measured column, with the caveat that "run-rate" is a vendor metric.
8. **Dedup boundary**: funding rounds (Cognition, Cursor, LangGraph-era fundraising) belong to §20; model capability claims belong to §1–5 (this section references them only as context for economics). Benchmark scores are cross-referenced, not relitigated here.
9. **2026 is the year "AGI forecast" became a distinct content genre with its own trackers** — skynetcountdown.com and 2agi.me normalize statements across blogs, interviews, and second-hand quotes. The RAG treats these as secondary attributions: useful for the narrative record, not primary evidence of what any executive actually said.
10. **The narrative-beats calendar doubles as a dating tool**: any 2026 claim about "the AI revolution" can be placed on the Jan→Sep calendar to see which phase produced it — performed revolution (Jan–Feb), economic rupture (Mar), softening (Apr–May), measurement turn (Jul–Aug), audit (Sept). Claims sourced from different phases are different evidence objects even when they use identical vocabulary.
11. **The labor thread has an unresolved tension the RAG should not paper over**: the May 2026 walkbacks (strong job numbers, Jevons Paradox) happened while enterprise agentic deployments (Wolters Kluwer 44%, finance teams) were accelerating. "More employment, not less" and "44% of finance teams deploying agents" are compatible only under a productivity-absorption model — which Amodei's Jevons Paradox framing supplies, but no one in the 2026 record measured. [DIRECTIONAL]
12. **Procurement-grade reading of §19**: if a reader uses this knowledge base to evaluate a vendor claim (e.g., "4.3× ROI," "1.3B agents by 2028"), the provenance column is the answer — class (a) forecasts and class (b) surveys are not evidence of realized value, and no 2026 source in this corpus provides an independent class-(c) audit of enterprise agentic ROI. The honest answer to "is the agent economy real?" is: the invoices are real (coding agents), the forecasts are loud, and the gap between them is the story.
13. **Watch the checkable dates**: Altman (AGI 2026–2028; automated researcher by March 2028), Amodei (powerful AI late 2026–2027), and Musk (individual parity end-2026) all have falsifiable horizons within ~18 months of the 2026-09-22 writing date. Future waves of this knowledge base should record hits and misses against these dates rather than refreshing the forecast list — the 2026 audit's purpose was attribution, and the next pass's purpose is accounting.
14. **The September audit month is a recurring pattern, not a one-off**: every 2026 narrative phase ended with an accounting beat (Sora's economics in March, job numbers in May, the benchmark audit in July). The revolution narrative survives each accounting by re-pricing — from existential stakes to enterprise plumbing to "enterprise plumbing with existential seasoning." The RAG's job is to keep the receipts for each phase so the re-pricing is visible.
