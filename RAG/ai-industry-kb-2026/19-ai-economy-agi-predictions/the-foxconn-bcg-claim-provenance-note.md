### The Foxconn/BCG claim — provenance note

- The single largest "measured value" figure in the 2026 agent-economy corpus ($800M, 80% of decision workflows) comes from a World Economic Forum release cited via PYMNTS (Jan 20, 2026) — i.e., from the event's own communications, reporting a vendor partnership (Foxconn + BCG). No independent audit was found in this pass.
- Its function in the narrative: Davos's "production value over prototypes" theme needed an on-stage number, and the Foxconn/BCG figure supplied it. The claim is evidence of *the event's framing needs*, not of measured enterprise value. Kept here with [VENDOR] marks because it is the figure most likely to be quoted downstream as proof that the agent economy arrived.

### Gartner's 40%/40% — the two halves belong together

- **Half 1 (quoted)**: 40% of business software will include AI capable of completing tasks independently by end-2026. This is the figure that appears in vendor decks and trend pieces.
- **Half 2 (dropped)**: 40%+ of enterprise agentic-AI projects will be **canceled by 2027** due to escalating costs, unclear business value, and inadequate risk controls. This is the figure the same firm's research produced — and the one that rarely survives syndication.
- The two halves are one finding, not two: the same enterprise buyer base that is being sold task-completing agents is projected to cancel nearly half of its agentic projects within a year of buying them. The audit keeps both halves adjacent because the narrative machinery systematically separates them (the event-narrative loop selects the optimistic half; the warning half has no vendor incentive to propagate).
- [DIRECTIONAL] The 40%-cancellation figure is the strongest single datapoint for the "forecasts-heavy, measurement-thin" verdict on the 2026 agent economy — it is a forecast *against* the forecast layer, from the same forecaster.

- **May 2026 softening wave** — after months of accelerationist framing, visible walkbacks:
  - Altman told reporters he was **"delighted to be wrong" about the jobs apocalypse**, citing strong job numbers.
  - Amodei, at a JPMorgan/Dimon briefing, invoked the **Jevons Paradox** — AI making labor more productive means *more* employment, not less.
  - Dario quoted in a newsletter summary: **"AGI isn't going to destroy jobs, it'll help companies grow."**
- **Sept 21, 2026 Newslaundry op-ed** — argues the mechanism directly: both the doomers and the accelerationists **monetize attention**; "the men building AI want us to fear it — we should, but not for the reasons they give." The "revolution" framing is market positioning, not measurement. (source: newslaundry.com/2026/09/21)

### The May 2026 softening — mechanics, not just quotes

- The walkbacks share a trigger: real employment data contradicted the displacement narrative, and enterprise adoption metrics gave the speakers a new story to tell.
  - **Altman** cited strong job numbers directly when telling reporters he was "delighted to be wrong" about the jobs apocalypse.
  - **Amodei** reframed at a **JPMorgan/Dimon briefing**: the Jevons Paradox — AI making labor more productive means *more* employment, not less. The venue matters: a bank CEO briefing, not a tech conference.
  - **Dario**'s line ("AI won't destroy jobs, it'll help companies grow") was quoted in a **newsletter summary** — third-hand, but consistent with the other two in timing and direction.
- The pattern generalizes: doom-and-boom forecasts got quieter as enterprise adoption metrics (ROI surveys, the 44% finance-teams figure, MCP production numbers) became the story. The revolution narrative does not die; it re-prices — from existential stakes to enterprise plumbing.
- [DIRECTIONAL] The softening cycle is the third mechanism of the audit's narrative model (event-narrative loop → attention market → softening cycle), and May 2026 is its cleanest 2026 instance.
