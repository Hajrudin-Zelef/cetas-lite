# 22. Robotics
Keywords: humanoid robots 2026, Figure 03, BotQ, Helix 02, BMW Spartanburg, Tesla Optimus, 1X Neo, Redwood AI, Agility Robotics, Digit v5, SPAC merger, Unitree IPO, STAR Market, AgiBot Zhiyuan, UBTech Walker S2, EngineAI T800, robot combat, teleoperation, Skild AI, Physical Intelligence, Gemini Robotics, NVIDIA Isaac, robot shipments, SoftBank ABB Robotics, demo versus deployment, paid deployment, Hugging Face robotics

## Summary

2026 split humanoid robotics into two narratives that must not be conflated: a shipments story dominated by China, and a deployment story that remains narrow, teleop-assisted, and audit-light. [DIRECTIONAL] On the shipments side, the global humanoid market shipped somewhere between ~19,100 (Smart Analytics Global) and >22,000 (Counterpoint Research) units in H1 2026, with Chinese vendors accounting for >97% of the vendor-origin share under Smart Analytics Global's tracker definition. AgiBot/Zhiyuan led both trackers (~8,400 / ~9,700), followed by Unitree (~5,900 / >7,000), then Galbot, UBTech, and Leju in the same order across both sources. The denominator is contested: the Humanoid Robot Scene Application Alliance estimated >30,000 H1 shipments in China alone (>18,000 bipedal + >12,000 wheeled), while the China Humanoid Robotics and Embodied Intelligence Committee of 100 put China's H1 volume above 40,000. Humanoid Analytics (August 2026) concludes no single global "sold" number is defensible — trackers differ on bipedal vs. wheeled, vendor-origin vs. customer-location, and shipment vs. recognized sale. The application split is directly contradictory: SAG reports industrial and commercial uses above 70% of H1 shipments, while Counterpoint assigns >60% to entertainment/performance, data production, and research, with intelligent manufacturing at 13% and warehousing/logistics at 5%. These taxonomies are incompatible and must not be averaged or blended.

Unitree's own STAR Market IPO filing is the evidentiary anchor of the period. The company reported 5,716 produced / 5,511 shipped / 5,215 sold humanoids in 2025 (pure humanoids; wheeled dual-arm models excluded), a 296-unit ship-vs.-sold gap from revenue recognition, RMB 867.8M in humanoid robot revenue at an average recognized price of RMB 166,400 per unit, and — most importantly — a January–September 2025 revenue mix of **73.6% research and education**, 17.39% commercial/consumer, and 9.01% industry. Of the small industry slice, only RMB 15.702M (29.29%) came from clearly operational scenarios (manufacturing, inspection, logistics). SemiAnalysis (June 2026) estimated ≤250 Unitree humanoids were in genuinely productive industrial pilots or deployments during 2025, noting many deployments remain software-limited and heavily teleoperated. This is the central RAG discipline of this chapter: **shipments are not sales, and sales are not productive deployment**.

The single largest capital event was Unitree's **IPO on Shanghai's STAR Market on 2026-08-19**: ~40.45 million shares at RMB 150.80 (10% float), an issue valuation of ~RMB 61B (~$9B), with the stock spiking ~600% intraday and closing ~460% up for a ~$51B market cap. The RMB 6.1B (~$904M) raise must not be conflated with the ~$51B debut market cap. On the Western side, 2026 was about real-but-narrow commercial traction and manufacturing ramps. Figure AI reached roughly one Figure 03 per hour at its BotQ facility (reported as a 24× ramp in 120 days), built its 1,000th Figure 03 on 2026-07-26, and reported ~740 robots deployed against 660 employees. Agility Robotics announced a **$2.5B SPAC merger with Churchill Capital Corp XI on 2026-06-24** (expected ~$620M gross including a $200M Foxconn PIPE, ticker AGLT, the first pure-play humanoid listing on a US exchange), backed by $300M in contracted Digit v5 orders [VENDOR] and 65,000+ operating hours [VENDOR]. 1X began consumer home deliveries of Neo — the first consumer humanoid deployment, at $20,000 purchase or $499/month, with disclosed teleoperation as its training mechanism. Tesla converted Fremont Model S/X floor space into a dedicated Optimus line (1M-unit/yr **design capacity**, not production), certified three Chinese mass-production suppliers in September 2026, and faces supply-chain reports of ~1,000 units/week by late September ramping to 2,000–2,500/week by year-end [SECONDARY/DIRECTIONAL] — but Musk said in January 2026 that Optimus was "not in usage in our factories in a material way," and 2025 guidance (≤10,000 units in 2025 per the Q4 2024 call) was missed several-fold (Omdia via AP: fewer than 500 shipped in 2025). His dates carry a poor record and must be discounted accordingly.

The "robot brain" thesis dominated venture funding: Skild AI raised **$1.4B at $14B+** on 2026-01-14 (SoftBank-led); Physical Intelligence reportedly negotiated ~**$1B at >$11B** on 2026-03-27, nearly doubling its $5.6B Series B valuation (November 2025) in four months. Google DeepMind's **Gemini Robotics 1.5 was announced 2025-09-25 — it is not a 2026 release**; no 2026 DeepMind robotics model release was found in this pass (flagged as a coverage gap, not absence). NVIDIA's 2026 robotics activity centered on the Isaac Root research platform (Computex 2026-06-01, Unitree H2 as flagship hardware) and the Halos safety architecture (first humanoid integration: Agility Digit v5, announced June 2026). On safety and spectacle, EngineAI's T800 knocked out human creator Frankie LaPenna in a San Francisco cage on 2026-09-18 — but the T800 was **remote-piloted**, per REK's own disclosures (TMZ), making it a person beating a person using a machine body, not an autonomy milestone. LaPenna reportedly suffered a hand/wrist injury — the one verified 2026 human injury from robot combat. SoftBank's $5.4B acquisition of ABB Robotics was announced in **October 2025**, not 2026; it is included because the close is expected mid-to-late 2026 and the strategic framing (Masayoshi Son's "Physical AI") matters for industrial humanoids.

**Robotics funding rounds are consolidated in §20**; only one-line cross-references appear here. Hugging Face robotics (HopeJR and the Reachy Mini refresh unveiled 2026-09-01) is covered in **§16** — cross-referenced only, not duplicated. Apptronik, Sanctuary AI, Boston Dynamics, Amazon warehouse robotics, and Covariant/Dexterity yielded no material 2026 findings in this pass and are flagged as thin-coverage gaps, not findings.

The year's commercial shape can be summarized in one sentence per actor: Figure proved a humanoid can be manufactured at robot-per-hour pace and paid for in logistics; Agility proved robots-as-a-service can bill real totes and is being capitalized as a public company; 1X proved a consumer will take a humanoid into a home; Unitree proved the capital markets will pay $51B for the story; and EngineAI proved a teleoperated robot can knock a man down on television. What none of them proved in 2026 is unsupervised, at-scale, economically decisive humanoid labor — the productive deployments of the entire industry fit inside a single warehouse. The BMW Spartanburg whistleblower suit (filed November 2025, outcome unknown) is the liability instrument through which that claim will eventually be tested: if a safety failure in a humanoid pilot produces a regulator's judgment rather than a press release, the industry's deployment timelines reprice. Until then, the discipline stands: classify every claim, trust filings over keynotes, and read "deployed" as "shipped to a pilot" unless the invoice says otherwise.
