## Summary

As of September 22, 2026, exactly **two** AI-adjacent companies have actually **priced** public offerings in 2026. Neither is a frontier AI lab: **Cerebras (CBRS, Nasdaq)** priced at **$185 on May 13, 2026** and debuted **May 14**, raising **~$5.55B** with a first-day pop of +68%; **SpaceX (SPCX, Nasdaq)** priced at **$135 on June 11, 2026** and debuted **June 12**, raising **$75B at a ~$1.77T valuation** — the **largest IPO in history**, more than double Saudi Aramco's $29.4B (2019) record, closing day one at $160.65 (+19%) above a $2T market cap. A third priced candidate entered the pipeline in the final days of this research: London neocloud **Nscale filed a public S-1 around September 18, 2026** (NYSE: NSCL), targeting **up to $3B at ~$30–35B** — **filed, not priced**; it may become 2026's third priced AI-adjacent IPO if the pricing window completes.

The two frontier labs are **filed but not priced**, and their trajectories have diverged. **Anthropic** announced a **confidential S-1 on June 1, 2026** — four days after closing a **$65B Series H at $965B** (May 28) — and, per Reuters (Sept 4), is targeting a **public prospectus late September**, a **mid-October roadshow**, and a listing **days before the November 3, 2026 US midterm elections**, with a **$15B revolving credit facility** being finalized alongside it. **OpenAI** confirmed its **confidential S-1 on June 8, 2026** [VENDOR] — but CEO **Sam Altman** told Fortune on **September 12, 2026** that a 2026 listing is **explicitly ruled out**: "I would say not 2026, yeah." CFO **Sarah Friar** had already told an all-hands on **August 19** that OpenAI expects to be public in **2027**. One digest dates the OpenAI confidential filing to **May 22** — flag as [UNVERIFIED]; the company-announced, widely reported date is **June 8**.

**xAI never IPO'd independently**: SpaceX acquired xAI in an **all-stock $1.25T merger announced February 2, 2026** (xAI ~$250B, SpaceX ~$1T), so the SpaceX IPO is the de-facto xAI public listing. **CoreWeave (CRWV)** has been public since its March 2025 IPO — its 2026 activity is follow-on financing only, including a **$3B convertible debt sale announced September 17, 2026** (Reuters). **Nebius (NBIS)** is public with no 2026 IPO — only serial convertible and equity raises, the latest a **$5.75B upsized convertible closed ~August 19–26, 2026**; the stock was **$232.80, +178% YTD** as of September 22. **Discord** filed a **confidential S-1 in January 2026** but its March debut target passed with no public registration — **filed, stalled**. The rest of the rumored queue — **Databricks, Figure AI, Glean, Cohere, Perplexity, Groq, Scale AI, Lambda, Crusoe** — had **filed nothing** as of September 22, 2026; most are explicitly 2027+ or 2028 candidates.

### Reading the pipeline: filed vs priced vs trading vs stalled

A confidential S-1 is a registration-draft review with the SEC, not a commitment to list: it buys optionality. Pricing sets the offer price and share count (the capital event); debut is first trading; trading covers everything after. "Stalled" means a filing exists with no forward progress and missed targets. The 2026 AI pipeline thus decomposes into: **priced and trading (2)** — Cerebras, SpaceX; **publicly filed, not priced (1)** — Nscale; **confidentially filed, not priced (3)** — Anthropic (active), OpenAI (deferred to 2027), Discord (stalled); **not filed (the long queue)** — everyone else. SEC mechanics matter to the calendar: the registration statement must be public at least 15 days before a roadshow, so OpenAI's non-appearance on EDGAR as of September 10, 2026 made any Q4 2026 listing procedurally impossible — consistent with Altman's September 12 statement.

### The macro shape of the 2026 story

The defining feature of 2026's AI public markets is that the capital event moved offstage: the year's largest valuations were printed in **private** rounds (Anthropic $965B, OpenAI $852B, xAI $230B, Databricks $134B — see §20), and the IPOs that actually priced are infrastructure businesses (inference silicon; a satellite-telecom/AI-buildout company that happened to acquire an AI lab). Public-market appetite in 2026 has been for AI *infrastructure* revenue, not lab economics — which makes Nscale's S-1, with its $1.02B H1 loss on $140.6M revenue, the stress test of that thesis. Anthropic's filing is the furthest advanced of the labs and sets the template for how a frontier lab reports financials publicly; per PitchBook's Harrison Rolfes, OpenAI may benefit from watching how investors price Anthropic first.

### The capital-event migration: where the money actually moved in 2026

Add up the year's AI capital formation and the public/private split is stark. **Private:** Anthropic $65B, OpenAI $122B [COMMUNITY], xAI $20B, Databricks (Series L at $134B), Crusoe $3.9B, Etched $700M — trillion-scale marks set without a single public filing. **Public IPOs priced:** Cerebras ~$5.55B, SpaceX $75B. **Public follow-ons:** Nebius ~$9B+ in converts plus a $1B equity sale, CoreWeave $3B converts + $2.92B ATM program. The public market's 2026 AI intake (~$95B across IPOs and follow-ons) is smaller than the private intake — and almost all of it is infrastructure balance-sheet funding, not lab equity. The implication for 2027: the labs' eventual listings are *liquidity* events for existing holders at private marks, not capital raises — the underwriting question is whether public buyers accept the private price, which is exactly the referendum Anthropic's pre-midterms window is designed to run first.

### Pricing mechanics and deal terms worth noting

- **Cerebras priced above range** — $185 vs a $150–160 marketed range — the classic signal of an oversubscribed book; the +68% day-one close confirmed demand, and the subsequent ~30% fade to the $237 range was textbook post-IPO hangover (lock-up fears, profit-taking), not a fundamental re-rating: Q1 beat the consensus two months later.
- **SpaceX priced fixed, take-it-or-leave-it** — $135/share, no marketed range — the issuer dictated terms; the ~4× oversubscription (~$250B demand) and 1.8M day-one options contracts (vs Meta's 364K in 2012) show the book absorbed it. The ~30% retail allocation (~$22.5B via Schwab, Fidelity, Robinhood, SoFi, E-Trade) is an order of magnitude above the typical 5–10% — a deliberate Musk-era retail-participation design.
- **Greenshoes:** Cerebras 4.5M shares (~$6.4B total proceeds potential); SpaceX 83.3M shares (~$11.2B) — both standard 15% over-allotments, both indicative of books that could take more.
- **Lockups as overhang calendar:** Musk's 366-day SpaceX lockup expires ~June 2027; Cerebras's standard 180-day lockup expired ~November 2026 — both are the dates when post-IPO selling pressure historically concentrates.
- **Control discounts and premiums:** Musk's ~82.4% voting control makes SPCX a controlled company with Nasdaq governance exemptions — a known valuation discount factor for governance-sensitive holders, evidently not binding on the June book. Cerebras has no such overhang; its OpenAI 750MW (>$20B) multi-year deal and AWS partnership are the demand-side anchors analysts cite (Morgan Stanley Overweight/$250; Citigroup $340; Needham Buy).
- **What "not priced" costs in practice:** Anthropic's $15B revolver (Morgan Stanley leading; Goldman, JPMorgan, Citi prominent) is pre-IPO balance-sheet ballast — the kind of facility that lets an issuer price into volatility rather than wait for it. OpenAI has no such disclosed facility; its deferral to 2027 is the alternative strategy — wait for the window rather than armor the balance sheet.
