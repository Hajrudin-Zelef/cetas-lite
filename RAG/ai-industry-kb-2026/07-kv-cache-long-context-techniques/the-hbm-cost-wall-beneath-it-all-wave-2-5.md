# The HBM cost wall beneath it all (Wave 2, §5)

- By September 2026, **1M-token input context is standard on flagship releases from every major lab** — but "the norm" does not mean universal; the claim must be qualified per product and deployment tier.
- Dated evidence: **Gemini 3.1 Pro** (2026-02-19, preview): 1M input / 64–66K output. **DeepSeek V4-Pro / V4-Flash** (2026-04-24; some sources say 04-23 — immaterial): 1M context; V4-Pro = 1.6T total / ~49B active, V4-Flash = 284B total / 13B active, both under the **MIT license**; at 1M tokens, V4-Pro uses **27% of the inference FLOPs and 10% of the KV-cache size of V3.2** (vendor); V4-Flash pushes to 10% FLOPs / 7% KV cache (vendor), via CSA+HCA derived from the DSA lineage. **Claude Opus 4.8** (2026-05-28): 1M / 128K — consistently reported in secondary sources (no Anthropic primary located; **Microsoft Foundry may cap Opus 4.8 at 200K** on some tiers). **GLM-5.2** (~2026-06-13 subscriber/API, open weights ~06-17): 1M input / 65K output. **NVIDIA Nemotron-3-Ultra-550B-A55B** (June 2026; arXiv:2606.15007): Mamba-2 + MoE + Attention hybrid ("LatentMoE"), MTP, 1M context, native NVFP4 distribution, OpenMDW-1.1. **GPT-5.6 family**: 1.05M, August 2026 (with a pricing cut).
- **GLM-5.2 IndexShare (VERIFIED, June 2026)**: a single sparse-attention indexer **shared across every four sparse-attention layers**, yielding a **2.9× reduction in per-token FLOPs at 1M context** and up to **20% improved MTP speculative-decoding acceptance length**; efficiency figures remain **vendor-reported** [VENDOR]. (ArXiv:2603.12201 underlies the design; indexing mechanism detail with part 07a's sparse-attention coverage.)
- Open-weight 1M evidence: **MiniMax M3** (2026-06-01): 1M-token window with a **512K guaranteed usable floor**; **GLM-5.3-Flash** (2026-08-26, MIT, 320B/18B): 1M context on a single 8-GPU node; DeepSeek-V4 lineage MIT-licensed. Artificial Analysis (June 2026) ranked M3 #1 among open-weight models on its Intelligence Index (score 55).
- **The qualifiers that keep the "1M is the new norm" claim honest**: (1) **Qwen3.6-35B-A3B** (April 2026, Gated-DeltaNet + Gated-Attention + MoE, MTP, Apache-2.0) ships at **256K**, not 1M; (2) platform/tier caps break the 1M claim in specific deployments (Foundry 200K for Opus 4.8); (3) **Gemini 3.5 is [UNVERIFIED]** — no citable source for the model or its context window; (4) most published full-1M needle-in-haystack scores are vendor-run; independent NIH-at-1M replication remains rare across all vendors, and vendor NIH-at-1M claims should be treated as directional. The honest formulation: 1M-token input is the **flagship-tier norm** among leaders as of September 2026, not a universal product reality.
- **DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA)** — V3.2 (Dec 2025): a learned "lightning indexer" selects a top-K subset (commonly **2,048 tokens**) for sparse attention; SGLang shipped an implementation including FP8 Q8KV8 sparse-MLA prefill (PR #30514). Crucial nuance for this part: **DSA reduces attention *compute*; it does not intrinsically shrink the KV cache**, because any historical token may be selected by the indexer later — the cache must retain full-fidelity KVs (or rely on separate compression like V4's CSA/HCA, which V4 does). Secondary sources sometimes conflate the two. (Full sparse-attention mechanism coverage sits in part 07a.)

### The HBM cost wall beneath it all (Wave 2, §5)

- Market structure: a three-supplier oligopoly — **SK Hynix ~53%, Samsung ~38%, Micron ~9%** — with SK Hynix holding pricing power through HBM3e yield leadership (Samsung reportedly 6–9 months behind). SK Hynix posted a **72% operating margin in Q1 2026** and filed for a reported **$28B US IPO**. The oligopoly has killed the price-war mechanism (1998: ~15 DRAM suppliers; today: 3); contracts have been rewritten in suppliers' favor (volume locked, prices floating upward; buyers financing supplier capex or paying large deposits).
- 2026 price action: **HBM prices surged ~40% in Q3 2026** (AI Cost Estimator), on top of **DRAM and HBM contract prices nearly doubling quarter-over-quarter in Q1 2026** (Counterpoint Research). Samsung and SK Hynix raised **HBM3e supply prices ~20% for 2026 contracts**; Samsung reportedly raised **HBM4 logic-die prices 40–50%** since early 2026. Researchers forecast memory prices climbing up to **130% for the full year 2026**; Samsung is reportedly fulfilling only ~60% of demand. Counter-view (Goldman Sachs, early 2026): a double-digit HBM price decline "next year" if new capacity (Samsung P5, SK Hynix M15X, targeting 2027–2028) lands into oversupply.
- Physical ceilings, not just prices: 2026 monthly HBM capacity of ~150–170k (SK Hynix) + ~110–130k (Samsung) + ~50–60k (Micron) 12-Hi-equivalent wafers → **~900M–1B 12-Hi stacks/year**, i.e. **~7.5–8M Rubin-class GPU shipments at 12 stacks/GPU — the physical 2026 ceiling**. Against this: top-5 hyperscaler 2026 capex of ~$680–740B, with HBM at **50–60% of GPU BOM** implying ~$100–150B of HBM-side demand vs ~$80–100B of industry HBM revenue — **demand exceeds supply by ~20–50%**. Industry consensus: **sold out through 2026–2027, tight through 2028**. Supply has two independent ceilings (wafer output *and* TSMC CoWoS packaging, 6–9 month lead times); SK Group chairman Chey Tae-won stated (Mar 2026) the wafer shortage likely persists until 2030; new fab capacity takes 4–5 years.
- Structural new demand created by the inference economy: (1) **DRAM** — AI inference KV cache must stay resident in DRAM throughout each session ("a structural new demand class"); (2) **NAND** — AI agents running long chain-of-thought workflows must dump state to disk ("NAND is now an active inference participant, not passive storage"). HBM consumes **≥20% of total global DRAM wafer output in 2026** (+70% YoY HBM demand). Consumer spillover is priced in: smartphones +14% in 2026, average handset **$523** (IDC record), sub-$100 phones "effectively disappearing."
- Economic frame: every KV-cache technique in this part — 7.43× reuse, 100–1,000× RDMA prefill restoration, 2× FP8, 3.56× FP4, 8–10× hybrid KV cuts — is **HBM-demand destruction**, not optimization: each gigabyte of KV cache not stored or not recomputed is HBM capacity not bought in a market sold out through 2028.
