# 7. KV Cache & Long-Context Techniques
Keywords: KV cache, long context, MLA, Multi-head Latent Attention, GQA, attention variants, HybridKV, TurboQuant, KV quantization, FP8 KV cache, FP4 KV cache, sparse attention, MiniMax Sparse Attention, DeepSeek Sparse Attention, DSA, Mamba-2, Gated DeltaNet, hybrid architectures, token eviction, reasoning-aware compression, DeepSeek-V2, Gemma 4, Kimi K2, GLM-5, Qwen3-Next, low-rank compression, PolarQuant, FlashMLA, needle-in-haystack

## Summary

The KV (key-value) cache remains the dominant bottleneck of LLM inference as of 2026-09-22: every decode step reads the entire cache, so decode is memory-bandwidth-bound while prefill (prompt processing) is compute-bound. KV-cache memory per request follows `KV = 2 (K+V) × num_layers × num_kv_heads × head_dim × seq_len × bytes_per_element` — linear in sequence length, head count, layer count, and precision. Reference point: Llama 3 70B (80 layers, GQA-8, head_dim 128) holds ~13.4 GB of KV cache per request at 4K context in BF16; at 128K it is ~42 GB per request, at 1M ~336 GB in BF16 — no single lever fits that on one 80 GB GPU, so 2026 is the year of stacking.

The 2026 state of the art attacks the wall on six fronts, all covered in this part:

1. **Attention variants baked into model weights.** GQA is the quiet industry default (~88% reduction vs MHA at >95% quality, Llama 3 / Mistral / Gemma / Qwen / MiMo families, typically 2:1 to 8:1 query-to-KV ratios). MLA (DeepSeek's Multi-Head Latent Attention, arXiv:2405.04434, May 2024) compresses along the *feature* axis instead: each token's K/V is compressed into a low-dimensional latent vector (typically 512 dims) plus a shared decoupled RoPE key (64 dims), reconstructed on the fly via up-projection (the "absorption trick"). Measured: **93.3% KV-cache reduction** vs standard MHA (DeepSeek-V2), **5.76× inference throughput**, quality matching or slightly beating MHA. At least eight model families besides DeepSeek adopted MLA by September 2026 (Kimi K2 series, GLM-5, GLM-5.3). MLA is a one-way architectural door: it must be baked in at pretraining and cannot be retrofitted onto a trained MHA/GQA checkpoint.
2. **Cross-layer sharing and hybrid attention layouts.** Gemma 4 (shipped 2026-04-02, Apache 2.0) introduced a shared KV cache: later layers reuse KV computed by earlier layers (implemented in llama.cpp as `n_kv_shared_layers`). Combined with interleaved local sliding-window + global attention (every ~6th layer global), most of the cache becomes O(1) in context length; only global layers are O(n). Gemma 4 31B at 256K context: ~40.8 GiB BF16, ~20.4 GiB with FP8 KV.
3. **Sparse attention (compute-selective, not necessarily cache-shrinking).** MiniMax M3 (June 1, 2026) uses MiniMax Sparse Attention (MSA): a two-stage sparse attention with a lightweight index branch selecting relevant KV blocks — vendor-reported >9× prefill and >15× decode speedups at 1M tokens, 28.4× less per-token attention compute, operating on *uncompressed* KV to preserve retrieval fidelity. DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA, DeepSeek-V3.2, Dec 2025) uses a learned "lightning indexer" selecting a top-K subset (2,048 tokens). Critical nuance carried through this part: **DSA/MSA reduce attention compute; they do not automatically shrink the KV cache**, because any historical token may be selected later — the cache must retain full-fidelity KVs unless separate compression (e.g. V4's CSA/HCA) is applied.
4. **Recurrent/SSM layers with O(1) per-layer state, paired with MoE.** 2026's converged design is hybrid-by-ratio: recurrent layers hold fixed-size state (O(1) in context length), attention layers keep exact recall. Shipped evidence: NVIDIA Nemotron-3-Ultra-550B-A55B (June 2026, "LatentMoE" = Mamba-2 + MoE + Attention, 1M context), Qwen3.6-35B-A3B (April 2026, Gated-DeltaNet + Gated-Attention + MoE, 256K), Qwen3-Next (Gated DeltaNet layers carry zero KV; recurrent state ~0.02 GiB), Qwen3.5-MoE (Feb 2026), IBM Granite-4.0-H-Small (Mamba-2 hybrid MoE). Jamba-1.5: 4–9 GB KV at 256K vs 32–80 GB for pure transformers (~10× lower). Terminology correction held throughout: O(n²) is attention *compute* (prefill); KV *memory* is O(n); hybrids make per-layer *state* O(1).
5. **Post-hoc KV compression.** HybridKV (VERIFIED: arXiv:2604.05887, 2026-04-07; ACL 2026 long paper) — head classification (static vs dynamic), top-down budget allocation, differentiated compression (text-prior pruning for static heads, chunk-wise retrieval for dynamic heads): up to **7.9× KV-cache memory reduction** (best case across 11 multimodal benchmarks on Qwen2.5-VL-7B), 1.52× faster decoding. TurboQuant (Google Research invention, arXiv:2504.19874, ICLR 2026, announced 2026-03-25) — 3-bit keys / 2-bit values via PolarQuant rotation + QJL unbiased residual correction: **6× memory reduction**, up to **8× attention-logit speedup on H100**, 99.5% attention fidelity, lossless on needle-in-haystack. Note: Red Hat published an *independent vLLM evaluation* (2026-05-11); Red Hat is not the inventor — the method is Google's.
6. **Quantized KV in production serving.** FP8 (E4M3) KV is the 2026 production serving choice: vLLM `--kv-cache-dtype fp8 | fp8_e4m3 | fp8_e5m2`; SGLang documents the broadest matrix — `fp8_e5m2 | fp8_e4m3 | fp4_e2m1 | nvfp4 | fp4_mx_block16 | int8 | int4 | int2` (FP4 experimental). DeepSeek's own FlashMLA (Feb 2025, custom 656 bytes/token FP8 KV) is the origin point; SGLang FlashMLA+FP8-KV+MTP (May 2025, PR #6109) measured ~30% speedup with KV halved; SemiAnalysis measured DeepSeek-R1 NVFP4 on B200 at 907 tok/s/GPU (Dec 2025–Jan 2026). **FP4/NVFP4 remain Blackwell/SM100-native or experimental paths — do not overclaim them as general production defaults.** INT4 KV quality is method- and setup-dependent.

The wider 2026 compression zoo is inventoried in full: token-eviction methods (H2O, StreamingLLM attention sinks, SnapKV, PyramidKV/PyramidInfer), quantization methods (KIVI 2-bit, KVQuant calibrated sub-4-bit evaluated to 10M tokens, FP8 KV), and low-rank/latent methods (Palu, LoRC, SVDq, CSKV, ReCalKV, plus the "Thin Keys, Full Values" retrofit giving 75% key-cache savings without pretraining). The 2026 frontier has moved to **reasoning-aware compression** of the reasoning trace itself (ThinKV: <5% KV cache, 5.8× throughput, ICLR 2026 Oral; R-KV: ~100% of full-KV performance at 10% of the cache, 6.6× throughput) and the older error-correction canon (GEAR's X ≈ D̂ + L + S, MiKV's runtime Dynamic Outlier Awareness, ResQ, WKVQuant, ZipCache/PrefixQuant/MiniKV, LESS) that underlies it. A cautionary 2026 incident anchors the discipline: Hopper's FP8 accumulation-precision bug silently collapsed 128K needle-in-haystack from 91% → 13% (fixed by FlashAttention-3 two-level FP32 accumulation, patches flash-attention#96/#91) — every KV-dtype change needs a long-context validation gate, not just a perplexity check.

**Scope discipline for this part.** Disaggregated/tiered KV storage (LMCache 7.43×, Mooncake, CacheGen), prefill/decode disaggregation, RDMA-shared KV (Lightbits 10.5M-token restoration, llm-d −88.2%), prefix caching (RadixAttention, vLLM automatic prefix caching), and 1M-context product status are sibling part **07b**'s territory — one-line cross-refs only. Engine *version-matrix* detail (per-release MLA backends, kernel priority rules) is part **06**'s (inference engines) territory — one-line cross-refs only. This part covers attention variants and compression: the memory wall as an architectural choice.

(b) "MLA exactly 4× batch/GPU" is only partially verified — no primary measurement pins that figure; use the measured 93.3% / 5.76× throughput / ~14.2× toy-arithmetic figures instead. (c) DSA reduces attention *compute* — do not equate it with KV-cache reduction. (d) The brief's "dynamic drift correction" names no single documented technique [UNVERIFIED]; the documented mechanisms are inventoried in §"Figures and metrics". ## Key dated facts

### Attention variants and MLA (architecture-level)

- **May 2024 — DeepSeek-V2 paper (arXiv:2405.04434).** Multi-Head Latent Attention (MLA) debuts: **93.3% less KV cache than DeepSeek 67B**, 5.76× higher max generation throughput, modeling quality matching or slightly beating MHA. The controlled MHA-vs-MLA ablation is ~96% (34.6K vs 860.2K cached elements/token); the raw per-layer element ratio (576 = 512 latent + 64 decoupled-RoPE vs 32,768 MHA elements) is ~98.2%. MLA's positional fix: decoupled RoPE — content keys/values go through the latent path while a small separate shared RoPE key (64 dims) carries position outside the compression, since standard RoPE rotation would break the low-rank absorption trick.
- **Sep 2023–2024 — GQA becomes the open-weight default.** Llama 2: only the 70B used GQA (7B/13B stayed MHA); **Llama 3 extended GQA across all sizes**; Mistral applied it from its first 7B release (Sep 2023); Gemma 2/4, Qwen, MiMo families all ship GQA variants (typically 2:1 to 8:1 query-to-KV ratios). Consensus 2026 figure: ~88% KV reduction vs MHA while retaining >95% of MHA quality. Its measurable weakness (worse modeling performance than MHA at matched size) pushed DeepSeek toward MLA.
- **Sep 2024 (SGLang v0.3) — "7× Faster DeepSeek MLA."** SGLang served MLA (DeepSeek-V2) a full quarter before the V3 wave. **Late 2024 (vLLM v0.6.x era):** DeepSeek-V2 MLA support landed with the Triton MLA attention path (`TRITON_ATTN` / `deepseek_mla`); DeepSeek-V3 support hardened in v0.7.x (Jan 2025). Exact vLLM minor version [UNVERIFIED] — treat "v0.6.x, late 2024" as the support window. TensorRT-LLM: native MLA for DeepSeek-V3/V3.2/R1-class models documented across NVIDIA 2025–2026 serving materials, exact release [UNVERIFIED] — "supported, version unpinned".
- **2026-03-21 — Hugging Face TGI archived read-only** (maintenance mode; HF recommends vLLM/SGLang). TGI only ever gained MLA on the **Intel-Gaudi branch** (v3.3.x, late 2025), never mainline CUDA. MLA support is therefore **3/4 engines** (vLLM, SGLang, TensorRT-LLM), not "all engines".
- **v0.23.0 (2026) — vLLM DeepSeek-V4 production hardening:** sparse MLA metadata decoupled from V3.2, a TRTLLM-gen attention kernel (TensorRT-LLM-derived) adopted back into vLLM for DeepSeek's attention pattern, EPLB for the Mega-MoE. **v0.28.0 (tagged 2026-08-26):** sparse MLA end-to-end (plain decode, MTP, DSpark speculative decoding); AMD Quark NVFP4 support. **2026 backend-priority rule:** for sparse MLA, FP8 KV cache *always* prefers `FLASHINFER_MLA_SPARSE`; with BF16 KV, `FLASHINFER_MLA_SPARSE` preferred at ≤16 query heads, `FLASHMLA_SPARSE` otherwise. 2026 MLA backends in vLLM mainline: `FLASHMLA`, `FLASH_ATTN_MLA`, `FLASHINFER_MLA` (+ sparse variants), `TRITON_ATTN`, `CUTLASS_MLA`, `XLA`.
- **2025-03-16 / 2025-03-29 / 2025-05 — SGLang MLA milestones:** FlashMLA backend (PR #4079), FlashAttention-3 backend for MLA, then **PR #6109 (May 2025): FlashMLA backend gains FP8 KV cache** — ~30% speedup from MTP+FP8-KV, KV cache halved. **v0.5.6 (Dec 2025):** MHA and MLA KV caches refactored to support **FP4**; SemiAnalysis measured DeepSeek-R1 NVFP4 on B200 reaching 907 tok/s/GPU at concurrency 4 (1.79× over 0.5.5).
- **By 2026-09 — at least eight model families besides DeepSeek adopted MLA**, including Moonshot AI's **Kimi K2 series** and Zhipu's **GLM-5 / GLM-5.3** (temperature2.com census, 2026-09-08). DeepSeek lineage: V2 (May 2024, MLA debut) → V3 (671B MoE, MLA retained) → R1 (reasoning, MLA) → V4/V4 Pro (1.6T-class, MLA). Research cited in 2026 survey literature shows MLA has **higher expressive power than GQA under the same KV-cache budget**.
- **2026-09-08 — temperature2.com analysis:** toy-model arithmetic at **14.2× smaller cache than MHA at full quality**. Caveat: toy arithmetic, not a production measurement.

### Shared and hybrid attention layouts

- **2026-04-02 — Gemma 4 ships (Apache 2.0).** Shared KV cache: the last N layers reuse KV computed by earlier layers (llama.cpp: `n_kv_shared_layers` / reuse of `n_layer_kv_from_start` KV blocks, confirmed in community runtime notes May–June 2026). Interleaved local sliding-window + global attention inherited from Gemma 2: E2B/E4B 512-token windows; 26B/31B 1024-token windows; every ~6th layer global (31B: 60 layers → 10 global + 50 local); final layer global. **GQA 2:1** (31B: 32 query heads, 16 KV heads, head_dim 256) with *dual head dims* (global head_dim vs SWA head_dim — tooling must read shapes per layer). Proportional RoPE (p-RoPE) on global layers. Community-verified KV math for Gemma 4 31B at 256K: ~40.8 GiB BF16 (dominated by the 10 global layers; the 50 local layers hold a constant ~0.78 GiB floor); with FP8 KV: ~20.4 GiB.
- **2026-06-01 — MiniMax M3 launches.** ~428B total / ~23B active MoE (128 experts, 4 active/token), 60 layers, native text/image/video multimodality, 1M-token context with **512K guaranteed usable floor**; weights + tech report (arXiv:2606.13392) ~June 11, 2026. **MiniMax Sparse Attention (MSA):** two-stage — lightweight index branch selects which KV *blocks* are relevant per input, main attention layer processes only those blocks ("KV outer gather Q" kernel — each KV block read from memory once, contiguous access). Vendor-reported: **>9× faster prefill, >15× faster decode** at 1M tokens vs previous generation; **28.4× reduction in per-token attention compute** vs full attention at 1M; per-token compute ≈ 1/20th of previous generation; kernel >4× faster than open-source sparse-attention alternatives (Flash-Sparse-Attention, flash-moba). Independent speed verification pending mid-2026 [VENDOR]. Deliberate tradeoff vs MLA: MSA operates on *uncompressed* KV, preserving long-context retrieval accuracy (NIH-style fidelity argument) at slightly higher memory cost.
- **2026 — Qwen3-Next.** Deliberately avoided MLA: **Gated DeltaNet** (linear-recurrent/SSM-style) layers for a large fraction of depth — these layers carry **zero KV cache**; KV growth confined to remaining full-attention layers. Sep 2026 FP8-KV deployment analysis: GDN recurrent state ~0.02 GiB; vLLM's `--mamba-cache-dtype` accepts only auto/bfloat16/float16/f32 (no FP8 option — irrelevant given the tiny state); `--kv-cache-dtype-skip-layers` keeps sensitive layer types at bf16 while quantizing the rest.

### Post-hoc KV compression methods

- **2026-04-07 — HybridKV (VERIFIED).** arXiv:2604.05887 (Bowen Zeng, Feiyang Ren, Jun Zhang, Xiaoling Gu, Ke Chen, Lidan Shou, Huan Li — Zhejiang University / Hangzhou institutes); accepted **ACL 2026 long paper** (Anthology ID 2026.acl-long.594, 64th ACL, San Diego, July 2026). Problem: in multimodal LLMs each visual input expands into thousands of tokens; KV cache scales linearly and stays resident through decoding. Method: (1) head classification as *static* or *dynamic* using text-centric attention; (2) top-down hierarchical budget allocation across layers and heads; (3) differentiated compression — static heads by **text-prior pruning**, dynamic heads by **chunk-wise retrieval**. Measured (Qwen2.5-VL-7B, 11 multimodal benchmarks): **up to 7.9× KV-cache memory reduction** (best case, not the mean), **1.52× faster decoding**, almost no performance drop — some benchmarks *above* full-cache baseline.
- **2026-03-25 — Google Research blog announces TurboQuant (VERIFIED).** arXiv:2504.19874; accepted **ICLR 2026**. Market reaction: Samsung −4.7%, SK Hynix −6.2%, Micron −3.4% ("Google's DeepSeek moment," per Cloudflare's CEO); one analyst note (Lighthouse Canton) argued the paper was "almost a year old" relative to the blog post and not production-deployed, so the selloff was disproportionate. **It is Google's invention — not Red Hat's.** Red Hat published an *independent vLLM evaluation* on 2026-05-11 (llmkube#308, incl. the 6×/8× figures).
- **TurboQuant mechanism (two stages):** (1) **PolarQuant** (AISTATS 2026) — rotates each key/value vector by a random orthogonal matrix into polar coordinates, redistributing variance uniformly so a simple analytically-computed scalar quantizer (Lloyd-Max codebooks over Gaussian marginals) is near-optimal, eliminating the expensive normalization step; (2) **QJL (Quantized Johnson-Lindenstrauss)** — 1-bit correction on the quantization residual (sign bits of a random Gaussian projection) yielding an *unbiased inner-product estimator*, fixing systematic bias of MSE-optimal scalar quantizers. Total: (b−1) bits for MSE quantization + 1 bit for QJL = b bits per dimension, default b=3 (**3-bit keys / 2-bit values**, ≈3 bits per dimension overall). Fully data-oblivious: no training, calibration, or fine-tuning — applies to any model at inference time. New tokens during generation stay in full precision (only cached past tokens are compressed).
- **TurboQuant measured results** (Gemma, Mistral, Llama-3.1-8B; LongBench, Needle-in-Haystack, ZeroSCROLLS, RULER, L-Eval): **≥6× KV-cache memory reduction** vs FP16; **up to 8× speedup in attention-logit computation on H100** (headline figure: 4-bit TurboQuant vs 32-bit unquantized keys); **99.5% attention fidelity**; lossless on needle-in-haystack; within ~2.7× of the information-theoretic limit; negligible runtime overhead. Ecosystem 2026: **not yet upstream in vLLM** (community integrations: `0xSero/turboquant` with Triton kernels + vLLM integration; turboquant-mlx for Apple Silicon, e.g. Qwen 27B at 128K on M4 Pro 48GB). Orthogonal to weight quantization (FP8/Q4_K_M/AWQ) since it targets only the KV cache.
- **2026-04-29 — MarkTechPost "Top 10 KV Cache Compression Techniques" survey** organizes the zoo into three families (eviction / quantization / low-rank). Scale reference: a 30B model at batch 128 × 1,024 input tokens can hold **~180 GB** of KV cache; a 7B model's weights are 14 GB while its KV cache can reach ~72 GB.
- **2026-03-31 — "Thin Keys, Full Values" (Sapient Intelligence, arXiv:2603.04427).** Post-hoc low-rank retrofit: factorize W_K ≈ A·B via truncated SVD, cache compact r-dimensional keys, absorb Bᵀ into W_Q at zero cost (queries are never cached). From scratch at 7B with d_select = d_model/4: matches full-attention perplexity (9.24 vs 9.25) with 12% fewer params, 8% faster training; as a retrofit, **SVD + QK fine-tuning (<1% of pretraining data) gives 75% key-cache savings at ~2% quality cost** on GPT-2 and Mistral-7B — composes with GQA and quantization for **up to 16× combined key-cache compression** (≈25 GB saved per user at 128K on a 7B, ~60% more concurrent users). [VENDOR] for vendor-run numbers.
- **2026 research frontier (survey-flagged, early-stage paper claims — treat as paper claims, not production numbers):** latent-space compaction ("Attention Matching," claimed **50× compaction**); reasoning-aware compression ("TriAttention," claimed **10.7× memory reduction on AIME25 at matched accuracy**) — compressing the *reasoning trace's* cache footprint, not just the prompt cache.

### Reasoning-aware compression (the trace, not just the cache)

- **Jan 2026 (late) — R-KV v4** (arXiv:2505.24133 v4, Jan 22; Cai et al., incl. Anima Anandkumar): redundancy-aware compression for reasoning models — **~100% of full-KV performance at 10% of the cache, 105% at 16%**, 90% memory saving, **6.6× throughput** over standard CoT inference.
- **May 2026 — ThinKV v2** (arXiv:2510.01290; v1 Oct 2025, v2 May 7 2026; **ICLR 2026 Oral**): *thought-adaptive* hybrid quantization+eviction for reasoning models. Attention sparsity reveals distinct thought types; ThinKV assigns precision by thought segment — reasoning segments fp8_e4m3, execution int4, transition ternary 2-bit — and progressively evicts less-critical thoughts, with a PagedAttention-extending kernel that **reuses evicted slots in place** (no compaction overhead). Measured on DeepSeek-R1-Distill, GPT-OSS, NVIDIA AceReason: **near-lossless accuracy at <5% of original KV cache** (AIME within 4% at 3.67% memory), up to **5.8× throughput** over SOTA baselines; average precision auto-adapts to 3.4–3.8 bits with problem difficulty. Subtlety flagged: backtracking transition segments (model abandons a path and pivots) are unusually important — evicting them causes endless loops.
- **Apr 2026 — FoveatedKV independent benchmark (Apr 4, Alexandru Vasile):** **importance-adaptive mixed-precision tiering** — top 10% of tokens by attention score stay fp16; bottom 90% compress to **fp8 E4M3 keys + INT4 values**; nothing evicted, everything still contributes to softmax. 75% KV cut with ≤3% quality loss. [COMMUNITY] for the independent benchmark.
- **Nov 2025 — Kitty** (arXiv:2511.18643): **2-bit quantization with dynamic channel-wise precision boost** — keeps top-k sensitive key channels at higher precision; the closest literature match to *dynamic* correction at serving time. **Oct 2025 — KVLinC** (arXiv:2510.05373): Hadamard rotation + **trainable linear-correction adapters** compensating key quantization error — closest literal match to "drift correction," but requires 2–11 h of adapter training on H200 and full-precision keys at runtime, so not a drop-in serving method.

### Quantized KV in production (dtypes, support, incidents)

- **Feb 2025 — DeepSeek's FlashMLA** (custom FP8 KV format: 656 bytes/token, Hopper SM90; 3000 GB/s decode, 410 TFLOPS sparse decode) is the origin point of FP8 KV in production — DeepSeek ships it in reference kernels; both vLLM and SGLang consume it.
- **2026-04 — vLLM-team FP8-KV benchmarks:** FP8 KV reduces the **inter-token-latency slope to 54% of the BF16 baseline on H100**, with **break-even starting at ~7K tokens** and **sub-0.3% accuracy cost** on standard benchmarks. Production DeepSeek-R1 / DeepSeek-V4-Flash serving recipes standardize on **`--kv-cache-dtype fp8_e4m3`** (paired with `--dtype fp8`); community production configs (DeepSeek-R1 on 8× GH200/H200, DP+EP; DeepSeek-V4-Flash on 4× GH200) use fp8_e4m3 with MLA attention backends. The DP+EP note matters — MLA requires DP+EP for KV-cache efficiency (avoids 8× duplication across TP ranks).
- **Dec 2025–Jan 2026 — SemiAnalysis InferenceX:** DeepSeek-R1 NVFP4 on B200 via SGLang 0.5.6 — 907 tok/s/GPU at concurrency 4 (1.79× over 0.5.5 on the same pool). **FP4/NVFP4 remain Blackwell/SM100-native or experimental paths** — do not overclaim as general production defaults. INT4 KV quality remains method- and setup-dependent.
- **2026 — Hopper FP8 accumulation bug (the 2026 cautionary incident).** Hopper's FP8 Tensor Cores suffer intermediate accumulation precision loss when the contraction dimension is large (the same hardware issue encountered during DeepSeek-V3 training). In early FP8-KV implementations this collapsed **128K needle-in-haystack accuracy from 91% → 13%** — a 78-point regression with no error logs, no OOM, no visible failure signal. Fix: **two-level FP32 accumulation**, shipped in FlashAttention-3 patches (`flash-attention#96`, `flash-attention#91`). Production red flag: **if NIAH recall at 128K drops below 50% after enabling FP8 KV cache, you are hitting this bug — do not route production traffic until the patch is confirmed present.** Practical guidance (Sep 2026 Qwen3.8 hybrid deployment analysis): `fp8_e4m3` vs `fp8_e5m2` is a precision-vs-dynamic-range choice worth trying when subtle visual/textual separators degrade; calibrated scales (e.g. via llm-compressor) beat naive per-tensor scales.
- **Sep 2026 — SGLang quantized-KV matrix (the broadest 2026 matrix):** `--kv-cache-dtype` supports `fp8_e5m2 | fp8_e4m3 | fp4_e2m1 | nvfp4 | fp4_mx_block16 | int8 | int4 | int2` (docs, Sep 2026). FP8: E5M2 (range ±57,344) vs E4M3 (precision; range ±240). FP4: MXFP4 E2M1 with per-block-16 microscaling, experimental; scales computed on-the-fly (no pre-quantized checkpoint needed); SM100-native recipes via `--prefill-kv-cache-dequant-dtype` (native `nvfp4` or dequant-to-`fp8_e4m3` workspace). INT8/INT4/INT2: Triton-based, dynamic affine per-token scales; `--kv-cache-quant-group-size` for group-wise head-dim quantization. Capacity accounting (SGLang docs): FP4 ≈ 1.78× more tokens than FP8, ≈ **3.56× more tokens than BF16** (scale overhead included). vLLM mainline remains `auto | fp8 | fp8_e4m3 | fp8_e5m2`; FP8-KV performance is kernel-fusion-dependent, not a dtype property (one AMD-fork benchmark: fp8_e4m3 KV faster with 2× pool on SGLang, but fp8 KV halved decode vs fp16 on vLLM-ROCm — [SECONDARY, HARDWARE-SPECIFIC]).

### Sparse attention — the compute-vs-cache distinction

- **Dec 2025 — DeepSeek-V3.2 introduces DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA):** learned **"lightning indexer"** selects a top-K subset (commonly **2,048 tokens**) for sparse attention. **2026 — SGLang implementation:** FP8 Q8KV8 sparse-MLA prefill (PR #30514). **2026-04-24 — DeepSeek V4** carries the lineage forward with hybrid **Compressed Sparse Attention (CSA) + Heavily Compressed Attention (HCA):** at 1M tokens, V4-Pro uses **27% of the inference FLOPs and 10% of the KV-cache size of V3.2**; V4-Flash pushes to 10% FLOPs / 7% KV cache. [VENDOR] for the V4 figures. **Critical nuance:** DSA reduces *attention computation*; it does **not intrinsically shrink the KV cache**, because any historical token may be selected by the indexer later — the cache must retain full-fidelity KVs (or rely on separate compression like CSA/HCA). Secondary sources sometimes conflate the two — they are not identical.
- **2026-06 — GLM-5.2 ships IndexShare** (VERIFIED): a single sparse-attention indexer **shared across every four sparse-attention layers**, yielding a **2.9× reduction in per-token FLOPs at 1M context** and up to **20% improved MTP speculative-decoding acceptance length**. The model provides **1M-token context (1M input / 65K output)**; subscriber/API release ~**2026-06-13**, open weights ~**2026-06-17** (arXiv:2603.12201 underlies the design). Efficiency figures remain **vendor-reported** unless independently replicated.

### Hybrid O(1)-state architectures (recurrent + attention)

- **Mar 2026 — Mamba-3 engineering deep dive:** "The Inference-First Architecture" — transformers are training-efficient but inference-expensive; Mamba-3 is inference-efficient by design (linear time, constant memory, predictable cost at scale) and explicitly "not a replacement, part of the next hybrid architecture stack." Honest tradeoff stated: Mamba removes attention for state evolution and **loses exact recall** in exchange for scalability.
- **Jun 2026 — Zamba2-VL (Zyphra):** Mamba2–Transformer hybrid VLM (1.2B/2.7B/7B, Apache 2.0) — fixed-size recurrent state; ~10× lower TTFT vs closest transformer baseline on 32K prefill; PixMoCount 62.5 (2.7B) vs 32.8 InternVL3.5-1B.
- **2026 — Jamba-1.5 (AI21):** 1:7 attention:Mamba hybrid — **4 GB (Mini) / 9 GB (Large) KV at 256K** vs 32 GB Mixtral / 80 GB Llama-3.1-70B — **~10× lower**; 256K effective on RULER.
- **Mar 2026 — Nemotron community benchmark (2× RTX 3060):** 250K context on consumer GPUs; TG 34 tok/s @250K vs 18 tok/s Qwen3.5-35B (**89% faster**); crossover beyond 64K; graceful degradation, no cliffs.
- **2026 — worked hybrid arithmetic:** a 64-layer model where 56 attention layers become Mamba compressors (8 attention layers retained): KV cache falls from **~67 GB to ~8.3 GB**; compressor state — **56 layers × d_model 8192 × d_state 16 × 2 bytes ≈ 14.7 MB per user** for Mamba (a rounding error), or **~117 MB per user** for linear-attention fast-weight grids (56 × 64 heads × 128 × 128 × 2 bytes — 14× the Mamba state, still dwarfed by the 58.7 GB saved). Total: weights 25 GB + 8.3 GB KV + 0.015 GB state + 6 GB overhead ≈ **39.3 GB** — one H100 with 40 GB headroom for concurrency, where the pure transformer needed two GPUs.
- **2026-06 — NVIDIA Nemotron-3-Ultra-550B-A55B** (arXiv:2606.15007): 550B total / 55B active **"LatentMoE" = Mamba-2 + MoE + Attention hybrid**, MTP, 1M context, native NVFP4 distribution, open weights (OpenMDW-1.1) — the first US-lab frontier-scale open-weight Mamba-2-hybrid MoE. **2026-04-15 — Qwen3.6-35B-A3B**: **Gated-DeltaNet (linear attention) + Gated-Attention + MoE** backbone, MTP, 256K context, Apache-2.0 (backfilled; architecture secondarily sourced). **Feb 2026 — Qwen3.5-MoE**: Gated-DeltaNet/Mamba + 256-expert MoE hybrid. **IBM Granite-4.0-H-Small**: hybrid Mamba-2 / Transformer MoE (32B / 9B active).
- **Documented tradeoff across all sources:** pure SSM loses the exact recall attention provides; the converged 2026 answer is *hybrid* — SSM constant-size state for the gist plus attention's exact recall on a recent window. Pure-SSM long-context *retrieval* parity claims (as opposed to perplexity) are [UNVERIFIED] until needle-style evaluations say otherwise.
