Conclusion
Even if it isn't already very clear, this system is impressive. High-end system configurations astonish with specifications such as 16 TB of DRAM, support for six small GPUs or two double-width ones, and more than enough space for significant storage capacity. The liquid loop is the key that makes it possible to combine power and density.
Although quad-processor systems are not the norm in most data centers, they continue to offer a quite unique value proposition for workloads that can take advantage of the massive DRAM footprint and CPU power. Take into account that this chassis can also accommodate a few GPUs, and you would be hard-pressed to find a server capable of doing more, in a 2U rack space.
The HP ProLiant DL560 Gen11 brings to the forefront the need for businesses to adapt to modern cooling systems for powerful servers. In this case, we are dealing with a simple closed-loop system to cool the four processors, which meets the need very well. But as TDPs increase in processors and advance in GPUs, data centers will need to plan for a larger loop or alternative liquid-based systems, there is no way around it. For now, the DL560 Gen11 represents a big step forward for dense computing platforms and an interesting consideration for compatible workloads.
