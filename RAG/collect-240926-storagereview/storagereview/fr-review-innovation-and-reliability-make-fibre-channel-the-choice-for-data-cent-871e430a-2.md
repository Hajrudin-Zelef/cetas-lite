# fr-review-innovation-and-reliability-make-fibre-channel-the-choice-for-data-cent-871e430a

Large data centers are full of servers, storage, switches, and various other hardware, trying to keep up with the latest technology trends. Of all the changes happening within the data center, one technology remains a mainstay for storage connectivity. Fibre Channel has been a data center pillar for many years, based on reliability and security. However, it's not just a first-round choice for the enterprise market. Many hardware and software vendors focus their testing on Fibre Channel to ensure products are ready for prime time before shipping to the customer.
Storage and switch vendors continue to add new features and enhancements to Fibre Channel, and software developers test Fibre Channel functionality before releasing software updates to the masses. Fibre Channel remains the predominant SAN technology in large data centers. Most VMware customers use Fibre Channel to take advantage of continuous innovation and the existing reliability, resiliency, security, and speed in place today.
VMware is committed to Fibre Channel and Marvell leads the way
If there were any questions about the importance and significance of Fibre Channel technology, VMware puts that to rest by prioritizing testing of new releases with Fibre Channel. With all the latest announcements from VMware Explore, vSphere 8 was the most interesting. VMware continued to focus on vVols storage engineering and added support for vVols in NVMe-oF.
As stated on the VMware website, "Initially, we will only support FC, but we will continue to validate and support other protocols supported with vSphere NVMe-oF." The consensus at VMware is that customers might move to NVMe-oF, iSCSI, and 100G Ethernet, but FC is preferred when it comes to high-performance and mission-critical applications. Therefore, Fibre Channel will continue to be at the forefront of technology advancements in vSphere. Although there is a shift toward other technologies, VMware does not see a decrease in demand for Fibre Channel.
And it's not just VMware. With all the buzz around automation, machine learning, and DevOps, Fibre Channel is still a priority for most IT professionals. Due to their inherent reliability, security, and low latency, SAN fabrics are essential for industries such as healthcare, finance, manufacturing, insurance, and government. Of course, Fibre Channel technology is not standing still and continues to evolve with 64G fabrics shipping today and a roadmap to 128G from progressive switch and HBA vendors. Marvell QLogic has continued to demonstrate unmatched leadership and innovation around Fibre Channel technology.
There's a reason Fibre Channel is the reference technology for storage networks: it just works. The vendor community is firmly committed to working together to advance the specifications to meet changing industry demands. Testing done by software vendors focuses on interoperability as well as security and reliability, and this is evident in the case of VMware vSphere 8. Vendors are committed to ensuring interoperability with Fibre Channel before releasing new updates or hardware into the data center.
FC gets a boost in vSphere 8, strengthens FC-NVMe implementation
VMware introduced several key features in vSphere 8, including enhancements to NVMe over Fabrics (NVMe-oF). Of course, there are several reasons for the growing popularity of NVMe-oF. Primarily, customers appreciate the high performance and throughput associated with NVMe-oF compared to traditional SCSI and NFS. Storage vendors are moving to NVMe arrays and have added FC-NVMe support in their product lines.
VMware followed suit and made significant improvements to vSphere 8 specific to NVMe-oF, including:
- Increased the number of namespaces and paths supported for NVMe-TCP and FC-NVMe. vSphere 8 now supports 256 namespaces and 2,000 paths with NVMe-TCP and FC-NVMe.
- It extended reservation support for NVMe devices for customers using Microsoft Windows Server Failover Cluster (WSFC) support for application-level disk locking. Initially for FC only, this allows customers to use clustered VMDK functionality with Microsoft WSFC with NVMe-oF datastores.
- Support for advanced NVMe/TCP discovery services in ESXi to help customers configure NVMe-oF in an Ethernet environment. NVMe Advance Discovery support was added, allowing vSphere to query the network for supported NVMe array controllers, which simplifies configuration. NVMe/TCP took advantage of the automatic discovery capabilities that Fibre Channel has supported since its inception.
The four pillars of digital transformation with Fibre Channel
Autonomous SANs
The buzz around all things autonomous is becoming reasonably loud. Since we're talking about SAN, specifically Fibre Channel SAN, this would be a great place to highlight how autonomous SAN works without getting caught in the weeds.
Autonomous SANs are essential because they support major industries such as healthcare, finance, government, and insurance. Fibre Channel SANs are widely deployed in these industries due to their inherent reliability and security.
An outage or downtime in these critical industries can be catastrophic. Depending on the duration, an outage can result in hundreds of thousands or millions of dollars in lost revenue. In healthcare, an outage can result in a delayed procedure or the inability to perform emergency services. With this in mind, the Fibre Channel industry standards body continues to improve reliability and availability.
To meet the requirements of the modern data center and advanced applications, industries are configuring devices to perform tasks not originally intended. It's not enough to have smart devices. Today, there is a need to have an installed base that is aware of everything around it. This is called "collaborative intelligence," where devices are not only aware of ongoing activities but also have the ability to act if necessary.
The first phase of the autonomous SAN effort is to develop an architectural element called Fabric Performance Impact Notifications (FPIN). Fabric notifications create a mechanism to notify network devices of events occurring in the fabric that will help with resiliency decisions. Two types of error conditions can occur. Logical errors can often be recovered by a retry or logical reset and are relatively non-disruptive to the system. On the other hand, physical errors often require the same type of intervention to complete the repair. Intermittent errors are more difficult to resolve and can take time.
With fabric notifications, the fabric (or the end device) detects the intermittent physical problem, monitors to see if the error is persistent, and if so, generates a message that is sent to the devices affected by the event. Through this information, the multipath solution knows the location and nature of the physical error and can "route around" it. The IT administrator does not need to be involved in identifying, isolating, or launching recovery commands to clear the error.
All of these mechanisms are controlled by the endpoints via capability exchange and operation registration. The fabric's Fibre Channel switches have visibility of other fabric components that allow them to collect information about the storage network, connected devices, and the overall infrastructure. These switches exchange this information across the fabric, creating a true vision of the autonomous SAN for self-learning, self-optimization, and self-recovery.
Fabric notifications ultimately provide information to fabric devices to eliminate wasted energy when troubleshooting, analyzing, and resolving issues that are automatically resolved by devices that correct problems impacting performance and outages.
VMware laid the foundation for delivering an intelligent, autonomous SAN with the process of receiving, displaying, and triggering alerts for FC fabric events using Fabric Performance Impact Notification (FPIN), an industry-standard technology.
