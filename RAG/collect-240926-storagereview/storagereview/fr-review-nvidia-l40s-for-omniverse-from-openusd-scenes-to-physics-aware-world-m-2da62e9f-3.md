# fr-review-nvidia-l40s-for-omniverse-from-openusd-scenes-to-physics-aware-world-m-2da62e9f

The Shift Toward Physics-Based AI Training
More importantly, the L40S offers features entirely absent from the H100. Its main asset lies in its 142 third-generation RT cores, which enable real-time ray tracing and photorealistic rendering. While the H100 lacks RT cores and therefore cannot provide graphics acceleration, the L40S's RT cores make it the ideal choice for applications requiring both AI computations and advanced graphics.
This distinction becomes increasingly crucial with the growing popularity of AI-generated 3D modeling, digital twin simulations, and OpenUSD (Universal Scene Description) workflows. The next stage of artificial intelligence is not limited to training larger language models; it is about developing world models that understand physics, spatial relationships, and interactions with the real world. This evolution requires a fundamental transformation in our approach to training data generation.
While training traditional AI models relies largely on NVIDIA's flagship compute GPUs, training the next generation of physical world models requires a different approach. These advanced AI systems require enormous amounts of training data that capture not only visual information but also physical properties, lighting behavior, material interactions, and spatial relationships. GPUs like the L40S become essential for large-scale training data generation, where their RT cores enable the creation of physically accurate synthetic environments that serve as the foundation for training more sophisticated AI models.
This advanced graphics rendering capability, powered by dedicated RT cores, is precisely what makes the L40S the ideal engine for NVIDIA's Omniverse platform and the broader ecosystem of physics-based AI development.
Omniverse
Omniverse is a development platform of APIs, SDKs, and services that enables developers to create applications and workflows based on Universal Scene Description (OpenUSD). It is designed to create and connect physically accurate, real-time 3D virtual worlds. This is made possible through the integration of NVIDIA's RTX technology for photorealistic ray-traced rendering, directly into industrial simulation and robotics workflows. The L40S, with its powerful RT cores, provides the essential hardware acceleration to handle these demanding RTX rendering workloads, enabling developers to simulate light, materials, and physics with striking realism.
More than just a visualization tool, Omniverse is a platform for developing the next generation of physical AI. By creating these realistic virtual worlds, or "digital twins," developers can simulate complex industrial facilities, test entire fleets of robots, and validate autonomous vehicles in a safe virtual environment before real-world deployment. These high-fidelity simulations form the foundation for generating vast amounts of physical synthetic data; an essential resource for training powerful AI models running on compute-dedicated GPUs.
Synthetic Manipulation Motion Generation for Robotics
The NVIDIA Isaac GR00T model for synthetic manipulation motion generation provides a framework for training robots, demonstrating how the RT core capabilities of the L40S enable the creation of vast datasets from minimal human demonstrations. This workflow addresses one of the main challenges in robotics development: the long and costly process of collecting enough high-quality training data for robotic manipulation tasks.
Traditional robotics training relies largely on supervised or imitation learning, where robots acquire new skills by observing and imitating expert human demonstrations. However, creating perfect demonstrations is complex, and collecting comprehensive, high-quality data in real-world settings is tedious, time-consuming, and expensive. A properly trained human operator typically takes about a minute to record a single high-quality demonstration, which is difficult to implement at scale due to the considerable human effort required and the risk of errors.
The Isaac GR00T model addresses this challenge by leveraging synthetic data generated from physically accurate simulations to accelerate the data collection process. Organizations can collect an exponentially larger volume of data in just a few hours of human demonstrations.
The Isaac GR00T model consists of three key elements that work together to create a complete synthetic data generation pipeline:
- GR00T-Teleop Allows skilled human operators to control virtual robots using spatial computing devices such as Apple Vision Pro. This system enables operators to demonstrate complex manipulation tasks in photorealistic virtual environments, capturing not only motion trajectories but also environmental context and object interactions. Apple Vision Pro transmits hand tracking data to Isaac Lab, which simultaneously returns an immersive view of the robot's environment, enabling intuitive and interactive robot control.
- GR00T-Mimic The recorded demonstrations are transformed into larger synthetic datasets through advanced simulation techniques. This component uses these recorded demonstrations as input data to generate additional synthetic motion trajectories in Isaac Lab, supporting one-armed or two-handed humanoid robot manipulation. The process involves annotating key points in the demonstrations and using interpolation to ensure the smoothness and contextual appropriateness of the synthetic trajectories.
- GR00T-Gen Leverages the NVIDIA Omniverse and Cosmos platforms to enrich synthetic datasets through 3D scaling and domain randomization. This component adds diversity by randomizing the background, lighting, and other scene variables. It enriches the generated images through NVIDIA Cosmos Transfer, achieving a photorealism that reduces the gap between simulation and reality.
This pipeline perfectly illustrates the specialized and complementary roles of modern data center GPUs. The L40S, with its dedicated RT cores, plays the role of universe creator. It is indispensable in the early stages of data generation, where it handles physics rendering, real-time ray tracing, and domain randomization necessary to create a photorealistic and diverse virtual environment.
This high-fidelity virtual world then serves as the foundation for the next generation. Generative AI models, such as those from NVIDIA Cosmos, run on high-compute GPUs like the H100 to produce the final training data. The H100 leverages the realistic environment created by the L40S to generate millions of dynamic and physically consistent scenarios.
AI Inference Performance Benchmarks
Another use case where cards like the L40S are popular is cost-effective inference. To evaluate the real-world AI inference capabilities of the L40S compared to the H100, we compared LLM performance using vLLM running Nvidia's Open Reasoning Nemotron 14B parameter model at BF16 with a maximum token length of 32K.
vLLM Benchmark Results
The H100 shows clear superiority in performance, delivering approximately 4.2 times the throughput of the L40S. This advantage stems directly from the doubled Tensor Core performance and more than doubled memory bandwidth of the H100 (2 TB/s vs 864 GB/s).
However, the performance-to-price ratio is quite different. The L40S typically costs less than a third of the price of an H100, making it more cost-effective for many inference workloads. Organizations running inference services where optimal performance is not essential often find that the L40S offers a better total cost of ownership.
Zooming into the section with linear performance, we see that the L40S delivers a very acceptable level of performance, being capable of handling 16 concurrent requests with a TPOT (Time Per Output Token) SLO of approximately 52 ms.
