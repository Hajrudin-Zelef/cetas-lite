# fr-review-boosting-data-center-efficiency-with-solidigm-ssds-and-liquid-cooled-s-43f0153a

A power savings of 200 W can be very useful in terms of storage density. This savings allows you to double the storage footprint from 12 to 24 SSDs in a liquid-cooled server compared to an air-cooled server if you have workloads oriented toward read-intensive workloads. With the Solidigm D5-P5336, this 24-bay server increased its storage capacity from 737 TB to 1,474 TB thanks to the liquid loop. If the workload is write-heavy, you can equip the server with about eight additional SSDs. However, these figures are for baseline power modes, so if you are willing to reduce high-end write performance, you can easily equip your server with 24 SSDs with a write-heavy workload at reduced performance.
Conclusion
Through our testing of Solidigm D5-P5336 SSDs, we were able to see that managing NVMe power states can have a significant impact on energy efficiency without significantly affecting performance. Data center operators looking to maximize energy efficiency can leverage these power states to achieve greater storage density or reduce operational costs, especially in AI-centric environments where power is paramount. Solidigm's high-density SSDs are well positioned for this, delivering excellent terabyte-per-watt efficiency, especially with modern liquid cooling technologies.
Our results reveal that even slight adjustments to power states can generate significant energy savings, which can be crucial in environments limited by power availability. Optimizing overall server power consumption improves storage density and promotes more sustainable data center operations.
Power management is becoming increasingly critical as modern servers are pushed to their limits, especially in AI-driven workloads. The combination of liquid cooling and efficient SSD management options offers a path forward for data centers looking to scale performance and storage density without exceeding power budgets.
