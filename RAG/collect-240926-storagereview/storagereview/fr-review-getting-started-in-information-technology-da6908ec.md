# fr-review-getting-started-in-information-technology-da6908ec

<!-- source: https://www.storagereview.com/fr/review/getting-started-in-information-technology -->

Our readers often ask us how to get started in IT. In response to this question, we asked our community (Check out our Discord!) and thought about what they had to say. In this article, we cover things like entry-level jobs, certifications, and more!
Certifications
A common approach for people to get their foot in the door is to obtain industry-related certifications. They offer an excellent opportunity to gain marketable skills and demonstrate to employers that you are competent and dedicated. Let's explore a few companies and the different options they offer.
CompTIA
CompTIA is a respected company specializing in beginner to intermediate certifications. They offer everything from Cloud, Security, or Linux certifications to management and penetration testing certificates. Many beginners often choose to obtain three to four certifications, each increasing in prestige and difficulty. Here, we will discuss the two most relevant for beginners.
CompTIA A+
The first certification, and by far the most well-known, is the CompTIA A+ certification, which consists of two ninety-minute exams. The new 1100 series exams feature a wide range of topics, including but not limited to mobile devices, networking, hardware, virtualization, operating systems, basic security, and even cloud computing. This comprehensive set of topics leads many to consider it one of the main entry-level benchmarks. It should be the starting point for anyone wishing to become familiar with computers and technology.
CompTIA Security+
Another highly respected option is the Security+ certification. It requires passing a single exam that is more difficult than the A+ exams. Therefore, this certification carries more recognition among hiring managers. It also complies with DoD 8570 directive, which is required for certain jobs. Covering policy, cloud, mobile, IoT, compliance, risk, and even laws, it's a great start for anyone who is an IT beginner and may not need the A+. When paired with a secret or top secret security clearance, you can become very marketable.
(ISC)2 Certified in Cybersecurity (CC)
(ISC)² is recognized for its professional certifications, which typically require a year of experience. As a result, they are difficult for beginners to access. However, (ISC)² recently launched a new certification: the Certified in Cybersecurity (CC). Designed as an introduction to cybersecurity for individuals without professional experience, it offers free exam vouchers to the first million people who pass the exam. After passing, one must pay annual fees of $2 and obtain 2 continuing education credits within three years to maintain the certification. The topics covered are similar to those of the CompTIA Security+ certification, and it is considered a good free practice test before taking the Security+, despite some minor content differences. Due to its recent launch, it is less well-known and less established, and therefore does not necessarily guarantee a job. Nevertheless, it represents an excellent option for individuals anxious about exams and wishing to practice before a more expensive exam, or for those who wish to join the (ISC)² ecosystem.
Google Career Certificates
Everyone knows Google. That's why their relatively new career certificates have exploded in popularity over the past few months. They have everything from marketing and project management to IT support and cybersecurity. The latter two are the ones we will discuss here. These certificates are priced on a subscription-based model. You must pay forty dollars per month to access a course, but a seven-day free trial is also included. Once you complete the course, you get the certificate. This means that if you are efficient enough, you could take the course for free! However, if you take some time, you might end up paying a few hundred dollars. There are also discounts for CompTIA exams after completing the certificates.
Google IT Support
The Google IT Support course covers all networking protocols, IT support, operating systems, and system administration. Some institutions also award college credit for completing the course. It is designed to prepare you for the A+, and a dual-title digital badge is awarded upon completion of both certifications. A considerable number of people claim that this certification alone secured them a job. However, it is unlikely that the certificate was the only determining factor in the process. This certificate will give you a solid foundation in everything related to IT, allowing you to specialize and dive deeper into the industry.
Google Cybersecurity Professional
The Google Cybersecurity Professional training is very similar, in terms of structure and operation, to the IT Support training. However, the topics covered are significantly different. This training notably covers programming in Python and SQL, and also deepens knowledge in Linux and networking. This certification constitutes the logical continuation of the IT Support training. Relatively recent and therefore not widely recognized, it nevertheless attests to skills sought after in the job market, which you can showcase on a personal project or a GitHub page.
Home Lab
Not everyone has the financial resources or interest to devote to certifications. Some prefer to learn through practice or a trial-and-error approach. Providing services or emulating a corporate network in the comfort of your own home is called Homelabbing. This is how I started, and it proved just as effective when I was able to show what I had learned.
Most people start their lab with a Raspberry Pi, but they can be difficult to find due to recent shortages and price increases. Other common devices like PCs or thin clients are fruitful options. My journey began with Nextcloud and a personal website. Over time, my lab exploded with new services after discovering incredible online communities that offered an abundance of ideas and examples. After a few months of labbing, I met a stranger and mentioned in passing that I had a homelab. That conversation led to me getting a job. If you can effectively articulate what you have learned and how it works, you can market these skills to employers.
Help Desk Jobs
Helpdesk, or L1 support, jobs are the most widespread entry-level jobs in IT. The requirements are generally minimal and are not meant to be long-term jobs. The value of getting a helpdesk or L1 support position is to gain experience for your resume and acquire skills. During your time in this job, continue to strive to improve your skills by pursuing new certifications, taking courses, or building your home lab to advance your career. Additionally, prior work experience, even if not tech-related, can be advantageous when applying for a job. Networking is also crucial at this stage. Reach out to people on social media, offer to shadow senior people in your company, and look for internships and job openings.
Conclusion
There are so many ways to get started in IT. Sometimes, a well-placed contact can provide an introduction that leads to a coveted position. Internships can lead to long-term employment and build that important contact list. While there is no single "best" method, focusing on degrees, home lab projects, and work experience can increase your chances of advancing your career and landing your dream job.
