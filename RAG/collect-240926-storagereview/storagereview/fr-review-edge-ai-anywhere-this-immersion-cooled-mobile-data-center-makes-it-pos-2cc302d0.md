# fr-review-edge-ai-anywhere-this-immersion-cooled-mobile-data-center-makes-it-pos-2cc302d0

<!-- source: https://www.storagereview.com/fr/review/edge-ai-anywhere-this-immersion-cooled-mobile-data-center-makes-it-possible -->

The DUG Nomad is a rugged, modular data center designed to deploy AI and high-performance computing (HPC) directly at the network edge, even in the most remote or extreme environments. Housed in standard shipping containers, Nomad systems offer rapid deployment and immediate ease of use, enabling businesses to process data where it is generated.
DUG's proprietary immersion cooling technology is essential to its efficiency. Hardware components are submerged in a specialized dielectric fluid, which significantly improves energy efficiency, reduces maintenance costs, and greatly extends hardware lifespan. This approach enables sustained AI/HPC workloads in harsh environments and ensures consistent performance through effective management of thermal conditions critical to high-intensity tasks.
To see this mobile data center in action, we traveled to Washington, DC, where ADACEN hosted a live deployment of the Nomad 10. On site, we explored how immersion cooling, dense storage, and efficient heat management make it possible to deliver data center capabilities directly at the network edge.
DUG Nomad — A mobile data center to go!
The DUG Nomad is designed to be versatile and quick to deploy. It features a rugged, self-contained enclosure specifically engineered for easy transport and rapid installation. Its robust construction ensures reliable operation in extreme conditions (from arid deserts to research vessels), where deploying traditional infrastructure would be difficult, if not impossible. Once delivered, the Nomad is operational within hours, providing immediate compute, AI, and HPC capabilities precisely where they are needed.
The technical highlight of the Nomad lies at the heart of its immersion cooling system, DUG Cool. This system immerses the computing hardware directly in a specialized dielectric fluid. In this particular case, the Nomad uses a Castrol data center oil, the same type you may already know from the automotive sector. Castrol's specialized oils improve immersion cooling efficiency, optimizing heat transfer and ensuring long-term hardware reliability.
This approach effectively manages the thermal load by dissipating heat evenly, allowing CPUs, GPUs, and even storage to operate at optimal performance levels without overheating or throttling. Beyond temperature regulation, immersion cooling protects components from environmental factors such as dust, humidity, oxidation, and corrosion—common threats in harsh environments—thereby significantly reducing maintenance costs and improving overall reliability.
One of the Nomad's technical feats lies in its heat management within a fully enclosed mobile environment. The immersion tank absorbs heat from all internal components (CPUs, GPUs, RAM, and storage) and circulates it through two integrated heat exchangers located at each end of the 26U rack. From there, a closed liquid loop circulates the heated fluid to the rear of the container, where a Carrier AquaSnap air-cooled liquid chiller expels the heat outside. This compact cooling architecture draws ambient air through louvered intake panels with protective mesh and exhausts hot air through the sides. With a differential of only 10 °C between intake and exhaust, the Nomad can operate reliably in virtually any environment anywhere on Earth—except active volcanoes.
DUG Nomad units are available in scalable container configurations of 10, 20, and 40 feet, catering to a wide range of computing needs. The Nomad we tested was 10 feet. Larger containers accommodate more immersion tanks, thereby increasing the data center footprint. The Nomad 10 you see here offers 26 rack units of usable space. Given the increased density of immersion-cooled servers, this can be sufficient for many use cases. For those needing more capacity, larger containers are the solution.
Hypertec Immersion-Born Servers
To ensure the Nomad operates properly at the edge, dedicated servers—not modified air-cooled servers—are essential. Hypertec's Ciara Trident servers, designed specifically for immersion, are engineered from the ground up for immersion cooling environments and are fanless. This design optimizes energy efficiency and allows for a higher density of components within these tanks.
Hypertec immersion-cooled servers stand out for their fanless design, which contributes significantly to their energy efficiency gains. Another advantage of these servers: all storage, I/O, and power connectors are conveniently positioned at the front (top), simplifying access and maintenance. Cables are designed for immersion cooling while remaining flexible and easy to manage. Many other key components, specially designed for immersion, have been developed by Hypertec. Additionally, Hypertec offers specialized heat sinks compatible with different oil viscosities, giving customers great flexibility in immersion fluids.
For situations requiring more than a simple hot-swap, servers can be easily pulled manually from the tank if it is a small Hypertec blade unit. A small crane system integrated into the Nomad is available for larger storage servers and GPU servers.
Solidigm – Ahead of the Liquid Curve
When thinking about edge AI use cases in a system like the Nomad, it's easy to be drawn to the importance and efficiency of compute and GPU servers. The appeal of this solution lies notably in its ability to collect and analyze data at the edge, since a direct connection to the data center can prove difficult or inefficient. Solidigm is a leader in SSD density, enabling businesses to achieve remarkable storage capacities of up to 122.88 TB. That represents up to nearly 2 PB in a compact 1U Hypertec storage space.
This density advantage is particularly crucial in immersion-cooled environments like the DUG Nomad, where physical space is very limited and every rack unit counts. Nomad customers can significantly optimize their storage footprint per unit thanks to Solidigm's ultra-dense storage solutions, freeing up valuable storage capacity for GPU servers. This strategic balance between dense, efficient storage and powerful compute resources maximizes the overall performance and capabilities of edge deployments, making Solidigm's dense SSDs the cornerstone of advanced, space-optimized data center designs.
That said, if customer needs require higher-performance storage, Solidigm offers the new D7-PS1010 SSD lineup. These drives leverage a PCIe Gen5 interface, delivering substantial improvements in throughput and latency, essential for real-time data analytics and demanding AI workloads. Speaking of immersion cooling, Solidigm is at the forefront of liquid cooling. The company recently launched the world's first liquid-cooled enterprise SSDs and, in late 2018, examined the impact of different SSD power states on the overall energy consumption of liquid-cooled servers.
Conclusion
The DUG Nomad embodies the future of immersion-cooled mobile data centers, delivering reliable, high-performance compute, GPU, and storage capabilities regardless of where data is generated. By combining DUG's proven immersion technology (over 400 immersion tanks are used in its own data center) with dedicated Hypertec servers and Solidigm's ultra-dense flash storage, this platform embodies the possibilities offered by edge computing. Whether deployed in remote deserts, on ships, or in urban environments, the Nomad delivers data-center-grade performance where traditional infrastructure cannot reach.
