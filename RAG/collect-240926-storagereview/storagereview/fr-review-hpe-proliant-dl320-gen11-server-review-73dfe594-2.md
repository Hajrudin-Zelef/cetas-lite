# fr-review-hpe-proliant-dl320-gen11-server-review-73dfe594

The HPE ProLiant DL320 Gen11, a 1U uniprocessor server, is designed to deliver an optimal combination of flexibility, density, and cost-effectiveness, making it a formidable choice in the modern IT landscape. Traditionally aligned with ROBO (Remote Office/Branch Office) and small-scale deployments, the DL320 family is stepping up its game with the latest iteration, introducing unexpected improvements that extend its applicability.
At its core, the DL320 Gen11 supports Intel Xeon processors with up to 32 cores. This server is further complemented by 16 DDR5 DIMM slots and 80x PCIe Gen5 lanes, laying the foundation for a system that not only meets the diverse needs of today's businesses but does so while keeping an eye on future scalability and performance.
At the heart of the DL320 Gen11 lies the power of 4th generation Intel Xeon Scalable processors, supporting up to 32 cores and 270 W. This robust processing capability is complemented by memory capacity of up to 2 TB at 4800 MT/s and high-speed PCIe Gen5 slots, which can accommodate up to four single-width GPUs or two double-width GPUs. This configuration positions the DL320 Gen11 as a powerful yet cost-effective solution, ideal for performance-demanding applications in a compact 1U form factor.
The rear panel layout of all HPE ProLiant DL320 Gen11 configurations is uniformly designed for consistency and ease of use. It includes slots 1 and 2 PCIe5 x16 FHHL for primary and secondary risers, a UID LED indicator for easy server identification, and an optional NS204i-u boot device for enhanced boot capabilities. The NS204i-u contains two m.2 SSDs, providing hot-swappable redundant boot storage. For connectivity, there are two USB 3.2 Gen1 ports as well as two hot-plug power supplies for reliable power management.
A dedicated iLO management port is included for advanced server management and diagnostics, while an OCP 3.0 slot adds further extensibility. An integrated 1 × 2 network port facilitates network connectivity, and additional interfaces include a video port (VGA) for local display and an optional serial port for legacy devices or other connectivity needs.
Internally, the HPE ProLiant DL320 houses two M.2 module connectors (not visible), offering flexible options for additional SSDs. The server's extensibility is further emphasized by two full-height, half-length (FHHL) PCIe5 x16 slots (primary and secondary risers), allowing for expansion and customization.
Like its other Gen11 counterparts, the DL320 is designed for hybrid environments, offering simple management of compute resources from edge to cloud. The inclusion of HPE GreenLake for Compute Ops Management enhances this experience by providing a simplified IT management platform as a service.
HPE ProLiant DL320 Gen11 Processor Model Support
Processor models compatible with the DL320 Gen11 range from 4th generation Intel Xeon processors, such as Bronze models (for good value), to Gold models (for superior performance), thus highlighting the server's adaptability to different business needs and budget constraints. The DL360 Gen11, meanwhile, uses high-end Silver and Gold processors, making it ideal for demanding data center environments where maximum performance and scalability are essential.
Let's take a closer look at the HPE ProLiant DL320 Gen11 processor offerings, showcasing the breadth and depth of its processing capabilities at different tiers.
At the entry level, the lineup offers the Intel Xeon Bronze 3408U processor, an 8-core processor clocked at 1.8 GHz, consuming 125 W and featuring 22.5 MB of L3 cache. Designed for optimal energy efficiency and affordability, this processor is ideal for basic computing tasks and environments where power consumption is an important factor.
In the mid-range segment, the DL320 Gen11 offers several Intel Xeon Silver processors, such as the 4410Y and 4416+. The 4410Y is a 12-core processor clocked at 2.0 GHz, consuming 150 W and featuring 30 MB of L3 cache, delivering balanced performance for mid-sized workloads. The 4416+, meanwhile, offers superior performance with its 20 cores, also running at 2.0 GHz, but with a slightly higher consumption of 165 W and 37.5 MB of L3 cache, making it suitable for more intensive tasks within mid-sized business environments.
It is at the high-end processor level of the DL320 Gen11 server that it truly stands out, thanks to a selection of Intel Xeon Gold processors. Among them, the 5416S, a 16-core processor clocked at 2.0 GHz, consuming 150 W and featuring 30 MB of L3 cache, is perfectly suited for demanding SMB applications, such as virtualization and database management. The offering extends up to the 6454S, which includes 32 cores, a clock frequency of 2.2 GHz, a substantial power consumption of 270 W, and an impressive 60 MB of L3 cache. This processor is designed for the most demanding applications and can handle advanced data analytics, complex virtualization environments, and high-traffic web applications.
With each increase in processor tier, from Bronze to Silver and then to Gold, the DL320 Gen11 demonstrates a growing ability to handle more complex, more data-intensive simultaneous tasks, offering businesses a wide range of options to meet their specific performance needs and budget constraints. This wide range of processor choices underscores the DL320 Gen11's adaptability and its ability to support evolving business requirements while maintaining a balance between power, performance, and cost-effectiveness.

HPE ProLiant DL320 Gen11 – GPU/Storage Variants
There are several different physical configurations of the DL320 Gen11, giving customers the flexibility to adjust their storage and expansion options based on their workload.
Our standard revision model supports 8 +2 2.5″ drives with a few different backplane options. HPE also offers the server with a 4x 3.5-inch hard drive configuration for use cases that require more capacity than performance. Both of these offerings are fairly standard in this category. But then HPE gets a little wild.
They offer a 12x HDD version of the DL320 Gen11, something you typically don't see on an entry-level server. The 12x LFF drive cage is arranged in a 3 × 4 row layout. This large drive cage provides a significant increase in storage capacity suited to data-intensive applications. This version of the server is a bit longer to accommodate the depth needed to house the hard drives.
