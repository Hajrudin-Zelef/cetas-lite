Dell's cable management system clears the center of the chassis for direct and optimal airflow over the components that need it most.
Topology and data flow of a PCIe switch
The PCIe subsystem of the XE7740 is built around four PCIe Gen 5 switches on the PCIe baseboard (PBB), designated SW1 through SW4. These switches form the backbone of the system's I/O architecture, connecting accelerators, networking, and storage to the two Xeon 6 processors in a carefully organized topology.
The 16 internal GPU slots are divided into two groups of eight, each group served by a pair of PCIe switches, each connected upstream to a processor. Within each group, pairs of adjacent double-width GPU slots are interleaved between the two switches. On the CPU0 side, switch SW1 serves GPU slots 21 and 25, as well as rear PCIe slots 8 and 9, while switch SW2 serves GPU slots 23 and 27, as well as rear PCIe slots 6 and 7. On the CPU1 side, switch SW3 serves GPU slots 29 and 33, as well as rear PCIe slots 3 and 4, while switch SW4 serves GPU slots 31 and 35, as well as rear PCIe slots 1 and 2.
Each CPU domain therefore has four double-width accelerator slots, four rear NIC/I/O slots, and four of the eight front E3.S NVMe storage bays. This switching topology has important implications for data flow, particularly for RDMA traffic. With each switch hosting both accelerator slots and rear NIC slots, an accelerator and a network card on the same switch can perform RDMA transfers entirely within the switching fabric. Data never needs to transit through the root processor, eliminating the CPU bounce buffer that would otherwise be required when a PCIe device on one root port communicates with a device on another. This reduces latency, avoids consuming valuable processor memory bandwidth, and frees up CPU cycles for other tasks.
Communication within the domain of the same processor, between its two switches, transits through the processor root complex but remains local to that socket. By contrast, inter-processor communication must use the UPI links between the two Xeon 6 processors. The 6787P provides four UPI 2.0 links at 24 GT/s each, offering significant inter-socket bandwidth. However, traffic between an accelerator in the switch bank of processor 0 and a network card in the switch bank of processor 1 will inherently exhibit higher latency than local transfers within the same switch or the same socket.
The switches themselves are not directly interconnected. All inter-switch traffic transits through the main processor; it is therefore important to understand the relationships between GPU slots, NIC slots, storage, and CPU sockets. To simplify this complexity for enterprises, Dell offers validated and optimized configurations for the most common accelerators.
Dual-zone power
The power supply of the XE7740 reflects its thermal architecture with a rather unusual dual-zone power design. The system supports up to eight hot-swappable power supply units, divided into two zones: zone 1 (CPU zone) houses power supplies 1 and 2, while zone 2 (GPU zone) houses power supplies 3 through 8.
The system requires at least one power supply per zone to ensure BMC power and backup power. In the event of a power loss in either zone while the system is operating, the system shuts down immediately to prevent data loss. The zones are interdependent for their operation, even though they are physically and electrically separated. For full redundancy, Dell recommends a 1+1 configuration in the CPU zone and a 3+3 configuration in the GPU zone, meaning that all eight power supply slots must be occupied for a fully redundant deployment.
Dell AIOps, management, and enterprise reliability
Dell's PowerEdge platform has built a solid reputation in the industry for its reliability and ease of maintenance. Enterprise customers regularly highlight the same points: PowerEdge systems are built to last, Dell's support service resolves issues quickly, and the management tools are performant and seamlessly integrated. The XE7740 continues this tradition, and Dell has made significant progress in hardware management and security with this generation.
iDRAC 10
The XE7740 server ships with Dell's next-generation iDRAC 10, a major redesign of iDRAC 9, which was already performant and used by Dell customers for years. Integrated as a secure data center management module (DC-SCM) compliant with the OCP DC-MHS standard, iDRAC 10 is not merely a firmware update; it is new hardware. The controller integrates four cores clocked at 1 GHz with a 64-bit architecture and 2 GB of DDR4 memory (double the previous generation), delivering significantly superior performance and responsiveness for management operations.
In terms of security, iDRAC 10 introduces several notable improvements. The platform offers enhanced cryptographic support at all levels, including SHA-384 and SHA-512 authentication and quantum-resistant AES-256 encryption, as the industry prepares for post-quantum cryptographic threats. A dedicated integrated security enclave within the iDRAC 10 chip handles cyber-resilience functions, such as device-level attestation and Dell's custom root of trust. This hardware root of trust ensures that all firmware (BIOS, iDRAC, and component firmware) is cryptographically verified before execution, protecting against supply chain attacks and firmware tampering.
Secure component verification ensures that systems shipped by Dell comply with customer specifications in terms of components and configurations, ensuring their integrity from manufacturing to deployment. The latest iDRAC 10 firmware update also offers a redesigned, modular user interface, simplifying day-to-day administration.
OpenManage Enterprise
For managing large-scale IT fleets, Dell OpenManage Enterprise centralizes monitoring, firmware updates, and configuration management for all PowerEdge deployments. Among the important new features for AI-dedicated deployments, OME now offers direct visibility into GPU and accelerator statistics: power consumption, temperature, utilization, error counts, etc., without requiring vendor-specific tools. For enterprises managing dozens, or even hundreds, of XE7740 nodes in an inference cluster, this unified management platform represents a significant operational simplification.
Intel Xeon 6
At the heart of the XE7740 are two Intel Xeon 6 6787P processors, the flagship of the Xeon 6700P series. Based on the Granite Rapids architecture and using Intel 3 nm technology, the 6787P offers 86 processing cores (172 threads) per socket for a TDP of 350 W, with a base frequency of 2.0 GHz and a turbo frequency of 3.8 GHz.
What makes Granite Rapids particularly relevant for AI infrastructure is the combination of a large number of cores and its memory subsystem. Each 6787P processor offers eight DDR5 memory channels up to 6,400 MT/s. With a dual-socket XE7740 server equipped with 32 DIMM modules, the system can be configured for a total memory capacity of up to 4 TB.
Memory capacity and bandwidth are essential for AI workloads, particularly when using key-value cache offload. As complex language models increase context length, the key-value cache grows proportionally and can consume a significant amount of accelerator memory. Offloading part of the key-value cache to system memory or fast storage allows more efficient use of the accelerator's HBM for active computation, reducing time to first token (TTFT) for multi-turn conversations.
