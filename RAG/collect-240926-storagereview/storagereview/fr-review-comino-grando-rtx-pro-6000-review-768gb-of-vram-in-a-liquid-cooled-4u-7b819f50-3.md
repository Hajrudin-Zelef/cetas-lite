# fr-review-comino-grando-rtx-pro-6000-review-768gb-of-vram-in-a-liquid-cooled-4u--7b819f50

At the heart of the system is a large water distribution manifold that supplies coolant to each GPU and CPU cold plate and ensures return to the radiator. All connections between the manifold and the GPUs and CPU use Comino's "TheQ" quick-connects. These stainless steel, leak-proof connectors feature red and blue rings to clearly identify the hot and cold sides of the loop, eliminating any ambiguity during installation or maintenance.
They leave minimal residue on the connection surface upon disconnection, allowing technicians to individually remove or replace GPUs or the CPU without draining the 450 ml reservoir or the rest of the loop. The Grando thus offers the maintenance simplicity of air-cooled systems on a high-performance liquid cooling platform.
CPU and Memory Cooling
The processor and its voltage regulators also benefit from a dedicated cold plate, directly connected to the cooling loop, preventing the processor from becoming a bottleneck under heavy multi-GPU workloads. Our test model was equipped with an AMD Turin/Genoa motherboard featuring an AMD EPYC 9474F 48-core processor. The cold plate, machined from solid copper and secured with stainless steel screws, offers the same quality as those on the graphics cards.
On either side of the processor are eight fully populated DRAM slots, compatible with configurations up to 2 TB of RAM. Our test model was equipped with 512 GB of DDR5 RAM. A support bar, perpendicular to the processor and GPU, is placed above the chassis, ensuring the safety of sensitive components like the GPU and the rigidity of the chassis during transport.
Radiator and Fans
Cooling is provided by an imposing triple 140 mm radiator mounted at the rear of the chassis, combined with three 140 mm high-speed fans capable of reaching 6,200 RPM and an airflow of 1,000 m³/h. The fin density of this thick radiator underscores the thermal headroom built into the platform, designed to dissipate up to 6.5 kW of continuous heat in our configuration.
Perhaps most surprising is that, despite this workload and fan speeds, the unit remains within an acceptable noise level range, with sounds exceeding 70 dB at full power. This is loud for a workstation but remarkably moderate for a system dissipating the heat generated by a small electric oven, demonstrating the effectiveness of Comino's liquid cooling loop in removing heat from components.
Front Panel and Telemetry Display
On the front, an LED screen displays key telemetry data in real time, including pump status, ambient temperature, coolant temperature, and fan speed. Menu navigation is done using the illuminated buttons on the cooling module: a short press scrolls through the available data. A long press on the PB2 button opens additional submenus, such as controls, service settings, and the event log. Additionally, the front I/O panel includes a VGA port for display, as well as a serial port, multiple USB ports, and network connections for connecting peripherals.
Power and Storage Architecture
Power Supply and Redundancy
To support such computing power, an equally robust power supply is essential. The Grando supports up to four hot-swappable 1,000 W or 2,000 W CRPS modules in a redundant configuration, providing up to 8.0 kW at a voltage of 180 to 264 V. Thanks to support for 4+0, 3+1, and 2+2 redundancy modes, the system can tolerate power failures while ensuring continuous 24/7 operation for AI and high-performance computing workloads.
Our test unit shipped with four hot-swappable Great Wall 2000W 80 Plus Platinum power supplies, forming the complete 8.0 kW configuration.
Power to each GPU runs through a centralized 12-pin power distribution board, mounted between the GPU matrix and the main cable. The Grando uses this board to centralize incoming power and then distribute it to each GPU in an organized and compact manner.
PCIe, Storage, and Networking
The Grando supports six GPUs without compromising slot bandwidth, and its chassis can accommodate up to eight cards for maximum density. Comino's ASRock Rack GENOAD8X-2T/BCM motherboard offers seven PCIe Gen 5 x16 slots and one x8, meaning seven of the eight GPUs run at full x16 bandwidth, with the eighth card running at x8. This is a trade-off between the number of PCIe lanes a single-socket processor can handle and Comino's reluctance to add the bulk, cost, and complexity of a PCIe switch board. Opting for a dual-socket motherboard would offer more PCIe lanes but even fewer slots, as the second socket would occupy the space normally reserved for PCIe slots in this compact form factor.
Using eight GPUs in a single-processor system consumes nearly all available PCIe lanes, which implies trade-offs. Our test model, based on AMD Genoa, has 128 PCIe Gen 5 lanes. With eight GPUs occupying 120 of these lanes, the remaining 8 lanes are split into four for each M.2 SSD slot. It is therefore impossible to simultaneously use eight GPUs and a full set of NVMe drives at the rear of the case, connected via the two MCIO connectors. In our eight-GPU configuration, only two M.2 slots were available for storage. Administrators who need additional NVMe capacity while maintaining maximum GPU density should know that adding hot-swappable NVMe drives at the rear, via the rear panel cages, will consume additional PCIe lanes and reduce their system's GPU capacity.
That said, storage is equally modular and offers large capacity, even though the configuration affects the number of PCIe lanes available for GPUs. It is therefore important to take this into account when planning usage. The rear panel of our test model features a 2.5-inch drive cage that can accommodate up to four 2.5-inch SSDs, either in a 4 x 7 mm or 2 x 15 mm configuration. A second optional configuration, which can accommodate up to four SSDs, is available in place of the fourth power supply slot. Since our test model required all four power supply slots to support the full 8-GPU configuration, we only had access to the first of the two hot-swap bays. Internally, the chassis can accommodate a 3.5-inch cage that can hold up to four 3.5-inch drives, four 15 mm 2.5-inch drives, or up to twelve 7 mm 2.5-inch drives, as well as four additional internal SSD slots for 7 mm 2.5-inch drives, optionally.
For network connectivity, the motherboard includes two 10 Gb/s RJ45 ports (driven by the Broadcom BCM57416) as well as a dedicated Gigabit Ethernet IPMI management port. Administrators can increase bandwidth by installing compatible PCIe network cards up to 400 Gb/s for high-speed network connectivity. Note: these additional PCIe network cards occupy GPU slots, thereby reducing the maximum number of GPUs the system can support.
Remote Management and System Intelligence
To protect hardware and optimize performance, the system incorporates the Comino Monitoring System (CMS). A separate, standalone controller board drives the CMS and acts as the server's "brain," independent of the main operating system. Specifically, this controller analyzes real-time data from numerous sensors that monitor air and coolant temperature, humidity level, coolant flow rate, and reservoir levels. Thanks to this standalone design, the CMS can perform self-diagnostics and trigger emergency shutdowns in the event of a leak or pump failure, thereby protecting expensive internal components.
A web GUI provides daily management, giving administrators full visibility into cooling performance, availability, and real-time power consumption of the processor and graphics cards. For enterprise-scale deployments, the CMS also connects to centralized monitoring tools via REST APIs, such as Zabbix, Grafana, and InfluxDB. All of these features allow administrators to ensure a three-year maintenance interval and optimal server operation, without thermal throttling, even in high-ambient-temperature environments.
