# fr-review-small-stack-big-impact-hpe-simplivity-and-vm-essentials-simplify-enter-c03a5525

<!-- source: https://www.storagereview.com/fr/review/small-stack-big-impact-hpe-simplivity-and-vm-essentials-simplify-enterprise-virtualization -->

HPE SimpliVity has long been renowned for its hyperconverged infrastructure simplifying IT in resource-constrained environments. Today, with the integration of HPE Morpheus VM Essentials software, HPE goes even further by offering a fully integrated platform where HPE provides the hardware, the hypervisor, and a single point of support. This platform is specifically designed for small businesses, point-of-sale deployments, and edge sites that require enterprise-grade reliability without unnecessary complexity. By bringing together HPE SimpliVity's always-on efficiency, native backup, disaster recovery, and high availability in just two nodes, HPE offers a cost-effective solution to modernize infrastructure while reducing operational costs and simplifying support.
Unlike other hyperconverged platforms that require at least three or four nodes to ensure meaningful redundancy, HPE SimpliVity achieves full high availability with only two nodes. This significantly lowers the barriers to entry while maintaining enterprise-grade resilience, a key advantage for small IT teams and distributed environments where every dollar and every rack unit counts. Combined with integrated virtualization and unified management from VM Essentials, this approach eliminates the licensing complexity and architectural sprawl often associated with other market offerings. It provides a simple, straightforward solution for deploying and scaling IT at the edge or across remote sites.
Key takeaways
- High availability in just two nodes delivers enterprise-grade resilience without requiring large cluster investments.
- Virtual machine provisioning takes less than a minute, with automated backup policies and simplified resource assignment.
- Built-in data protection includes inline deduplication and persistent backups, even after a virtual machine is deleted.
- A single, unified support stack means no need to juggle vendors or compatibility lists; everything is validated and supported by HPE.
- Private cloud capabilities, such as reusable compute plans and centralized image libraries, make self-service simple and consistent.
What is HPE Morpheus VM Essentials software?
VM Essentials is a KVM-based hypervisor and management layer that transforms SimpliVity into a turnkey virtualization platform. Unlike traditional stacks, VM Essentials tightly integrates compute, storage, virtualization, and backup within a unified interface, simplifying deployment and management for small IT teams.
VM Essentials offers enterprise-grade capabilities, including two-node high availability, live migration, intelligent workload placement, and policy-based automation, without the complexity of separate hypervisor licenses. Provisioning automation also extends to VMware vSphere virtual machines, allowing customers to manage both hypervisor types through the same wizard or REST APIs.
The deep integration goes beyond software. VM Essentials relies on reference architectures, validated hardware compatibility matrices, and integration with external storage (NFS, iSCSI, Fibre Channel) to optimize deployments. Within the same ecosystem, the underlying firmware, hypervisor, and management stack are co-tested and supported by HPE.
Because VM Essentials is tightly integrated with HPE SimpliVity and benefits from HPE's deep engineering resources, it is not a static solution. HPE is committed to rapidly evolving the platform, adding new features based on customer feedback and real-world needs. Since its launch, VM Essentials has delivered improvements such as GPU passthrough, virtual CD-ROM support, AppArmor plug-ins to secure hypervisor operations, and cluster layout optimization. These updates underscore HPE's investment in making VM Essentials a practical and comprehensive platform, designed for edge deployments, retail environments, and small IT teams that need enterprise-grade capabilities without unnecessary overhead.
As customers grow, the same VM Essentials infrastructure can be seamlessly upgraded to HPE Morpheus Enterprise Software, providing multi-hypervisor orchestration, governance, and automation across hybrid cloud environments, from edge to core.
What stands out: our favorite SimpliVity features with VM Essentials
With the availability of VM Essentials for HPE SimpliVity, we were able to test the platform before it became available to customers. HPE gave us access to several clusters, which allowed us to explore the solution in real-world configurations. We also went on-site with HPE to deepen our understanding of the management experience, performance, and operational workflows. This hands-on experience allowed us to see how SimpliVity with VM Essentials constitutes a fully integrated platform. With this foundation, we can explore some key elements of the solution and demonstrate its strengths.
1) Enterprise-class high availability, without the complexity
During our first evaluation of VM Essentials with SimpliVity, we were particularly impressed by its ability to deliver true high availability in a compact, resource-efficient design. Even in a minimal two-node cluster, VM Essentials ensures resilience through an external witness, which maintains synchronization and guarantees workload availability in the event of a node failure. This witness can reside in different locations and, although not officially supported at present, even a cloud-based witness can work if the relatively low latency specification is met.
This configuration ensures availability and reinforces the platform's lightweight nature. For organizations with limited space, power, or IT staff (frequent constraints at edge sites and small offices), running a complete, enterprise-grade stack with only two nodes represents a considerable advantage. The absence of dedicated quorum nodes or external storage reduces hardware and management costs.
When clusters reach three or four nodes, the external witness is no longer needed. Synchronization and quorum are managed internally, allowing the system to scale while retaining its simplicity. HPE recommends limiting clusters to four nodes to optimize performance, cost, and operational clarity. This architecture is therefore a smart choice for organizations looking to benefit from private cloud capabilities without the bulk and complexity of larger systems. If customers need a larger environment with the ability to scale storage and compute resources independently, HPE also offers VM Essentials as part of its Private Cloud Business Edition solution.
2) From zero to protected in less than a minute
HPE VM Essentials provisioning is one of its most powerful features. Virtual machines can be deployed in less than a minute after an administrator clicks the "Add" button in the "Provisioning" tab. The process launches an intuitive and detailed user interface that guides administrators through VM creation with simplicity similar to VMware, but with increased flexibility built directly into the workflow.
The workflow begins with selecting the instance group and cloud, then assigning the virtual machine to the appropriate environment. Next, administrators configure the compute plan, resource pool, and volumes. Volumes can be assigned automatically for quick configuration or mapped to specific datastores for more precise control. Network configuration is just as simple, allowing a VM to be linked to the right segment with minimal effort.
What makes VM Essentials unique is the integration of automation tasks and backup policies directly into the provisioning process. Administrators can apply preconfigured backup policies during deployment, ensuring automatic protection of every new VM from the moment it launches. These policies define frequency, retention, and storage location, and can use local or remote SimpliVity clusters. Because backups are integrated into provisioning, recovery points are guaranteed without administrator intervention.
The final verification step provides a clear summary of the virtual machine, including compute resources, network, volumes, and the assigned backup policy. Once confirmed, the virtual machine is deployed in just a few minutes, with automation and protection applied.
This streamlined provisioning workflow makes it possible to deliver production-ready workloads in a fraction of the time required by traditional methods, while ensuring consistency, resilience, and governance are maintained across the entire environment.
3) Private cloud-like efficiency with reusable compute plans
A unique feature we discovered while getting hands-on with VM Essentials is its ability to design VM deployment through the lens of a private cloud. Instead of relying on static templates or manual VM sizing, administrators can create plans that act as reusable compute profiles. Each plan defines the number of CPU cores, amount of memory, storage, GPU resources (if applicable), and even the provisioning type (for example, KVM).
This allows IT teams to present users with roles in a self-service catalog of options, such as CPU-intensive, memory-intensive, or GPU-accelerated virtual machines. End users don't need to know the intricacies of resource allocation; they simply select the plan suited to their workload, and the system provisions a VM with consistent, policy-based settings.
HPE positions this approach as a private cloud experience within the data center. It not only improves IT staff efficiency by reducing configuration errors and manual setups, but also aligns with cloud-like consumption, where VM profiles are predefined and ready to launch on demand. This enables businesses to scale their production workloads while maintaining governance and standardization.
4) Built-in, policy-based backup and recovery
Data resilience is a cornerstone of any hyperconverged infrastructure (HCI) solution, and HPE VM Essentials with HPE SimpliVity meets this need through its simplicity, efficiency, and robust protection mechanisms. As soon as a virtual machine (VM) is deployed, administrators can apply preconfigured backup policies defining retention schedules, frequency, and storage targets. In a few clicks, backups run automatically, whether stored locally or replicated to remote SimpliVity clusters for greater resilience.
At the heart of this feature is inline deduplication, which eliminates redundant data blocks before they are written to storage. Only unique data is retained, significantly reducing backup size without impacting performance. This ensures backup efficiency (even when protecting large data sets), while avoiding unnecessary I/O overhead during backup, mirroring, or snapshot operations. HPE states that SimpliVity often achieves data reduction ratios of up to 40:1, with compression further improving efficiency at an average ratio of 1.5:1. The result is a faster, more space-efficient, and highly scalable backup process.
In HPE VM Essentials, backup visualization and management are simplified compared to other HCI solutions. In the Backups > Backups section, administrators can quickly view all backup instances created. Each entry displays the timestamp of the last backup, its duration, the total size of the protected VM, the number of available backups, and the success of the task. This simplified view provides clear, immediate visibility into backup status, allowing administrators to easily confirm protection status and intervene if necessary.
In the event of a disaster, such as accidental deletion of a VM, HPE SimpliVity ensures fast and reliable recovery. Additionally, SimpliVity backups in VM Essentials are retained independently of the VM. So even if a critical workload is deleted, administrators can access the Backups page, select the affected VM, and restore it directly. By separating backups from the VM lifecycle, HPE provides protection that simplifies disaster recovery and minimizes downtime.
We tested this method to see how simple and fast the process is. After deleting a VM, we accessed the "Backups" page, selected our Ubuntu-Host entry, and from the "Actions" menu, chose "Restore." After clicking "Restore," the GUI prompts you to create a new instance of the VM from the backup. Then select the group the VM will belong to, assign a name to the restored instance, and select the datastore where it will reside. This simple process ensures the restored VM is properly placed in your environment, giving you control over organization and storage placement, while simplifying the recovery process.
Within seconds, the virtual machine was back online, demonstrating the effectiveness of the SimpliVity approach to simplifying disaster recovery while minimizing downtime. Of course, results vary depending on the size of the virtual machine and the volume of data to be restored.
5) Unified support matrix: a single source of truth across the stack
With the complete solution offered by HPE (SimpliVity software, ProLiant servers, HPE switching, and the VM Essentials hypervisor layer), customers benefit from a unified support matrix that simplifies lifecycle management. This eliminates the uncertainties associated with multi-vendor mesh solutions. Whether you're planning updates, deploying new clusters, or troubleshooting a remote site, HPE provides a comprehensive, engineered, and up-to-date compatibility guide.
Every component of the SimpliVity with VM Essentials stack has been tested and validated by HPE engineering. This includes the hypervisor, drivers, firmware, and built-in data protection features. The result is a pre-qualified matrix that ensures your deployment goes smoothly, with no risk of incompatibility. IT teams no longer need to consult multiple vendor documents or rely on untested configurations. Everything is aligned and fully supported from initial configuration. This model is particularly beneficial for organizations managing many small sites, such as retailers, banks, or regional offices. It enables consistent, repeatable deployments, systematically using the same validated stack. Even when these deployments are managed by small IT teams or remotely, the unified support structure simplifies operations, reduces overhead, and minimizes support complexity.
6) Centralized image library for easy deployment
The HPE VM Essentials library serves as a one-stop shop for virtual machine images, simplifying the management and deployment of operating systems or preconfigured templates. Administrators can import ISO files or custom VM images directly into the library, making them immediately available for provisioning new instances. This means no more constantly searching for installation media or managing scattered image repositories—everything is centralized and consistent.
By supporting both raw ISOs and preconfigured images, the library simplifies deployment workflows for both testing and production. For example, administrators can maintain a catalog of standard operating systems, as well as reference images that already include enterprise applications or baseline configurations. When creating a new VM, the appropriate image can be selected directly from the library, significantly reducing the steps needed to get workloads running.

HPE SimpliVity with VM Essentials did more than check boxes during our testing. Its design was thoughtfully crafted for small teams that nonetheless need enterprise discipline. Two-node high availability remains the highlight, but what convinced us was its operational simplicity. The external witness simplifies quorum without requiring a third node, and once you reach three or four nodes, you can drop it and keep going. This gives you real resilience in a footprint perfectly suited to edge cabinets and retail back rooms.
Provisioning gave us plenty to be pleased about. The wizard is fast, clear, and insightful—and relevantly so. We were able to configure a VM in under a minute, attach a backup policy as part of the same process, and move on. No side tasks to add protection later. Deleting that VM and restoring it from the Backups view took a few clicks and only a few seconds in our case, which is precisely the experience administrators want when something goes wrong. Day-to-day backup visibility and control are accessible directly from the VM panel, without having to resort to external applications. The quality-of-life improvement is a major asset.
Reusable compute plans let you publish sensible sizes, such as high-CPU or high-memory workloads, and maintain self-service consistency without users becoming platform engineers. The centralized image library eliminates hunting for ISOs and standard images. Additionally, VM sizing characteristics can be assigned to specific images to help users deploy their applications correctly. Moreover, the unified support matrix is more important than it appears. With a single vendor validating firmware, drivers, hypervisor, and data protection, repeatable branch deployments become a practical guide, not a project.
The scope and trajectory are also clear. This stack is optimized for edge sites and small core sites that need private cloud capabilities without license proliferation or heavy clusters. If you later need greater scalability or mixed hypervisors, migrate to HPE Morpheus Enterprise and the full Private Cloud Business Edition to gain orchestration and governance without starting from scratch.
