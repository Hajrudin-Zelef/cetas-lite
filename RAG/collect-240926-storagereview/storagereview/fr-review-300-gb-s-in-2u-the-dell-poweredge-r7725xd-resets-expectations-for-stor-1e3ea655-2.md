# fr-review-300-gb-s-in-2u-the-dell-poweredge-r7725xd-resets-expectations-for-stor-1e3ea655

Some servers extend existing capabilities, while others redefine expectations. The Dell PowerEdge R7725xd belongs to the second category. In our recent tests, configured with 24 Micron 9550 PRO PCIe Gen5 NVMe SSDs and four 2x 200 GbE network cards, this 2U server achieved raw storage throughput higher than any system we have measured to date. Internally, the platform sustained over 300 GB/s across the entire NVMe pool. On the network, it delivered 160 GB/s via standard RDMA, without any additional complexity.
This is not simply a faster storage server. It is a system that revolutionizes the architecture of data-intensive computing. Modern AI training and inference pipelines are often limited not by GPU power, but by the speed at which data can be prepared, streamed, shuffled, and checkpointed. High-capacity GPU nodes will remain idle if storage cannot keep up. To overcome these limitations, teams use caches, overprovisioning, and complex tiering to ensure that accelerators receive data fast enough to justify their cost.
The Dell PowerEdge R7725xd eliminates the bottleneck at the source. It is built around a 24-bay U.2 backplane, with each drive benefiting from a dedicated PCIe Gen5 x4 link directly to the AMD EPYC processor. The absence of bandwidth sharing and backplane expansion reduces contention. Performance scales seamlessly thanks to a hardware architecture that aggregates throughput without contention. In a typical dual-socket configuration, the processors are connected by four XGMI links for inter-socket communication. In the R7725xd, one of these links is reallocated to provide 16 additional PCIe Gen5 lanes per processor, bringing the server total to 160 PCIe Gen5 lanes (96 for the front SSDs and 64 for the four rear PCIe slots). Combined with Micron 9550 PRO SSDs, designed for sustained write workloads and high endurance, the system becomes a high-throughput data engine capable of supporting checkpoint-intensive and streaming workloads.
We layered PEAK:AIO on top of this architecture to leverage parallel submission paths and maintain efficiency despite increased concurrency. The result: optimal and consistent performance, even under heavy load. The platform can function as a local execution node for preprocessing, training, or transformation, or as a high-bandwidth storage space distributed across multiple GPU systems via the network. For the more daring, it is even possible to combine both.
Key takeaways
- Unprecedented throughput on a single node: The R7725xd sustained internal bandwidth of over 300 GB/s and 160 GB/s via NVMe-oF RDMA, rivaling multi-node storage clusters within a 2U chassis.
- True Gen5 architecture, no switches, no lane distribution: All 24 Micron 9550 PRO SSDs receive dedicated PCIe Gen5 x4 lanes directly from the processor, enabling maximum-throughput scaling without contention.
- Powered by AMD EPYC 9005 series processors: The two AMD EPYC 9575F processors provide the lane count, memory bandwidth, and NUMA topology needed for sustained high-concurrency I/O.
- Designed for AI-intensive, analytics, and checkpoint workloads: This system eliminates the I/O bottlenecks that slow down modern GPU pipelines, enabling continuous, high-bandwidth data transmission.
- PEAK:AIO unlocks full parallelism: The PEAK:AIO software stack keeps queue structures saturated under load, delivering enterprise-grade performance at a very favorable dollar-per-GB ratio.
Designed specifically to optimize NVMe throughput
In its latest generation of servers, Dell abandoned the use of PCIe switches in high-density storage configurations. Models like the PowerEdge R770 or R7725 offer PCIe Gen5 x4 bays, supporting up to 16 SSDs, and switch to x2 bays for configurations with a larger storage backplane. Previous-generation servers, such as the PowerEdge R760, included a PCIe switch in 24-bay NVMe configurations. To simplify assembly and eliminate the complexity associated with a PCIe switch, the new servers adopted a strategy of reducing the number of PCIe lanes in high-density storage configurations. At least, until the R7725xd.
The difference between the standard R7725 and the R7725xd lies in how the platforms allocate PCIe root complex resources. The basic R7725 distributes PCIe lanes among storage, GPU expansion, and general I/O. The "xd" variant reallocates these resources so that the NVMe subsystem becomes the primary consumer of PCIe bandwidth. The 24 U.2 bays are wired directly to the processor's PCIe Gen5 roots, with each SSD benefiting from its own x4 endpoint, rather than a shared uplink accessible via a PCIe switch or a reordering tree. This gives each drive independent queue structures and independent DMA paths to the memory controller.
The backplane and expansion card topology reflects this commitment. Dell groups the NVMe connectors and PCIe slots across both AMD EPYC sockets, so that each processor directly manages a portion of the drives. In practice, this creates two symmetric NVMe domains, each with its own latency characteristics and full read/write concurrency. When installing four Broadcom dual-port 200 GbE network cards as expansion cards, the slot positioning allowed each card to be integrated into a PCIe domain aligned with the corresponding NVMe group. In NVMe over RDMA, network traffic thus remained localized to the socket managing the associated drive's I/O, avoiding the inter-socket Infinity Fabric hop that typically adds latency and consumes bandwidth under load.
Thermal behavior also ensures sustained throughput. The U.2 interface remains advantageous in dense Gen5 configurations because it offers a defined airflow channel and a predictable heat dissipation surface for each device. The high-static-pressure fan modules and the chassis duct system of the R7725xd ensure constant airflow across all 24 bays, enabling full and continuous write workloads without throttling. The mechanical design complements the electrical design: each drive can maintain its maximum performance because the platform is designed to cool 24 Gen5 devices operating simultaneously at full load.
This combination of root complex alignment, consistent NUMA (non-uniform memory access) lane layout, socket-aware network card placement, and a thermally stable U.2 enclosure allows the system to achieve maximum-throughput I/O at scale. The architecture avoids bottlenecks and optimizes performance.
Overview of the Dell PowerEdge R7725xd iDRAC 10
This generation of the R7725xd, like many other 17th-generation platforms we have tested, incorporates Dell's new iDRAC 10 platform, which centralizes remote management, system health monitoring, and out-of-band control. The dashboard provides an immediate overview of overall system health, storage status, and recent activity. On our test unit, the system and storage health report is green, confirming normal server operation. Essential system information, such as model, hostname, BIOS version, iDRAC firmware level, IP address, and licensing information, is displayed on the right side of the interface.
The dashboard also includes a task summary panel displaying completed, pending, and in-progress operations. Below, a list of recent logs lists chassis intrusion events and power-related messages, providing a quick overview of hardware state changes without requiring navigation through menus. The virtual console panel is accessible in the lower right corner for full remote KVM control.
The storage section of iDRAC 10 offers a comprehensive overview of all physical drives installed in the R7725xd. The summary panel displays the total number of connected drives, accompanied by a pie chart illustrating their status. In this configuration, 24 NVMe SSDs are active and ready for use, and two additional boot devices are present in the system, separate from the main front NVMe bank.
