# fr-review-vergeio-high-performance-vmware-alternative-040958db

Businesses seeking alternatives to VMware can look to VergeIO, which offers high-performance software infrastructure that eliminates expensive add-ons. While most infrastructure software is designed from multiple distinct applications grouped behind a graphical user interface, VergeIO is unique in its integration of virtualization, storage services, and networking into a single software. The result is simplified operations and maximum hardware efficiency.
We first introduced Verge.io nearly two years ago, highlighting the need for hyperconverged alternatives in the face of uncertainty surrounding Broadcom's licensing policy for VMware. Since then, VMware's pricing model has become considerably clearer. Large enterprises that use most of VMware's features will likely continue to use it, but a large portion of the enterprise and SMB market will need an alternative.
In the complex world of enterprise virtualization, licensing often becomes a maze of confusion and unexpected costs. VergeIO's licensing model contrasts sharply with VMware, Windows Server, and Nutanix. At the same time, these competitors use complex licensing schemes based on cores, processors, or feature sets. Customers can configure four-processor servers with more than a hundred cores without having to explain why the software costs twice as much as the hardware. VergeIO opts for simplicity. VergeIO uses a per-node licensing model and, as mentioned earlier, includes all features in its base license. This means access to advanced storage tiering, disaster recovery, and other enterprise-grade features at no additional licensing cost. All while being 50% cheaper than VMWare.
We set up an All-Star kit in the Cincinnati lab to evaluate the VergeIO cluster. Our goal is to evaluate its features, functionality, and performance. This evaluation also highlights modern infrastructure trends. We use dense liquid-cooled nodes from Giga Computing. These nodes are equipped with dense, high-performance flash storage from Solidigm. To ensure compute performance is not an issue, we have AMD EPYC 9554 64-core high-efficiency processors, 512 GB of DDR5, and NVIDIA 100 GbE dual-port network cards.
Benefits of VergeIO
VergeOS, the cornerstone of VergeIO's offering, represents what they call ultraconverged infrastructure. Unlike VMware's product suite, VergeOS manages networking, storage, and compute in a complete package. This unified approach allows for simplified administration and license management. VergeIO aggressively positions itself as a VMware replacement, a strategy reflected in its product offerings.
Imagine a scenario that many businesses might find familiar: you are a large enterprise currently using the VMware stack, but the renewal quotes have shocked you. As you consider alternatives, VergeIO appears as a potential solution.
VergeIO offers one of the most comprehensive feature sets in the industry. It can be compared to cutting-edge solutions such as Nutanix and HyperV. Additionally, it includes notable features. We particularly appreciated the Storage Tiering feature. Moreover, during our testing, VergeIO's migration implementation proved to be the simplest and smoothest.
VergeOS supports native WireGuard integration for remote access. This can be configured for internal access within hypervisor networks or for external networks to access all resources in your infrastructure. The seamless implementation adds a layer of security and flexibility that many IT administrators will appreciate.
Another notable feature is VergeIO's catalog, which resembles the XenOrchestra Hub. The catalog includes various cloud-init configurations for one-click VM deployment, simplifying the process of creating new instances. Additionally, it allows for the creation of recipes and VM repositories. Combined with VergeIO's Terraform provider, these features can significantly accelerate orchestration processes.
VergeIO is not just about convenience. Maintaining high SLAs is essential for enterprise-grade virtualization. VergeOS is packed with features, supports high availability and live migration, allowing VMs to migrate between nodes in a cluster.
Regardless, disaster recovery is the area where VergeIO truly excels. First, VergeOS enables independent snapshots that act as clones. These snapshots do not depend on previous clones, allowing for better performance. This is due to their IOClone, which deduplicates on the fly. Furthermore, VergeIO goes even further. They encapsulate the entire data center into their multi-tenant virtual data center (VDC), which allows for replication and management of the data center as a single object.
Combined with IOGuardian, VergeIO offers near-real-time replication (with intervals as short as 15 minutes). This replication process also incorporates inline deduplication to ensure maximum disk utilization. Replication includes a complete replica of the original data center, including all configuration details. This ensures functionality even on different hardware configurations. Additionally, VergeIO allows leveraging IOGuardian replication for rapid recovery of replaced disks. These features allow users to implement a 3-2-1 backup strategy entirely within the VergeOS environment.
VergeIO's approach to system updates also helps maintain high SLAs. First, VergeOS operates on a quarterly update cycle, ensuring each release is mature and thoroughly tested. Then, updates are scheduled for automatic application. The system intelligently manages this process by migrating VMs and applying updates to one host at a time in maintenance mode. Each update has a system snapshot with a 12-hour lifespan for easy rollback.
VM Migration
When customers consider using VMware alternatives, migrating to a new platform is a major concern. Migration begins with a remarkably fast initial configuration of the VergeIO cluster with VSAN (which differs from VMware's vSAN). What is truly impressive is the migration itself, which can be completed in minutes.
The VergeIO dashboard has a Backup/DR tab that allows direct connection to your vCenter or ESXi using their native APIs. This feature allows you to view your VMware VMs and select those to copy. The system initiates a synchronization process, ensuring your VMs are regularly updated.
Once synchronization is established, you can begin automatic VM conversion during this process. At this point, VergeIO offers the ability to select your preferred storage tiers (a feature we will discuss later) and retain your MAC addresses for existing DHCP reservations. The ongoing synchronization process ensures your VMs remain up to date until you have completed testing and are ready to migrate completely, allowing you to confidently remove VMs from your VMware environment.
With your VMs now running on VergeOS, a world of management possibilities opens up. The platform allows creating sites, tenants, clusters, and data centers, enabling virtual segmentation of resources. Administrators can finely control user access, allowing them to manage specific resource sets. Like vCenter, the system meticulously logs all actions. VergeIO goes even further in security by offering the ability to protect the console with a password, adding an extra layer of protection and offering OIDC integration for SSO authentication.
VergeIO VSAN and Storage Tiering
One of the most notable features of VergeOS is its storage tiering capability. VergeOS allows users to store data on different types of media based on performance, availability, and recovery requirements. The system seamlessly integrates all of this into its vSAN. It is important to note that VergeIO makes this available in the base version of VergeOS with a single license and no additional fees for consumed capacity, which contrasts sharply with VMware's multiple licensing tiers.
