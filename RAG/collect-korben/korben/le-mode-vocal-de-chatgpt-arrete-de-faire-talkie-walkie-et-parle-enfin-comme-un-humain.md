## Full summary

The article reports that OpenAI launched GPT-Live, a new voice mode for ChatGPT that can speak and listen at the same time, finally removing the awkward silences of the classic "walkie-talkie" interaction where each party waits their turn. Until now, talking out loud with a chatbot was painful, resembling a walkie-talkie exchange with embarrassing pauses after every sentence fragment, constantly reminding you that you are talking to a machine rather than a friend on the line.

GPT-Live runs full-duplex, which changes everything. Like a real phone call where both speakers can genuinely talk at the same time without it becoming a mess. Concretely, you can interrupt it mid-sentence, ask it to slow down if it mumbles too fast, and it even knows to stay quiet when it senses you are searching for your words.

The cleverest part plays out when it needs to fetch information from the web. Instead of leaving you in heavy silence, it quietly hands the complex question to a more powerful model churning in the background, and keeps chatting with you while the answer surfaces. It also supports live translation, with a small delay, and can display on-screen cards for weather or a match score while talking to you.

On deployment, OpenAI did not waste time: it is already launched on iPhone, Android, the web and even CarPlay in the car. Free accounts get a lighter version called GPT-Live-1 mini, while Go, Plus and Pro subscribers get the full model, smarter and more responsive. The article notes the underlying strategy: the more fluid the conversation, the more we tend to forget we are talking to a robot, and the more we tend to reveal slightly secret things without even thinking. It is impressive and a bit scary at the same time. An AI that speaks without the slightest blank and listens continuously is ultra comfortable, but it is also the best way to forget that you are confiding in a machine.
