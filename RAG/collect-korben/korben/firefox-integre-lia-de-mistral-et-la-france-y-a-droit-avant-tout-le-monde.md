## Full summary

The article reports that Mozilla announced a partnership with Mistral, the Paris-based startup positioned as Europe's answer to OpenAI. Mistral's model, Mistral Small 4, becomes the recommended engine of the "Fenêtre intelligente" (Smart Window), the AI-assisted browsing mode that Firefox is rolling out in beta.

The Smart Window is a separate space from classic browsing where an assistant can summarize the page currently being read or help recover a tab that was closed too quickly. Until now it was reserved for the United States and Canada. Firefox 155 opens it to France, the first European country served, with an assistant that finally officially speaks French, ahead of the United Kingdom and Germany by the end of the year.

In France, Mistral Small 4 will be recommended by default, whereas across the Atlantic it is simply added to the list of existing options. Mozilla says it selected the model for its multilingual performance, and the model has another argument well suited to the Firefox spirit: it is published under the Apache 2.0 license, meaning anyone can download it, inspect it, and run it on their own machine.

Nothing is imposed, however, since the selector keeps other models available, including a Gemini from Google and the Chinese Qwen, and users can even enter their own API key, the key that connects the browser to a third-party AI service. On the data side, conversations are not stored on Mozilla's servers by default, and Mistral commits to zero retention, meaning nothing is kept once the answer is delivered. If all this bothers the user, the feature can be fully disabled.

The article notes that France is a special market for Firefox, where it retains about 12% of the desktop market, more than double its global level, thanks to a loyal audience rather attached to free software, for whom a French AI resonates more than a Californian service. For Mistral, this is a mainstream showcase of some 200 million monthly users claimed by Firefox.
