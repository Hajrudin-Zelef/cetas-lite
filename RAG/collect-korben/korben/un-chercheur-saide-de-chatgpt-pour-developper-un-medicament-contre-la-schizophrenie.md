# Un chercheur s'aide de ChatGPT pour développer un médicament contre la schizophrénie

## Full summary

The article reports on Douglas Yao, a researcher who uses computer science to study biology and is trying to develop a new treatment for schizophrenia, a psychiatric illness that can cause hallucinations and disrupt thought, with the help of ChatGPT.

His project concerns a molecule called PAC-3310, a chemical substance whose effects on the brain he wants to study. It could become the basis of a drug if the research succeeds, but the results presented concern only cells and mice. No patients so far. On 8 September, Yao showed a vial claiming that ChatGPT had designed the molecule and that he had then manufactured it in a laboratory installed in his garage. The AI reportedly proposed the chemical structure of the product, while its manufacture required laboratory work carried out by the researcher.

Yao created the company Pace Pharmaceuticals to develop this type of research. Its site presents a report on PAC-3310, which makes it possible to examine what was actually tested behind the announcement. The garage narrative draws attention, but the key question is whether this substance can help patients.

To understand the avenue explored, the article recalls how the brain works: its cells exchange chemical messages received by proteins called receptors. PAC-3310 seeks to activate one of them, named M4, while acting as little as possible on the others, in the hope of limiting side effects. In their trials, the researchers observed activity on this receptor in cells cultured in the laboratory. In mice made artificially hyperactive, the substance reduced excessive movements. This result serves to explore a treatment avenue, but a mouse that moves less does not demonstrate that a patient will have fewer hallucinations.

The mouse trials were not conducted blindly: the people conducting the tests knew which treatment each animal received. This can influence the evaluation, a limitation the authors indicate in their report. Drugs acting on this family of receptors already exist. In the United States, Cobenfy was authorized in September 2024 against schizophrenia in adults, after patient trials. PAC-3310 still has to prove itself: its efficacy in humans and its safety are not established by the experiments presented. The article concludes that the project deserves interest, but a demonstration with ChatGPT does not replace the trials needed to offer a drug to patients.

## Key points

- Researcher Douglas Yao claims ChatGPT designed the molecule PAC-3310, which he then manufactured in a garage lab.
- The molecule targets the M4 receptor while minimizing action on others, to limit side effects.
- Tests showed activity on the M4 receptor in lab-cultured cells and reduced excessive movement in hyperactive mice.
- No patient trials have been conducted; efficacy and safety in humans are unproven.
- Mouse trials were not blinded (testers knew the treatment), a limitation acknowledged by the authors.
- Yao founded Pace Pharmaceuticals to develop the research; a report on PAC-3310 is published on its site.
- Comparable existing drug: Cobenfy, authorized in the US in September 2024 for adult schizophrenia after patient trials.
- Conclusion: a ChatGPT demonstration does not replace the clinical trials required to offer a drug.
