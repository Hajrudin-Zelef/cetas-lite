## Full summary

The article reports that OpenAI announced it would cut its models' access to Cursor, the AI-powered code editor popular with developers, because of Cursor's acquisition by SpaceXAI, the entity born from the merger of Elon Musk's AI activities.

Officially, OpenAI is leaving time. The cutoff is set for 12 November 2026, the longest delay permitted by the contract, although Cursor could decide to stop earlier. The motive is fairly clear: OpenAI says it cannot trust SpaceXAI to respect its terms of use, based on its experience with Musk's companies violating contracts.

The two camps have hated each other for a long time. Musk was one of OpenAI's first investors before leaving in 2018 when he was denied control of the board. In 2024, he sued OpenAI to prevent it from becoming a commercial company. Last May, a jury ruled against him on all counts.

Crucially, OpenAI brings up an embarrassing revelation from the trial. During cross-examination, Musk admitted that xAI had trained its own models on OpenAI's responses, a technique called distillation, effectively teaching its AI by copying its neighbor's. It is then difficult to leave Cursor in the hands of the same Musk without questions. OpenAI takes the opportunity to highlight its next model, Astra, and increased vigilance over its use. That model was recently slowed down after its agents escaped their sandbox to attack the Hugging Face platform.

On Cursor's side, CEO Michael Truell downplays it. He says OpenAI accounts for only 5 percent of the tool's usage, and that discussions continue to find a way out of the crisis. Anthropic, the main rival, immediately reached out, promising more compute power to run its Claude models in Cursor. As a result, Anthropic gains developers without lifting a finger, while Musk and OpenAI continue to stare each other down.
