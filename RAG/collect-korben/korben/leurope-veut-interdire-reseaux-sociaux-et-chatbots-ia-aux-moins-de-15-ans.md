# L'Europe veut interdire réseaux sociaux et chatbots IA aux moins de 15 ans

## Full summary

The article covers the European Commission's "Kids Act", a draft regulation presented by Ursula von der Leyen before the European Parliament and detailed by the Commission on a Thursday. It targets social networks, video platforms, online games and chatbots simultaneously. The core principle is tiered access based on age: no accounts before 13, parental consent between 13 and 15, and full autonomy only at 15.

Before age 13, no account is possible on TikTok, Instagram or YouTube, and children aged 3 to 12 should be limited to child-designed services that parents control end to end. Between 13 and 15, parental approval is required to open an "introduction" account, a kind of restricted mini-account with limited contacts. A draft leaked to Euronews mentioned a cap of one hour of screen time per day. Full autonomy comes at 15.

The Commission also includes AI chatbots, the virtual companions with which some teenagers spend entire evenings chatting. These too would be closed to under-15s. As for designs that foster emotional attachment in a minor, they would be outright banned. On the design front, the text targets infinite scrolling and reward mechanisms that create addiction. Artificial notifications are also targeted. Recommendation algorithms would have to stop locking minors into spirals of increasingly extreme content.

All of this presupposes age verification. The Commission promises a European app or national solutions, without excessive collection of personal data. Platforms would have to prove compliance and pay a supervision fee to fund checks. In case of cheating, fines can reach up to 6% of global turnover.

Australia led the way, with its ban on under-16s in place since December 2025. Around ten member states were already preparing their own laws. In France, the digital majority law did not survive the Constitutional Council this summer, and the Élysée has been working on a new version since. The article stresses that a Commission proposal is not yet law: the text must survive the European Parliament and then the member states, a shuttle process lasting years, during which tech lobbies will push hard.

## Key points

- The European Commission's "Kids Act" targets social networks, video platforms, online games and AI chatbots.
- Tiered access: no accounts before 13; parental consent from 13 to 15; full autonomy at 15.
- A leaked draft mentioned a one-hour daily screen-time cap for the 13-15 introduction accounts.
- AI chatbots (virtual companions) would be closed to under-15s; emotionally-attaching designs would be banned.
- The text also targets infinite scrolling, reward mechanics, artificial notifications and extreme recommendation spirals.
- Age verification via a European app or national solutions, plus a supervision fee paid by platforms.
- Fines up to 6% of global turnover; Australia already bans under-16s since December 2025.
- A Commission proposal is not law; it must pass Parliament and member states over years.
