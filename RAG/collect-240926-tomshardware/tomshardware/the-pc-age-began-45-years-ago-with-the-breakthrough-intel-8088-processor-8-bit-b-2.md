# the-pc-age-began-45-years-ago-with-the-breakthrough-intel-8088-processor-8-bit-b

 After the 8080, Intel's main CPU project was actually the iAPX 432, which was supposed to be ground-breaking with many new features... but much like the later Itanium, it was a bloated expensive design that was slower than its predecessor.
 Because that would take a long time to get to market there was a gap in the market, and Intel designed the 8086 from the 8080 with 16-bit extensions to fill that gap.
 The 8086 is not backwards-compatible but the similarities are obvious in the opcode map.
 The 8088 is a variation with an 8-bit data bus to allow systems for it to be made less expensive.
 
 The term "personal computer" was coined long before the "IBM PC". It meant simply that it had one user (at a time), to differentiate it from mainframe computers that were used for one task, or by multiple users at a time. Whether it was for home or office use was of no matter.
 Then IBM co-opted the term for their product.
So "The PC age" is really a short form of "The personal computing age".

Saying IBM made the decision to allow pc clones to be made is some borderline revisionism. The fact is that IBM did make an open architecture by choosing to use off the shelf parts, but they never intended for other companies to make whole IBM PC compatible machines. They made the BIOS and copyrighted the code and sued the pants off anyone that tried to use it or a modified version in their PC clones. That was IBMs strategy, you can't have a PC clone unless it runs PC specific code, and that to IBM meant running their copyrighted code.Reply
 
The reason PC clones took off at all is because Compaq came around and pioneered the use of the clean room method of reverse engineering. They took the BIOS and worked out all its functions as plain English and handed that to a separate team who could claim to have never seen IBMs code and reimplement all its functions with a clean room version. IBM couldn't do anything about it so Compaq was free to sell its machines as pc compatible. Other companies followed suit and the rest is history.
