# how-openai-and-anthropic-turn-compute-into-profit

The more interesting question is what happens to compute pricing itself. If labs can profitably pay $25 million, $40 million, or more per megawatt while general-purpose compute renters still operate around $10 to $15 million, the market bifurcates. Patel notes this is already visible: it has become relatively straightforward for smaller operators to rent GPU capacity, run open-weight models, and turn a profit at that baseline price point, which itself is starting to push the floor price of compute upward. Labs with far higher revenue per megawatt simply have more room to keep paying more, which is precisely the mechanism concentrating an increasing share of global compute in a small number of hands.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does “revenue per megawatt” actually measure?

It’s the amount of money a lab generates annually from serving AI models on a given unit of compute capacity, compared against the cost of running that capacity. It’s a rough proxy for gross margin on inference and is used to gauge how efficiently a company converts raw compute into revenue.

### When did OpenAI and Anthropic become profitable?

Based on industry discussion, Anthropic is believed to have turned cash-flow positive around the second quarter of this year, with OpenAI following in the third quarter, helped by growth in products like Codex and newer model releases.

### Why can frontier labs outbid other companies for the same compute?

Because their revenue per megawatt is reportedly several times higher than the baseline cost of compute, they can afford to pay more for GPUs, TPUs, and data center capacity than companies operating on thinner margins, letting them absorb a growing share of new compute coming online.

### Is the chip and data center supply chain able to scale fast enough?

Not immediately. Building the tools and facilities needed to expand compute capacity takes years, and component makers throughout the chain are still catching up to demand projections that have shifted rapidly over the past year.

### Does this mean two companies will eventually control most global compute?

Current trends suggest OpenAI and Anthropic are taking an increasing share of incremental compute, potentially reaching 40 to 50% of new capacity added next year, though how long that trend continues depends on supply chain expansion, pricing dynamics, and how other buyers respond.
