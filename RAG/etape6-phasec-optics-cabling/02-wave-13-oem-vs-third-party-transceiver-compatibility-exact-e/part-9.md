# Wave 13 — OEM vs third-party transceiver compatibility: exact error messages, TAC/warranty policy, FEC, failure behavior (part 9)

| OEM / OS | Third-party optic enforcement | Official policy posture | Compatibility-check tooling | Support (TAC) implications |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cisco** (IOS / IOS XE / NX-OS) | Vendor-ID lock on Catalyst IOS/IOS XE by default: non-Cisco optic → port err-disabled (`gbic-invalid`). Documented syslogs: `%PHY-4-SFP_NOT_SUPPORTED`; `%PLATFORM_PM-6-MODULE_ERRDISABLE`; `%PM-4-ERR_DISABLE: gbic-invalid error detected … putting … in errdisable state` [vendor-reported — Cisco TAC article] (http://www.static-cisco.com/c/en/us/support/docs/interfaces-modules/gbics/200296-Unsupported-GBIC-SFP-in-sub-module-of.pdf). Documented workaround on ISR1000: `service unsupported-transceiver` [official] (https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/td/docs/routers/access/1100/software/configuration/xe-16-10/cisco_1100_series_swcfg_xe_16_10_x/config-third-party-sfps.html); on Catalyst also `no errdisable detect cause gbic-invalid` (IOS 12.2(25)SE+) [vendor-reported/secondary]. Nexus reported less strict than Catalyst — [unverified/community consensus]. | Hardest enforcement posture of the seven platforms in this matrix [secondary analysis across sources]. | **Optics-to-Device Compatibility Matrix (TMG matrix)** [official] (https://tmgmatrix.cisco.com/home); **Optics-to-Optics Interoperability Matrix** [vendor-reported] (https://tmgmatrix.cisco.com/iop). See §14.4. | **Official Non-Entitlement Policy v2.0 §5.1:** if Cisco believes a fault "can be traced to the use of third-party … interface components … Cisco may withhold support under warranty or a Cisco support program"; Cisco "reserves the right to charge You for services" when the root cause was a non-supported third-party product [official] (https://www.cisco.com/c/en/us/products/warranties/warranty-doc-c99-740959.html). Practice: "TAC will then resume troubleshooting after the 3rd-party optic is replaced by a Cisco optic" [vendor-reported — Cisco employee, community.cisco.com/t5/optics-and-optical-networking/sfp-compatibility/td-p/4832838]. |
| **Aruba / HPE** (AOS-CX / AOS-S / Instant On) | `allowunsupportedtransceiver` (UTmode) "allows usage of non-Aruba/HPE transceiver products" [official doc, mirrored]. **UTmode is ENABLED BY DEFAULT on current AOS-CX** (10.09.0002: 8360/8325/8320/10000, 1G–100G; 10.10.0002: 8400/6xxx, 1G–100G; 10.10.1000: 8100, to 100G; 10.11.0001: 9300-32D, 40G–400G). On AOS-S 16.02 it is NOT default — CLI consent with warning: "The use of unsupported transceivers, DACs, and AOCs is at your own risk and may void support and warranty" [official doc, mirrored] (AOS-S and AOS-CX Transceiver Guide Ed. 17; e.g. https://media.bechtle.com/asrc/180712/1c4b3d4ee288fc9434f5175bf56070570/c3/-/adc4eb3ee288fc9434f5175bf56070570/transceiver-hpe-aruba-1g-sfp-lc-sx-karta-danych-1 and https://wiki.3av.us/lib/exe/fetch.php?media=networking:multigigabit:aos-s_and_aos-cx_transceiver_guide.pdf). Official caveats: used "at their own risk"; "No guarantees are implied that a thirdparty transceiver will continue to work from release to release" [official doc, mirrored]. **Instant On** does not block unsupported transceivers but does not guarantee proper operation [official doc, mirrored — Instant On Transceiver Guide]. | Consent-gated, open-by-default on AOS-CX; best-effort support once enabled [official doc, mirrored]. | **AOS-S and AOS-CX Transceiver Guide (Ed. 17)** with per-transceiver compatibility tables and minimum software versions; **Instant On Transceiver Guide**; per-switch datasheets/QuickSpecs [official doc, mirrored]. No interactive web matrix tool found — not found. | Third-party optics require explicit consent acknowledgment (support/warranty may be voided) [official doc, mirrored]. Instant On: third-party transceivers "are not supported by Aruba Instant On support team … interoperability, quality, and reliability cannot be guaranteed" [official doc, mirrored]. |
| **Dell** (OS9 / OS10 / SONiC) | **OS9 tiered behavior, officially documented:** cables/adapters (DAC, AOC, breakout, 1G optics, QSA) — non-Dell-qualified are detected but made operational, with syslog `%IFAGT-5-UNSUP_OPTICS: Non-qualified optics in slot 1 port 49`. Optical transceivers (LR4, SR4, LM4, PSM4) — only Dell-qualified supported; non-qualified → interface error-disabled: `%IFAGT-2-TRANSCEIVER_UNSUPPORTED_ERROR: Transceiver in slot 1 port 50 unrecognized, putting interface in operational-down state`; `show interfaces` reports `line protocol is down(error-disabled[Transceiver Unsupported])` and "Non-qualified pluggable media present" [official] (https://www.dell.com/support/manuals/en-sg/dell-emc-os-9/c9010-9.14.2.8-config-pub/Non-Dell-Qualified-Transceivers). **SONiC / Enterprise SONiC:** no explicit vendor-lock documentation found in official Dell materials; community sources report Dell SONiC generally does not hard-lock third-party optics — [secondary/unverified]. | Tiered: warn-and-operate for cables, error-disable for optical transceivers on OS9 [official]. | **SmartFabric OS10 Hardware Compatibility List** (per-release, in OS10 User Guide) [official] (https://www.dell.com/support/manuals/en-lv/smartfabric-os10-emp-partner/smartfabric-os-user-guide-10-6-0-x/smartfabric-os10-hardware-compatibility-list) — platform-vs-OS matrix, not transceiver-vs-switch. **Dell Networking Transceiver and Cable Solutions spec sheet** (optics table: model, connector, wavelength, distance, Tx/Rx power, notes; some "qualified on a case-by-case basis", "SONiC OS ONLY" notes) [official] (https://www.delltechnologies.com/asset/en-us/products/networking/technical-support/Dell_EMC_Networking_Optics_Spec_Sheet.pdf). No single interactive transceiver↔switch web tool found — not found. | No official Dell support-policy document on third-party optics found — not found. Secondary claim only: "If the fault caused by third-party hardware, Dell has right to refuse to repair it" [secondary — fs.com blog] → treat as [unverified]. |
| **Ubiquiti** (UniFi / EdgeSwitch) | **No artificial restrictions:** "We do not impose any artificial restrictions to limit third-party SFP modules or DAC cables. However, we also do not formally test these" [official-mirrored] (UniFi help article "SFP Modules and DAC Cables", via https://www.ubiquitidubai.com/unifi-sfp-modules). EdgeSwitch help article publishes a community-tested third-party module list (Addon, Brocade, Cisco GLC-SX-MM/GLC-SX-MMD/SFP-H10GB-CU1M, Dell FTLF series, Finisar, HP J4858C/J4859B, MikroTik S+85DLC03D/S+31DLC10D, TP-LINK, etc.) with disclaimer "should work, but we cannot assure that they will"; "Ubiquiti only offers direct support for Ubiquiti branded SFP/SFP+ modules and DAC cables" [official] (https://help.ui.com/hc/en-us/articles/205220850). Note: not all third-party modules support DDM/DOM, so Tx/Rx data may not show [official] (same). **Ubiquiti UACC-SFP-Wizard** (~$49): portable SFP/SFP+/SFP28/QSFP+/QSFP28 programmer/unlocker marketed against vendor lock-in ("SFP Liberation Day"); Ubiquiti simultaneously cut own module prices (SFP+ ~$9, QSFP28 100G ~$39) [independent] (https://www.notebookcheck.net/Unifi-Ubiquiti-unlocks-SFP-and-QSFP-modules-with-new-SFP-Wizard.1149839.0.html). | Most open posture of the seven: no lock, explicit encouragement of third-party ecosystem [official/independent]. | No interactive matrix — not found. Closest official artifact: the community-tested list in the EdgeSwitch help article [official] (https://help.ui.com/hc/en-us/articles/205220850). | "Ubiquiti only offers direct support for Ubiquiti branded SFP/SFP+ modules and DAC cables" [official] (same). No lock; no formal entitlement gating found. |
