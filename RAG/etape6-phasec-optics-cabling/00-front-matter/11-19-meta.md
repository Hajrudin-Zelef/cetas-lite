# 11.19 Meta

### 11.19 Meta

- Meta's 2024 Llama 3 infrastructure: two **24,576-GPU** Grand Teton clusters — one RoCE Ethernet fabric (Arista 7800, Wedge400, Minipack2), one NVIDIA Quantum-2 InfiniBand fabric — both described around 400G endpoints, not 800G NICs [vendor-reported].
- Grand Teton: twice the network bandwidth of Zion, four times host-to-GPU bandwidth, Open Rack v3 deployment [vendor-reported].
- Broadcom/EE Times reported Meta accumulated over one million 400G-equivalent port-device hours without data drops on Tomahawk 5 "Bailly"; this is vendor-reported validation, not evidence of deployed 800G host NICs [vendor-reported].
- A Korean secondary article claims Meta announced 800G infrastructure for next-generation data-center expansion; primary confirmation still needed [secondary].
- A financial-content article claims a 3 nm MTIA integrates 800G/1.6T Ethernet; weak source — treat as unverified [unverified].
- **Do not overstate Meta as a confirmed fleet-wide 800G NIC deployment** on current evidence [independent].

### 11.20 Google TPU Ironwood

- TrendForce-derived February 2026 reporting: Ironwood uses a 3D torus and Apollo optical circuit switching; short connections use high-speed copper; inter-rack links use all-optical paths; 800G/1.6T modules expected [secondary].
- Global shipment share of 800G-and-above optical modules projected to rise from 19.5% in 2024 to over 60% in 2026; projection of nearly 4 million Google TPUs shipped in 2026 demanding over 6 million 800G+ modules — **analyst projections, not completed shipments** [secondary].
- Secondary architecture estimates: ~1.5 optical transceivers per TPU, up to 9,216 chips per Ironwood pod, 13,824 optical ports; do not generalize beyond this architecture [secondary].

### 11.21 AWS Trainium / EFA

- Official AWS Trn1 page: Trn1 up to **800 Gb/s EFAv2** network bandwidth; Trn1n up to **1,600 Gb/s EFAv2**; UltraClusters scale to 30,000 Trainium chips [official].
- Trn1 was AWS's first EC2 instance with up to 800 Gb/s EFA bandwidth; uses NeuronLink for intra-instance scale-up and EFA for scale-out [official].
- Trainium2 announcement: up to 100,000 chips in next-generation UltraClusters with petabit-scale EFA network; no exact physical 800G-NIC count was found [vendor-reported].

### 11.22 Oracle Zettascale10 (Stargate, Acceleron RoCE) and Oracle/AMD Helios

- Oracle unveiled **OCI Zettascale10** at Oracle AI World in Las Vegas (October 2025), calling it the largest AI supercomputer in the cloud [vendor-reported].
- Zettascale10 targets up to **800,000 NVIDIA GPUs** per cluster across multi-gigawatt campuses within a ~2 km radius; availability guided for H2 2026 [vendor-reported].
- Networking: Oracle **Acceleron RoCE** architecture with built-in GPU NIC switching and multiple isolated network planes; the system underpins the Stargate supercluster with OpenAI in Abilene, Texas [vendor-reported].
- Acceleron uses **Linear Pluggable Optics (LPO) and Linear Receiver Optics (LRO)** to reduce cooling/power costs while sustaining 400G/800G throughput [vendor-reported].
- Zettascale10 represents a tenfold performance increase over the prior Zettascale generation (131,072 NVIDIA GPUs, CloudWorld 2024) [vendor-reported].
- Coverage: convergedigest.com [independent]; morningstar.com PR [vendor-reported]; oracle.com roundup [official].
- **Oracle/AMD Helios**: Oracle plans to deploy 50,000 AMD Instinct MI450 GPUs (by 2026) in a liquid-cooled Helios rack design (72 GPUs per rack) with UALink rack-level memory interconnect and UALoE scale-up [secondary].
- Each GPU can connect with up to **three 800 Gbps AMD Pensando "Vulcano" AI-NICs**; networking aligns with UEC standards; AMD Pensando DPUs handle data ingestion and secure traffic management [secondary].
- This is the strongest public 800G-NIC-per-GPU deployment claim found, but it remains a plan/announcement, not verified production [independent].

### 11.23 Microsoft Maia 200

- Microsoft's 2026 Maia 200: Ethernet-based two-tier scale-up network, integrated/on-die NIC, custom Maia AI Transport Layer, 2.8 TB/s bidirectional dedicated scale-up bandwidth per accelerator, up to 6,144 accelerators, four Maia accelerators per tray [vendor-reported].
- Initial Azure US Central deployment, more regions planned [vendor-reported].
- **No source gathered states Maia's NIC uses 800GbE ports**; use Maia as evidence of Ethernet entering scale-up, not as a verified 800G NIC deployment [independent].

### 11.24 DeepL and STX adopters

- DCD reported in 2026 that DeepL deployed Europe's first DGX SuperPOD with GB200 NVL72 at EcoDataCenter in Falun, Sweden (announced at GTC Paris, June) [independent]. Deployment scale was undisclosed; multiple racks can use Quantum InfiniBand [independent].
- NVIDIA's March 2026 BlueField-4 STX announcement named early adopters for context-memory storage: CoreWeave, Crusoe, IREN, Lambda, Mistral AI, Nebius, OCI, and Vultr [vendor-reported]. These are storage-platform adoptions, not necessarily 800G NIC deployments; STX systems are Spectrum-X Ethernet networked with BF-4 (800G) silicon [vendor-reported].

### 11.25 Optics count math (derived scenarios, not sourced facts)

- All figures below are **derived arithmetic** labeled as assumptions, not vendor deployment facts [independent].
- Scenario 1 — minimum endpoint optics: a 100,000-GPU cluster with one 800G link per GPU, each point-to-point optical link needing two transceivers → **200,000 transceivers** endpoint-only [independent].
- Scenario 2 — nonblocking two-tier leaf-spine: total optics exceed endpoint count; leaf-spine links add more transceivers depending on radix, oversubscription, and breakout design [independent].
- Scenario 3 — oversubscribed fabric: oversubscription ratios (e.g., 3:1) reduce spine-facing optics proportionally but increase congestion risk [independent].
- Scenario 4 — copper to ToR: DAC/AEC server-to-ToR links eliminate server-facing optics entirely in that tier [independent].
- Scenario 5 — 400G endpoints on 800G switches (Colossus pattern): one 800G switch port breaks out to 2×400G endpoints, so 800G transceiver count is not 1:1 with GPU count [independent].
- Google Ironwood's ~1.5 transceivers per TPU is architecture-specific (optical circuit switching) and must not be generalized [secondary].
- NVL72 internal NVLink is copper, so GPU count cannot be multiplied by optical transceivers without considering the external fabric design [vendor-reported].
