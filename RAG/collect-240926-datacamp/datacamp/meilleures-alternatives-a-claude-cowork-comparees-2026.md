# meilleures-alternatives-a-claude-cowork-comparees-2026

<!-- source: https://www.datacamp.com/fr/blog/claude-cowork-alternatives -->

Cursus

Claude Cowork is a solid tool, powered by high-performing models, and it would be dishonest to claim otherwise.

That said, autonomous execution in the cloud remains a beta feature, and anything touching your local files still requires the desktop app to be open. For most users, this means: if your computer goes to sleep, so does your agent. You also need a Claude Pro subscription at $20/month (heavy users quickly moving up to Max at $100/month or $200/month), you remain locked into the Claude model family, and it's not ideal for many team workflows with shared access.

Plenty of reasons to look at other options. I compared 9 alternatives across four criteria that really drive the purchasing decision: cost, the ability to keep working when you close your computer, supported model families, and the setup effort required.

For context on Cowork and its place in Anthropic's lineup, see our Claude Cowork tutorial and our guide to learning Claude.

## In brief

- If you want the closest like-for-like swap, **ChatGPT Agent** is the best option.
- If your work revolves around email, calendar, and meetings, **Lindy** handles everything in the cloud while you're offline; if it's code, **Cursor**'s cloud agents are the way to go.
- If you're looking for a **free alternative**, **OpenClaw** is open source and free to run; you only pay for your own API keys and hosting.
- If **team collaboration** is the issue, **Notion Agents** acts on your shared workspace, or **Copilot Cowork** if you're already equipped with Microsoft 365.

## The best general-purpose Claude Cowork alternatives

General-purpose cloud agents are the closest replacements: hosted agents that take a task, work on the web, and deliver a finished result.

### ChatGPT Work

ChatGPT Work is OpenAI's autonomous task executor and the closest thing to a like-for-like Cowork replacement. It's one of the three modes in the new ChatGPT app, between Chat and Codex, and the alignment is almost a mirror of Anthropic's lineup: Chat, Work, and Codex versus Chat, Cowork, and Claude Code. If you've used Cowork, the mental model transfers immediately.

It browses the web on its own, executes multi-step tasks in a hosted environment, and, in the business and enterprise context covered by ChatGPT Work, maintains persistent workflows at the team level, rather than tied to a single person's session.

The difference that matters versus Cowork is where the work runs. Work has relied on OpenAI's infrastructure since launch, so a long research-and-compile task continues while your computer is in a bag. Cowork can now do this too, but only via a beta cloud mode, and never for tasks that touch your local files.

The trade-off is a model lock-in of a different kind. You get the GPT family and nothing else, which is fine until the day you want to compare outputs across providers. If you're mainly weighing model choice, our GPT-5.6 Terra vs Claude Sonnet comparison on benchmarks and pricing is a better starting point than any marketing page.

#### Key features

- **Autonomous web browsing:** The agent navigates sites, fills out forms, and extracts data without click-by-click supervision.
- **Hosted execution:** Tasks run server-side, without depending on your machine's sleep state.
- **Multi-device persistence:** Work conversations sync across web, mobile, and desktop, and persist via Projects and Scheduled Tasks. Team sharing goes through workspace projects, not shared live sessions.

#### Pricing

Work has no standalone price. It is included in the ChatGPT and Codex plans and deducted from your quota, like Codex. Free ($0) and Go ($8/month) do not include Work. Plus, at $20/month, is the entry point, similar to Anthropic's structure.

#### Limits

You are on GPT models without a BYOK option, so cost control across a mix of workloads is limited to the tier you purchased. Agent runs are also fairly opaque: you see the result, but reconstructing exactly which pages the agent read and in what order is harder than with a tool that logs every step.

### Perplexity Computer

Perplexity Computer executes desktop-type tasks with its built-in search engine, so the output includes live web sources rather than an unsourced summary. We tested it in parallel workflows in our hands-on Perplexity Computer tutorial, and source traceability is what sets it apart from every other tool here.

Where Cowork dives into your local files, Computer turns to the web. If your work is competitive analysis, market sizing, or anything that raises the question "where does this number come from?", that's a real asset. If your work is reorganizing a folder of spreadsheets on your Mac, it isn't.

#### Key features

- **Sourced task execution:** Every claim in a research deliverable links back to its source, which holds up during a colleague's review.
- **Auto-routed model selection:** Perplexity chooses the model per request from Claude, GPT, Gemini, and its Sonar family.
- **Cloud task execution:** Computer handles multi-step tasks in Perplexity's cloud, without depending on your machine.
- **Education pricing:** Verified students and teachers access Pro features at $10/month via SheerID.

#### Pricing

Perplexity publishes six tiers, and access to Computer is limited to the two highest: Max ($200/month) and Enterprise Max ($325/seat/month). Which brings us smoothly to the limits.

#### Limits

Computer is reserved for the Max plan at $200/month, the same price as Claude's Max 20x tier, and 10 times the Pro plan most people start on. Model routing is automatic with no BYOK option, so you can't force a specific model when you already know which one performs better.

## The best Claude Cowork alternatives by role

Next come tools designed in depth rather than in breadth, tailored to a specific use, such as your inbox or your codebase.

### Cursor (cloud agents)

Cursor is an AI-minded code editor, and it belongs here for one reason: cloud agents. If you're leaving Cowork because your agent stops when your computer goes to sleep, and the work in question is engineering, Cursor already solves the problem. We detail the setup in our guide on Cursor automations and always-on code agents.

Cursor also supports the Claude, GPT, and Gemini model families in the same editor, to run a refactor pass on one and test writing on another, without switching tools. It's the direct answer to Cowork's single-vendor constraint. New to the topic? Start with our selection of the best ways to learn Cursor.

Obviously, Cursor won't sort your inbox or prepare a slide deck. Its scope is deliberately narrower than Cowork's, and within that scope, it goes much further.

#### Key features

- **Agent mode:** Multi-file changes from a single instruction, with the agent running commands and verifying its own work.
- **Cloud agents:** Background work continues on Cursor's infrastructure, not your machine.
- **Tab completions:** Inline autocomplete, unlimited on paid individual plans.
- **MCP support:** Connecting tools and data sources via Model Context Protocol.
- **Extended contexts:** Larger context windows to reason over a large codebase in one pass.
- **Agentic code reviews and team marketplace:** Shared rules and skills, with agent-managed review passes.
- **Cursor SDK:** Drive code agents in TypeScript.

#### Pricing

As with Claude Cowork and ChatGPT Work, the cloud agent feature is accessible with a $20/month subscription, Cursor's Pro tier.

#### Limits

Everything happens in a code editor. If your use of Cowork was 70% on documents and spreadsheets, Cursor replaces almost nothing.

### Lindy AI

Lindy lets you build no-code assistants that run in the cloud on a schedule, the cleanest solution to Cowork's desktop dependency if your work involves email, calendar, and meetings rather than code. The assistants handle inbox triage, drafting replies, scheduling appointments, and meeting notes, without installing anything locally.

Browser automation and model choice are included on the Pro plan and above, so the Plus tier at $49.99 is more limited than its price suggests. Good to know before subscribing, especially if you expect behavior equivalent to Cowork.

#### Key features

- **Email triage and drafting:** The assistant sorts incoming messages and prepares replies for approval.
- **Connected inboxes by tier:** 2 on Plus, 3 on Pro, 5 on Max.
- **Computer use and browser automation:** Included on Pro and up, where Lindy really starts to cover Cowork's use cases.
- **Model selection:** Also on Pro and up, covering the Claude, GPT, and Gemini families.
- **SMS and iMessage support:** Assistants can act beyond email.
- **Integrations:** Lindy connects to hundreds of applications.
- **Enterprise controls:** SSO, SCIM, audit logs, HIPAA compliance, dedicated support, and onboarding.

#### Pricing

Lindy's official pricing page lists four tiers:

- **Plus:** $49.99/month, 2 connected inboxes.
- **Pro:** $99.99/month, 3 connected inboxes, adds computer use and model selection.
- **Max:** $199.99/month, 5 connected inboxes.
- **Enterprise:** Custom pricing, adds SSO, SCIM, audit logs, and a compliance package.

There is no permanent free plan, but you can try Lindy with a 7-day free trial.

#### Limitations

At $49.99/month, Lindy's entry tier costs more than double Claude Pro at $20, and the features that make it a true Cowork substitute only appear at $99.99. It is also an "assistant" tool rather than a general-purpose one: you configure Lindys for specific jobs, which requires more setup than opening Cowork and describing a task.

## The best alternatives to Claude Cowork, native to an ecosystem

Ecosystem-native agents live in the suite where your team already works and act on the content found there.

### Notion Agent

Notion Agent is the option to consider if your team's documents, wikis, and project databases already live in Notion. Notion overhauled its AI features into Agent in version 3.0, with this promise: the agent works on the actual content of your workspace, not on scattered files on a computer.

This is the structural difference from Cowork. Cowork reads what is on your machine; a Notion Agent reads what your whole team has written, which makes handoffs far less painful.

#### Key features

- **Custom Agents:** Configurable agents as an add-on for Business and Enterprise, working with Notion credits.
- **AI Meeting Notes:** Transcription and summarization linked to the pages related to the meeting.
- **Enterprise Search:** Search across the entire workspace in the full AI bundle.
- **Research Mode:** Produces in-depth research, full access on Enterprise.
- **Zero data retention with LLM providers:** AI feature and security for Enterprise.
- **SCIM provisioning and audit logs:** Enterprise admin controls.

#### Pricing

To access Agents, you need Notion's Business subscription ($20/month) or the custom-priced Enterprise tier.

Custom Agents work with Notion credits, billed at $10 for 1,000 monthly credits on top of the per-seat cost. Model access covers the Claude, GPT, and Gemini families.

#### Limitations

Agents work on Notion content. Point one at a folder of CSVs on your desktop, and it has nothing to do. The credit model also makes it hard to forecast heavy usage: two teams on Business at $20/seat can end up with very different bills depending on their agents' activity.

### Microsoft 365 Copilot

Copilot Cowork is the agent layer in Microsoft 365 Copilot, and yes, Microsoft gave it the same name as the tool you are leaving. You describe an outcome to achieve, and it works on your emails, meetings, files, and Teams conversations, then delivers a finished output. If your company already runs on Microsoft 365, this is the closest thing to Cowork and it lives where your data already is.

It also fixes Cowork's desktop problem: Cowork runs autonomously in your Microsoft 365 tenant, not on your laptop, so a long task keeps going while you are offline. Another advantage: permissions. Cowork reads what is on your machine, while Copilot Cowork already knows who can see which document and inherits that visibility, without having to hand files over to an app.

The downside is billing. Cowork requires a Microsoft 365 Copilot license upfront, and the work you delegate is metered in credits on top, so the sticker price says little about the actual bill.

#### Key features

- 
**Work IQ grounding:** Reasons over your organization's emails, meetings, files, and Teams conversations, for outputs based on your real data.
- 
**Autonomous execution:** Plans and executes multi-step work in your tenant, sending emails, booking meetings, and drafting documents, then delivers a finished output.
- 
**Inherited permissions:** Respects Microsoft 365's existing access controls without requiring separate file access.
- 
**Live cost control:** Type `/cost` in a task to see credits consumed.

#### Pricing

Two layers, only the first is fixed:

- **Microsoft 365 Copilot license:** Required, $30/user/month (annual), itself an add-on to an eligible Microsoft 365 plan.
- **Copilot credits:** Agent work is metered at $0.01/credit on a pay-as-you-go basis. No credits are included with the license.
- **Cost per task:** Rough estimates: ~$1–3 for light, $4–7 for medium, and more for heavy, depending on model, context, tool calls, and duration.

#### Limitations

Pricing is the real limit. A single heavy task can cost more than a day of user seats, and since cost scales with delegated work rather than seat count, the "license everyone and forget it" model doesn't hold up.

It's also 100% Microsoft. Cowork makes the most sense if your work lives in Outlook, Teams, and SharePoint, and serves little purpose otherwise. And you can't choose the model for a Cowork task the way you would when building a custom agent in Copilot Studio.

## The best open source alternatives to Claude Cowork

Self-hosted agents are BYOK options that you run on your machine or server, in exchange for a bit of configuration for full control over models and data.

### OpenClaw

OpenClaw is the open source project that solves both of Cowork's constraints at once: model lock-in and where it runs. It's a self-hosted personal agent, MIT-licensed, that you can point at your own API keys and run on your machine or a VPS. Created by Peter Steinberger (founder of PSPDFKit), it has become the default name when people talk about open source Cowork clones. We compare it directly in our OpenClaw vs. Claude Code article.

Instead of a desktop app, OpenClaw runs as a local gateway that connects the chosen model to your files and messaging apps like WhatsApp, Telegram, and Discord. You talk to it where you already are, and it acts: clearing your inbox, managing files, watching a GitHub repo, triggering a deployment. Hosted on a server, it keeps going while your computer is closed.

The trade-off is that with self-hosting, configuration and security are on you, and an agent with access to local files and the shell represents a real surface to lock down.

#### Key features

- **Bring your own key:** Model-agnostic, compatible with Claude, GPT, Gemini, DeepSeek, Mistral, and local models via Ollama, swappable at any time.
- **SKILL.md skills:** Each skill is a folder with a Markdown instruction file, on the same principle as Claude's skills, plus a SOUL.md that defines the agent's personality and rules.
- **Messaging interface:** Acts via WhatsApp, Telegram, and Discord rather than a separate window, with persistent memory.
- **Self-hosted and auditable:** The full code is on GitHub under MIT, you can audit everything and keep data on your own infra.

#### Pricing

The software is free. Your costs: API usage for the chosen model, plus hosting if you run on a VPS rather than your machine. Third-party managed hosting exists (OneClaw, from about $9.99/month) if you don't want to manage the server, but that's a separate product, not OpenClaw itself.

#### Limitations

Setup and maintenance are on you, and by default it runs locally, so the "works while my computer sleeps" benefit only arrives once it's hosted on a server. No official support.

It also looks more like a personal assistant via messaging than an office agent. If your use of Cowork was drafting documents and reorganizing spreadsheets, the overlap is partial; if it was "watch this and act," OpenClaw fits well.

### Other open source options

If OpenClaw doesn't fit, a few other open source agents cover the same ground, with one thing in common: BYOK. You provide the API credentials, and the software doesn't care about the provider, so Claude, GPT, and Gemini are all accessible through configuration.

Rowboat is the most polished. It's an open source multi-agent builder, with background agents triggered by events and schedules, MCP support, and hundreds of integrations, self-hostable or in the cloud. It predates Cowork and was designed as a general-purpose framework, so it's less a clone than a platform to shape into a digital colleague.

OpenWork, OpenWorker, Hermes Agent, and Eigent round out the group. All work with your own keys, and Eigent and Hermes bet on independence from models and user-defined workflows. Host any of them on a server, and the agent runs whether your computer is open or not.

#### Key features

- **Bring your own key:** All work with your API credentials, so Claude, GPT, Gemini, or a local model are accessible via config, with no vendor lock-in.
- **Self-hosted and private:** You run them on your machine or server, data stays with you. An asset for regulated environments or those with data residency constraints.
- **Run continuously once hosted:** Put them on a server and the agent keeps going while your computer is closed.
- **Rowboat's background agents:** Launches triggered by events and schedules, MCP connections, and hundreds of integrations, the most turnkey of the bunch.

#### Pricing

The software is free. Your costs: API usage from the chosen provider, plus hosting if you run on a server rather than your machine. No published tiers for these projects, so no published support commitments either.

#### Limitations

Setup and maintenance fall to you, and several are local-only or self-hosted, which reintroduces exactly the machine dependency you were trying to avoid with Cowork. None publish a support matrix of tested and validated model families: expect to learn it yourself. I only recommend this path if you have a strict data residency requirement or you like to tinker.

## How to choose the right AI agent for work

The decision often comes down to two questions: does the work need to continue when your computer is closed, and does it live in code, documents, email, or the open web?

Entry prices vary more than you might imagine, from Notion Plus at $10 per seat/month and ChatGPT Agent or Cursor Pro at $20/month, up to Microsoft 365 Copilot at around $30/user/month plus credits, and Lindy Plus at $49.99/month.

| If you need… | Choose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| The closest replacement for Cowork's general task execution | ChatGPT Work | Autonomous browsing and hosted execution, with a free trial tier |
| Agents that work overnight on email and calendar | Lindy AI | Hosted assistants with browser automation starting at the Pro plan at $99.99 |
| Research your colleagues can verify | Perplexity Computer | Live web sources attached to every result |
| Multi-file changes and background engineering work | Cursor | Cloud agents + Claude, GPT, and Gemini in a single editor |
| An agent that already knows your team's documents | Notion Agents | Works on workspace content rather than one person's local files |
| An organization-wide Microsoft 365 deployment | Microsoft 365 Copilot | Inherits tenant permissions, with no connector work |
| Data that cannot leave your infrastructure | OpenClaw (or Rowboat) | Self-hosted with local models and your own API keys |

The closest head-to-head with Claude Cowork pits ChatGPT Work against Perplexity Computer, since both run hosted multi-step tasks on the web. The tiebreaker comes down to cost versus traceability: Computer requires the Max plan at $200/month, while Work starts at $20 with Plus. If no one downstream will verify your sources, the $180 gap is hard to justify.

The other key choice pits convenience against control. Go with Copilot Cowork or Lindy if you want a managed agent running in a vendor's cloud, and with OpenClaw if you want to choose the model and keep your data on your own infrastructure.

## Final thoughts

Claude Cowork remains a good choice if you already pay for Claude Pro, your work is on your machine, and you've never needed a task to run while you sleep. The quality of Anthropic's models isn't the weak point, and tooling benchmarks confirm it.

My three picks for switching are ChatGPT Work, Lindy, and OpenClaw. ChatGPT Work is the closest structurally, hosted rather than tied to the desktop, and its entry tier is the same $20 you were already paying at Anthropic. Lindy is the one that smooths out pro/personal logistics (albeit a bit pricey). OpenClaw is the Swiss Army knife if you're comfortable managing your setup and hosting.

The rest is more about fit than ranking. Cursor if the work is code, Notion Agents if the work is already in Notion, Copilot if procurement decided for you.

## Claude Cowork alternatives: FAQ

### Is Claude Cowork's price worth it?

Yes, if your work is on your own machine and you already pay for Claude Pro at $20/month, which Cowork requires. Anthropic's published evaluations place Claude Opus at 77.3% on MCP-Atlas for tool use at scale and 78.0% on OSWorld-Verified for computer use, so model quality isn't the weak point. The real question is accepting limited access to Claude models and a cloud mode still in beta, with local-file work tied to the desktop app.

### Can I use ChatGPT Work instead of Claude Cowork to automate my daily tasks?

Yes, and it's the closest like-for-like swap in this category. ChatGPT Work handles autonomous web browsing and multi-step task execution on OpenAI's infrastructure, not your computer, so tasks continue once the lid is closed. The trade-off is being restricted to GPT models, with no BYOK option.

### Which Claude Cowork alternative runs in the cloud so I can close my computer?

ChatGPT Work, Perplexity Computer, Lindy AI, Notion Agents, and Microsoft 365 Copilot all run in the cloud. Cursor also offers cloud agents for background engineering work on plans starting at $20/month. Self-hosted options like OpenClaw or Rowboat can run continuously as well, but only if you deploy them on a server rather than your machine.

### Which AI agent for work is best suited to beginners?

Start with Claude Cowork or ChatGPT Work, especially if you're already subscribed to either one's $20 tier, since they're the most versatile and easiest to get started with. If your team already uses Notion, I recommend Notion Agents as a simple and affordable option, at $10 per seat/month on Plus. Lindy and the open-source clones require substantial setup before they're useful, and Perplexity Computer is very expensive compared to the other tools.

### What is the best free alternative to Claude Cowork?

OpenClaw. It's open source under the MIT license, so the software costs nothing, and you only pay for your API keys and hosting, which also lets you run any model on your own infrastructure rather than being locked into Claude.
