Cursor also supports the Claude, GPT, and Gemini model families in the same editor, to run a refactor pass on one and test writing on another, without switching tools. It's the direct answer to Cowork's single-vendor constraint. New to the topic? Start with our selection of the best ways to learn Cursor.

Obviously, Cursor won't sort your inbox or prepare a slide deck. Its scope is deliberately narrower than Cowork's, and within that scope, it goes much further.

#### Key features

- **Agent mode:** Multi-file changes from a single instruction, with the agent running commands and verifying its own work.
- **Cloud agents:** Background work continues on Cursor's infrastructure, not your machine.
- **Tab completions:** Inline autocomplete, unlimited on paid individual plans.
- **MCP support:** Connecting tools and data sources via Model Context Protocol.
- **Extended contexts:** Larger context windows to reason over a large codebase in one pass.
- **Agentic code reviews and team marketplace:** Shared rules and skills, with agent-managed review passes.
- **Cursor SDK:** Drive code agents in TypeScript.

#### Pricing

As with Claude Cowork and ChatGPT Work, the cloud agent feature is accessible with a $20/month subscription, Cursor's Pro tier.

#### Limits

Everything happens in a code editor. If your use of Cowork was 70% on documents and spreadsheets, Cursor replaces almost nothing.

### Lindy AI

Lindy lets you build no-code assistants that run in the cloud on a schedule, the cleanest solution to Cowork's desktop dependency if your work involves email, calendar, and meetings rather than code. The assistants handle inbox triage, drafting replies, scheduling appointments, and meeting notes, without installing anything locally.

Browser automation and model choice are included on the Pro plan and above, so the Plus tier at $49.99 is more limited than its price suggests. Good to know before subscribing, especially if you expect behavior equivalent to Cowork.

#### Key features

- **Email triage and drafting:** The assistant sorts incoming messages and prepares replies for approval.
- **Connected inboxes by tier:** 2 on Plus, 3 on Pro, 5 on Max.
- **Computer use and browser automation:** Included on Pro and up, where Lindy really starts to cover Cowork's use cases.
- **Model selection:** Also on Pro and up, covering the Claude, GPT, and Gemini families.
- **SMS and iMessage support:** Assistants can act beyond email.
- **Integrations:** Lindy connects to hundreds of applications.
- **Enterprise controls:** SSO, SCIM, audit logs, HIPAA compliance, dedicated support, and onboarding.

#### Pricing

Lindy's official pricing page lists four tiers:

- **Plus:** $49.99/month, 2 connected inboxes.
- **Pro:** $99.99/month, 3 connected inboxes, adds computer use and model selection.
- **Max:** $199.99/month, 5 connected inboxes.
- **Enterprise:** Custom pricing, adds SSO, SCIM, audit logs, and a compliance package.

There is no permanent free plan, but you can try Lindy with a 7-day free trial.

#### Limitations

At $49.99/month, Lindy's entry tier costs more than double Claude Pro at $20, and the features that make it a true Cowork substitute only appear at $99.99. It is also an "assistant" tool rather than a general-purpose one: you configure Lindys for specific jobs, which requires more setup than opening Cowork and describing a task.

## The best alternatives to Claude Cowork, native to an ecosystem

Ecosystem-native agents live in the suite where your team already works and act on the content found there.

### Notion Agent

Notion Agent is the option to consider if your team's documents, wikis, and project databases already live in Notion. Notion overhauled its AI features into Agent in version 3.0, with this promise: the agent works on the actual content of your workspace, not on scattered files on a computer.

This is the structural difference from Cowork. Cowork reads what is on your machine; a Notion Agent reads what your whole team has written, which makes handoffs far less painful.

#### Key features

- **Custom Agents:** Configurable agents as an add-on for Business and Enterprise, working with Notion credits.
- **AI Meeting Notes:** Transcription and summarization linked to the pages related to the meeting.
- **Enterprise Search:** Search across the entire workspace in the full AI bundle.
- **Research Mode:** Produces in-depth research, full access on Enterprise.
- **Zero data retention with LLM providers:** AI feature and security for Enterprise.
- **SCIM provisioning and audit logs:** Enterprise admin controls.

#### Pricing

To access Agents, you need Notion's Business subscription ($20/month) or the custom-priced Enterprise tier.

Custom Agents work with Notion credits, billed at $10 for 1,000 monthly credits on top of the per-seat cost. Model access covers the Claude, GPT, and Gemini families.

#### Limitations

Agents work on Notion content. Point one at a folder of CSVs on your desktop, and it has nothing to do. The credit model also makes it hard to forecast heavy usage: two teams on Business at $20/seat can end up with very different bills depending on their agents' activity.

### Microsoft 365 Copilot

Copilot Cowork is the agent layer in Microsoft 365 Copilot, and yes, Microsoft gave it the same name as the tool you are leaving. You describe an outcome to achieve, and it works on your emails, meetings, files, and Teams conversations, then delivers a finished output. If your company already runs on Microsoft 365, this is the closest thing to Cowork and it lives where your data already is.

It also fixes Cowork's desktop problem: Cowork runs autonomously in your Microsoft 365 tenant, not on your laptop, so a long task keeps going while you are offline. Another advantage: permissions. Cowork reads what is on your machine, while Copilot Cowork already knows who can see which document and inherits that visibility, without having to hand files over to an app.

The downside is billing. Cowork requires a Microsoft 365 Copilot license upfront, and the work you delegate is metered in credits on top, so the sticker price says little about the actual bill.

#### Key features

- 
**Work IQ grounding:** Reasons over your organization's emails, meetings, files, and Teams conversations, for outputs based on your real data.
- 
**Autonomous execution:** Plans and executes multi-step work in your tenant, sending emails, booking meetings, and drafting documents, then delivers a finished output.
- 
**Inherited permissions:** Respects Microsoft 365's existing access controls without requiring separate file access.
- 
**Live cost control:** Type `/cost` in a task to see credits consumed.

#### Pricing

Two layers, only the first is fixed:

- **Microsoft 365 Copilot license:** Required, $30/user/month (annual), itself an add-on to an eligible Microsoft 365 plan.
- **Copilot credits:** Agent work is metered at $0.01/credit on a pay-as-you-go basis. No credits are included with the license.
- **Cost per task:** Rough estimates: ~$1–3 for light, $4–7 for medium, and more for heavy, depending on model, context, tool calls, and duration.

#### Limitations

Pricing is the real limit. A single heavy task can cost more than a day of user seats, and since cost scales with delegated work rather than seat count, the "license everyone and forget it" model doesn't hold up.

It's also 100% Microsoft. Cowork makes the most sense if your work lives in Outlook, Teams, and SharePoint, and serves little purpose otherwise. And you can't choose the model for a Cowork task the way you would when building a custom agent in Copilot Studio.
