Model distillation is a related technique that involves training a smaller or less capable model from the outputs of a larger and more powerful model. The goal is to transfer the knowledge and behaviors of the larger model to the smaller one, often resulting in faster and more efficient models with comparable performance. For example, since the release of Deepseek R1, many smaller models have been developed from its responses and have achieved remarkable quality relative to their size.

### What is the next-token prediction task and why is it important? What are base models?

Next-token prediction, also known as autoregressive language modeling, is the core training task behind most large language models. The model is trained to predict the next token in a sequence from all previous tokens. This simple objective allows the model to learn grammar, facts, reasoning patterns, and even some planning abilities. The result of this initial training phase is called a "base model."

Assistant models are foundation models that have been fine-tuned to provide more effective, safer, and more conversational assistance. This fine-tuning typically involves techniques such as supervised instruction tuning and reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF). reinforcement learning from human feedback (RLHF), which guide the model to respond more like an assistant rather than simply completing text.

### What is the "human-in-the-loop" (HITL) approach?

The "human-in-the-loop" (HITL) approach refers to the involvement of humans in the training, evaluation, or real-time use of an LLM system or an agentic AI system. Human intervention can occur at different stages: during model training (for example, data labeling, ranking responses), during fine-tuning (as in RLHF), or even during execution, where a human can guide or approve an agent's actions.

For example, if a chatbot asks you to choose the best of two responses, you are actively participating in a HITL process. This approach helps improve the quality, safety, and alignment of models by incorporating human judgment where automation alone may prove insufficient.
