## T6. 2026 cost shock — memory prices, tariffs, and their SMB impact (new dimension)

A 2026-wide component/tariff shock is directly reshaping SMB networking prices — material for total-cost-of-ownership research [independent/secondary]:

- **Ubiquiti Memory Surcharge — official notice, posted July 1, 2026** (Streakwave mirror) [official via partner doc]: applies to "global memory and storage market conditions" plus tariffs. Switch table: ECS-Aggregation $3,999 + **$315**; ECS-Core $4,999 + **$394**; ECS-48-PoE $3,499 + **$276**; ECS-48S $2,999 + **$236**; ECS-48S-PoE $3,999 + **$315**; USW-16-PoE $299 + $23; USW-24 $225 + $17; USW-24-PoE $379 + $29; USW-48 $399 + $31; USW-48-PoE $589 + $46; USW-Aggregation $269 + $21; USW-Enterprise-8-PoE $479 + $37; UDB-Switch $299 + $23; USP-RPS $399 + $31.
  Source: https://blog.streakwave.com/hubfs/Ubiquiti/2026/Notice-Ubiquiti-Memory-Surcharge-July-2026-with-Tariffs.pdf
- **Cisco price hikes (Q2 2026 earnings call, Feb 2026)** [secondary]: Cisco raised hardware list prices to cover memory-cost inflation; CEO Chuck Robbins said Cisco will "continue to monitor market trends and make additional adjustments as necessary"; CFO Mark Patterson said no customer deferrals observed. Q2 FY26 revenue $15.3B (record, +10% YoY).
  Source: https://www.theregister.com/on-prem/2026/02/12/cisco-hikes-prices-to-cover-memory-cost-rises/4904306
- **Dell'Oro Q2 2026 campus-switch report (Sep 8, 2026)** [secondary/vendor-reported via PRNewswire]: component shortages hit campus-switch availability and prices; **average selling price +19% YoY**; "every vendor outside China grew campus switch ASP on a year-over-year basis"; memory shortages expected to shape the market "well into 2027"; **Cisco, Arista, Ubiquiti and Extreme gained share** YoY; public cloud-managed switch revenue "bounced back and outgrew the total market."
  Source: https://www.wcia.com/business/press-releases/cision/20260908SF41495/market-prices-skyrocket-driving-campus-switch-market-to-growth-in-2q-2026-according-to-delloro-group/
- **FCC ban on Chinese-made routers (2026)** [secondary]: US FCC action against Chinese networking hardware reshapes the market — Morgan Stanley estimated average US router prices could rise **15–25%** as TP-Link inventory drains (channel stock estimated 3–6 months); Netgear singled out as a margin beneficiary (29% gross margins could expand); enterprise procurement (NIST 2024 supply-chain guidelines, NDAA/TAA) further advantages US/allied vendors. TP-Link is the most exposed brand [secondary — analysis, not official FCC text].
  Source: https://www.webpronews.com/fccs-ban-on-chinese-made-routers-reshapes-the-networking-hardware-market-and-creates-clear-winners/
- Tariff commentary for SMB buyers [secondary]: 10% tariffs on non-China imports; industry estimates of 20–30% networking-equipment price increases; availability delays as manufacturers rethink supply chains.
  Source: https://www.schillingit.com/uncategorized/a-new-tariff-in-town-navigating-rising-costs-in-computer-and-network-hardware/
- **Flag:** TP-Link's exposure to the FCC ban could materially change its SMB price-leader position — the defining 2026 SMB-market variable, unconfirmed outcome [unverified].
