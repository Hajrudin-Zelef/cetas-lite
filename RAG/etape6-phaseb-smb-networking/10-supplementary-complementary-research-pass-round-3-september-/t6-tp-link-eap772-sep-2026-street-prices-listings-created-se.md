## T11. Supply/availability context — the 2026 memory-price crisis ("memflation") [independent/secondary]

- Directly relevant to 2026 networking hardware pricing (explains Ubiquiti's $394 "memory surcharge" on the Enterprise Campus Switch Core, §1.1/S9):
  - **TrendForce (Feb 2026)**: Q1 2026 conventional DRAM contract prices **+90–95% QoQ** (up from earlier 55–60% forecast); NAND **+55–60% QoQ**; enterprise SSD **+53–58% QoQ** — record quarterly increases [secondary].
  - **Gartner**: DRAM forecast **+47% in 2026**; separately coined **"memflation"** — forecast ~130% combined DRAM+SSD surge by end 2026 [secondary].
  - **Counterpoint (Feb 2026)**: consumer/legacy DRAM & NAND for broadband equipment **up >600% YoY**; memory now **>20% of BoM in low-to-mid routers** (was ~3% a year ago); routers/gateways/set-top boxes hit hardest; prices expected to keep rising at least through June 2026 [secondary via Slashdot summary].
  - **SK Hynix**: HBM/DRAM/NAND capacity **"essentially sold out" for 2026** (Oct 2025 call); **Micron exited the consumer memory market** to focus on enterprise/AI; Samsung raised 32GB DDR5 modules to $239 from $149 (+60%) in Sep 2025 [secondary].
  - **Q3 2026 (TrendForce)**: increases cooling to +13–18% QoQ DRAM / +10–15% NAND as consumers hit affordability ceilings — but supply remains tight; AI/inference demand keeps pressure on through 2027 [secondary].
- Implication for networking buyers 2026: BOM pressure on switches/routers (memory + packet-buffer + flash); vendor surcharges and shorter price-list validity; prioritize in-stock checks over MSRP for budget planning [independent assessment].
