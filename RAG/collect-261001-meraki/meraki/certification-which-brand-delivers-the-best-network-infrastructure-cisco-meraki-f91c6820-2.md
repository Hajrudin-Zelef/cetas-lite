# certification-which-brand-delivers-the-best-network-infrastructure-cisco-meraki--f91c6820

Aruba Networks offers a wide range of products designed to meet the needs of modern enterprises. The company’s solutions are built around the concepts of security, flexibility, and performance. Aruba provides customers with options for cloud-based, on-premises, and hybrid networking deployments, allowing organizations to choose the best option for their network infrastructure. One of the most significant differences between Cisco Meraki and Aruba Networks is their approach to cloud management. Cisco Meraki has a strong cloud-first philosophy, meaning that all of its products, from wireless access points to switches and security appliances, are managed through a centralized, cloud-based dashboard. This centralized management platform provides a simple, intuitive interface for network administrators to configure, monitor, and troubleshoot their network from anywhere in the world. The Meraki approach eliminates the need for extensive technical expertise and allows IT teams to manage their networks with minimal on-site intervention. This approach is particularly well-suited for businesses that prioritize ease of use and remote management, especially SMBs or organizations with distributed locations.

Aruba Networks, on the other hand, offers greater flexibility in deployment options. While Aruba Central provides a cloud-based management platform, Aruba also allows for on-premises and hybrid deployment models. This flexibility appeals to businesses that require more control over their network infrastructure, whether due to compliance, security, or specific operational needs. For example, some organizations may prefer to manage certain aspects of their network locally to ensure tighter control over sensitive data or to meet specific regulatory requirements. Aruba’s ability to cater to both cloud and on-premises management provides businesses with more deployment choices compared to Meraki’s strictly cloud-managed platform.

**Winner: Tie – depends on your deployment preference.**

Cisco Meraki is widely recognized for its ease of use, particularly for organizations with limited technical resources or smaller IT teams. The Meraki dashboard is designed to be intuitive and user-friendly, allowing administrators to quickly configure and manage their network. The platform’s simplicity is one of its most significant selling points. Meraki’s plug-and-play deployment and cloud-first architecture make it easy to set up and scale networks with minimal training. The lack of reliance on command-line interface (CLI) commands also makes it an appealing option for businesses that do not have highly specialized network administrators.

Aruba Networks, while also providing a user-friendly interface, offers more complexity and customization options, particularly for businesses with more advanced networking needs. Aruba’s platform allows for greater granularity in network configuration and management, which is particularly useful for larger enterprises or organizations with specialized IT staff. The ability to control more aspects of the network through CLI or customized configurations gives experienced network administrators greater flexibility but may require a higher level of expertise compared to Meraki’s streamlined approach.

**Winner: Cisco Meraki – especially for beginner-friendly management.**

Both Cisco Meraki and Aruba Networks provide robust SD-WAN solutions, but they differ in the level of customization and feature sets. Meraki’s SD-WAN capabilities are integrated into its MX series appliances, offering features such as VPN auto-provisioning, policy-based routing, and application-aware traffic shaping. These features are easy to set up and manage, making Meraki’s SD-WAN ideal for businesses that require basic SD-WAN functionality and prefer an easier configuration process. The simplicity of Meraki’s SD-WAN is well-suited for small to medium-sized businesses or companies with less complex WAN requirements.

Aruba Networks offers more advanced SD-WAN capabilities through its EdgeConnect platform. Aruba’s SD-WAN solution includes features such as tunnel bonding, path conditioning, and unified security. These advanced features are designed for enterprises with more complex networking needs, particularly those with large-scale distributed environments. Aruba’s SD-WAN solution is better suited for large organizations that require more control and customization of their WAN environment, offering greater flexibility for network traffic optimization and redundancy.

**Winner: Aruba – offers a deeper SD-WAN feature set.**

When it comes to security, both Cisco Meraki and Aruba Networks offer robust solutions, but the approach each takes differs in terms of depth and focus. Meraki integrates security features directly into its platform, including unified threat management, intrusion prevention, and integrated VPN capabilities. Meraki’s security features are automated and simplified, with firmware updates pushed automatically through the cloud to ensure that devices are always running the latest security patches. This makes Meraki’s security approach easy to manage and is ideal for organizations with limited security expertise or those looking for a streamlined, hands-off approach to network security.

Aruba Networks takes a more advanced, enterprise-grade approach to security with its Zero Trust security model. Aruba’s ClearPass Policy Manager enables businesses to enforce granular access control policies based on user identity, device type, and location. This allows for more detailed and customizable security enforcement, making it suitable for businesses that require sophisticated security policies across their network. Aruba’s emphasis on Zero Trust security is critical for enterprises that need to secure a wide range of devices, including mobile devices and IoT, across large and complex networks.

**Winner: Aruba – slightly more robust for enterprise-grade security.**

Both Cisco Meraki and Aruba offer switches that cater to different types of networks. Meraki’s switch portfolio includes both Layer 2 and Layer 3 switches that are fully integrated with the Meraki Dashboard. These switches offer ease of use, with features such as Power over Ethernet (PoE), multicast routing, and Quality of Service (QoS). Meraki’s switches are ideal for organizations looking for a plug-and-play solution that integrates seamlessly with the Meraki platform and requires minimal configuration.

Aruba, on the other hand, offers a broader range of high-performance switches, many of which are designed for larger, more complex environments. Aruba’s switches support Layer 3 routing, and many models come with lifetime warranties. Additionally, Aruba’s switches often provide more granular control over network configurations, making them a better fit for enterprises that require advanced features or CLI-based management. Aruba also supports more complex network topologies, making it suitable for organizations with high-performance needs or those requiring redundancy and advanced routing features.

**Winner: Aruba – better performance and value for long-term deployments.**

Cisco Meraki follows a subscription-based licensing model, which includes support, firmware updates, and access to the cloud management platform. This model provides businesses with an all-inclusive package, simplifying budgeting and management. However, Meraki’s subscription-based approach can become costly for larger deployments, as businesses will need to pay ongoing subscription fees based on the number of devices or users.
