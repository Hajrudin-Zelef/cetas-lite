# Code World Model: The True Rival of Genie 3?

What I like about Code World Model isn't the promise of replacing Genie 3 tomorrow morning — clearly, that's not the case, and the researchers themselves present it as a proof of concept. What matters is the architectural bet: instead of staking everything on a single giant neural network to carry the logic *and* the visuals of an entire world, CWM puts a brick of determinism — code, which can be read and corrected — back at the heart of a generative system. We find a bit of the same intuition as in the modular coder-agent architectures emerging elsewhere: clearly separating what reasons, what decides, and what executes, rather than mixing everything into a single black box.

Let's be honest: for now, Genie 3 remains well ahead on visual quality and fluidity, and CWM is anything but a finished product. But if the idea of separating logic and rendering holds up at a larger scale, it could become the real answer to the problem I already described in my article on the death of LLMs in the face of world models: a world that truly remembers what happens in it, instead of a world that dreams with each frame. One to watch very closely.
