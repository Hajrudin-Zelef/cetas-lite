# The Death Of LLMs: World Models Will Change Everything In 2026

For 2 years, AIs have been speaking better than ever. They write, explain, program. At first glance, a decisive milestone has been crossed. And yet, a malaise persists. Because **these systems know how to describe the world, but not how to live in it**. They talk about the world without ever having experienced it. And it's precisely this limitation that could change everything in 2026.

## Yann LeCun's diagnosis

Yann LeCun, one of the fathers of Deep Learning, has been hammering home a brutal but coherent observation for years: **language models are brilliant but fundamentally blind to reality**. They learn to talk about the world by reading texts, as if one could understand physics by leafing through a dictionary.

For LeCun, language is not the origin of intelligence. It's a late layer built on something far more fundamental: an intuitive understanding of the world, of movement, of objects, and of the physical consequences of our actions. And that's precisely where everything shifted.

## From discourse to experience: World Models

The "all-language" paradigm has hit a ceiling. Another approach is emerging — more radical: teaching AI to no longer predict words, but to **predict the world itself**. This shift has a name: *World Models*.

A World Model is not a pair of eyes. It's a **multisensory internal simulation engine**. It no longer learns through libraries of PDFs but through the massive ingestion of video streams coupled with force, pressure, and motion data. It doesn't try to guess the next word but to build an understanding of Newtonian laws.

## The JEPA architecture: predicting in the abstract

At the heart of this revolution: the **JEPA** architecture (Joint Embedding Predictive Architecture). The rupture: the difference between *generating* and *predicting*.

Sora attempts to reconstruct every pixel — a titanic task that collapses in the face of noise. JEPA works exclusively in a **latent space**. It transforms the world into abstract mathematical vectors. The model doesn't ask itself "what will be the exact color of the pixel," but "what will be the semantic and physical state of the object." By ignoring superfluous details, it focuses on physical invariants and causality.

The key module, the *controller*, develops an **intuitive physics**. It doesn't see a cup fall — it infers its mass, its friction, its inertia. This is exactly what your brain does: if you push a cup, you don't calculate the trajectory of every atom. You instantly project the state "object broken on the floor" and your motor system adjusts the pressure of your fingers.

## The war of doctrines

**Yann LeCun and AMI Labs** (which he founded after leaving Meta) are betting on radical efficiency with V-JEPA. To understand the world, one must predict it in the shadows, in latent space. The goal: to achieve a learning efficiency close to that of living beings. Where an LLM reads the entire internet to learn a rule, V-JEPA learns causality by observing a few hours of raw video.

**OpenAI with Sora 2 Pro** remains faithful to its philosophy: the bigger it is, the smarter it is. Making the laws of physics emerge through the *brute force of generation* ("video autoregressive modeling"). By recreating ultra-realistic videos down to the pixel, OpenAI hopes the model will eventually deduce physics to avoid visual errors. Costly in GPUs, with no guarantee of causal understanding.

**Nvidia with Cosmos** stunned CES 2026 with World Foundation Models designed to be the operating system for factories and autonomous cars. Nvidia literally tokenizes physical reality to make it digestible by its Blackwell chips.

## Why we don't yet have an efficient domestic robot

The answer in one word: **unpredictability**. Classical robotics relied on rigid programming. The robot fails as soon as reality deviates by a millimeter from its code. It was a *reactive* intelligence: an error had to occur for the machine to correct it.

With World Models as a motor brain, that changes. Robots like Tesla Optimus Gen 3 or Figure 02 **simulate the action in their latent space before executing it**. When a robot grasps a slippery object, its world model senses the coming instability and adjusts its pressure before the physical accident. We're moving from a machine that endures reality to a machine that precedes it.

## The future: LLM + World Model

The World Models revolution doesn't mean the death of LLMs. We're heading toward a fusion: **LWM** (Large World Models). The architecture is inspired by human neurobiology:

- **The LLM = the prefrontal cortex**: language, long-term planning, abstraction. It understands concepts and intentions.
- **The World Model = the motor cortex**: sensory perception, spatial geometry, real-time dynamics. It doesn't think in words, it acts in force vectors and trajectories.

The LLM breaks down the sentence (object: blue cup, constraint: fragile, mission: displacement). It sends a high-level command to the World Model, which simulates the path, anticipates obstacles, and adjusts each servomotor. **The LLM gives the order, the World Model guarantees that the order survives physical reality.**

## My opinion

2026 marks a turning point. AI is definitively leaving the virtual world to anchor itself in physical reality. It no longer settles for answering — it understands the constraints of the real world, anticipates the consequences of its actions, and acts coherently. **We're moving from an AI that talks about the world to an AI that acts in the world, alongside us.**
